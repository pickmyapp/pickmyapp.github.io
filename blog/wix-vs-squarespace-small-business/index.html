Wix vs Squarespace for Small Business in 2026?

  • Website Builders
  • Published Oct 9, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most small businesses, Wix is the better pick: it is cheaper to start ($17/mo vs $19/mo annually), has a far larger template library and app market, and includes ecommerce from the $29/mo Core plan. Choose Squarespace if design matters more than features — its templates look polished with minimal effort, it handles blogging and client scheduling well, and its editor is harder to mess up. After Squarespace's July 2026 price increase, both cost $29/mo at the mid tier. Prices checked October 2026.

Wix and Squarespace are the two website builders most small businesses shortlist when they want a professional site without hiring a developer. Both are all-in-one platforms: hosting, templates, a visual editor, and business tools in one subscription. But they have opposite philosophies. Wix gives you maximum freedom — drag anything anywhere, bolt on hundreds of apps — while Squarespace gives you maximum guardrails — a constrained editor whose templates look good almost by default. This guide compares them for small businesses of roughly 1 to 50 people: pricing, ease of use, templates, ecommerce, SEO, and blogging, with prices checked against both vendors' pricing pages in October 2026. If you are open to a self-hosted alternative with deeper SEO control, our cheap WordPress hosting guide covers the other side of this decision.

What is Wix in 2026?

Wix is the most feature-rich all-in-one website builder, built around a drag-and-drop editor and a large app market. The product most owners use is the classic Wix editor (Wix Studio is its pro-grade sibling for designers): pick from over 2,000 templates, place elements freely on the page, and extend the site with more than 800 apps covering bookings, restaurants, events, chat, and marketing. Wix also offers an AI website builder that generates a starter site from a prompt, which you then refine by hand.

Every plan includes a custom domain (free for the first year), managed hosting, AI creation tools, and 24/7 customer care. Wix is the only one of the two with a free plan — useful for testing, though it shows Wix ads and uses a wixsite.com subdomain, so it is not a real business presence. Premium plans come with a 14-day money-back guarantee. The main long-term caveat: you cannot switch templates after your site is live. A redesign means rebuilding the site, so choose carefully.

What is Squarespace in 2026?

Squarespace is the design-first website builder, famous for templates that look finished with minimal tweaking. All templates run on the same 7.1 framework, which means you can switch designs freely after launch — the opposite of Wix's lock-in. The Fluid Engine editor works on a constrained grid: fewer choices per page, but the default typography and spacing are excellent, and it is genuinely hard to produce an ugly site.

Squarespace's business toolkit is built in rather than bolted on: scheduling and client bookings (via its Acuity integration), invoicing, email campaigns, memberships and courses, and a blog that supports both categories and tags. Every plan includes a free custom domain for the first year, built-in AI content tools, and a free Google Workspace email account for the first year on the Core plan and above. There is no free plan — only a 14-day free trial. Note that Squarespace renamed its plans (Personal became Basic, Business became Core, the commerce tiers became Plus and Advanced), so older reviews use different names.

How do Wix and Squarespace pricing compare?

The pricing story changed in 2026. Squarespace raised its prices in July 2026 — Core went from $23 to $29 per month on annual billing — which erased the price advantage most older comparisons give it. At the tier most small businesses actually buy, the two platforms now cost exactly the same. Most published guides still quote the old Squarespace prices, so check the vendor pages before deciding.

What does the same business pay on each?

Take a typical service business that needs a custom domain, a booking page, and a small store. On Wix, Core at $29 a month annually covers it: basic ecommerce, scheduling, and payments are all in. On Squarespace, Core at $29 a month annually covers it too — with no transaction fees on the store, which Wix's Core also avoids. At the mid tier, the decision is about the product, not the price.

The difference shows at the edges. At the entry tier, Wix Light ($17) undercuts Squarespace Basic ($19) — but Squarespace Basic can sell products (with a 2 percent fee) while Wix Light cannot sell at all. At the top tier, Wix Business Elite ($159) is far more expensive than Squarespace Advanced ($99), though few small businesses need either. Watch Wix's app market for hidden costs: paid apps stacked on top of the plan can push your real monthly bill above the headline price. Squarespace builds most of its tools in, so the plan price is closer to the total.

Quick fact: Squarespace's July 2026 price increase raised Core from $23 to $29 per month on annual billing. If a comparison tells you Squarespace is the cheaper mid-tier option, it is working from outdated numbers.

Which is easier to use?

The editor philosophy

Wix is a free canvas: drag any element anywhere, resize by pixel, layer freely. That freedom is powerful, but independent testers consistently report the same side effect — beginners build cluttered pages, and the mobile version often needs manual fixing because the editor positions by pixel rather than adapting to the screen. Squarespace's Fluid Engine is the opposite: a structured grid with fewer choices, which means fewer ways to go wrong. Typography pairs well out of the box, and layouts stay clean on mobile without extra work. If you do not want to think about design, Squarespace holds your hand. If you want total control, Wix hands you the keys.

Getting launched

Both get a simple business site live in a few days. In timed tests, Squarespace tends to edge ahead (roughly three and a half hours to a publishable site versus about four for Wix), because its templates need less cleanup before they feel cohesive. Wix's AI website builder narrows the gap: answer a few prompts and you get a reasonable starting point, though you will still spend an afternoon refining it.

How do the templates compare?

Wix ships over 2,000 templates across industries — restaurants, salons, portfolios, stores — so you will almost always find one close to your business. The catch, again, is that the template locks at publish: changing it later means rebuilding. Squarespace offers around 200 templates, all built on the same 7.1 framework, and you can switch between them freely after launch. In practice, testers report finding three Squarespace templates they would ship unmodified, versus ten Wix contenders that mostly needed cleanup first. Quantity favors Wix; ready-to-ship quality favors Squarespace.

Which is better for selling online?

Starting a small store

Both handle a small catalog well: product pages, Stripe and PayPal, Apple Pay, discount codes, and tax calculation. Squarespace's entry point is friendlier — you can sell on the $19 Basic plan (with a 2 percent transaction fee), while Wix requires the $29 Core plan for ecommerce. Squarespace also charges no transaction fee from Core upward, and its setup flow is faster for a catalog under 30 products. Wix counters with more built-in selling features at each tier: abandoned-cart recovery, more shipping options, and a deeper app market for marketplace integrations.

Fees that actually matter

Look past the plan price to the per-sale math. On Squarespace, payment processing starts at 2.9 percent plus 30 cents (dropping to 2.5 percent on Advanced), and digital products and memberships carry an extra fee — 7 percent on Basic, 5 percent on Core, 1 percent on Plus, zero on Advanced. That digital-content fee matters if you sell courses or memberships: on Basic it is a real bite. Wix does not break out transaction fees the same way; its ecommerce costs are bundled into the plan plus payment processing. For more than about 50 products, or multi-channel selling across marketplaces, neither platform is the right answer — a dedicated commerce platform like Shopify is. But for a small business selling its own products from its own site, both are legitimate.

How do they compare on SEO and marketing?

SEO toolkit

Wix has the broader SEO toolkit of the two. It covers the essentials — editable titles, descriptions, URLs, sitemaps, redirects — and its app market adds deeper options for schema markup and bulk editing as your content grows. Squarespace covers the basics competently but offers less control: fewer advanced settings, a thinner ecosystem of SEO add-ons, and less flexibility around technical elements. For a business that will publish content to win local or organic search, Wix gives you more room to grow. For a business whose site is a digital brochure plus a contact form, either is fine.

Marketing built in

Both bundle email campaigns, social posting, and basic automation. Wix's marketing suite is deeper at higher tiers, and its app market lets you add specialized tools — loyalty programs, reviews, live chat — that Squarespace either lacks or charges for separately. Squarespace's email campaigns are well-designed and simple, which suits a small list. If email becomes a serious channel, price a dedicated tool: our cheapest email marketing software guide shows what it costs to do properly.

How do they compare for blogging?

Squarespace wins on blogging. Its blog supports both categories and tags, the post editor is clean, and posts inherit the template's typography, so articles look good with no effort. For a business whose blog is a real marketing channel — or a creator whose site is the business — Squarespace's blogging experience is the best among all-in-one builders. Wix's blog is functional but plainer, and its strengths lie elsewhere. If your content plan is ambitious, also consider that neither builder matches WordPress for publishing power; see our WordPress hosting guide for the full trade-off.

How do they compare on performance and mobile?

Both host your site on their own infrastructure with strong uptime, so there is nothing to configure. Independent Lighthouse tests tend to score Squarespace slightly higher on mobile (roughly 89 versus Wix's 84), reflecting its more disciplined templates. The practical gap is smaller than the numbers suggest — both are fast enough for a small business site — but the mobile editing story differs more. Squarespace's structured editor keeps layouts intact on phones with little intervention. Wix's pixel-positioned editor means you should check and adjust the mobile view of every page before publishing. Budget that time.

One more long-term consideration: platform lock-in. Neither Wix nor Squarespace lets you export a clean, portable copy of your site. Moving to a different platform later means rebuilding. Treat the choice as a two-year commitment and pick the platform whose limits you can live with, not just the one whose demo impressed you.

Which should you choose: Wix or Squarespace?

Choose Wix if

  • You want maximum design freedom and a huge template library.
  • You need specialized features from the app market (bookings, restaurants, events).
  • You plan serious content marketing and want the broader SEO toolkit.
  • You want the cheapest professional start ($17/mo) or a free plan to experiment.
  • Your store needs deeper ecommerce features as it grows.

Choose Squarespace if

  • Design quality matters more than feature quantity — you want polish fast.
  • You are a creative, portfolio, or design-led brand.
  • Blogging is a core channel (categories, tags, and a clean editor).
  • You take client bookings and want scheduling built in, not bolted on.
  • You sell digital products or memberships and want the fee structure simple.

You do not have to guess. Wix's free plan and 14-day money-back guarantee, and Squarespace's 14-day trial, let you build a real homepage in each before paying. Try the exact pages your business needs — the booking flow, the store checkout, the blog — not just the homepage. Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Wix or Squarespace?

At the entry tier, Wix: Light costs $17 per month billed annually against Squarespace Basic at $19. At the tier most small businesses buy, they are now level — Wix Core and Squarespace Core both cost $29 per month billed annually after Squarespace's July 2026 price increase. Wix is much more expensive at the top tier ($159 vs $99). Note that most published comparisons still quote Squarespace's older, lower prices.

Is Wix better than Squarespace for SEO?

Wix has the edge for small businesses that plan to do serious content marketing. Its SEO toolkit is broader and its app market adds options like advanced schema and redirection tools. Squarespace covers the basics well — titles, descriptions, sitemaps — but offers less control. Neither matches WordPress for SEO depth; if organic search will drive your growth, read our cheap WordPress hosting guide first.

Can I sell products on the cheapest Wix and Squarespace plans?

On Squarespace Basic ($19 per month annually) you can sell, but you pay a 2 percent transaction fee on the online store plus payment processing. Wix Light ($17 annually) does not include ecommerce — you need Core ($29 annually) for that. Both charge no extra transaction fee once you step up to their mid tiers.

Can I switch my website from Wix to Squarespace later?

There is no clean export between the two. Moving means rebuilding the site on the new platform and manually migrating content, images, and products. Export tools and CSV imports can move blog posts and product lists, but the design must be rebuilt. Pick with a two-year horizon, not a two-month one.

Does Squarespace or Wix have a free plan?

Wix has a free plan — it runs Wix ads and a wixsite.com subdomain, so it is for testing, not for a real business. Squarespace has no free plan, only a 14-day free trial. For a professional site with a custom domain, both require a paid plan: $17 per month annually for Wix Light or $19 for Squarespace Basic.

Conclusion

For most small businesses, Wix is the more capable choice: a lower starting price, far more templates, a much larger app market, and ecommerce from the $29 Core plan. Squarespace earns its place for businesses where the website is the brand — creatives, portfolios, and design-led services — with templates that look finished fast, the best built-in blogging of any all-in-one builder, and scheduling that just works. Since the July 2026 price increase, the mid tier costs the same $29 on both, so let the product decide: try the actual pages your business needs in each trial, and pick the one your team will happily maintain in year two.
