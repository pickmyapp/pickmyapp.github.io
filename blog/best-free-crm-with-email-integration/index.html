What “email integration” actually means (sync vs tracking vs templates)

Vendors all say "email integration," but they mean three different things, and the difference decides whether the free plan is useful to you or a trap.

Two-way sync is the real thing. Connect Gmail or Outlook, and every email you send or receive with a contact is automatically logged on that contact's record — no copy-paste, no BCC address to remember. Freshsales includes this free, and Capsule syncs Gmail and Outlook free too. Demand this if your problem is "nobody knows what was promised to the customer last Tuesday."

Email tracking tells you when a recipient opened your email or clicked a link — useful for sales follow-up, but it does not log the conversation. HubSpot's free plan includes tracking. Templates are stored standard replies you send in a click; HubSpot free includes templates and scheduling, Zoho free includes templates. Templates save typing time, but on their own they are barely integration — nothing is logged unless someone does it by hand.

Ask one question when comparing free plans: does an email I send appear on the contact record without me doing anything? If yes, you have sync. If the answer is "BCC a special address" or "log it manually," you have a workaround — and at 50 emails a day, workarounds collapse within a week.

HubSpot CRM free: the biggest free toolkit, now with real limits

HubSpot's free CRM is still the most capable free plan on the market — just no longer the unlimited one. Checked November 8, 2026, it gives you 2 users, 1,000 contacts, 1 deal pipeline, 2,000 marketing emails a month, plus email tracking, templates, scheduling, live chat, forms, and Breeze Assistant AI. That marketing allowance alone beats most rivals: 2,000 emails a month means a small list can run a real newsletter without paying.

The catch is the ceiling. Two users means you plus one colleague, and 1,000 contacts means a business adding 100 contacts a month outgrows it in under a year. One pipeline is fine until you run two kinds of sales — say, new business and renewals — and need them tracked separately. Our HubSpot free vs paid guide breaks down what free leaves out, and our HubSpot vs Pipedrive comparison covers the paid decision. HubSpot fits a solo operator or pair who accepts paying $20 per user per month (Starter) within a year; at 5 users that is $100 a month, so budget for it honestly.

Zoho CRM free: the most contacts for free, with strings attached

Zoho's free plan is the quiet value pick: 3 users, 5,000 records, 5 workflow automation rules, custom modules, fields and layouts, email integration with templates, and 1 GB of file storage. Five thousand records is five times HubSpot's free allowance, and three users covers a small team. The five workflow rules are a real bonus — enough to assign new leads or send a welcome email automatically, which no other free plan here offers.

The strings are real, though. Free includes no third-party integrations, so your CRM cannot sync with your other tools unless you pay — and for a CRM, integrations are half the product. Email integration here means templates and an inbox connection, not the full two-way sync Freshsales offers, and Zoho's setup takes longer than HubSpot's or Capsule's. Paid Standard starts at $14 per user per month, and Bigin — Zoho's small-business CRM — starts at $7. Our best free CRM for startups guide puts Zoho alongside the other startup-friendly options.

Freshsales free: the only true two-way email sync at $0

Freshsales (from Freshworks) wins this roundup on the one feature the headline promises. Its free plan covers up to 3 users, with built-in phone, two-way email sync, Freddy AI contact scoring, one sales pipeline, and a Kanban view. Two-way sync means emails you send and receive are logged to the contact automatically — the difference between a CRM people actually use and a database people update on Fridays and resent.

Freddy AI scoring is an unusual free extra: the system scores contacts so you can see at a glance who is warm. The limit that matters is automation: there is no workflow automation on the free plan, so lead assignment and follow-ups are manual until you pay. Freshsales is the pick if your team lives in email and you want the CRM to keep itself updated; it is less compelling if you need marketing emails at volume — that is HubSpot's strength. Paid Growth starts at $9 per user per month billed annually, the cheapest upgrade here; Pro is $39 and Enterprise $59, per the cluster brief checked November 5, 2026. See our Freshsales vs Pipedrive comparison for the head-to-head.

Capsule free: the simplest pick — and Bitrix24, the unlimited-users outlier

Capsule is the smallest, cleanest free plan here: 2 users, 250 contacts, 1 sales pipeline, email integration with Gmail and Outlook, tasks, and 10 MB of file storage. Two hundred fifty contacts sounds tiny, and it is — but for a freelancer or a two-person consultancy with a short client list, it is enough, and the payoff is a CRM with almost no learning curve. Connect Gmail or Outlook, emails sync to contacts, tasks keep follow-ups visible. Paid Starter is $18 per user per month billed annually (30,000 contacts); Growth at $36 adds automation and multiple pipelines. If you work alone, read our best CRM for freelancers guide — Capsule features there for the same reasons.

Bitrix24 is the outlier: its free plan has no user cap and 5 GB of storage. With five, ten, or twenty people and zero budget, it is the only free CRM here that does not make you choose who gets a seat. The trade-off is the interface — Bitrix24 packs CRM, project management, chat, and more into one product, and it feels heavier than any of the four above. Email integration is included, but expect a longer setup. It suits a larger team that genuinely cannot pay yet, not a solo operator who values simplicity. (Also worth a glance: EngageBay free offers 15 users, 250 contacts, and 1,000 emails a month.)

The wall you'll hit first — and what “free” costs you later

Every free CRM has a wall, and the honest way to choose is to pick the wall you will hit last. HubSpot's wall is 1,000 contacts: adding 50–100 contacts a month, you hit it within a year; the fix is Starter at $20 per user per month, though the 2-user limit usually bites first. Zoho's wall is integrations: 5,000 records last a long time, but the moment you want your CRM talking to your email tool, accounting software, or Slack, you pay — budget Standard at $14 per user per month as Zoho's real price.

Freshsales' wall is automation: three users and two-way sync carry you far, but manual lead assignment gets painful around 20–30 active deals. Growth at $9 per user per month is the cheapest fix here, making Freshsales the cheapest to outgrow gracefully. Capsule's wall is 250 contacts: a freelancer with a healthy network hits that in months, though Starter at $18 buys 30,000 contacts. Bitrix24's wall is complexity: no user cap means the wall is the day your team stops using the CRM because it feels like admin — the most expensive wall here, because it has no price tag.

The practical rule: estimate your contacts 12 months from now, count the people who need seats, and check whether the free plan still fits. If not, compare the paid prices above — the cheapest free plan is not always the cheapest CRM a year later.

Which one for your team size

Solo (just you). Capsule for the simplest setup with a list under 250 contacts; HubSpot for email marketing and AI help free with 1,000 contacts. Freshsales is overkill for one person but harmless — its sync still saves you time.

Two to three people. This is the sweet spot for almost every free plan here, so choose on the feature, not the seat count. Freshsales if email sync is the priority; Zoho if your list is already large or you want five free automations; HubSpot if one of you runs marketing emails. Avoid Capsule unless your list is genuinely small — 250 contacts split across three people runs out fast.

Five or more. Bitrix24 is the only free option that seats everyone; EngageBay's 15 free users are the runner-up. Everyone else charges per user from the third seat onward, so price the paid plan for your whole team before migrating data: at five users, Freshsales Growth is about $45 a month, Zoho Standard about $70, HubSpot Starter about $100 — all defensible, none of them free.

The decision in one line: name the one email feature you need — sync, tracking, or templates — and the choice makes itself. Sync points to Freshsales, tracking and marketing to HubSpot, templates plus contact headroom to Zoho, simplicity to Capsule. Use it for a month with real emails, then decide whether to pay. The wrong free CRM is not the one with the smallest limits; it is the one your team quietly stops opening.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free CRM with email integration?

Freshsales, with true two-way sync — emails are logged to contacts automatically — for up to 3 users free. HubSpot is best if you also want email marketing (2,000 marketing emails a month, tracking, templates); Zoho is best if you need the most free contacts (5,000 records for 3 users).

Is HubSpot CRM really free?

Yes, but with limits that changed in 2026: 2 users, 1,000 contacts, 1 deal pipeline, and 2,000 marketing emails a month, with tracking, templates, scheduling, live chat, and forms. Older guides quoting unlimited users and contacts describe the pre-2026 plan. Starter starts at $20 per user per month.

Which free CRM has two-way email sync?

Freshsales includes true two-way email sync on its free plan, and Capsule syncs Gmail and Outlook free. HubSpot and Zoho offer email integration with tracking and templates on free, but that is not the same as automatic two-way sync of every email. Check the current plan page, because sync features move between tiers.

What is the best free CRM for a small team of 3?

Zoho CRM or Freshsales. Zoho gives 3 users, 5,000 records, and 5 free workflow automations; Freshsales gives up to 3 users with two-way email sync and built-in phone. HubSpot only seats 2 free, so a 3-person team pays for the third seat. For 5 or more, Bitrix24 is the only free plan with no user cap.

What happens when I outgrow a free CRM?

You hit one limit first — usually users, contacts, pipelines, or automation — and then you pay the upgrade price. Cheapest here: Freshsales Growth at $9 per user per month (annual billing) and Zoho Standard at $14; HubSpot Starter is $20, Capsule Starter $18. Check current pricing before migrating, and export your data regularly so switching stays possible.

Conclusion

There is no single best free CRM with email integration — there is a best one for the email feature you actually need. Want emails logged to contacts without lifting a finger? Freshsales' free two-way sync is the honest answer. Want to send marketing emails and track opens free? HubSpot. Big list, small team? Zoho's 5,000 records win. One person who just wants the inbox and the contact list to agree? Capsule. Limits were checked on November 8, 2026 and free plans change — confirm them on the vendor's page, estimate where your contacts and team will be in a year, and choose the wall you will hit last.
