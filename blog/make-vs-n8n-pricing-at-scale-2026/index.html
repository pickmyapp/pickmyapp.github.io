Make scenarios vs n8n workflows: what pricing at scale really looks like (2026)

Quick answer

At scale, Make and n8n are priced on different units — and that decides everything. Make bills <strong>credits</strong> (one per module execution; 10,000/month starts at $9/mo annual and scales on a slider to 8M), while n8n bills <strong>executions</strong> (one per complete workflow run; Cloud Pro is €50/mo for 10,000, Pro 50K is €1,450/yr). At 100,000 workflow runs a month, Make lands around $99–$410/month (150k–500k credits on the slider, annual), n8n Cloud lands at €121–€1,438/month depending on the tier — but the winner flips with workflow shape: short, high-frequency workflows favor Make; long, loop-heavy workflows favor n8n, because one n8n run can contain dozens of Make credits. Above roughly 80–100k credits/month, self-hosted n8n on a $10–20 VPS (unlimited executions) starts beating both — if you have someone to run the server.

Stop comparing plans. Convert your volume first.

Every "Make vs n8n" pricing comparison makes the same mistake: it lines up the plans side by side. Starter vs Core, Pro vs Pro. That's comparing apples to bicycles, because the two platforms don't bill the same thing at all. The same billing-unit thinking applies to stores: our Zapier vs Make guide for ecommerce stores converts the comparison into cost per order.

Make bills credits. One credit = one module execution. A scenario with 12 modules that runs once burns 12 credits. Since August 2025 the billing unit is officially called "credits" (many guides still say "operations" — same thing, new name; if a blog says operations, it's either old or sloppy).

n8n bills executions. One execution = one complete workflow run, however many steps it has. A 40-node workflow that runs once burns 1 execution. Unlimited steps included.

This is the entire pricing-at-scale question in one paragraph: Make charges per step, n8n charges per run. Everything below — the sliders, the traps, the crossover math — follows from that. If you take nothing else from this article, take this: don't ask "which is cheaper." Ask "how many credits does my workload burn, and how many executions?" Our n8n vs Make overview covers features and fit; this article is the money.

How Make really counts credits (and where they leak)

Make's paid tiers all start at the same place, which surprises people: every paid plan includes 10,000 credits/month, and you scale from there on a slider. Core starts at $9/month annual ($10.59 monthly) for 10k; Pro at $16/month ($18.82) adds priority execution and full-text log search; Teams at $29/month ($34.12) adds roles and shared templates. The free plan gets 1,000 credits and 2 active scenarios with a 15-minute minimum interval — fine for learning, not for production.

The slider (annual billing) runs: 10k $9 → 20k $16 → 40k $29 → 80k $55 → 150k $99 → 300k $182 (Core caps here); Pro continues to 500k ($410.27), 1M ($748.73), 2M ($1,377.67), 8M ($4,811.40). Per-thousand-credit pricing falls from $0.90 at 10k to $0.60 at 8M — real volume discounts, but monthly billing runs 15–30% higher and extra credit packs carry ~25% markup. Unused credits roll over one month, a small mercy.

Now the traps — the places credits burn faster than newcomers expect:

  • Polling triggers burn credits on every poll. A 1-minute poll is ~43,200 checks a month before any action runs. This is the single biggest leak on Make, and it's why webhook triggers — 1 credit per real event — are the highest-ROI optimization most teams can make.
  • Filters cost a credit. On Zapier, filters are free. On Make, each filter evaluation is a module execution. A filter-heavy scenario doubles its expected burn.
  • Iterators multiply. Processing 10 line items through an iterator is 10 operations, not one. Routers and aggregators add operations too.
  • The Make Code app bills 2 credits per second. A 30-second script is 60 credits a run — the kind of line item that hides in "why is my bill so high" investigations.
  • Data transfer caps matter at volume: 5 GB per 10,000 operations. Media-heavy scenarios can hit this before the credit count does.

The practitioner rule of thumb for SMB bands: light (2–5k credits) lives on Free/Core; moderate (15–40k) needs Core/Pro with a bigger bucket; heavy (100k+) means Pro or Teams high on the slider — and that's where you should be pricing n8n against it.

n8n's execution math: generous and treacherous at once

n8n Cloud is simpler to read and harder to predict. The tiers (EUR, October 2026, annual): Starter €20/month for 2,500 executions; Pro €50/month for 10,000; Pro 50K €1,450/year (~€121/month) for 50,000; Business (self-hosted license) from €8,000/year for 40,000, sliding to 500,000 executions at €52,450/year.

The generous part: steps are free. A 40-node workflow with loops, code nodes and AI calls costs exactly 1 execution per run. The treacherous part: everything that triggers counts. A 5-minute poller is ~8,640 executions/month — over triple Starter's limit. A 15-minute poller is ~2,880 — already past Starter. Starter's real capacity is about three hourly workflows and nothing else, as we show in our n8n self-hosting cost breakdown — which is why so many teams skip Starter entirely.

The third path is self-hosted Community Edition: €0 license, unlimited executions, on a $6–20/month VPS. The software is free; the server and your maintenance time are the price. It's the option that changes the whole comparison above ~80k runs a month.

The workflow-shape rule: the takeaway worth memorizing

Here's the decision rule that replaces the plan-comparison spreadsheet:

  • Short, high-frequency workflows favor Make. A 2–3 module scenario firing constantly is a few credits a run — Make's per-step billing is cheap when there are barely any steps, and its 1-minute intervals and webhook-first design are built for exactly this.
  • Long, loop-heavy workflows favor n8n. A 30-node workflow with iterators, code and AI calls is still 1 execution. The more steps per run, the bigger n8n's advantage grows — at the extreme, an n8n run can contain dozens of Make-equivalent credits.
  • Polling-heavy workloads punish both Cloud plans and are the #1 reason to either switch to webhooks or self-host. A 1-minute poller burns ~43,200 Make credits or ~43,200 n8n executions a month. On webhooks, the same workload is a fraction of that.

This is the insight missing from most comparisons: the question isn't "Make or n8n," it's "what shape are my workflows?" Audit your five busiest automations, count steps-per-run and runs-per-month, and the answer usually falls out of the math.

The crossover: where self-hosted n8n wins

Put the two Cloud curves together and they cross at roughly 80–100k credits/month: below it, Make's Core slider ($55–99/mo) is the value pick for most SMB workloads; above it, the Make bill climbs past $100/month while self-hosted n8n sits flat at $10–20 on a VPS with unlimited executions.

The honest version of the crossover has a condition attached: self-hosted wins on money only if someone runs the server. 2–5 hours to set up, 1–3 hours a month of updates and backup checks — at $25/hour that's ~$50/month of time, roughly n8n Cloud Pro. Teams without server comfort should read the crossover as "the point where n8n Cloud Pro 50K (€121/mo) becomes the honest price" rather than "the point where everything is free."

Between the two Cloud options at scale, the tiebreaker is workflow shape again: short workflows keep Make competitive surprisingly high (its per-thousand pricing drops to $0.60 at 8M credits), while long workflows make n8n Cloud's per-run billing look cheap even at Business-tier prices. And if you're migrating rather than choosing, our Make vs Zapier guide is the gentler on-ramp before this math matters.

Verdict

At small volume, Make wins on price — Core at $9/month for 10,000 credits is the best value in automation, full stop. At serious volume, the billing units take over: convert your workload to credits-vs-executions before you compare a single plan. Short and frequent goes to Make; long and loopy goes to n8n. And past ~100k runs a month, the real question stops being Make-vs-n8n and becomes Cloud-vs-self-hosted — where a $15 VPS with unlimited executions beats every Cloud tier on money, and loses on exactly one thing: somebody has to babysit the server.

Frequently asked questions

Is Make or n8n cheaper at scale?

It depends on workflow shape, not volume alone. Make bills per module-step (credits); n8n bills per complete run (executions). Short, high-frequency workflows favor Make — Core's slider is $9–99/mo up to 150k credits. Long, multi-step workflows favor n8n, since a 40-node workflow still costs 1 execution. Above ~80–100k credits/month, self-hosted n8n on a $10–20 VPS (unlimited executions) undercuts both Cloud options.

What is a Make credit vs an operation?

They're the same billing unit — Make renamed 'operations' to 'credits' in August 2025. One credit = one module execution. Many guides still say 'operations'; if a comparison uses that term, its pricing data is probably stale. Current pricing: every paid tier starts at 10,000 credits/month ($9/mo Core annual) and scales on a slider to 8M credits.

Why are my Make credits running out so fast?

The usual culprits: polling triggers (a 1-minute poll burns ~43,200 credits/month before any action runs), filters (1 credit each — free on Zapier), iterators multiplying per line item, and the Code app at 2 credits/second. The highest-ROI fix is switching polling triggers to webhooks (1 credit per real event).

How many executions does n8n Cloud Starter really include?

2,500/month — but the practical capacity is much smaller. A 5-minute poller alone uses ~8,640 executions/month (over triple the limit); a 15-minute poller uses ~2,880 (already past it). Starter realistically holds about three hourly workflows. Most real setups land on Pro (€50/mo, 10,000 executions) or self-host for unlimited runs.

At what point should I self-host n8n instead of using Cloud?

Roughly when your Cloud bill passes $50–100/month — typically around 80–100k runs/month on polling-heavy or long workflows. A $10–20/month VPS gives unlimited executions, but budget 2–5 hours setup and 1–3 hours/month maintenance (~$50/month at $25/hr). If nobody on the team wants server duty, the crossover point is where n8n Cloud Pro 50K (€121/mo) becomes the honest price.

Conclusion

Make vs n8n at scale isn't a plan comparison — it's a unit conversion. Count your credits (steps × runs, plus the polling and filter leaks), count your executions (runs, whatever the length), and the cheaper platform reveals itself. Make owns the small-volume game and short workflows; n8n owns long workflows and the self-hosted endgame. The teams that overpay are the ones who picked a platform and never did the conversion.
