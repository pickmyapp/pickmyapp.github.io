ClickUp vs Asana for Marketing Teams (2026)

  • Project Management
  • Published Nov 10, 2026
  • Updated: November 2026
  • 12 min read
  • By PickMyApp
Quick answer

For most marketing teams, ClickUp is the better value: its Business plan costs $12 per user a month billed annually — $60 a month for five people — and includes proofing, workload views, and native time tracking that Asana only unlocks on Advanced at $24.99 a seat ($124.95 for five). Choose Asana if your team runs lots of client approvals and values its cleaner interface and unlimited free guests — but budget for Advanced, since Starter lacks approvals and proofing.

Marketing work is not generic project work. A campaign is a dozen moving parts — strategy, copy, design, video, paid media, social, email — each with its own owner and deadline, and all of it has to land on the same launch date. ClickUp and Asana are the two tools marketing teams most often shortlist, and both will happily run your campaigns. But they are built for different jobs: ClickUp tries to do everything in one place, while Asana is a polished, structured home for cross-team projects. This guide compares them the way a marketing lead actually buys — campaign planning, content calendars, briefs and approvals, client work, and what five or ten seats really cost. Prices below were checked November 10, 2026.

What a marketing team actually needs from a project tool

A marketing team's week runs on four jobs that a generic task list does not cover well. First, campaign planning: one launch date with dozens of dependent tasks, where a slipped design asset delays the email, the social posts, and the paid ads behind it. Second, the content calendar: a single view of everything publishing this week and next, by channel, owner, and status. Third, briefs and approvals: a repeatable intake form, a workable brief, and a review loop where stakeholders approve or request changes on the asset itself. Fourth, client and stakeholder work: freelancers, agencies, and clients who need to see progress and approve work without a paid seat or a view of your internal mess.

Judge both tools against those four jobs. A five-person in-house team running one campaign a month has a different answer from a ten-person agency juggling six clients.

Campaign planning and the content calendar

Both tools give a marketing team the views that matter, but they get there differently. ClickUp ships 15+ views — calendar, board, timeline, Gantt, workload — and custom fields for channel, campaign, owner, and asset type, so one list becomes a content calendar, a launch timeline, and a workload check. Asana's calendar and timeline are cleaner and faster to learn, and its dashboards give a tidy weekly overview of what is due and late. ClickUp has the deeper kit; Asana's restraint suits teams that want the simplest weekly view.

Dependencies and launch timelines

Asana puts timeline and Gantt on Starter ($10.99 per user a month billed annually), so dependency planning is available from the first paid tier. ClickUp includes Gantt on paid plans, with sprints on every plan including free — useful for teams that run campaigns in two-week cycles. Set dependencies up on day one; a calendar with no links between tasks is just a list with dates.

Briefs, approvals and creative proofing

Asana handles this with approvals as a task type and proofing, where reviewers comment directly on images and PDFs. The catch is the tier: approvals and proofing sit on Advanced at $24.99 per user a month billed annually. Starter at $10.99 covers timelines, dashboards, forms, and custom fields — but a team that needs formal sign-off cannot stay on it.

ClickUp puts proofing on Business at $12 per user a month billed annually, alongside workload views, goal folders, and custom permissions. Custom statuses let a team model a real creative workflow — Draft, In Review, Changes Requested, Approved — so the board shows where every asset sits. For five people, that is $60 a month against $124.95 for the same approval loop: less than half the price.

The honest trade-off: Asana's approval flow is simpler to explain to a non-technical stakeholder — approve, request changes, done. ClickUp's version is more powerful but needs someone to set up statuses, proofing, and permissions first. If nobody owns that setup, Asana's cleaner flow may be worth the premium — but you are paying for simplicity, not a feature ClickUp lacks.

What ClickUp vs Asana costs a marketing team of 5 or 10

Headline per-seat prices hide the real comparison, because the tier a marketing team needs differs on each tool: Asana's is Advanced ($24.99), since Starter lacks approvals and proofing; ClickUp's is Business ($12), where proofing and workload live. Price the team, not the headline — at the tier you will actually use.

The headline number: at five seats, the marketing-ready tier costs $60 a month on ClickUp Business against $124.95 on Asana Advanced — more than double. At ten seats the gap grows to $120 against $249.90, nearly $1,560 a year. Even ClickUp Business with Brain AI on every seat ($105 a month for five) undercuts Asana Advanced — and Asana already includes AI on both paid plans, so the AI math does not rescue its price.

Two warnings before you budget. First, monthly billing carries a real premium: ClickUp Business is $19 a month per user billed monthly against $12 annual — a 58% jump — and Asana is $13.49 against $10.99 on Starter, $30.49 against $24.99 on Advanced. Pay annually if you are sure about the seat count. Second, ClickUp is mid-migration to new plan names — Core, Business, Business Plus, Max — with a second lineup in its pricing page code at Core $9 and Business $17, and price updates beginning no earlier than January 2027. Re-check clickup.com/pricing before you buy; do not treat $7/$12 as settled past that window. Our ClickUp pricing breakdown covers the full tier list and the migration.

One more Asana cost: if you bill clients by the hour, the Timesheets and Budgets add-on is $5.99 per user a month on top of the seat price, taking a Starter seat to about $17. ClickUp includes native time tracking from Unlimited at $7.

Free plans: where each one stops working

Both tools offer a free plan, but they are shaped for opposite users. ClickUp Free Forever allows unlimited tasks and members, so a small team can run a real campaign board without paying — the walls you hit first are 60 MB of storage for the whole workspace, which a handful of design files can exhaust, plus 5 Spaces, 1 form, and 3 whiteboards. Heavy attachers outgrow it fast; the $7 Unlimited plan is the next step. Asana's free Personal plan is capped at two users: a solo marketer and one colleague. A team of three or more must pay from day one, so Asana's free plan is a personal trial, not a team trial. Our Asana free vs paid guide walks through each tier, and our free tools roundup compares the category's free options.

Treat the free tier as a way to test the interface, not a long-term home: neither free plan includes the approval and proofing workflow a marketing team eventually needs.

Client work, freelancers and guests

For agencies and in-house teams working with clients, the question is who pays for the people outside the team. Asana is generous here: paid plans include unlimited free guests, so clients and freelancers can view tasks, comment, and approve work without a seat. That alone can justify Asana for an agency with many occasional reviewers.

ClickUp handles guests with a seat pool instead: Unlimited includes 5 permission-controlled guest seats plus 2 per extra member, Business 10 plus 5 per extra member. Beyond that pool, each extra permissioned guest is billed as a full member seat. Two or three regular freelancers fit the pool easily; a dozen clients who each need permissioned access can quietly add up — count your external reviewers before you choose, because this is the one place ClickUp's headline price understates the real bill. For oversight, Asana Advanced adds portfolios and goals; ClickUp Business answers with dashboards, goal folders, and workload views. Our Asana vs Monday vs ClickUp comparison for small teams covers the same trade-offs from the small-team angle.

Automations and AI

Marketing work repeats itself: a submitted brief should assign a designer, a task moved to "Approved" should notify the channel owner, a campaign task should recur every month. Asana includes unlimited automations from Starter. ClickUp includes 1,000 automation executions a month on Unlimited, with a higher cap on Business — enough for routine rules, though a team automating every status change across six campaigns should watch the count.

On AI, the two vendors charge in opposite ways. Asana bundles AI Teammates and Dash into both paid plans: 5 AI requests per user a month, pooled and capped at 50 per account, plus 50,000 AI Studio credits a month on Starter and 75,000 on Advanced. ClickUp sells Brain separately at $9 per user a month, with a higher Everything AI tier at $28. For most marketing teams, neither AI package decides the purchase: test summaries and brief drafting on Asana's included allowance or ClickUp's trial, and only pay once a specific workflow depends on AI every week.

Which should your marketing team choose?

Choose ClickUp if

  • Business at $12 a seat gives you proofing, workload, and time tracking for less than half of Asana Advanced.
  • One flexible system — 15+ views, custom fields, Docs, Chat — instead of several tools.
  • Someone on the team will own the setup — statuses, views, permissions — up front.
  • You run campaigns in sprints and want native time tracking without an add-on.

Choose Asana if

  • Many non-technical stakeholders need the simplest possible review flow.
  • Lots of clients and freelancers: unlimited free guests on paid plans.
  • A clean, structured interface the team will actually open every day.
  • Several client campaigns at once, with portfolios and goals on Advanced.

One last consideration: if your marketing work is mostly documents and wikis, read our Notion vs ClickUp comparison for startups first — neither tool is the natural home for that. Otherwise, trial both free plans with a real campaign: build the brief, run the approval, and check the invoice math at your actual seat count. The tool that survives a real launch week is the right one.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper for a marketing team, ClickUp or Asana?

ClickUp is cheaper at the tier a marketing team actually needs. For five seats, ClickUp Business is $60 a month billed annually, while Asana Advanced is $124.95 a month — more than double. The gap exists because Asana puts approvals and proofing on Advanced, its top paid tier, while ClickUp includes proofing on Business, its mid tier — and even ClickUp Business plus Brain AI ($105 a month for five) undercuts it.

Does Asana Starter include approvals and proofing?

No. Approvals and proofing — the review loop where stakeholders approve an asset or request changes — are Advanced features at $24.99 per user a month billed annually. Starter at $10.99 covers timelines, dashboards, forms, custom fields, and unlimited automations, but formal creative sign-off needs Advanced.

Can I run my marketing team on ClickUp's free plan?

For a small team, yes — with limits. ClickUp Free Forever allows unlimited tasks and members, so a real campaign board works. The walls you hit first are 60 MB of storage for the whole workspace, which a handful of design files can exhaust, plus 5 Spaces, 1 form, and 3 whiteboards. Heavy attachers will need the $7 Unlimited plan soon.

Which is better for a marketing content calendar, ClickUp or Asana?

Both have calendar views, so the difference is depth versus simplicity. ClickUp offers 15+ views including calendar, board, timeline, Gantt, and workload, plus custom fields — more flexible, but more to set up. Asana's calendar and timeline are cleaner and faster to learn, and its dashboards give a tidy weekly overview. Pick ClickUp if one calendar must serve many channels; pick Asana for the simplest weekly view.

Do I need ClickUp Brain or Asana AI for marketing work?

Probably not on day one. Asana includes AI Teammates and Dash on both paid plans, with 5 AI requests per user a month pooled and capped at 50 per account — enough to try, not enough to run on. ClickUp sells Brain separately at $9 per user a month on top of the base plan. Start with the core plans, test summaries and brief drafting on the included allowance, and only pay once a specific workflow depends on AI.

Conclusion

For most marketing teams, ClickUp wins on value: Business at $12 a seat delivers the proofing, workload, and time tracking Asana reserves for Advanced at $24.99, saving a five-person team about $780 a year. Asana wins on polish and guest access — unlimited free guests and the simplest approval flow make it the better pick for agencies with many client reviewers and stakeholders who will not learn a complex tool.

  • Budget-first in-house team: ClickUp Business, $60 a month for five seats.
  • Agency with many client reviewers: Asana Advanced, $124.95 a month for five seats — the guest access earns it back.
  • Still unsure: trial both free plans with one real campaign before you pay.

Price the tier you will actually use, count your guests, and re-check both pricing pages before you buy — ClickUp's plan migration means these numbers carry a January 2027 review date.
