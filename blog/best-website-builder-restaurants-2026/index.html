Best Website Builder for Restaurants (2026)

  • Website Builders
  • Published Oct 11, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

A restaurant website is really three products in one: an online menu, an ordering system, and a reservation tool. For most independent restaurants, Wix is the best website builder in 2026 — its Restaurants module bundles a menu builder, commission-free online ordering, and table reservations into one dashboard, and the Core plan at $29 a month (annual billing) unlocks the full ordering system. Squarespace (Core $29 a month) wins on design for restaurants where atmosphere sells, but reservations and ordering rely on add-ons or integrations. Toast (POS from $0 a month) is the pick for full-service restaurants that need a point-of-sale first and a website second. ChowNow (from about $229 a month) suits established brands that want commission-free direct ordering plus marketing muscle. Avoid building on GloriaFood — Oracle is shutting it down.

Most "best website builder" roundups judge restaurant sites by the wrong criteria — template prettiness and drag-and-drop ease. A restaurant website has a harder job than a portfolio or a blog: it must show a menu that reads perfectly on a phone at 7pm on a Friday, take an order without a 30 percent commission bleeding you dry, and book a table while you are busy cooking. This guide judges four platforms — Wix, Squarespace, Toast, and ChowNow — on exactly those three functions, plus the real monthly cost including the add-ons vendors prefer to hide. Prices were checked against vendor pricing pages and current third-party pricing research in October 2026. For the general website-builder landscape beyond restaurants, see our Wix vs Squarespace comparison for small business.

What a restaurant website actually needs

Before comparing platforms, define the job. A restaurant site stands or falls on four things:

  • An online menu that loads fast on mobile, updates instantly when a price or dish changes, and handles dietary labels (vegan, gluten-free, halal) without a redesign.
  • Online ordering — pickup and delivery — with customer data you own. The critical number here is commission: third-party marketplaces take 15 to 30 percent of every order, which is why a commission-free system on your own site pays for itself quickly.
  • Reservations and table management — even a simple book-a-table form with confirmation emails covers most independent restaurants; full-service venues may want floor-plan management.
  • Local discoverability — Google Business Profile integration, location pages, and fast mobile load times, because most restaurant searches happen on a phone within a few miles of the door.

Everything else — photo galleries, event pages, gift cards — is a bonus. Judge each platform on the four items above and the decision gets much clearer.

Wix: the best all-in-one for independent restaurants

Wix is the only general website builder with a genuinely restaurant-native module: menus, online ordering, and table reservations are built into the platform rather than bolted on as plugins. The menu builder supports categories, item photos, prices, modifiers ("extra cheese," "no onions"), and dietary labels, and changes go live immediately. The ordering system handles pickup, delivery, and scheduled orders with zero commission — you pay only the Wix subscription and standard card-processing fees (2.9% + $0.30 per transaction with Wix Payments). The built-in reservation tool covers table management, party sizes, and automated confirmation emails; it is simpler than dedicated tools like OpenTable but adequate for independent restaurants.

Wix's restaurant features ship inside its standard plans, so there is no separate "restaurants" subscription. The plan that matters is Core at $29 a month (annual billing): it unlocks online payments and the full ordering system. Light ($17) removes Wix ads and adds a custom domain but cannot take payments, so it cannot run ordering. Business ($39) adds marketing tools and more storage; Business Elite ($159) is for high-volume operations. Note that Wix also sells a POS system and hardware (card readers around $49, receipt printers $149-199) if you want the website and the counter on one platform — the Core plan includes full POS access with card-reader support.

Quick fact: Wix added kitchen ticket printing and real-time order tracking to its restaurant module in late 2025, closing much of the gap with dedicated restaurant platforms for the order-to-kitchen flow.

Where Wix falls short

Wix is not ideal for complex multi-location setups with different menus per location and deep reporting — chains outgrow it. Its reservations are basic (no floor-plan visualization), and the template catalog, while large, can look generic without good food photography. Design-wise it cannot match Squarespace's polish.

Squarespace: the design-led pick

Squarespace is the best-looking website builder at any price, and for restaurants where atmosphere is the product — bistros, cocktail bars, fine dining — that matters. Its templates render menus and food photography beautifully, and the editor keeps everything consistent without design skill. Squarespace renamed and repriced its plans in mid-2026: the current lineup is Basic ($19 a month annual billing), Core ($29), Plus ($49), and Advanced ($99), with monthly billing running $25/$39/$65/$139. There is no free plan — only a 14-day trial.

For restaurants, the honest starting point is Core at $29 a month: it removes the 2% transaction fee that Basic charges on store sales and unlocks full code injection for embedding third-party widgets. The catch is that Squarespace has no built-in restaurant ordering system. Online ordering comes through integrations — notably Toast — and reservations come through the Squarespace Scheduling add-on ($16 to $49 a month) or a third-party tool like OpenTable. That means a Squarespace restaurant stack is really $29 (Core) + $16-49 (Scheduling) + whatever your ordering integration costs — realistically $45 to $80 a month before ordering fees. If you are comparing the two builders on broader small-business grounds, our Wix vs Squarespace guide covers the full feature split.

Where Squarespace falls short

The add-on math is the problem: by the time you bolt on reservations and ordering, Squarespace costs more than Wix's all-in-one $29. Its ordering integrations are competent but not native, so the checkout experience and order dashboard feel less unified than Wix's built-in flow. And there is no POS story at all — Squarespace is a website that can take orders, not a restaurant operating system.

Toast: the POS-first pick for full-service restaurants

Toast is a restaurant point-of-sale platform first and a website builder second — which is exactly the right priority for full-service restaurants. If your operation lives and dies by the dining room — table turns, split checks, kitchen display systems — Toast's POS is purpose-built for it, and its online ordering and website tools extend that system to the web. POS plans start at $0 a month for the starter tier (hardware sold separately), with fuller tiers around $69 a month; online ordering is a paid add-on rather than a built-in feature.

The economics are per-order rather than flat. Direct orders through Toast's own ordering pages carry card-processing-style fees (third-party research puts its direct-ordering Webshop around 2.5% + 29 cents per order), while orders through its delivery marketplace tiers can cost 15 to 30 percent commission depending on the plan — the same ballpark as DoorDash and Uber Eats. That makes Toast the strongest choice when the POS is the purchase and the website is the extension, and a weaker choice when you only need a website: you are paying restaurant-software prices for website-builder functionality. Toast's pricing page does not publish every commission figure, so get the add-on quote in writing before committing.

ChowNow: the commission-free pick for established brands

ChowNow sells commission-free direct ordering as a managed service: your own branded ordering site and mobile app, with no per-order commission — just a flat monthly fee plus card processing (2.95% + $0.29 per transaction). It also bundles restaurant websites, email and SMS marketing, loyalty features, and customer-data ownership, with 20+ POS integrations including Toast and Square. Annual plans start around $229 a month for Launch, rising to roughly $319 (Grow) and $409 (Elevate), with one-time setup fees of $119 to $499.

The math only works above a certain volume. At $229 a month, ChowNow costs about eight times Wix Core — it earns that premium if you are doing enough order volume that 15-30% marketplace commissions dwarf the subscription, and if you will actually use the marketing tools. For a single-location café doing a handful of online orders a day, it is overkill; for a multi-location group running direct ordering as a channel, it is purpose-built. Note that ChowNow's published plan names and prices have shifted over 2026, so confirm the current tiers on its pricing page.

GloriaFood: do not build a new site on this

GloriaFood was the beloved free option — a $0 online ordering system with paid add-ons ($29 a month for online payments, $9 for a sales-optimized website, $59 for a branded app). It is now a dead end: Oracle announced that GloriaFood is shutting down, with the sunset reportedly scheduled for April 30, 2027. Any "free restaurant ordering" guide that still recommends it is out of date.

If you are already on GloriaFood, start planning a migration now — a year of runway is comfortable, not infinite. The natural commission-free landing spots are Wix ($29 a month, zero commission) for independents or ChowNow for brands that want the marketing suite. Do not sign a new restaurant up for a platform with a published shutdown date.

What about DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub?

They are not website builders, but they compete for the same orders, so factor them in. The three major marketplaces charge 15 to 30 percent commission on delivery orders depending on the plan (per a September 2026 breakdown of their published plans: roughly 15% on basic tiers, 25% on plus tiers, 30% on premium tiers, with pickup orders around 6-7%). On a $40 order at 30%, the app keeps $12 before you have paid for food, packaging, or labor — $15,000 a year on $5,000 a month in marketplace sales at 25%.

That does not mean you should leave them. Marketplaces are a customer-acquisition channel: their diners, their drivers, their marketing. The profitable playbook most restaurants land on is to run both — let the apps acquire first-time customers, then convert repeat buyers to your own commission-free ordering, where you keep the margin and own the customer data for email and SMS marketing. Your own site is the retention channel; the marketplace is the acquisition channel. Once you have that customer data, a lean email program (see our guide to the cheapest email marketing software) keeps regulars coming back to your own ordering page.

Our verdict by restaurant type

Choose Wix if

  • You run an independent restaurant and want menu, ordering, and reservations in one $29-a-month dashboard.
  • Commission-free ordering matters — Wix charges zero commission on its built-in system.
  • You want the option of adding a POS (card readers, kitchen printing) on the same platform later.
  • You can launch in a day rather than managing multiple vendor logins.

Choose Squarespace if

  • Design is your differentiator — bistros, cocktail bars, and fine dining where the site's look wins bookings.
  • You accept a $45-80-a-month stack (Core + Scheduling + ordering integration) for that polish.
  • Reservations volume is modest and a scheduling add-on covers your needs.
  • You are comfortable wiring an ordering integration rather than using a built-in system.

Beyond those two: choose Toast when the point-of-sale is the real purchase and the website is its extension — full-service restaurants with complex dining-room operations. Choose ChowNow when you are an established brand or group doing enough order volume that a $229-a-month flat fee beats 15-30% marketplace commissions, and you will use the bundled marketing tools. And whatever you do, do not start on GloriaFood — build on a platform with a future. Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest way for a restaurant to take online orders?

Wix Core at $29 a month (annual billing) with its built-in ordering system and zero commission is the cheapest credible option: you pay only the subscription and card processing. GloriaFood was the free option, but Oracle is shutting it down, so new restaurants should not build on it. Marketplace apps like DoorDash and Uber Eats charge 15 to 30 percent commission per order.

Can I use Squarespace for a restaurant website?

Yes, and design-led restaurants often should: Squarespace menus and templates look better than anything else at the price. Start on the Core plan at $29 a month (annual billing). Reservations come through the paid Scheduling add-on ($16 to $49 a month) or a third-party integration, and online ordering works through integrations such as Toast rather than a built-in system.

Is GloriaFood still available?

No — not for new restaurants. Oracle announced that GloriaFood is shutting down, with its sunset reportedly scheduled for April 30, 2027. Existing users should plan a migration now. Commission-free alternatives include Wix online ordering ($29 a month, no commission) or ChowNow (from about $229 a month annual billing) for established brands that want a branded app and marketing tools.

How much does Toast cost for online ordering?

Toast POS plans start at $0 a month for the starter tier, with higher POS tiers around $69 a month, and online ordering is a paid add-on. Direct orders through Toast carry per-order processing fees (reported around 2.5% plus 29 cents); orders through its delivery marketplace tiers can cost 15 to 30 percent commission depending on the plan. Verify current add-on pricing on the Toast site, since commission tiers change.

Do I still need DoorDash or Uber Eats if I have my own ordering?

Usually yes, as a marketing channel rather than your main ordering pipe. The marketplaces charge 15 to 30 percent commission but bring new customers who would never find your site. The profitable playbook is: let the apps acquire customers, then convert repeat buyers to your own commission-free ordering (Wix, Squarespace, or ChowNow), where you keep the margin and own the customer data.

Conclusion

The best website builder for restaurants in 2026 is the one that bundles your three jobs — menu, ordering, reservations — at a price your margins can carry. For most independent restaurants, that is Wix: the Core plan at $29 a month is the only sub-$30 option with native commission-free ordering and built-in reservations. Squarespace earns its higher effective price for restaurants where design wins the booking, at the cost of add-on math. Toast is the right answer when the POS leads and the website follows. ChowNow is the right answer when your order volume makes a $229 flat fee cheaper than marketplace commissions. And the marketplaces themselves remain useful — as acquisition, not as your main till. Whatever you choose, verify the current prices before you buy, and do not build on GloriaFood: Oracle has given it a shutdown date, and your restaurant's website deserves a longer future than that.
