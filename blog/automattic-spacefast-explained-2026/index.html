What Spacefast actually is - and what it is not

Automattic, the company behind WordPress.com, launched Spacefast on November 9, 2026 as a new company inside the group, led by Batuhan İçöz. It had soft-launched about seven weeks earlier, Mullenweg wrote on September 23. The public launch is the moment it became something a small team can plan around, and the first thing to understand is the sentence on its own site: "Spacefast is not an AI builder."

That sentence is the whole product. Spacefast does not write your app. It does not generate a landing page from a prompt, and it does not compete with the tools that do. Instead it is the layer after the building: a publishing and hosting home for whatever your AI already made. You ask ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Claude Code, Codex, Grok Bot, OpenClaw, Hermes, Muse, or one of more than twenty supported agents to build something, and Spacefast gives that agent expert building instructions and a database before it starts, then puts the result online with a private link your team can comment on.

Every project lives in a "Space" - a hosted address with its own sharing controls, version history and a built-in database. The first publish needs no account on the Personal plan and no card. That matters for the way small teams actually work: one person can prototype in Claude, another can iterate in ChatGPT, a third can fix a bug in Cursor, and all three land in the same shared space instead of three separate accounts and three separate deploy pipelines.

Spacefast runs on WordPress.com infrastructure, which Automattic says handles more than 15 billion page views a month. That is the quiet reassurance in the launch: the hosting underneath is not a startup's first data center, it is the platform that already serves a large share of the web. The Enterprise tier adds SOC 2 compliance, SAML single sign-on, SCIM and audit logs for teams that need them.

How publishing works: four ways to get an app live

Spacefast lists four ways to publish, and the differences between them decide who the product is actually for.

1. From an agent chat. This is the headline path. Spacefast is an official plugin on Claude, ChatGPT and Grok Bot, and any other agent can connect through an API, a command-line tool or an MCP server - the Model Context Protocol, the open standard that lets AI apps reach outside tools. You connect ChatGPT, Claude or Cursor once, ask for a website, an app or a slide deck, and get a live link back. Ask for changes the same way, in the same chat. The MCP server works on every plan, including the free Personal plan.

2. Drag and drop. Drop a folder or a zip onto the dashboard and it goes live. This is the path for the app you already built somewhere else, or the one your agent exported as files.

3. From Git. Connect a repository and pushes to the main branch deploy to production, while other branches get preview links. This is the path that makes Spacefast usable by a team with real review habits rather than only by a solo prototyper.

4. From the command line. A CLI for the developers who want deploys in a script rather than a browser tab.

What you can publish is broad: websites, full apps built with Next.js, Astro, TanStack Start or PHP, and slide decks. The launch release is explicit that the agent does the building and Spacefast does the publishing, and that split has a billing consequence worth naming: the inference bill stays with whichever assistant you already pay for. You pay Spacefast only to publish and run what you build. If you are already paying $20 a month for ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro, Spacefast does not replace that subscription - it sits on top of it.

What Spacefast costs - and where the launch numbers disagree

Spacefast's pricing page lists four tiers. Quote the pricing page, not the launch release, because the two disagree on details - and the disagreements are exactly the kind of thing that costs a small team money if it budgets from the press release.

Go adds hourly database backups, datacenter selection and email support. Pro adds team roles and wildcard domains, priority email support, and up to five members free - then $15 a month for each additional member. Enterprise adds SSO, SAML and SCIM, subteams, network policies and an egress firewall, log drains and dedicated support with an SLA.

Now the discrepancies. The launch release describes Go as including "unlimited spaces" and Pro as "$15 a month per member." The pricing page lists 100 Spaces for Go and charges per member only beyond five on Pro. The pricing page is the document that bills you, so budget from it - and treat the release figures as marketing copy that was written before the page was final. A second gap matters more: the page does not state overage rates once your included usage runs out. A $5 Go plan with $5 of included usage is a good deal until a side project gets traffic; without published overage pricing, the monthly bill for a popular app is genuinely unknown. Check spacefast.com before you commit, and check it again before you put a client project on it.

The fair comparison for the money: the free tier is genuinely usable. Twenty Spaces, twenty custom domains and commercial use allowed, with no card, is more generous than the free tiers of most app hosts. The $5 Go plan is the interesting one for side projects - it is priced like a coffee, and for a team that already pays for an AI assistant, it is the cheapest way to turn "the agent built it" into "here is the link."

Spacefast vs Vercel, Netlify and Render - the honest comparison

The question most coverage skips is the one a small team actually asks: why not just deploy to Vercel or Netlify, which already host AI-built apps perfectly well? The answer is that Spacefast is not competing on hosting power. It is competing on the step before hosting.

On Vercel, Netlify, Render or Railway, the workflow is: build in your agent, export the code, push it to Git, connect the repository, configure the deployment, add the database, set the environment variables. Every one of those steps is a place a non-developer gets stuck and a place a developer loses twenty minutes. Spacefast's pitch is that the agent does all of it from inside the chat - the agent asks Spacefast for the database and the domain and the deploy target instead of asking you. The product's own framing is that it "pre-answers the setup questions an agent would normally ask."

That is a real advantage for a specific team: a founder and a designer and a marketer, none of them deploying apps every day, shipping a prototype they built with Claude. It is a smaller advantage for a team that already lives in Git and already has a Vercel pipeline, because for them the deploy step is already solved and Spacefast adds a second home for projects that could live in the first. Our VS Code vs Cursor for beginners guide covers the coding-agent side of that workflow, and our Entire explained guide covers the agent-first Git forge for teams that want the repository to be the centre of the workflow instead.

The other honest comparison is with the AI builders themselves. ChatGPT, Claude and Cursor can already show you a preview of what they built, and tools like Vercel's v0 ship a deploy button. Spacefast's difference is that it is agent-neutral: the same Space can hold work from Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor at once, and the team shares one place rather than each person publishing from their own tool's preview link. If your whole team uses one AI tool, that neutrality buys you little. If your team is split across three, it is the whole point.

Who should try it - and who should wait

Try it this week if you are a small team or solo builder who already builds with an AI assistant and wants the shortest path from "the agent built it" to a live link; if your team is split across Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor and you want one shared home for the output; if you are prototyping a side project and the free tier's 20 Spaces and $1 of usage cover it; or if you are a non-developer founder who has been stuck at the deploy step and wants the agent to handle it.

Wait if you need predictable hosting bills - the unpublished overage rates make a traffic spike a billing unknown; if you already have a working Vercel or Netlify pipeline and your team lives in Git; if you need scheduled jobs to run longer than a few seconds, which an early tester flagged as a current limit; or if you are putting a client-facing production app on it in week one, before the rough edges found by early testers are fixed.

The middle path is the sensible one: put the prototype on the free Personal plan, ship it to a real link, and see whether the agent-publish workflow saves your team time. The cost of trying is zero and the cost of leaving is a Git repository you already own. That is a reasonable bet for a side project, and a bad bet only if you treat a two-day-old launch as a permanent home on day one.

The honest risks of a two-day-old launch

A launch-week article that only praises the tool is not a review, so here is the other side. Spacefast launched publicly on November 9, 2026. There are no independent reviews yet, no published uptime history, and no long-term pricing commitment. Every source for this article is Automattic's own release, the product's own pages, or launch coverage that largely repeats them.

Early hands-on testing, reported by TechTimes, found three things worth knowing: a setup link behaved like an admin credential, scheduled jobs were limited to five seconds, and the documentation contradicted its own project template in places. None is fatal for a prototype, but they are the reason to keep a production app on infrastructure you already trust for now.

The pricing disagreements matter too: when the press release and the pricing page disagree about Go's Spaces cap and Pro's per-member wording, the page that bills you wins. Check spacefast.com on the day you sign up, not on the day you read this.

Three questions to ask before you move a real project in: what is the overage rate when a Space gets real traffic? Can you export everything, including the database, if you leave? And what does the setup link actually grant? If the answers are good, the free tier is the cheapest way to find out whether agent-native publishing fits your team. Conclusion

Spacefast is a small, well-aimed launch: not another AI builder, but the missing step after the builder - a place where apps made by ChatGPT, Claude and Cursor go live without leaving the chat. Its case is the free tier, the $5 Go plan, the agent-neutral shared Space and the WordPress.com infrastructure underneath. Its limit is its age: it launched November 9, 2026, the pricing page and press release disagree on details, overage rates are unpublished, and early testers found rough edges. Try a prototype on the free plan, check spacefast.com before you budget, and keep production on infrastructure you already trust.
