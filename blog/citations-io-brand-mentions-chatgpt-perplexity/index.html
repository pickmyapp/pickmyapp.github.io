Citations.io explained: see what ChatGPT and Perplexity say about your brand (2026)

A growing share of buying decisions now starts with a prompt instead of a search box. A customer types “what's the best CRM for a small agency?” into ChatGPT or Perplexity, reads three recommended names, and never touches Google. If your brand isn't in those answers, you never entered the shortlist — and your traditional SEO rankings never had a chance to matter. Citations.io launched on October 13, 2026, to make that invisible layer visible: an AI search intelligence platform that tracks how the major AI engines mention, rank, recommend, and cite your brand. This guide explains what it does, how it works, what it costs (or doesn't publish), and how it compares to the fast-growing field of AI-visibility tools.

What is Citations.io?

Citations.io is an AI search intelligence platform that shows brands how AI engines represent them (citations.io). Headquartered in Newcastle upon Tyne, UK, it was founded by Josh Tulip, a former agency boss with roughly 15 years across technical SEO, search intelligence, and digital growth. The platform launched on October 13, 2026, and was bootstrapped — Tulip self-funded development and is now assembling the founding leadership team ahead of a first funding round. Notably, around 70% of initial customers are US-based, a sign that American marketing teams are hunting for AI-visibility tooling fastest.

The product tracks how ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude mention, rank, recommend, and cite a brand in answers to customer research questions, then connects that visibility to competitor intelligence, citation analysis, Source Influence, and Implementation Packs — the company's term for the actionable briefs it generates. In short: it turns “what do the AI engines say about us?” from a vibes question into a measured one.

Quick fact: Citations.io belongs to the new category usually called GEO (generative engine optimization) or AI search visibility. Traditional rank tracking can't measure it, because the "ranking" happens inside a generated paragraph, not on a results page.

Why AI-search visibility matters now

The shift is structural, not hype. Google said its AI Overviews reached 1.5 billion monthly users, and ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Claude all ship cited, sourced answers. Three things make this a new discipline rather than a new SEO trick:

Mentions and citations are different events

A citation records that an engine borrowed a page as a source; a mention records that the engine named your brand. 2026 industry research found that fewer than 30% of AI responses mention and cite the same brand, and that only about 13% of AI mentions come from a brand's own domain. Your content can power an answer without your name ever appearing — you need to track both, separately.

Engines disagree — wildly

Visibility varies by engine far more than most marketers expect: one 2026 analysis found 86% brand-mention visibility on Perplexity but only 30–50% on ChatGPT and Gemini for the same client. ChatGPT in particular follows neither Google rankings nor brand popularity closely. A single-platform snapshot can seriously mislead you; multi-engine measurement is the whole point of tools like Citations.io.

AI answers are where shortlists form

When a buyer asks “best accounting software for freelancers” and the answer names three brands, the consideration set is built inside that paragraph. Brands that know which sources shape those answers — review sites, publisher lists, forums, their own docs — can act on it. If you're evaluating the AI tools themselves rather than your visibility inside them, our roundup of the best AI writing tools for small business covers the engine side of the ecosystem.

How Citations.io works

The workflow is straightforward, and it mirrors the basic GEO loop every tool in this category runs:

1. You define what to watch

You enter your brand, your competitors, and the prompts that matter — the questions your buyers actually ask, like “best project management tool for a 5-person team.” Good prompt design is half the job: too generic and you get noise, too narrow and you miss the real buying conversations.

2. The platform queries the engines

Citations.io runs those prompts against ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude and captures the generated answers, including how the brand is mentioned, whether it is recommended, where it sits in the answer, and which source URLs are cited. This repeated sampling matters because AI answers are probabilistic — ask the same question twice and you can get a different answer.

3. Signals become scores and source maps

The platform analyzes brand mentions, recommendation status, answer position, and source URLs, and maps Source Influence — which third-party sources the engines pull from when answering your category's questions. Since third-party sites drive the majority of AI brand mentions, knowing which review sites, publishers, and forums shape your category is the actionable insight. Competitor benchmarking runs alongside: which rivals are being recommended instead of you, and where.

4. Gaps become Implementation Packs

This is Citations.io's signature feature. Instead of a dashboard of red numbers, the platform converts visibility gaps into Implementation Packs — prioritized briefs written for SEO, content, digital PR, and brand teams: pages to write, schema to add, mentions to earn. The format is aimed at small teams where the same person reads the report and does the work.

What the reports and Implementation Packs look like

We haven't seen inside a live Citations.io dashboard — the product is two days old — but the company's launch materials describe the outputs clearly enough to set expectations.

Visibility dashboards

Expect per-engine views of how often your brand is mentioned, cited, and recommended across tracked prompts, trended over time, with competitor share-of-voice alongside. The most useful number for most brands is recommendation share — how often you're named as a pick, not just cited as a source — because recommendations correlate with shortlist inclusion far better than raw citation counts.

Source influence maps

The part traditional SEO tools can't give you: the domains and pages AI engines actually draw on when answering your category's questions. If Perplexity answers every “best CRM” question from the same three review sites, your PR strategy has a clear target list. This is also where the work shifts from technical SEO to digital PR and partnerships.

Implementation Packs

The Packs are the differentiator Tulip is betting on: instead of “you're invisible on 40% of prompts,” you get pages to write, schema markup to add, and third-party mentions to earn, assigned to the teams that can do them. For small marketing teams without a dedicated GEO strategist, that translation from data to tasks is genuinely valuable. The open question is quality: a brilliant brief depends on the platform correctly identifying why you're invisible, and that's the hard part of this whole category.

One practical note: the briefs will often tell your content team to write more, which is where our comparisons of Copy.ai vs Writesonic for marketing teams and Jasper vs Copy.ai for blog writing can help you pick the tooling to execute the packs.

Pricing: what we know (and don't)

Citations.io has not published pricing. The site offers the platform internationally, but as of October 15, 2026, there is no pricing page, no plan tiers, and no self-serve pricing we could verify. That's not unusual for an enterprise-leaning launch — remember 70% of early customers are US enterprises — but it means small businesses can't budget for it yet.

For market context, here is what comparable products charge, so you can calibrate:

  • CiteLens (a rival GEO platform): snapshot audit at $499, fuller done-for-you service at $1,499, agency pricing from $3,000+, with a free audit waitlist.
  • White-label AI visibility providers: roughly $350 per month per client for backend services, per 2026 market reporting.
  • Frase (content suite with AI visibility tracking): plans from $39/month billed yearly, AI visibility included at every tier.

The category spans from $39/month self-serve tools to enterprise platforms on custom quotes. Where Citations.io lands will tell you who it's really for — and that's a question only their sales team can answer right now. Given Tulip's own words about US enterprise demand and the bootstrapped economics, expect pricing aimed at marketing teams and agencies rather than solo founders.

Who needs it?

  • Brands in AI-answered categories. If your buyers ask AI assistants for recommendations — software, travel, finance, health, local services — you are being evaluated by engines whether you track it or not.
  • Marketing teams losing the shortlist. If competitors keep appearing in AI answers and you don't, a visibility tool tells you whether it's a source problem, a content problem, or an authority problem.
  • Agencies. White-label AI visibility services are already being resold; agencies that can show clients their AI-engine presence have a new retainer line.
  • PR and content teams. Source Influence data turns “we need more coverage” into a named list of sites to target.

It is probably not for you yet if your buyers don't research via AI assistants, if your category is too small for meaningful prompt sampling, or if you can't staff the follow-up work. A dashboard that reports invisibility nobody acts on is just an expensive mirror. Limitations and honest caveats

Measurement is not control

The most important caveat: no tool can make ChatGPT recommend you. AI answers are probabilistic, they change with model updates, and the engines don't publish their ranking logic. Citations.io measures the weather; it doesn't steer the storm.

The product is two days old

No independent reviews, no public case studies with named brands, no verifiable track record of Implementation Packs producing results. The founder's 15 years in SEO is a genuine credential, but GEO is a new discipline and the playbook is still being written.

Answers vary; samples can mislead

AI engines answer the same prompt differently across runs, locations, and time. Visibility numbers are statistical estimates, and small prompt panels produce noisy data. Ask any vendor how many runs back their scores before trusting a single percentage point.

Most of the fix is off-platform

With only ~13% of AI mentions coming from a brand's own domain, most of the real work is third-party: review sites, publisher lists, forums, partnerships, digital PR. The Implementation Pack can name the targets, but earning coverage is slow, human work.

Pricing opacity favors enterprises

No published pricing plus a 70%-US-enterprise early base strongly suggests pricing for marketing teams and agencies, not indie hackers. Small businesses should start with a cheaper self-serve alternative or a one-off audit.

Consider Citations.io if

  • Your buyers research via ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Gemini and you're losing the AI shortlist.
  • You have a team (or agency) that can act on content, schema, and PR briefs.
  • You want measurement that turns into tasks, not just dashboards.
  • Published pricing lands within your budget once it's announced.

Look elsewhere if

  • You need a self-serve tool with published pricing today — try Otterly.AI, Frase, or a CiteLens audit.
  • Your category is too small for meaningful prompt sampling.
  • Nobody on your team can execute the follow-up work the packs will prescribe.
  • You expect any tool to guarantee engine recommendations — none can.

Frequently asked questions

What does Citations.io actually track?

Citations.io tracks how ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Claude mention, rank, recommend, and cite your brand in answers to customer questions. It analyzes brand mentions, recommendation status, answer position, and source URLs, then layers competitor intelligence on top so you can see who is recommended instead of you.

How much does Citations.io cost?

Citations.io has not published pricing as of October 2026: no pricing page, no plan tiers, no verifiable self-serve pricing. For market context, rival CiteLens sells snapshot audits at $499 and fuller services at $1,499, and white-label AI visibility providers charge roughly $350 per month per client. Ask Citations.io for a written quote before budgeting.

Can Citations.io make ChatGPT recommend my brand?

No tool can make an AI engine recommend your brand; the engines decide based on their training data, live sources, and internal ranking logic. Citations.io offers measurement and direction: where you appear, which competitors win, which sources influence the answers, and Implementation Packs that turn gaps into content, schema, and digital PR tasks. The execution, and the results, are still on you.

How is Citations.io different from Peec AI, Otterly.AI, or Profound?

The category is crowded and the differences are in execution, not concept: all of these tools monitor brand visibility inside AI-generated answers across engines like ChatGPT and Perplexity. Citations.io's stated differentiator is its Implementation Packs, which convert visibility gaps into prioritized briefs for SEO, content, digital PR, and brand teams. Peec AI, Otterly.AI, Profound, and Scrunch cover similar ground with their own dashboards, prompt libraries, and alerting; compare engine coverage, prompt counts, and how actionable the reports are before choosing.

Is AI search visibility worth tracking for a small business?

It depends on how your buyers research. If your customers ask ChatGPT or Perplexity for shortlists in your category, then invisible means unfound, and tracking what the engines say about you is genuinely useful. But AI answers are volatile, they vary by engine and prompt, and only 13 percent of AI mentions typically come from a brand's own domain — so most of the work is third-party presence and PR, not a dashboard subscription. Weigh a one-off audit against a monthly tool.

Conclusion

Citations.io arrives at exactly the right moment: buyers are shifting from search boxes to prompts, and brands have had no way to see themselves through the engines' eyes. The concept is sound, the founder's SEO background is real, and the Implementation Packs idea — turning visibility data into tasks for real teams — is the sharpest differentiator in a crowded field. But the product is two days old, pricing is unpublished, and the fundamental limit of the category applies: measurement is not control, and no tool steers the engines. If your category lives inside AI answers and you can staff the follow-up work, put Citations.io on your demo list alongside Peec AI, Otterly.AI, and Profound — and make them all answer the same question: show me, on my brand, what changes after 90 days.
