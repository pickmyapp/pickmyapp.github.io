Squarespace vs Shopify for Selling Online (2026)

  • Website Builders
  • Published Oct 12, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 9 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Pick Squarespace if you sell a small catalog (under ~50 products) and design is the selling point — its Core plan is $29 a month billed annually, charges no platform transaction fee on physical products, and looks polished with minimal effort. Pick Shopify if selling is the business itself: Basic is $29 a month billed annually, but it brings 8,000+ apps, full POS, native dropshipping and multi-channel selling that Squarespace cannot match. The honest rule: choose by catalog size and growth stage, not by which logo you prefer.

Most Squarespace vs Shopify articles treat this as a head-on fight for "best ecommerce platform," and almost all of them still quote Squarespace's pre-2025 plan names. Both facts are misleading. Squarespace is a website builder with commerce added; Shopify is a commerce engine with a website around it. Squarespace overhauled its plans in early 2025 (Basic, Core, Plus, Advanced) and raised prices again in mid-2026, so anything mentioning "Personal" or "Business" plans is out of date. This guide uses October 2026 pricing from the vendors' own pages and gives a verdict keyed to catalog size and growth stage — because a 12-product candle brand and an 800-SKU sneaker store should never get the same answer.

What does the same order cost on each?

Take a $50 online order paid with a standard US card. On Squarespace Core ($29/mo annual), you pay 2.9% + 30¢ processing and no platform fee: about $1.75. On Shopify Basic ($29/mo annual, using Shopify Payments), the math is identical. At entry level, per-order costs are almost identical — the difference is what the subscription unlocks.

The fees diverge where the platforms diverge. Sell digital products and Squarespace taxes them at the plan level: a $40 online course on Basic loses 7% to Squarespace plus 2.9% + 30¢ to the processor — roughly $4.26 of a $40 sale. The same course on Shopify Basic loses just the 2.9% + 30¢, about $1.46. Digital-product sellers should skip Squarespace's lower tiers entirely; only Plus ($49/mo annual, 1% digital fee) and Advanced (0%) make courses and downloads reasonably priced.

On physical goods the shoe is on the other foot for very small sellers: Squarespace Basic charges a 2% platform fee on top of processing, so a $50 order costs about $2.75 in fees versus Shopify Basic's $1.75. The practical floor for selling physical products on Squarespace is Core at $29/mo annual — the 2% fee wipes out Basic's subscription saving once you pass roughly $500 a month in sales.

Quick fact: Squarespace raised plan prices in mid-2026 — Plus went from $39 to $49 a month on annual billing, and Basic from $16 to $19 — while Shopify's plan prices have held steady since its 2024 restructure that renamed the middle tier "Grow." Most third-party comparison articles still quote the pre-increase Squarespace numbers.

Who is each platform really built for?

Squarespace is a website builder first. Its templates look finished with minimal editing, the editor is genuinely pleasant, and content — portfolios, lookbooks, journals, booking pages — sits at the center. Commerce is excellent but added onto a publishing platform. For a photographer selling prints, a restaurant selling merchandise on the side, a consultant selling digital guides, or a boutique brand where the site itself is the marketing, Squarespace gets you a beautiful result faster than anything else.

Shopify is a commerce platform first: the admin opens on orders and inventory, not pages and galleries. Abandoned-cart recovery (all plans), multi-location stock, staff permissions, tax automation, and the 8,000-app marketplace all assume selling is the point. If revenue comes mostly from product sales or you run promotions constantly, Shopify's depth pays for itself. The trade-off: a Shopify storefront needs more design work — often a paid theme — to look as polished as Squarespace. Catalog size: where each platform hits its stride

Both platforms advertise unlimited products. The difference is in the tooling around the catalog.

Squarespace handles a small catalog elegantly: variants, limited-availability labels, customer reviews, and automatic discounts all work well for a few dozen products. But bulk product editing, multi-location inventory, and sophisticated variant structures are thin. Past about 100 SKUs, managing products on Squarespace starts to feel like workarounds instead of features.

Shopify's product management was built for volume: bulk editor, inventory across up to 1,000 locations, low-stock alerts, and apps for size charts, bundles, and purchase orders — exactly what a 500-SKU apparel store needs. Note that Shopify Basic includes staff accounts and permissions, while Squarespace Basic caps you at two contributors.

POS and in-person selling

If you sell at markets, pop-ups, or a physical shop, this category is not close. Shopify POS runs on the same inventory as your online store on every plan, with in-person card rates of 2.6% + 10¢ on Basic, 2.5% + 10¢ on Grow, and 2.4% + 10¢ on Advanced. An in-person sale deducts from the same stock pool your website sells from, and promotions apply in both places.

Squarespace's POS is far more limited: US only, a smaller hardware ecosystem, and designed for occasional sales rather than daily retail. If in-person revenue is more than a side note, choose Shopify — or pair Squarespace with a dedicated POS like Square, which adds another system to reconcile.

Dropshipping and multi-channel selling

Shopify is the default platform for dropshipping: native integrations with suppliers and apps like DSers, Zendrop, and Spocket, automated order routing, and built-in channels for Amazon, Instagram, TikTok, Google, and eBay. You can run a store that never touches inventory entirely inside Shopify's ecosystem.

Squarespace supports dropshipping only through limited third-party extensions and manual workflows — no equivalent of Shopify's one-click supplier apps. For a serious dropshipping or print-on-demand business, Shopify is not just better; Squarespace is the wrong tool. The exception: an artist selling their own print-on-demand work as a sideline to a portfolio site, where Squarespace's simplicity may still win.

One cost to budget: neither platform includes serious email marketing. Whichever you choose, you will likely add a dedicated tool for cart flows and newsletters — our guide to the cheapest email marketing software for small business covers the budget options that pair well with either store.

App ecosystems: 8,000 apps vs 30 extensions

This is the single biggest structural difference. Shopify's marketplace holds 8,000+ apps: loyalty programs, subscriptions, wholesale pricing, advanced search, reviews with photos, currency switchers, ERP connectors. The cost is real — useful apps commonly add $5 to $100 a month each — but the capability ceiling is enormous.

Squarespace offers roughly 30 extensions, mostly for shipping, accounting, and scheduling. The built-in set is genuinely good, so you need fewer add-ons to start, but the ceiling is low. Want a subscription box builder, a wholesale portal, or a custom loyalty program? On Squarespace you hire a developer or switch platforms. Squarespace gives you a finished room; Shopify gives you the whole hardware store — a "cheap" Squarespace plan that forces a platform migration in year two is the most expensive option of all.

Design and ease of use

Squarespace wins on design with little argument. Its templates are consistently excellent, the editor is fluid, and image handling — galleries, portfolios, full-bleed photography — is best in class. A non-designer can produce a site that looks like a studio made it. Our WordPress vs Webflow comparison makes a similar point about design quality when the site itself is the product.

Shopify's themes are clean but commerce-first; making one distinctive usually means custom code or a premium theme. The admin is powerful but busier, organized around products and orders. With no design background, Squarespace takes days versus Shopify's weeks; hiring a designer, Shopify's flexibility becomes the asset. That math shifted in October 2026: Shopify's new Canvas workspace lets you redesign your whole store by chatting with its Sidekick AI — with two irreversible catches to know before you use it on a live theme.

Can you switch platforms later?

Yes — and most moves go one direction: Squarespace to Shopify, as the store outgrows the builder. Products, customers, and orders export from Squarespace as CSV files and import into Shopify. What does not transfer: your design (rebuild it), blog posts and pages (migrate or rebuild), URL structure (plan 301 redirects to protect SEO), and platform-specific features like memberships or scheduling. Budget a weekend of rebuild time and expect a few weeks of SEO turbulence.

The migration reality argues for deciding by growth stage now: if you can already see the 500-SKU future, starting on Shopify saves you the rebuild.

Our verdict: choose by catalog size and growth stage

Choose Squarespace if

  • You sell fewer than ~50 products and design is part of the pitch.
  • Your site is mostly content or portfolio with a shop attached.
  • You want the fastest path from signup to a beautiful store.
  • You sell in one currency to a consumer audience with simple tax needs.

Choose Shopify if

  • Selling products is the business, not a side feature.
  • Your catalog is growing past 100 SKUs or will within two years.
  • You need POS, dropshipping, subscriptions, or multi-channel selling.
  • You want the app ecosystem and the room to scale internationally.

To make it concrete: launching a 12-product jewelry brand with a lookbook → Squarespace Core. Scaling a 300-SKU apparel store with wholesale and TikTok Shop → Shopify Basic or Grow. Selling courses or downloads → Shopify Basic, or Squarespace Plus at minimum. Running a market stall plus a website → Shopify, for the unified POS alone. Frequently asked questions

Is Squarespace cheaper than Shopify for selling online?

At the entry level, yes. Squarespace Core costs $29 a month billed annually with zero transaction fees on physical products, while Shopify Basic costs $29 a month billed annually plus card processing at 2.9% + 30 cents. But Shopify's transaction-free tier arrives sooner: Squarespace Core at $29 a month drops the 2% commerce fee that the $19-a-month Basic plan charges, so very small sellers pay more per order on the cheapest Squarespace plan.

Which is better for a small catalog of under 50 products?

Squarespace is usually the better pick for under 50 products. Its templates look finished with minimal effort, the editor is simpler, and Core at $29 a month billed annually includes unlimited products with no platform transaction fee. Shopify's commerce depth — variants, bulk editing, multi-location inventory — only earns its keep once the catalog grows past roughly 100 SKUs.

Can I dropship with Squarespace?

You can, but it is clunky. Squarespace has limited built-in dropshipping integrations — the commonly cited option is Spocket or a manual workflow through a third-party tool. Shopify was built for this model, with native or app-based integrations for major suppliers, automated fulfillment flows, and marketplace selling on Amazon, TikTok, and Instagram. For a serious dropshipping business, Shopify is the clear choice.

Which platform is better for in-person selling with a POS?

Shopify. Shopify POS runs on the same inventory as your online store on every plan, with in-person card rates of 2.6% + 10 cents on Basic, dropping to 2.5% + 10 cents on Grow. Squarespace's POS is more limited, works in the US only, and is better suited to occasional markets or pop-ups than to daily retail trade.

Can I migrate from Squarespace to Shopify later?

Yes. Products, customers, and orders can be exported from Squarespace as CSV files and imported into Shopify, and Shopify provides store-migration tooling. Blog posts, pages, and design do not transfer directly and must be rebuilt. Expect to redirect URLs and recreate memberships, digital-product delivery, and any custom layout work, so plan for a weekend of rebuild time.

Conclusion

Squarespace vs Shopify is not a fight with one winner — it is a fork in the road. Take Squarespace when the website is the business and commerce supports it: small catalogs, design-led brands, creators, and service businesses get a beautiful store on Core ($29/mo annual) with no platform fee on physical goods. Take Shopify when commerce is the business: growing catalogs, POS, dropshipping, subscriptions, and international selling belong on a platform built for them, starting at $29/mo annual on Basic. The expensive mistake is picking for who you are today when you can already see who you will be in two years — choose by catalog size and growth stage, do the fee math on your own average order, and commit.
