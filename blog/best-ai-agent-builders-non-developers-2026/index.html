An AI agent builder is a no-code platform for creating AI agents — software that takes a goal, reads data, and acts on it — without writing code. This guide compares the six builders non-technical buyers shortlist most, with vendor pricing checked in October 2026. If you only need classic step-by-step automation without AI, read our cheaper automation tools guide first.

What is an AI agent builder?

An AI agent is software that combines a language model with tools and permissions, then works through a task on its own: reading data, making decisions, and taking actions like sending emails or updating a CRM. An agent builder is the platform that lets you assemble that combination — giving the model its instructions, connecting your apps, and setting rules for what it may and may not do. The difference from old automation tools is flexibility: where Zapier or Make execute steps you defined in advance, an agent can handle messy inputs (a rambling email, an invoice PDF) by reasoning about them first.

That flexibility is the catch: none of these tools publishes a complete per-task cost table, so treat every price here as a starting point, not a promise.

What a non-developer actually needs from an agent builder

Most reviews rank "power" first. For a buyer who does not code, four things matter more:

  • Natural-language setup: describe the agent in sentences, or learn a canvas of nodes?
  • Guardrails: can the agent be forced to ask before sending, deleting, or spending?
  • App coverage: does it already connect to your apps, or will you be wiring webhooks?
  • Predictable cost: is the billing unit forecastable, or does it depend on how the model behaves that day?

Every tool below passes the first test. They differ sharply on the other three.

1. Lindy — the easiest start, if you live in Slack

Lindy is an AI teammate you talk to inside Slack. Describe the job — "triage my inbox every morning and draft replies" — and Lindy builds a routine. Agents run on schedules, connect 1,000+ integrations, and use "Computer Use" on Pro and above for apps with no API. Its best feature is the approval system: the agent can be forced to ask before it acts.

It handles inbox and calendar management, meeting notes, lead research, and multi-step routines well — but Lindy works for your team, not your customers, so customer-facing chat is out. There is no visual canvas, so when a routine misbehaves you debug by rewriting instructions, which gets vague fast.

The non-developer limit

Credits. Lindy publishes no per-action credit table — the vendor's own ranges put an everyday ask at 2–250 credits and a "big build" at 1,000–2,500. One reviewer burned a month's 3,000 Plus credits in a day. And every active user takes a paid seat, including anyone who @mentions Lindy in Slack for the first time, so costs multiply as your team adopts it.

2. Gumloop — best value, unlimited seats

Gumloop is a no-code canvas for AI agents and workflows, fronted by Gumball, a chat-based agent that builds flows from your description. Pro costs a flat $37 a month with 20,000 credits and unlimited seats — a team of five pays $37 total where Lindy would charge roughly $150. Bring your own OpenAI or Anthropic API key and AI node costs drop to 1 credit per call.

Batch content jobs, lead research and enrichment, document processing, and scheduled workflows are its strengths — but with roughly 125 native connectors, niche apps need custom webhook or MCP connections. The product is young and the UI has quirks.

The non-developer limit

Credit math. Advanced AI calls cost 20–30 credits each, compute costs 5 credits per session-minute, and an 8% orchestration fee stacks on top (16% if you bring your own key). Reviewers warn that one complex research workflow can burn hundreds of credits per run, and Pro credits do not roll over. Model a real workload during the 14-day Pro trial before committing.

3. Zapier Agents — best if you already use Zapier

Zapier Agents is the agent layer inside Zapier: small purpose-built agents that trigger from schedules, chat, or app events and act through your Zaps. The reason to choose it is the ecosystem — 9,000+ apps, unmatched here — and Agents usage (activities) is metered separately from your Zap task allowance. If your business already runs on Zaps, your integrations and credentials are waiting.

Agents that read from and write to nearly any SaaS app, chat-based agents, and scheduled research routines are its strengths — but it is not a visual flow builder, and it assumes you know Zapier's vocabulary already. Complex branching belongs in regular Zaps underneath.

The non-developer limit

The two-meter problem. You pay for Agents (400 free activities/month, then Pro at $33.33/month billed annually for 1,500 activities) and separately for your Zapier core plan ($29.99/month for Professional with 750 tasks). An agent that fires Zaps burns both meters at once — and agents multiply Zapier's already steep cost curve.

4. Relay.app — best for approval-heavy workflows

Relay.app is a visual automation builder with AI steps and human-in-the-loop approvals baked in. Any workflow can pause for a person to approve before continuing — sending a quote, posting content, refunding an order — making it the most trustworthy pick where an AI mistake has visible consequences. It scores near 5 stars on G2 and Capterra for ease of use. Connect your own API keys to bypass credit consumption.

Approval workflows, AI-assisted email and document processing, scheduled multi-step jobs, and shared team playbooks are its strengths — but the catalog is about 100 integrations, thin for niche tools, and workflow analytics are shallow, so optimizing a running agent means guesswork.

The non-developer limit

The free plan is genuinely usable (200 steps + 500 AI credits), but Professional is $19/month billed annually versus $38 month-to-month — a markup that effectively forces an annual commitment before you know whether the tool fits. And 5,000 AI credits goes faster than it sounds when every workflow asks the AI questions at each step.

5. Relevance AI — cheapest for multi-agent builds

Relevance AI builds multi-agent "workforces" for sales, research, and support tasks. It has the cheapest entry here — Pro at $19 a month billed annually ($29 monthly) with 2,500 actions — and a simple billing unit: one tool run equals one action. "Vendor Credits" pass model costs through at wholesale with no markup.

Multi-agent systems, sales and research automation, and lead qualification are its strengths — but it expects you to think in tools, actions, and data pipelines, a real learning curve if you have never automated anything. The Team plan is a steep $349/month, so the value sits at the solo Pro tier.

The non-developer limit

Complexity. This is the least "describe it and go" tool here; the concepts (actions vs. vendor credits, workforces, projects) take study. A failed tool run still counts as an action, so flaky agents tax your quota twice — in cost and in frustration.

6. Botpress — most capable, least beginner-friendly

Botpress is the oldest and most production-grade platform here: autonomous AI agents that deploy to web, WhatsApp, and Slack from one configuration. It offers a drag-and-drop Studio, LLM-agnostic model choice, 190+ integrations, and native human handoff. Since May 2026 it bills per conversation with AI usage included in the quota.

Production customer-support agents, multi-channel deployment, and knowledge-base agents are its strengths — but Botpress is honest about being developer-oriented: when an agent must do real work, you are into JavaScript execute cards, API wiring, and the ADK.

The non-developer limit

Price and skill. The free plan (100 conversations, 3 agents) is a prototype tier. Plus starts at $150 a month for 250 conversations — five times Lindy's entry price — and Team is $750. Crossing your quota auto-adds conversation packs ($65 per 100 on Plus) that expire at month end and cannot be turned off. Choose Botpress only if the project has a technical teammate. The limits a non-developer will hit, whichever tool you pick

Before you buy, know the four walls every non-technical buyer eventually reaches:

1. Credentials and permissions

Every agent acts as you, which means connecting your Gmail, CRM, and accounts. OAuth connections break when passwords change, and an agent with "full access" can do damage a human never would. Give agents the narrowest permissions possible and use approval steps for anything that sends, deletes, or spends.

2. Agents are unreliable in ways workflows are not

A traditional automation does exactly what you built. An agent reasons — so it can misread instructions, hallucinate facts, and repeat a bad assumption on a schedule. Guardrails (Relay.app's human-in-the-loop, Lindy's approvals) matter more than raw intelligence. Never put an agent on a schedule without a monitoring plan.

3. Usage billing punishes experimentation

Every tool here meters something — credits, actions, activities, steps, conversations — and none publishes a complete per-task cost table. Start on a free tier or trial, run your heaviest realistic week, then price that week times four before committing annually.

4. The 80% wall

No-code builders get you to about 80% of what you imagined quickly. The last 20% — the odd API format, the conditional nobody documented, the integration that doesn't exist — is where non-developers stall and either simplify the task or bring in help. Budget for that: assistants like IrisGo or a few hours of freelancer time.

Quick fact: as of 2026, none of the six builders here bills flat per workflow run the way n8n does — every one meters some form of per-action usage, so cost grows with workload volume.

Our verdict

Choose Lindy if

  • You work in Slack and want the agent to meet you there.
  • You want to describe the job in plain language, not learn a canvas.
  • Built-in approvals matter more to you than inspecting every step.

Choose Gumloop if

  • You want the most AI agent for the least money ($37/month, unlimited seats).
  • You already pay for OpenAI or Anthropic API keys (BYOK drops call costs to 1 credit).

Choose Zapier Agents if

  • Your business already runs on Zapier — 9,000+ apps beats every other catalog.
  • You can tolerate the two-meter bill (Agents + core Zapier plan).

Choose Relay.app if

  • Your workflows need a human to approve before acting (refunds, posts, outreach).
  • You want near-perfect ease-of-use ratings and a usable free tier.

Choose Relevance AI if

  • You want multi-agent systems at the lowest entry price ($19/month billed annually).
  • You can invest time learning the builder's concepts.

Choose Botpress if

  • You are building a production customer-support agent with a technical teammate.

If you only need one recommendation: start with Lindy for personal productivity or Gumloop for team-wide AI work — both are genuinely buildable without a developer, and both have trials. Skip Botpress until the project justifies technical help.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest AI agent builder for a non-developer?

Lindy is the easiest for a complete beginner because you describe the agent in plain language and it works inside Slack, with no canvas to learn. Relay.app and Zapier Agents are the next easiest. Botpress and Relevance AI have visual builders but expect more comfort with logic and data.

How much does it cost to run an AI agent as a small business?

Entry points range from free tiers (Relay.app, Botpress, Gumloop) to about $19-$40 a month for real use: Relevance AI Pro at $19, Relay.app Professional at $19 billed annually, Lindy Plus at $29.99 per user, Zapier Agents Pro at $33.33 billed annually, and Gumloop Pro at $37. Budget 20-50% above the sticker price — usage billing rises with real workloads.

Can a non-developer really build an AI agent?

Yes, for well-defined tasks: lead triage, meeting notes, inbox handling, data extraction, and FAQ chatbots are all realistic. You still connect app credentials, write clear instructions, and test the output. Tasks needing custom API work, code, or complex branching are where most non-developers hit a wall.

Which AI agent builder has the most integrations?

Zapier Agents inherits Zapier's catalog of more than 9,000 apps, which is far larger than any rival here. Lindy advertises over 1,000 integrations plus MCP connections, Gumloop has roughly 125 native connectors plus MCP, and Relay.app offers 100+ integrations.

Is Botpress free for small businesses?

Botpress has a free plan with 100 conversations a month, 3 AI agents, and 3 seats, which works for a prototype or very light use. The first paid tier, Plus, starts at $150 per month with 250 conversations, so it becomes expensive fast for a small business compared with Gumloop or Relay.app.

Conclusion

The best AI agent builder for a non-developer is the one you can finish building on. Lindy removes the interface, Gumloop removes the seat tax, Zapier Agents removes the integration problem, Relay.app removes the fear of the agent doing something wrong, Relevance AI removes the price barrier, and Botpress gives production-grade power in exchange. Run your heaviest real week on a trial, and price that week before paying for a year.
