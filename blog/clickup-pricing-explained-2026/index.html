ClickUp Pricing Explained: Is It Worth It in 2026?

  • Project Management
  • Published Oct 12, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

ClickUp costs $0 on the Free Forever plan, $8 per user per month on Core and $17 per user per month on Business when billed annually (about 50% more on monthly billing), with Enterprise on a custom quote. It is worth it for most small teams: it bundles tasks, docs, whiteboards, chat, and native time tracking into one subscription that undercuts Monday and Asana at comparable tiers. Skip it if you only need a simple Kanban board, where a free tool does the job for $0.

ClickUp is one of the most aggressively priced project management tools on the market, which is both the draw and the trap. The headline numbers look cheap: a genuinely free plan, then $8 and $17 per user per month. But the plan you actually need depends on limits that are easy to miss — storage caps, lifetime feature counters, guest seat allowances, and a monthly-billing premium of about 50 percent. This guide explains every plan using the official pricing page, checked October 2026, with real-world cost examples for teams of one to twenty people. Note that the old "Unlimited" plan is now called Core, so many older comparison articles use a plan name that no longer exists.

ClickUp pricing at a glance

ClickUp has four main plans: Free Forever at $0, Core at $8 per user per month, Business at $17 per user per month, and Enterprise on a custom quote — plus a newer MAX plan at $100 per user per month built around ClickUp's full AI engine. These are billed-annually rates; monthly billing costs roughly 50 percent more. Every member of a workspace must be on the same plan, so the real monthly cost is the per-seat price times your headcount.

Free Forever: what you actually get

ClickUp's Free Forever plan costs $0 for unlimited tasks, subtasks, and members, with Kanban boards, collaborative docs, sprint management, native time tracking, and video calls included. For a solo freelancer or a tiny team running basic task lists, it is one of the most capable free plans in the category — most competitors cap members, projects, or tasks at this tier, while ClickUp caps none of them.

The limits are elsewhere. Storage is just 60 MB per workspace, and file attachments are the only things that count against it — a team uploading design files can burn through that in days. You also get only one form, and several premium features exist as lifetime "uses": cumulative counters that never reset, so once your allowance of dashboards, custom fields, or automations is spent, those features go read-only until you upgrade. The structural catch: an entire workspace upgrades together, so every member becomes a paid seat — no mixing free and paid users in one workspace.

Core plan: the real entry point

Core is ClickUp's first paid plan at $8 per user per month billed annually ($12 billed monthly), and it removes the four constraints that push most teams off the free plan. Storage becomes unlimited, custom fields become unlimited, Gantt charts become unlimited, and you get 1,000 automation runs per month — ten times the free allowance. Integrations are also unlimited, and ClickUp Chat, goals, and guests with permission controls join the package.

This is the plan most small businesses land on. The standout value is native time tracking carried over from the free plan plus custom fields at full capacity, which means a team can run projects, budgets, and simple pipelines in one place without a separate time-tracking subscription. That is the asymmetric advantage over tools like Asana or Monday: the price per seat is lower and the feature set per seat is wider, so consolidation is where the savings come from. The remaining gaps are reporting and control: no workload view, timesheets, private views, or SSO, and dashboard counts that stay capped. If you are comparing tool stacks, our Asana vs Monday vs ClickUp comparison for small teams shows how the three price out head to head.

Business plan: the automation tier

Business costs $17 per user per month billed annually ($25 billed monthly) and is the plan where ClickUp becomes a genuine management system: 10,000 automations per month, advanced dashboards with custom widgets, workload and resource management, timesheets, time-in-status reports, webhooks and automation integrations, Google SSO with SMS two-factor, and private views and docs. It also includes unlimited standard AI models, which fold the basic AI assistant into the plan rather than charging for it separately.

Ten thousand automations a month is the number that matters here. On Core's 1,000, a team with active status-change rules and multi-step workflows across several spaces can hit the ceiling in a busy month; Business multiplies it by ten and adds webhooks, so ClickUp can talk to your CRM, billing, or support tools without middleware. The reporting unlocks are the other reason teams step up: workload view balances assignments across people, timesheets feed invoicing, and private dashboards keep sensitive numbers away from the wider team. Enterprise and MAX

Enterprise is custom-priced and aimed at organizations that need governance: white labeling, advanced permissions, 1 million automations per month, SAML SSO and SCIM provisioning, audit logs, the enterprise API, and HIPAA options with a dedicated success manager. For a team under roughly fifty people, this tier is almost never necessary — Business already covers the operational features.

MAX is ClickUp's newer AI-first plan at $100 per user per month billed annually ($120 monthly): the full Max AI engine — unlimited AI fields, AI notetaker, autopilot agents, and image generation — bundled with every Enterprise control. It is priced for AI-heavy teams rather than typical small businesses. The notable shift is that standard AI models are now folded into Business and Enterprise, so a small team does not need MAX to get basic AI summaries and writing help inside ClickUp.

The hidden costs

Every member becomes a paid seat

ClickUp bills per workspace, and a workspace upgrades as a unit. When you move to Core or Business, every member becomes a paid seat — there is no way to keep some colleagues free. Worse for the budget, anyone from inside your organization whom you add to the workspace is automatically billed as a paid user, even if they only look at one dashboard. The fix is discipline: keep internal viewers on read-only guest access, which is unlimited and free on paid plans, instead of inviting them as members.

Guest allowances are limited on paid plans

Here is the quirk that surprises teams: on the Free plan, guests get full create and edit permissions at no charge. On paid plans, guests get permission controls, but each workspace starts with limited guest seats — 5 on Core and 10 on Business for the first paid user, plus 2 per extra member on Core and 5 on Business. A 10-person Core team gets 23 guest seats; a client-heavy agency can outgrow that and end up paying for extra seats. Unlimited read-only guests are free on paid plans, so structure client access as read-only dashboards where possible.

Monthly billing costs about 50 percent more

The annual prices are what ClickUp advertises, but monthly billing is steep: Core jumps from $8 to $12 per user, and Business from $17 to $25 — roughly a 50 percent premium in both cases. On a 10-person team, that is $960 versus $1,440 a year on Core, or $2,040 versus $3,000 on Business. The one safety net is ClickUp's 100% satisfaction guarantee: a full refund is available within 30 days of purchase, which makes a paid plan a low-risk test once you have outgrown free.

The AI upsell

Standard AI models now come with Business and Enterprise, but heavy AI usage sits behind the $100-per-user MAX plan, and AI add-ons have historically been priced around $7 to $9 per user per month. Teams evaluating ClickUp partly for its AI assistant should price AI as a separate line item. When it is worth it and when a cheaper tool wins

ClickUp is worth it when

  • You are replacing two or three subscriptions. One ClickUp seat covers tasks, docs, whiteboards, chat, and time tracking — the bundle effect is where the real savings live.
  • You want the most features per dollar in the category. At $8 to $17 per user, it undercuts Monday and Asana at comparable tiers while including native time tracking they charge extra for.
  • You need automations and reporting that free tools cannot do. Core's 1,000 and Business's 10,000 automation runs per month cover most small-team workflows.
  • Your whole team will actually live in it. A workspace everyone uses daily justifies the seat model; a workspace three people visit weekly does not.

A cheaper tool wins when

  • You only need a simple Kanban board. Free kanban tools handle basic boards at $0 with no seat math to do.
  • Your work is mostly docs and knowledge, not task execution. A wiki-first team gets more from a docs-native tool; our Notion vs Asana comparison breaks down that tradeoff.
  • You have many occasional users. ClickUp's per-workspace, every-member-is-a-seat billing punishes teams with lots of infrequent viewers.
  • You only need a few AI summaries a week. Paying $100 per user for the MAX plan to get heavy AI is overkill when standard models are already included in Business.

The cost argument against ClickUp's rivals is asymmetric, not head-on. Seat for seat, Monday's comparable tiers run roughly $12 to $24 per user per month on annual billing and Asana's roughly $11 to $25 — both above ClickUp's $8 and $17, and both typically charge extra for things ClickUp includes, like native time tracking and unlimited custom fields on Core. So ClickUp usually wins the spreadsheet. But that misses the real cost question: a team that needs almost nothing from its project tool should not be paying per seat at all. Free tiers exist for a reason, and a tool nobody opens is the most expensive tool of all.

Frequently asked questions

Is ClickUp really free?

Yes, the Free Forever plan costs $0 for unlimited tasks and members, including Kanban boards, docs, and sprint management. The catch is storage: only 60 MB, so teams that attach many files will run out fast. There is no time limit, but you cannot add paid-plan members to a free workspace without upgrading everyone.

How much does ClickUp cost per user in 2026?

As of October 2026, ClickUp's Core plan is $8 per user per month billed annually ($12 billed monthly), and Business is $17 per user per month billed annually ($25 billed monthly). Enterprise pricing is custom. Monthly billing costs about 47 to 50 percent more than annual billing.

What is the difference between ClickUp Core and Business?

Core unlocks unlimited storage, Gantt charts, custom fields, 1,000 automations per month, and guests with permissions. Business adds advanced dashboards, 10,000 automations per month, workload and resource management, timesheets, webhooks, Google SSO, and private views and docs.

Are there hidden costs with ClickUp?

The main ones are seats and guests. Upgrading a workspace turns every member into a paid seat, and paid plans only include a limited number of guest seats (5 on Core, 10 on Business for the first paid user, plus a few per extra member). Monthly billing costs about 50% more than annual, and the full AI engine lives on the $100-per-user MAX plan.

Is ClickUp worth it compared to cheaper tools?

It is worth it when one subscription replaces two or three others: at $8 to $17 per user, it undercuts Monday and Asana at comparable tiers while bundling docs, whiteboards, chat, and time tracking. It is not worth it if you only need a simple Kanban board, where a free tool like Trello does the job for $0.

Conclusion

ClickUp is worth it for most small businesses because the math is genuinely favorable: Core at $8 per user per month undercuts comparable competitors while bundling time tracking, docs, whiteboards, and unlimited storage into the same seat. Start on Free, move to Core when the 60 MB storage cap or lifetime feature counters bite, and step up to Business only when automations or workload views earn their extra $9 per seat.
