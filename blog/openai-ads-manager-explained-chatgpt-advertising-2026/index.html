OpenAI Ads Manager explained: advertising inside ChatGPT, what it costs, and what small businesses should do first (2026)

  • Emerging Tech
  • Published Oct 17, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026

Quick answer

OpenAI is putting ads inside ChatGPT and selling them through a self-serve Ads Manager at ads.openai.com. Ads appear as clearly labeled "Sponsored" blocks beneath AI responses — never inside the answer — and are matched to conversation context, not keywords. They show only to Free and Go plan users; Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu users see none. Buying is on CPC or CPM bidding with conversion measurement built in, but no public CPC or CPM benchmarks exist yet. The timeline: US testing began January 16, 2026, the Ads Manager beta opened in May, it expanded across India, Europe, and the Middle East/North Africa in August, and by September OpenAI reported a $1 billion annualized ad revenue run rate in under 200 days, with ads live in 60+ countries.

What OpenAI launched, and when

Advertising inside ChatGPT went from rumor to a billion-dollar run rate in under 200 days. The rollout was methodical: principles first, US test next, then a self-serve buying tool, then global expansion. Here is the verified timeline:

OpenAI Ads Manager rollout timeline
DateMilestone
Jan 16, 2026OpenAI announces it is testing ads in the US on Free and Go plans, with stated principles: answer independence and conversation privacy
Feb 9, 2026US ad test begins
May 2026Self-serve Ads Manager beta opens to US businesses — CPC bidding and conversion measurement
Aug 31, 2026OpenAI reports a $1 billion annualized ad revenue run rate in under 200 days; ads live in 60+ countries; Ads Manager opens in India, Europe, and Middle East/North Africa
Sept 16, 2026Test of Sponsored Agents with select US advertisers; Ads Manager plugin inside ChatGPT; AI creative suggestions; HubSpot becomes first CRM partner, Shopify first ecommerce partner

Scale is the headline. OpenAI's Dave Dugan, VP and Head of Global Ads Solutions, called the launch "the beginning of a new chapter for advertising," framing ads as the way to fund free and low-cost ChatGPT access. The numbers back up the framing: a $1B annualized run rate reached faster than nearly any ad product in history, "tens of thousands" of advertisers on board, and Similarweb reporting that 26% of US ChatGPT replies now carry sponsored ads.

How the ads actually appear

The format rules are the most important thing to understand, because they define what money can and cannot buy here. Ads appear as clearly labeled "Sponsored" blocks beneath AI responses — they never appear inside the answer itself. That separation is the "answer independence" principle from the January announcement: advertisers cannot buy their way into the model's own words.

Targeting is the real break from search advertising. Ads are matched to conversation context and intent, not keywords. The signals are the conversation itself, your landing page, your ad title and copy, plus optional advertiser "context hints" — which are hints, not exact-match keywords. There is no keyword bidding screen. If you are used to bidding on "best CRM for freelancers," you will need to think in scenarios instead: what is the user trying to do when your ad should appear?

Audience limits matter for reach planning. Ads are shown only to Free and Go plan users. Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, and Edu users stay ad-free — which means the most affluent, most professional slice of the audience is walled off by definition. Ads are also kept away from sensitive and regulated topics: no placements next to health, mental health, or politics conversations.

On privacy, the lines are clearly drawn: ad personalization can draw on past chats and memories only if the user opts in (off by default in the EEA and Switzerland), and advertisers only ever see aggregated views and clicks — never the conversations themselves. Signing up: who can advertise

The entry requirements are simple but real. You sign up at ads.openai.com, and your legal entity must be based in a country where the Ads Manager is available — currently 60+ countries (the help centre lists 63). Three buying routes exist:

  • Self-service Ads Manager — the DIY route most small businesses will use.
  • OpenAI's Ads Solutions team — the direct sales route for larger budgets.
  • Agency and tech partners — the holding companies are already in: dentsu, Havas, Omnicom, Publicis, and WPP are named launch partners.

One thing to file under unconfirmed: a single trade source has claimed there is no minimum spend requirement. That is single-sourced, so do not plan a budget around it — confirm the current policy inside the Ads Manager before assuming a $50 test campaign is allowed.

What it costs: CPC, CPM, and the missing benchmarks

OpenAI confirmed CPC and CPM bidding models, with conversion measurement built into Ads Manager so you can track what happens after the click. That is the full extent of what is publicly verified about pricing. And here is the sentence most coverage skips: no public CPC or CPM benchmarks exist yet. Any article quoting an "average ChatGPT ad CPC" in 2026 is inventing it. The auction is months old, the advertiser base is still onboarding, and OpenAI has published no pricing data.

What does that mean practically? Early auctions in new ad platforms usually run cheap — fewer bidders, lower clearing prices — which is exactly why testing early has an expected-value case. But "usually" is not data. Treat your first spend as price discovery: bid conservatively, measure conversions through the built-in measurement, and build your own benchmarks before scaling.

OpenAI reached a $1 billion annualized ad revenue run rate in under 200 days — with tens of thousands of advertisers — making this one of the fastest ad-business ramps on record.

ChatGPT ads vs Google Ads: the real differences

How ChatGPT advertising differs from Google Ads
DimensionChatGPT Ads ManagerGoogle Ads (search)
Targeting basisConversation context and intent; keyword bidding does not existKeyword matching, audiences, topics
Where ads appearLabeled "Sponsored" blocks beneath AI responses — never inside the answerAbove, below, and beside search results; also inside some AI Overviews
Who sees themFree and Go users only; paid tiers are ad-freeEveryone searching, regardless of plan
Sensitive topicsNo ads near health, mental health, or politicsRestricted but broader eligibility
Advertiser visibilityAggregated views/clicks only; conversations stay privateSearch terms, auctions, quality scores visible
BenchmarksNone published; price discovery phaseDecades of public CPC/CPM data

The strategic upshot: Google Ads captures declared intent ("buy standing desk"), while ChatGPT ads capture emergent intent — the moment someone is working through a problem in conversation and a relevant offer appears underneath. That is a genuinely new placement, not a cheaper version of search. It also means your best Google keyword lists do not transfer; the skill to build is scenario targeting.

What a small business should do first

1. Check eligibility, then set up conversion measurement before spending

Confirm your legal entity's country is on the list, sign up at ads.openai.com, and wire up conversion measurement first. With no public benchmarks, your own measured cost-per-conversion is the only number that matters.

2. Think in context hints, not keywords

Write down five conversations your ideal customer is having right before they need you. Selling bookkeeping software? The conversation is "my invoicing spreadsheet is a mess and tax season is coming" — that scenario, plus your landing page and ad copy, is your targeting. Use the optional context hints to steer it. Our guide to AI writing tools for marketing teams can help draft the ad copy variants you will need to test.

3. Start with a small price-discovery budget

Run a contained test to learn what clicks actually cost in your category. Because no minimum spend is unconfirmed, verify the floor inside the platform before you plan around a tiny test.

4. Keep the answer-independence rule in your favor

You cannot buy your way into the model's answer — but you can earn your way in. Brands that get cited by AI answers organically (through good content, reviews, and structured data) get the best of both worlds: the answer and the sponsored block beneath it. Ecommerce sellers should also note the Shopify integration — if you sell online, our Shopify vs Squarespace comparison and the Marklo AI marketing agent guide are useful companions while the commerce integrations mature.

5. Exclude what you cannot afford to be near

Health, mental health, and politics are excluded by default, but review your context hints for adjacent territory — a wellness brand does not want to appear next to a medical-advice conversation even if the platform allows it.

Sponsored Agents: what comes next

The September 16 test of Sponsored Agents with select US advertisers is the signal of where this goes. Instead of a static sponsored block, advertisers get agentic placements — and OpenAI is already shipping the infrastructure around it: an Ads Manager plugin inside ChatGPT, AI creative suggestions, and first-party integrations with HubSpot (the first CRM partner) and Shopify (the first ecommerce partner). The September test is limited to select US advertisers, so small businesses should treat this as preparation time, not a buying opportunity.

What to prepare: clean product feeds and landing pages (agents will act on them), CRM data hygiene for the HubSpot integration, and creative variants that an AI can assemble rather than single fixed banners. The advertisers who win the Sponsored Agents phase will be the ones whose catalogs and offers are machine-readable. Content teams should also keep an eye on content optimization tools — the pages that rank in AI answers today are the landing pages agents will draw on tomorrow.

Conclusion

OpenAI built a billion-dollar ad business in under 200 days by selling something Google never had: a labeled slot beneath the answer, targeted at the conversation rather than the keyword. For small businesses, the opportunity is real but the data is thin — no public benchmarks, an unconfirmed minimum spend, and an audience that excludes every paid ChatGPT tier. The smart first move is not a big budget; it is a small, measured price-discovery test built on context-hint thinking, with conversion tracking wired up from day one. Do that, keep your landing pages machine-readable for the Sponsored Agents phase, and you will have your own benchmarks — the one asset no competitor can copy — before the auction gets crowded.
