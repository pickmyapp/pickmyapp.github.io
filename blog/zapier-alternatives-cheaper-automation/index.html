Zapier Alternatives: 7 Cheaper Automation Tools Compared (2026)

  • AI Automation Tools
  • Published Oct 9, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

The cheapest like-for-like Zapier replacement is Make: its $9-a-month Core plan includes 10,000 credits, versus 750 tasks on Zapier's $19.99 Professional plan. Technical teams should self-host n8n for roughly the cost of a $5 server. Pabbly Connect's flat-rate plans suit simple high-volume automations. All seven tools are compared below with prices checked in October 2026 — pay attention to what each platform counts as a billable "unit," which matters more than the sticker price.

Zapier is the easiest automation tool to start with and one of the most expensive to scale. Its Professional plan begins at $19.99 a month billed annually for 750 tasks, and the bill climbs steeply from there because every successful action step in a Zap counts as a task — a five-step automation running 1,000 times a month burns 5,000 tasks. That pricing shape is exactly why smaller teams go looking for alternatives. Below are seven cheaper options worth a real look in 2026, with verified prices, who each one suits, and the catches to know about before you switch.

Why small businesses leave Zapier

Almost every departure story starts the same way: the bill outgrows the value. Zapier's task model is honest but punishing — triggers and filters are free, but each app action costs a task, so long multi-step workflows and high-frequency triggers multiply the invoice. A Zap that watches a form, checks a CRM for duplicates, creates a record, and sends two notifications might use five tasks per run. At 2,000 runs a month, that is 10,000 tasks — well past the entry tier.

The other two reasons are control and complexity. Zapier is cloud-only, so customer data always passes through its servers. And its linear step-by-step model handles branching and loops less gracefully than visual canvas builders. None of this means Zapier is bad — with 9,000-plus apps it still has the largest integration catalog in the category — but if the invoice stings, the market now offers genuine alternatives at a fraction of the cost.

How we compared these tools

We judged each alternative on four things that matter to a small business: entry price for a real production workload, what counts as a billable unit (tasks, credits, executions, or active flows), the free tier's usefulness, and who the tool is actually built for. Prices were checked against the vendors' own pricing pages in October 2026. This is a "cheaper than Zapier" roundup, so every tool here undercuts Zapier's $19.99 entry point for comparable volume — with one personal-use exception we flag below.

1. Make — best overall cheaper alternative

Make is the most natural first move off Zapier. You build "scenarios" on a visual canvas — modules linked by lines that show exactly how data flows — with native routers for branching, iterators for processing lists, and error handlers that decide what happens when a step fails. The company claims more than 3,000 apps, covering everything a typical small business uses.

Pricing is where Make wins. The Core plan is $9 a month billed annually ($10.59 monthly) for 10,000 credits, and since September 2026 that annual price is about 25% lower than it was earlier in the year. One credit covers roughly one module action, and triggers are free. Against Zapier's 750 tasks at $19.99, that is roughly half the price for over thirteen times the units. The free plan ($0, 1,000 credits, two scenarios) is enough to rebuild and test your key workflows before paying anything. The main tradeoff is learning curve: the canvas concepts take a few hours to click if you are coming from Zapier's linear steps. Our full n8n vs Make breakdown digs deeper into this matchup.

2. n8n — best for technical teams

n8n is the cheapest serious automation platform that exists, provided someone on your team can run a server. Its Community Edition is free, open-source software with no execution limit — you pay only for hosting, about $5 to $10 a month for a small virtual server, or even less on budget VPS hosts. If you would rather not self-host, n8n Cloud starts at €24 a month billed monthly (€20 billed annually) for 2,500 executions.

The billing model is what makes n8n special: one execution equals one complete workflow run, no matter how many steps it contains. A twenty-step workflow counts the same as a two-step one. For complex, high-volume automations — especially AI agent workflows, where n8n's dedicated agent nodes and JavaScript/Python code nodes shine — nothing else comes close on price. The catch is real: n8n assumes comfort with JSON, API credentials, and expressions. Non-technical teams should budget a learning curve of a week or two, and self-hosters own updates, backups, and uptime.

3. Pabbly Connect — best flat-rate pricing

Pabbly Connect is the budget pick for simple, high-volume automations. Its pricing is refreshingly flat: plans start from about $16 a month billed annually for 12,000 tasks, triggers and internal steps are free, and the company sells lifetime deals from around $249 for buyers who would rather pay once. That structure favors exactly the workloads Zapier punishes — long, simple workflows running thousands of times.

The tradeoff is polish. The builder is functional rather than delightful, the app catalog is smaller than Make's, and advanced branching is more limited. But for the classic small-business use cases — form to spreadsheet, new orders into an email list, payment notifications — it does the job at a fraction of Zapier's cost. The free plan (100 tasks a month) is thin, so use the trial period to confirm your apps are covered before committing.

4. Integrately — best for beginners

Integrately is built for people who never want to learn an automation builder at all. It advertises millions of one-click templates: pick two apps, choose a ready-made automation, activate it. For a solo founder who just wants new Shopify orders in a Google Sheet, the setup takes minutes.

Entry pricing is $19.99 a month billed annually for 2,000 tasks — essentially matching Zapier's headline price while more than doubling the task allowance, and higher tiers scale more gently. It is genuinely the easiest tool on this list. The ceiling is lower, though: complex branching, custom logic, and unusual apps are not Integrately's world. Think of it as the fastest route from "I need this connected" to "it works," and look elsewhere when your automations start needing real logic.

5. Activepieces — cheapest open-source cloud

Activepieces is the open-source middle ground between Make's ease and n8n's freedom. Like n8n, it can be self-hosted for free with unlimited usage. Unlike n8n, its managed cloud pricing is unusually gentle: from about $5 a month per active flow, which means a business running three automations pays roughly $15 a month with no usage meter to think about.

The builder is clean and visual, the app catalog is growing, and the per-flow pricing is refreshingly predictable. The risk is maturity: Activepieces is younger and smaller than its rivals, so expect fewer integrations, thinner documentation, and a community that cannot yet match n8n's. For a small team that wants open-source pricing with a friendlier setup than self-hosted n8n, it deserves a test drive.

6. Microsoft Power Automate — best for Microsoft 365 shops

If your business lives in Microsoft 365, Power Automate may be the cheapest option of all — because you might already be paying for it. Limited automation is bundled with several Microsoft 365 plans, and the standalone premium plan is $15 per user a month billed annually. Nothing integrates with Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, and Excel more deeply, and desktop RPA features can automate legacy Windows software that has no API at all.

Outside the Microsoft ecosystem, the appeal drops fast. The builder is powerful but widely considered clunky, per-user pricing adds up quickly for larger teams, and integrations with non-Microsoft apps trail the dedicated platforms. But for a team already paying for Microsoft 365 that mainly automates between Microsoft apps, it is hard to justify paying Zapier instead.

7. IFTTT — cheapest personal automation

IFTTT Pro starts at $2.99 a month, making it the cheapest paid automation tool on this list — with a caveat. IFTTT is built for personal use: smart home routines, social media cross-posting, phone notifications, simple "if this, then that" applets. The free plan allows only two applets, so Pro is where real use begins.

Do not buy IFTTT for business workflows. It lacks multi-step logic, error handling, and team features entirely. But if your Zapier bill is mostly personal automations — cross-posting content, logging tasks, smart-home triggers — moving those to IFTTT and downgrading Zapier can cut your total spend meaningfully.

How to choose the right one

Do not start with the feature list. Start with the constraint that is pushing you off Zapier:

  • Bill too high, workflows ordinary: go with Make. The $9 Core plan covers what most small businesses automate, and the builder is the gentlest upgrade from Zapier's model.
  • Someone technical on the team: self-host n8n. Nothing else matches free unlimited executions, and its AI agent nodes are the strongest in this price range.
  • Simple workflows, huge volume: Pabbly Connect's flat rate makes costs predictable no matter how long your workflows get.
  • No time to learn anything: Integrately's one-click templates get you running in minutes.
  • Already paying for Microsoft 365: check what Power Automate coverage your license includes before buying anything else.
  • Mostly personal automations: move those to IFTTT at $2.99 and downgrade Zapier instead of replacing it.

Switching without breaking things

There is no universal one-click importer for Zaps, so plan a rebuild rather than a migration. Start with your three most-used automations and recreate them in the free tier of your chosen tool — Make's free plan and n8n's trial both support this. Keep Zapier running while you test the rebuilds side by side for a week or two, checking that triggers fire, data maps correctly, and error paths behave. Then move the rest over one workflow at a time. Turn off the Zapier version of each automation only after its replacement has run cleanly in production. Document your app credentials as you go; reconnecting them is the most tedious part of any switch.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest Zapier alternative?

For personal use, IFTTT Pro starts at $2.99 a month. For business use, the cheapest credible options are Make's Core plan at $9 a month billed annually (10,000 credits), Pabbly Connect from about $16 a month for 12,000 tasks, and n8n self-hosted — free software, where you only pay roughly $5 a month for a server.

Is Make really cheaper than Zapier?

Yes, usually by a wide margin. Zapier's Professional plan starts at $19.99 a month billed annually for 750 tasks, while Make's Core plan is $9 a month billed annually for 10,000 credits. Because Zapier counts every successful action step as a task, multi-step automations multiply the cost quickly. Prices checked October 2026.

Can I migrate my Zaps to another automation tool?

There is no universal one-click importer, so migration means rebuilding each Zap in the new tool, reconnecting your app credentials, and retesting. Start with your three most-used Zaps, keep Zapier running while you rebuild them, and switch the rest over one at a time.

What happened to Relay.app?

Relay.app, an AI-first automation challenger, shut down in September 2026 and deleted its users' workspaces. Several older comparison articles still recommend it, so treat any list that names Relay.app as stale and verify current alternatives against live pricing pages.

Which Zapier alternative is easiest for beginners?

Integrately is built for beginners: it offers millions of one-click templates where you pick two apps and activate a pre-made automation. Make is the next easiest step up — its visual scenario builder takes a little longer to learn but handles much more complex logic. Pabbly Connect is simple too, though its interface feels less polished.

Conclusion

Most small businesses leaving Zapier should start with Make: at $9 a month for 10,000 credits it is dramatically cheaper than Zapier's $19.99 for 750 tasks, and the visual builder handles real-world logic well. Technical teams will save even more by self-hosting n8n, high-volume simple workflows fit Pabbly Connect's flat rate, and Microsoft 365 shops should check Power Automate before buying anything. Whatever you choose, compare the billing unit — not just the price — and rebuild your top three Zaps on a free tier before you cancel anything.
