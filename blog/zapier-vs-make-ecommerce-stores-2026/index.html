Zapier vs Make for ecommerce stores (2026)

  • AI Automation Tools
  • Published Oct 24, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most ecommerce stores, Make is the cheaper automation tool in 2026, but only if your flows are built the right way. Make paid plans start at 10,000 credits for $9 per month billed annually, while Zapier Professional gives you just 750 tasks for $19.99 per month billed annually. The catch is how each counts usage: Zapier charges per successful action step, with triggers and filters free, while Make charges one credit per module execution, including filters and polling checks. A one-minute polling trigger can burn about 43,200 credits a month on its own, and an order with 10 line items can multiply through an iterator. Build on webhooks and keep polling rare, and Make usually wins on cost per order. Stick with Zapier for its 9,000-plus app catalog or simpler builder.

Quick verdict for stores

Most store owners ask the wrong question first: they compare headline prices, $19.99 against $9, and declare a winner. The right question is cost per order: how many billable units does one real order consume, from checkout to fulfillment to follow-up? That number decides your monthly bill.

Here is the short version:

  • Choose Make if you run webhook-based flows, process hundreds of orders a month, and will learn a visual builder. Your cost per order will usually be a fraction of Zapier's.
  • Choose Zapier if you value the largest app catalog, the gentlest learning curve, and simple flows your team can edit. You will pay more per order, but ship faster with fewer surprises.
  • Reconsider both if you are technical and want to self-host: n8n is the open-source alternative, covered in our Make vs n8n pricing at scale and n8n vs Make for small business guides.

All prices were checked in October 2026; confirm them on vendor pricing pages before buying. Annual prices are the per-month rate when billed yearly.

How the billing units actually differ

Zapier and Make do not sell the same thing, even though both call it automation. For a store, the billing unit is the whole game: a small per-order difference compounds into hundreds of dollars a month.

Zapier counts tasks. One task is one successful action step in a Zap. The trigger is free, filters are free, and a failed step does not count. A Zap that watches for a new Shopify order, checks whether it is over $50, then creates a fulfillment record and sends a Slack message costs two tasks, not four. That free trigger and filter make Zapier predictable for simple flows.

Make counts credits (renamed from "operations" in August 2025). One credit is one module execution, and almost everything counts: trigger, action modules, filters, and polling checks. A webhook trigger costs one credit per real event. A polling trigger costs one credit every time it checks, whether or not anything is new. That single difference is where Make bills explode.

Two more Make mechanics matter. Iterators multiply credits: an order with 10 line items passing through an iterator and a spreadsheet module can consume 20 credits for that stretch alone. Unused credits roll over for one month only, softening a quiet month without banking a surplus forever. If you sell bundles or wholesale cases, model the iterator path before you commit.

Quotable fact: at a one-minute polling interval, a single Make polling scenario makes about 43,200 checks a month (60 checks an hour x 24 hours x 30 days), each costing a credit. That alone is more than four times the 10,000 credits on every entry-level Make paid tier. Source: Make's credit model, checked October 2026; confirm on Make's pricing page.

Pricing at realistic store volumes

Headline plans, checked October 2026: Zapier Professional is $19.99 per month billed annually ($29.99 month to month) for 750 tasks, and Team is $69 billed annually ($103.50 month to month) for 2,000 tasks. The Professional slider runs roughly 5,000 tasks for $89, 10,000 for $129, and 20,000 for $189 a month. Zapier is cloud-only, with 9,000-plus apps.

Make's paid tiers all start at 10,000 credits: Core $9 billed annually ($10.59 month to month), Pro $16 ($18.82) with priority execution, and Teams $29 ($34.12) with roles. The Core slider scales, billed annually, from 10,000 credits at $9 to 20,000 at $16, 40,000 at $29, 80,000 at $55, 150,000 at $99, and 300,000 at $182.16 a month. The free plan offers 1,000 credits a month, two scenarios, and a 15-minute interval. Make connects roughly 3,000-plus apps.

Worked examples assume a typical webhook order flow: order in, fulfillment record created, tracking email sent, sheet row added. On Zapier that is about 3 tasks per order; on Make, about 4 credits.

At 500 orders a month: Zapier needs about 1,500 tasks, so the 750-task Professional plan falls short; you are into Team territory at roughly $69 a month or more. Make needs about 2,000 credits, fitting comfortably in the $9 Core plan. Cost per order: roughly 14 cents on Zapier versus under 2 cents on Make.

At 2,000 orders a month: Zapier needs about 6,000 tasks, roughly $89 to $129 a month on the slider. Make needs about 8,000 credits, still inside the $9 Core plan. Cost per order: roughly 5 cents on Zapier versus under a cent on Make.

At high volume with many line items, Make's advantage narrows. Suppose 5,000 orders a month average 5 line items and your flow iterates them into a sheet: that stretch alone can add 25,000 to 50,000 credits, pushing Make toward the $55 tier, while Zapier does not multiply per line item the same way. Zapier still costs more in most builds, but the gap shrinks fast, and a polling trigger on top would erase it. See also our guide to cheaper Zapier alternatives.

Ecommerce workflows compared

Price only matters if the workflow works. Here is how the six flows every store needs compare.

Typical ecommerce workflows: which platform handles each more cheaply.

Abandoned cartEasy with email toolsCheaper at volumeTiming delays on polling
Inventory and low-stock alertsSimple threshold ZapsCheap on webhooksInventory polls every minute
Refund and return handlingClear multi-step ZapsCheaper, more branchingMulti-line refunds multiply
Review requestsSimple delayed sendsCheap scheduled sendsDelay modules still run
Multi-app chainsExpensive per stepCheaper per moduleLong chains add up either way

Order to fulfillment is the bread-and-butter flow. Zapier's version is easiest to build and debug, especially with a standard 3PL or ShipStation setup. Make's costs far less per order at volume, and its canvas makes failures easier to spot mid-flow.

Abandoned cart recovery is where timing matters: an email sent three hours late converts worse than one sent in 30 minutes. Zapier's trigger fires when the cart event happens. On Make, a webhook trigger is equally fast, but a polling trigger can add a full polling interval of delay, so check for an instant trigger before you build.

Inventory and low-stock alerts are the classic polling trap. On Zapier, frequent polling costs no tasks, only the actions that follow. On Make, a one-minute poll costs about 43,200 credits a month per scenario, dwarfing the alert actions. Use a webhook or longer interval on Make, or keep this flow on Zapier if your inventory app offers no webhook.

Refund and return handling rewards Make's branching: one scenario can route full refunds, partial refunds, and exchanges down different paths. Remember each filter costs a credit on Make and nothing on Zapier, and multi-line refunds iterate. Review requests are simple on both, though Make's cheaper units make multi-touch post-delivery sequences far more affordable.

Multi-app chains, such as Shopify to Sheets to Slack to email, expose Zapier's weakness: every extra step is another task, so a five-step chain on 2,000 orders is 10,000 tasks. Make handles the same chain for a similar number of credits but from a much larger allowance. Pair this decision with our ActiveCampaign vs Klaviyo and Mailchimp vs Klaviyo pricing comparisons, since the email tool often drives the automation design.

App coverage for stores

Zapier connects 9,000-plus apps; Make roughly 3,000-plus. For a typical Shopify, WooCommerce, or BigCommerce store, that gap rarely bites: both cover Shopify, WooCommerce, Stripe, PayPal, ShipStation, Sheets, Slack, and major email platforms in depth, including order, refund, and customer triggers. The gap shows up in the long tail: a niche 3PL, regional gateway, small loyalty app, or industry ERP is more likely to exist on Zapier as a mature integration.

Depth matters too. Zapier's Shopify and Stripe integrations tend to expose more triggers out of the box. Make's are often deeper in data handling, letting you map nested fields and branch on complex conditions without extra steps. Before choosing, search both directories for your three least-common apps: if all three exist on Make, the gap is theoretical; if one is missing, it can force a workaround.

Ease of use and the visual builder

Zapier's builder is linear: trigger, then steps, top to bottom. A non-technical owner can build a first Zap in an afternoon, and a new hire can read one without a tutorial. That simplicity is Zapier's real moat, and why many stores stay despite the price. See our guide to Make vs Zapier for beginners for the first-week experience on both.

Make's builder is a visual canvas: modules connected by lines, with routers, filters, iterators, and aggregators laid out like a flowchart. It is more powerful for complex logic and faster once learned, but more intimidating at first, and debugging a failed scenario takes more skill than reading a failed Zap. Budget a weekend to get comfortable.

When Zapier wins, when Make wins

Zapier wins when

  • Your store runs simple flows and values speed over unit cost.
  • You need a niche app only Zapier covers well.
  • Your team is non-technical and edits flows often.
  • Your flows rely on frequent polling.
  • You want the largest template library to copy from.

Make wins when

  • You process hundreds of orders or more each month.
  • Your flows are webhook-based, not polling-based.
  • You run multi-step, branching, or multi-app chains.
  • Cost per order is a line item you track.
  • You will learn a visual canvas builder.

Migration notes

Do not migrate everything at once. Start with your highest-volume flow, usually order to fulfillment, and rebuild it while the old one still runs. Run both for two to four weeks and compare real usage: units consumed, failures, and delays.

Watch for three gotchas. Triggers are not identical: a "new order" event may fire at payment on one platform and at fulfillment on the other, so test with real orders. Field names differ, especially for line items, discounts, and taxes, so remap carefully. Turn off the old flow only after the new one has handled a full cycle, including refunds and a low-stock event.

Verdict

For most ecommerce stores in 2026, Make is the better buy on cost per order, and it is not close at low to mid volumes: a $9 Core plan can absorb what costs $69 or more on Zapier. But Make only stays cheap if you build on webhooks, avoid one-minute polling, and design around iterator multiplication. Ignore those three rules and Make can surprise you just as unpleasantly as Zapier's task meter.

Zapier remains right when simplicity, catalog breadth, and team familiarity matter more than the bill, and when your flows lean on polling or niche apps. Either way, measure your real order flow for a month, not just a pricing page. Build the flow, count the units, then choose.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zapier or Make cheaper for a small ecommerce store?

For most small stores, Make is cheaper at low to mid volumes, because its paid tiers start at 10,000 credits for $9 per month billed annually, while Zapier Professional starts at 750 tasks for $19.99 per month billed annually. The exception is a store with heavy polling or many line items per order, where Make credits can multiply fast. Always model your own order flow before choosing, and confirm current prices on the vendor pricing pages.

How do Zapier tasks differ from Make credits?

On Zapier, one successful action step counts as one task, while triggers, filters, and failed steps are free. On Make, one module execution counts as one credit, and that includes filters and polling checks, so a filter or a poll that finds nothing still costs a credit. Webhook triggers on Make cost one credit per real event, which is why webhooks are usually the cheapest way to start a Make scenario.

Does Make polling really burn credits when nothing happens?

Yes. A Make polling trigger checks on a schedule and each check costs a credit, even when there is no new order. At a one-minute interval that is about 43,200 checks per month for a single scenario, which alone can exceed a 10,000-credit plan. Use instant webhook triggers for Shopify, WooCommerce, and Stripe where available, and reserve polling for apps that offer no webhook.

Why does Make cost more when an order has many line items?

Because Make charges one credit per module execution, and an iterator runs once per line item. An order with 10 line items that passes through an iterator and a follow-up module can therefore use 10 credits for the iterator plus 10 for the next step, instead of one. Zapier charges per action step instead, so the same order does not multiply in the same way, though Zapier multi-step flows can still get expensive.

Should an ecommerce store switch from Zapier to Make?

Switch if your Zapier bill is driven by volume and your flows are mostly webhook-based, since Make usually cuts the cost per order sharply. Stay on Zapier if your team relies on its huge app catalog, its simpler builder, or apps that Make covers weakly. Either way, rebuild one high-volume flow first, measure real usage for a month, and only then migrate the rest.

Conclusion

Zapier vs Make for ecommerce is not a feature contest; it is a billing-unit contest. Zapier sells simple steps at a premium, with free triggers and filters. Make sells far more units for far less, but charges for nearly everything, including polls and filters, and multiplies on line items. Build webhook-first on Make and you will usually pay pennies per order; value Zapier's catalog and simplicity and you will pay more, knowing why. Count your units per order, test for a month, and let that number decide.
