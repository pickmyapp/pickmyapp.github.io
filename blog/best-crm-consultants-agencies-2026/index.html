Quick answer

Pipedrive is the best CRM for most agencies: its visual pipelines were built for exactly the work agencies do — tracking proposals, follow-ups, and retainer renewals client by client. HubSpot wins if you want a genuinely free start with a marketing suite to grow into, Zoho CRM is the value pick for multi-client operations on a budget, Insightly is the only one here with real project management built in, and Streak is the no-new-tool option if your team lives in Gmail. What agencies and consultancies actually need from a CRM

A generic sales CRM tracks leads becoming customers. An agency CRM has to track something messier: clients becoming repeat revenue. The checklist we scored each tool against:

  • Client pipelines, not just lead pipelines. Agencies run two motions at once — winning new business and expanding existing accounts. You need multiple pipelines: one for prospects, one for active engagements, one for renewals and upsells.
  • Retainer and recurring-revenue visibility. A retainer agency's health is monthly recurring revenue per client, not one-time deal value. The CRM should track MRR on a deal, flag upcoming renewals, and separate recurring from project income.
  • Proposal-to-delivery handoff. In product sales, "won" is the end of the story; in agencies, it is the midpoint. The best tools connect the signed proposal to onboarding tasks and milestones without a second system.
  • Proposals and invoicing. No CRM here does full accounting, but proposal generation, quotes, and invoicing integrations matter. Switching tools five times to send one proposal is how billable hours die.
  • Contact-level relationship history. Agency work is relationship work. Every email, call note, and meeting across every contact at a client account should live in one record, so a handoff from founder to account manager loses nothing.
  • Per-seat pricing that survives growth. Agencies hire in spurts. A tool that costs $29 per seat today and $90 per seat when you need forecasting is fine — as long as you see the cliff before you walk off it.

If you are a solo operator rather than an agency, some of this is overkill — see our guide to the best CRMs for freelancers and solo consultants for a lighter-weight shortlist.

Tool-by-tool: which agency each CRM suits

Pipedrive — best for sales-led agencies

Pipedrive is the agency pipeline specialist. Its drag-and-drop deal boards are the clearest visual pipeline UI at this price, and you can run separate pipelines for new business, active clients, and renewals. Email sync and tracking arrive on Advanced ($34/user/month annually); Professional ($49) adds the AI Sales Assistant, lead scoring, and forecasting. The Smart Docs add-on builds proposals with e-signatures from deal data.

The honest caveat: add-ons (LeadBooster, Web Visitors, Campaigns, Smart Docs) are billed separately, and real invoices show the true per-seat cost often lands 50 to 100 percent above the sticker price. Budget roughly $245/month for a 5-person team on Professional, before add-ons. Pipedrive does no invoicing — pair it with QuickBooks, Xero, or FreshBooks.

HubSpot — best free start, and the growth platform

HubSpot's free CRM is the most generous free tier in this comparison — real pipelines, contact management, email tracking, and meeting scheduling for up to 2 users. For a two-person consultancy, that can be your entire CRM for $0. Starter ($15/seat/month annual) adds multiple pipelines and sequences; Professional ($90/seat/month plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee) unlocks forecasting and full automation.

The real pitch is the suite: Sales, Marketing, Service, and Content Hubs share one data model, so the agency that starts free can add client marketing and support ticketing without migrating. Our Zoho vs HubSpot comparison breaks down where that platform play beats a cheaper specialist. The trap: Marketing Hub scales on marketing contacts — a second cost axis — and Professional's onboarding fee makes the jump from Starter brutal. Budget the tier you will need in 12 months, not the one you can afford today.

Zoho CRM — best value for multi-client agencies

Zoho CRM is the budget champion for agencies that need depth, not just a pipeline. Standard ($14/user/month annual) covers multiple pipelines, custom modules, and workflow rules; Professional ($23) adds Blueprint — visual process automation, genuinely useful for standardizing client onboarding; Enterprise ($40) adds Zia AI, client portals, and journey orchestration.

The killer combination is the Zoho suite itself: Zoho CRM plus Zoho Books gives you CRM-to-invoice in one ecosystem at a fraction of HubSpot's cost. The free edition (3 users, 5,000 records) is enough for a boutique firm to trial. Downsides: the interface feels busier than Pipedrive or Attio, and you will spend a weekend on setup. If budget is the deciding factor, see our free CRM roundup before paying for anything.

Freshsales — cheapest paid start with AI built in

Freshsales undercuts every paid plan here. Growth costs $9/user/month annually and includes kanban pipelines, contact lifecycle stages, and built-in chat, email, and phone. Pro ($39) adds Freddy AI lead scoring and sales sequences; Enterprise ($59) adds forecasting and permissions.

Freddy AI is the differentiator at this price: lead scoring and next-best-action suggestions that competitors gate behind plans costing two to three times more. The weakness is agency-specific depth — retainer tracking, client portals, and proposal workflows are thinner than on Pipedrive or Insightly.

Attio — best modern pick for relationship-driven consultancies

Attio is the CRM built for how consultancies actually win work: relationships, not transactions. Its flexible data model lets you model clients, engagements, and stakeholders however you like. AI attributes auto-fill records — summarize a client, classify an opportunity, or run web research on a prospect's funding stage — genuinely useful when your pipeline is fifty relationships deep. The free plan (3 seats, 50,000 records) is generous.

For agencies, the friction points: the Plus plan ($29/user/month annual, $36 monthly) caps at 10 seats, forcing a jump to Pro ($59 annual / $86 monthly) as you grow, and Attio meters AI and automation with credits on top of seat pricing. It is also a younger product, so the integration library is smaller than HubSpot's or Pipedrive's. Best fit: a consultancy where the founders' network IS the pipeline.

Streak — best if your agency lives in Gmail

Streak's entire pitch is zero learning curve: the CRM lives inside Gmail as a browser extension. Pipelines appear as columns in your inbox, contact history shows in a sidebar on every email, and mail merge and tracking work from the compose window. For a consultancy where every deal starts and ends in email threads, adoption is instant because there is no new tool.

But count the cost honestly: Pro is $49/user/month annually (Pro+ $69, which you need for automations), making a 5-person agency pay $2,940 a year — more than Zoho Enterprise or Pipedrive Professional for a less complete product. The free tier includes email tools but no CRM pipelines at all. And Streak is Gmail-only; Outlook shops need not apply. Pick it when inbox-native speed matters more than features per dollar.

Insightly — best CRM-plus-project-management for agencies

Insightly is the only tool here that understands agencies do two jobs: win the client and deliver the work. A won opportunity converts directly into a project with milestones, tasks, and assigned team members — no separate project tool, no delivery handoff lost between systems. Built-in quotes and workflow automation (Professional, $49/user/month annual) handle the proposal side; entry Plus ($29) covers pipelines for smaller teams.

Quick fact: replacing a separate CRM and project management tool is where Insightly's math works best — if you currently pay for both, Insightly often comes out cheaper than either specialist combo.

This is the pick for project-heavy consultancies: dev shops, creative agencies, implementation firms. The trade-offs: no free plan (14-day trial only), and Insightly's marketing and integration add-ons scale costs fast — keep it to CRM plus projects and it stays reasonable.

Retainer and proposal workflows: the agency-specific test

How each tool handles the three workflows agencies live on.

Retainer tracking (monthly recurring revenue per client)

Insightly leads because delivery lives in the same system — a monthly retainer becomes a project with recurring milestones. Zoho CRM's Enterprise plan supports recurring-revenue reporting and client portals; HubSpot tracks MRR natively in deal properties and forecasts recurring revenue on Professional. Pipedrive handles retainers with custom MRR fields, while Freshsales, Attio, and Streak need custom fields or workarounds.

Proposals and invoicing

Pipedrive's Smart Docs add-on generates proposals from deal data with e-signatures — the smoothest proposal flow here. Insightly has built-in quotes; Zoho CRM builds quotes natively and pushes approved ones into Zoho Books as invoices; HubSpot quotes live on paid Sales Hub tiers. Attio, Freshsales, and Streak rely on integrations like PandaDoc or DocuSign. No CRM on this list is an accounting tool — set that expectation now. Everyone else integrates with QuickBooks, Xero, or FreshBooks through native connectors or Zapier-style automation.

What a 5-person agency actually pays in 2026

Sticker prices are per seat, but agencies budget per team. The annual math for five users on entry-level paid plans, annual billing:

  • Freshsales Growth: about $540/year — the cheapest real CRM on this list.
  • Zoho CRM Standard: about $840/year — multi-pipeline depth for under $1,000.
  • Pipedrive Essential: about $840/year — but most agencies will need Advanced ($2,040/year) for email sync and automation.
  • Insightly Plus: about $1,740/year — and it replaces a separate project tool.
  • Attio Plus: about $1,740/year annual ($2,160 on monthly billing) — plus possible credit costs for heavy AI/automation use.
  • Streak Pro: about $2,940/year — the price of inbox convenience.
  • HubSpot: $0 for two people; Starter for five runs about $900/year; Professional for five costs $5,400/year plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee.

Two budget rules: first, add 50 to 100 percent headroom on Pipedrive once you count add-ons; second, price the tier you will need in a year, not the one you can afford today. If budget is the deciding factor, our free CRM roundup shows how far $0 can stretch before you pay for anything.

The verdict: best CRM by agency type

Sales-led agencies (new business is the engine)

  • Winner: Pipedrive — clearest pipelines, Smart Docs proposals, and automation that keeps follow-ups from slipping.
  • Runner-up: Freshsales — AI lead scoring at a third of the price, for outbound-heavy shops.

Retainer and project agencies (delivery is half the business)

  • Winner: Insightly — won deals become tracked projects; no delivery handoff lost between tools.
  • Runner-up: Zoho CRM Enterprise — client portals plus Zoho Books invoicing for the full client-operations loop.

Boutique consultancies (2–10 people, relationship-driven)

  • Winner: Attio — flexible relationship data model with AI research, generous free plan for tiny teams.
  • Runner-up: Streak — if every deal lives in Gmail, the zero-learning-curve pick.

Budget-conscious agencies (under $1,000/year)

  • Winner: Zoho CRM — Standard at $840/year for five users with real multi-pipeline depth.
  • Runner-up: Freshsales — $540/year for five users; the absolute floor for a real paid CRM.

One decision shortcut: if you are not sure, start with HubSpot free. It costs nothing for two users, it handles real pipelines, and when you outgrow it you will know exactly which features you are paying for next.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free CRM for consultants?

HubSpot CRM is the best free starting point: its free tier covers up to 2 users with real pipelines, contact management, and email tracking. Zoho CRM's free edition allows 3 users, Freshsales' free plan also covers 3 users, and Attio's free plan supports 3 seats with 50,000 records. For a full comparison, see our roundup of the best free CRMs for startups in 2026.

Do agencies really need a CRM, or is a spreadsheet enough?

A spreadsheet works until you have about 5 active clients. After that, follow-ups slip, proposals sit in email threads nobody can find, and nobody can see the true pipeline value. A CRM gives every client one record, tracks retainer renewals automatically, and shows new-business and existing-account health in one view.

Which CRM is best for tracking retainer revenue?

Insightly is strongest here because it pairs CRM with project management, so a signed retainer converts straight into a tracked engagement with milestones and tasks. Zoho CRM's Enterprise plan adds client portals and recurring-revenue reporting, and Pipedrive handles retainers with custom fields for monthly recurring revenue on each deal.

Can a CRM handle proposals and invoicing, or do I need other tools?

No CRM in this comparison does full invoicing on its own, so expect at least one companion tool. Pipedrive's Smart Docs add-on builds client-ready proposals with e-signatures, Insightly has built-in quotes, and Zoho CRM integrates natively with Zoho Books for invoicing.

How much should a small agency budget for a CRM in 2026?

For a 5-person agency, expect $540 to about $2,940 per year on entry paid plans: Freshsales Growth is roughly $540/year, Zoho Standard about $840/year, Insightly Plus about $1,740/year, and Streak Pro about $2,940/year. Add roughly $1,000 to $2,000 for onboarding or add-ons. HubSpot can stay free for a 2-person shop, but Sales Hub Professional runs $90 per seat per month plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee.

Conclusion

Pipedrive wins for agencies that live and die on new business, Insightly for agencies where delivery is the business, Zoho CRM for agencies that need depth on a budget, Attio for relationship-driven boutiques, and HubSpot for anyone who should start free and grow into a platform. Pick the one whose pipeline matches your client lifecycle, set up retainer and renewal tracking on day one, and give it 90 days.
