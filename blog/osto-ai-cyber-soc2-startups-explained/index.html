OSTO explained: the AI-native cyber platform promising SOC 2 readiness in 7 days for startups and small teams (2026)

  • Personal Cybersecurity
  • Published Oct 15, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 9 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

OSTO is an AI-native, all-in-one cybersecurity platform for startups and SMBs that launched publicly on October 15, 2026. It bundles security controls, compliance automation, cyber insurance, penetration testing (VAPT), and virtual CISO services into one monthly subscription, and claims SOC 2 and ISO 27001 readiness in seven working days. We could not independently verify pricing, customer results, or the 7-day claim: the company has published neither a pricing page nor public audit outcomes. Treat the 7 days as audit-ready preparation (evidence, templates, controls), not a finished certificate — the actual audit still takes an independent auditor and, for SOC 2 Type II, months of observation.

If your startup has ever been handed an enterprise security questionnaire with a deal attached, you know the panic. Traditionally, getting serious about security meant juggling ten to twelve vendors, months of integration work, and spending tens of thousands before a single control was live. OSTO launched on October 15, 2026, with a pitch aimed squarely at that pain: one AI-native platform covering security, compliance, insurance, penetration testing, and a virtual CISO, as a monthly subscription — with SOC 2 and ISO 27001 readiness, normally six to eight weeks, promised in seven working days. This guide explains what the product actually is, how that claim works in practice, what remains unverified, and how it stacks up against Vanta and Drata.

What is OSTO?

OSTO is an AI-native cybersecurity platform built specifically for startups and small businesses (osto.one). Co-founded by repeat cybersecurity entrepreneurs Karmesh Gupta (CEO) and Praveen Gupta, it launched publicly on October 15, 2026, backed by Finvolve (the venture fund of IA), PointOne Capital, and GSF. It is headquartered in San Francisco with operations in Gurugram, India. At launch the company reported a team that grew from 12 to 27 in six months, customers across the US, India, UAE, Latin America, and Australia, and more than one billion requests processed per month — company-reported figures we could not independently verify.

The core idea is consolidation. A startup chasing enterprise-grade security usually assembles a patchwork: endpoint and cloud vendors, a compliance tool like Vanta, a pen-testing firm, an insurance broker, a consultant as a part-time security lead — each with its own deployment, dashboard, and budget. OSTO folds the whole stack into one monthly subscription it says can be deployed in one to two hours.

Quick fact: OSTO's launch claim is that its security stack deploys in one to two hours and that SOC 2 / ISO 27001 readiness takes seven working days. Readiness is the key word: it is not the same as holding a finished audit report.

OSTO's five pillars

OSTO describes its offering around five pillars:

1. Security across the stack

Cloud, application, code, endpoint, network, identity, and email security — the controls auditors and enterprise buyers ask about — arriving pre-configured rather than assembled tool by tool. For context on where identity controls are heading, see our guide to passkey-based Windows logon.

2. Compliance automation

The pillar behind the 7-day claim: policy templates, control mappings, and automated evidence collection across SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, PCI DSS, GDPR, CCPA, India's DPDP, and others.

3. Cyber insurance

Bundled into the subscription. Many enterprise contracts require cyber insurance, and buying it as a 10-person company is slow and expensive — but coverage limits and premiums are set by the underlying insurer, so read the policy.

4. VAPT (vulnerability assessment and penetration testing)

Automated testing runs continuously and catches misconfigurations; manual testing finds the subtle flaws scanners miss. Confirm whether manual tests are included in the subscription or billed separately.

5. vCISO services

A part-time senior security leader to answer the security questionnaire, run risk reviews, and talk to your enterprise customer's CISO. OSTO says typical customers are 2-to-10-person teams, and the best time to start is the moment your MVP is live.

How the 7-day SOC 2 claim actually works

OSTO's boldest claim is that compliance readiness for SOC 2 and ISO — normally six to eight weeks — can be achieved in seven working days. Here's the plausible mechanism, and the hard limit.

What compresses the timeline

  • Controls deployed first. OSTO deploys its security controls and maps them to SOC 2 and ISO 27001 requirements, so many controls are satisfied by the platform itself.
  • Policy templates. Security policies, acceptable-use documents, and incident-response plans arrive as templates instead of being drafted from scratch.
  • Automated evidence collection. Integrations with your cloud, identity, and code tools pull audit evidence continuously.
  • Guided completion. OSTO's team helps you finish the remaining human controls: training, access reviews, vendor questionnaires.

The traditional timeline is mostly waiting: on consultants, on drafting, on evidence gathered across tools that were never connected. For a small team with simple infrastructure, that waiting really can collapse into days.

What seven days cannot mean

Readiness is not certification. No platform can issue a SOC 2 report — only an independent CPA firm can. And for SOC 2 Type II, the report covers how controls operated over an observation period of typically 3 to 12 months, which software cannot compress. Seven working days gets you audit-ready — controls deployed, policies signed, evidence flowing — and you could move into a Type I audit quickly. But a Type II report still takes months.

Quick fact: SOC 2 Type I attests that controls are designed properly at a point in time; Type II attests they operated effectively over a period (usually 3–12 months). Enterprise buyers almost always want Type II. Factor the observation period into your timeline before promising a customer a report date.

Pricing: what we know (and don't)

OSTO has not published pricing. The company sells a monthly subscription "at startup speed and cost," but there is no pricing page, no plan tiers, and no per-user or per-framework figures we could verify as of October 15, 2026. Any numbers quoted elsewhere for OSTO should be treated as rumors until the company publishes them.

To calibrate expectations, here are 2026 industry price bands for the established platforms (all sell on custom quotes):

  • Vanta: entry quotes around $10,000–$18,000 per year; fuller bundles $30,000–$60,000+.
  • Drata: roughly $7,500–$25,000 per year for starter tiers; large rollouts exceed $100,000.
  • Secureframe: roughly $7,500–$45,000 per year.
  • Delve (budget disruptor): roughly $6,000–$15,000 per year bundled.

OSTO's pitch is that bundling five pillars into one subscription beats paying for each separately. That could be true, but check what is included versus billed separately: manual pen tests, insurance premiums, and the external auditor's fee are all candidates for separate line items. And the auditor's fee is always separate: first-year SOC 2 audits typically run $10,000–$50,000, and no platform fee replaces them.

OSTO vs Vanta vs Drata vs Secureframe

The comparison isn't quite apples to apples — and that's OSTO's whole point. Vanta, Drata, and Secureframe are compliance automation platforms: they track controls, collect evidence, and get you through an audit. OSTO says it also delivers the underlying security itself, plus insurance, penetration testing, and a virtual CISO: compliance built on top of real security.

One thing OSTO can't claim yet: trust earned over time. Vanta and Drata have auditor ecosystems, published integrations, and years of customers surviving audits. Choosing OSTO trades a proven evidence pipeline for a broader bundle. If your biggest pain is the questionnaire on a live deal, the bundle may win. Who is OSTO for?

  • Startups negotiating their first enterprise contract. When a security questionnaire is the only thing between you and revenue, a bundled platform that deploys in hours is exactly the pitch you want.
  • Teams of 2 to 10 with no security hire. OSTO says it serves founders, CTOs, and DevOps leads directly, giving a first security hire "10x leverage" instead of requiring one.
  • Companies in regulated markets. The framework coverage (SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, PCI DSS, GDPR, CCPA, DPDP) maps to the questions buyers in health, finance, and enterprise SaaS actually ask.
  • Founders who dislike vendor sprawl. One subscription replacing up to a dozen vendors is a clean story — if the quote confirms it.

OSTO is probably not for mid-market companies with an existing security team and a working GRC program, or for solo founders with no enterprise customers yet — if nobody is asking for a SOC 2 report, spend the money on the product first.

Limitations and honest caveats

Because OSTO launched the same day this was written, skepticism is the correct default.

The 7-day claim is unverified

No independent customer has publicly documented a 7-day SOC 2 readiness run yet. Plausible for preparation, but the distance between "audit-ready" and "auditor-signed report" is real, and the Type II observation period is the biggest piece of it.

No published pricing or independent reviews

No pricing page, no G2 or Capterra presence we could find, no public named case studies. Early adopters are paying for a promise.

Vendor lock-in risk

Bundling is convenient until it isn't. If OSTO's agent, evidence exports, or insurance terms don't satisfy a demanding auditor, migrating one pillar means rethinking all five. Ask how evidence exports work if you leave, and whether pen test reports are portable.

Auditor independence still applies

Whoever helps you get ready cannot also audit you. Confirm which independent audit firms OSTO's evidence works with, and engage your own auditor early — their timeline, not the platform's, sets your certification date.

Automation is not judgment

Automated controls catch misconfigurations, not business-logic flaws or social engineering. The bundled vCISO and manual VAPT help, but they are human services with finite bandwidth — ask how many vCISO hours your tier includes.

None of this makes OSTO a bad bet — it makes the launch claims marketing until customers prove them, which is true of every new platform in this space. Worth a demo if

  • An enterprise deal or funding round is blocked on security readiness right now.
  • You have no security hire and no appetite to manage five separate vendors.
  • You need SOC 2 Type I readiness or a credible readiness story fast, with a Type II plan behind it.
  • The written quote confirms what is bundled versus extra.

Wait and watch if

  • You already have a working compliance program on Vanta, Drata, or Secureframe.
  • A specific enterprise customer demands a SOC 2 Type II report by a fixed date this quarter.
  • You need public customer references and published pricing before committing budget.
  • Your environment is complex enough that best-of-breed tools fit better than a bundle.

Frequently asked questions

Can OSTO really get a startup SOC 2 ready in 7 days?

OSTO claims seven working days to SOC 2 and ISO 27001 readiness — meaning audit-ready preparation: deployed controls, completed policies, automatically collected evidence. It is not a finished certificate. A formal SOC 2 Type II audit requires an observation period of typically 3 to 12 months that no platform can compress, and the external auditor is always a separate firm. Treat the 7 days as preparation speed, and confirm the auditor timeline with your audit firm.

How much does OSTO cost?

OSTO has not published pricing as of October 2026. It sells a monthly subscription covering security, compliance, insurance, VAPT, and virtual CISO services, positioned at startup speed and cost, but there is no public pricing page or plan tiers. Ask for a written quote, and check what is included versus billed separately — VAPT work, insurance premiums, and external audit fees.

How is OSTO different from Vanta or Drata?

Vanta and Drata are primarily compliance automation platforms: they track controls and collect audit evidence. OSTO positions itself as a full-stack security platform that also delivers the underlying security itself, plus cyber insurance, penetration testing (VAPT), and virtual CISO services on one subscription. The established tools help you prove compliance; OSTO says it also builds the security underneath it.

Does OSTO replace a security engineer?

Not entirely, but that is the bet. OSTO says founders, CTOs, and DevOps leads can manage security from one platform without hiring a dedicated security engineer first. For early-stage teams that can be realistic. For regulated data or larger systems, most auditors and investors still expect a named person owning security decisions — treat the platform as leverage, not a replacement for human accountability.

Does OSTO replace the SOC 2 auditor?

No. SOC 2 audits must be performed by an independent CPA firm; no platform can certify you. OSTO prepares you by deploying controls, generating policies, and collecting evidence automatically, but the auditor's fieldwork and report are separate services with their own fees, typically $10,000 to $50,000 for a first audit. Ask which audit firms its customers use and whether evidence exports work smoothly with them.

Conclusion

OSTO is one of the more ambitious launches of 2026: not another compliance checklist, but a bundled security stack promising to compress months of readiness work into seven working days. The pitch is coherent and the bundle addresses the real cost of vendor sprawl — but everything important is still unverified: pricing, customer outcomes, and the 7-day claim itself. If you're blocked on an enterprise deal today, book the demo and get every promise in writing. If you're choosing your long-term compliance home, let early customers go first. Whatever platform you pick, no software replaces the independent auditor, the Type II observation period, or a team that actually cares about security.
