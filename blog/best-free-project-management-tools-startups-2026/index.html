Best Free Project Management Tools for Startups in 2026: 8 Compared

  • Project Management
  • Published Oct 14, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most startups, ClickUp has the strongest free plan in 2026: unlimited users, unlimited tasks and core views at $0 with no expiry. Trello is the simplest free start (unlimited cards, but only 10 boards), Notion is best when docs and tasks live together, Linear and Jira are the engineering-led picks, Asana and Todoist have the tightest free tiers, and Basecamp is a single-project freebie that becomes unbeatable value at scale on its flat $299/month plan. Always check the free tier's ceiling — seat caps, board caps, storage, automation limits — before your team builds on it.

A free project management plan is not a product demo — for a startup, it is the operating system the whole company builds on, and most founders discover the limits only after the team is already running on it. This guide compares the eight free plans startups actually shortlist in 2026: ClickUp, Notion, Trello, Linear, Jira, Asana, Todoist and Basecamp. We focus on what matters most: where the free tier breaks, and what it costs when it does. Prices checked October 2026; confirm before you buy.

How we compared the free plans

Every tool was judged on five questions: how many people fit before a seat cap forces a paid decision, how much real work fits inside, which features are metered rather than truly unlimited, where the first wall a growing team hits, and what the upgrade that removes it costs.

We also weighted what generic listicles skip: whether a free tier meters views, automations and storage separately from seats. A free plan can be generous on people and brutal on usage at the same time — ClickUp is the textbook case, with unlimited free members but storage and automation ceilings. Our ClickUp pricing breakdown covers that math.

ClickUp — the most generous free plan, with metered ceilings

ClickUp's Free Forever plan is the only one here with no seat cap and no task cap: unlimited users, unlimited tasks, forever. You also get list, board, calendar and timeline views, docs, 3 whiteboards, sprint management and 24/7 support. For a bootstrapped team expecting to add people fast, it is the closest thing to a permanent free workspace.

The free limits

The free plan meters usage rather than people: 60 MB of shared storage, 5 Spaces, 100 automation actions a month. Gantt, Timeline, Workload, Dashboards and Custom Fields each get 60 one-time uses for the whole workspace — never reset. Use them up and those features need a paid plan; existing tasks stay available.

Where the free tier breaks

Storage fills first for teams uploading videos, design files or client docs. A busy team also burns through the automation budget or the 60 Gantt uses sooner than expected. When either wall is hit, Unlimited at $7/user/month (annual) clears them all and removes the one-time counters. See our full ClickUp pricing breakdown. Best for: seed-stage teams of 3-20 wanting one free workspace for tasks, docs and sprints.

Notion — free docs powerhouse, team plan it is not

Notion's free plan is genuinely generous for individuals: unlimited pages and blocks, real-time collaboration, integrations, and a limited AI trial of roughly 20-40 responses. It shines when project management is really documentation: specs, roadmaps and meeting notes in one database.

The free limits

The moment you add a second workspace member, collaborative blocks are capped at 1,000. Guests are capped at 10, file uploads at 5 MB per file, version history at 7 days; automations are view-only. For a startup building a real operating hub, the block cap and the file-size cap bite first.

Where the free tier breaks

A two-person team can burn through 1,000 collaborative blocks in weeks, and the 5 MB cap blocks uploading pitch decks or mockups directly. Plus at $10/user/month (annual) removes the block limit, lifts uploads to unlimited, extends history to 30 days and raises guests to 100. For the structured options, our Notion vs Asana comparison covers project-tracking trade-offs. Best for: tiny teams whose work is inseparable from writing.

Trello — the simplest free start, ten boards deep

Trello's free plan is the fastest to adopt: unlimited cards, unlimited collaborators per workspace, unlimited Power-Ups, and a near-zero learning curve. Anyone who has seen a Kanban board can use it in five minutes — which is why it remains the default for very small teams.

The free limits

Up to 10 boards per workspace, 250 Butler automation runs a month, 10 MB attachments, and board view only — no timeline, calendar, dashboard, table or map views, and no custom fields beyond basics. Ten boards sounds like plenty until every project, department and client wants one.

Where the free tier breaks

Two ceilings arrive together: the 10-board cap, and the 250 automation runs a month that a single active Butler rule on a busy board can exhaust in weeks. Standard at $5/user/month (annual) lifts the board cap, adds timeline view and custom fields, and raises automation to 1,000 runs; Premium at $10 adds unlimited automation and all views. Best for: solo founders and teams of 2-5 who value adoption speed over depth.

Linear — the engineering-led free tier with a hard issue cap

Linear's free plan is unlimited on members, and it is the fastest, most keyboard-driven issue tracker in this list. Cycles, labels, saved views, GitHub integration and the API are all included, making it genuinely usable for a small product team running sprints. Our Linear vs Jira deep dive compares the two engineering tools head to head.

The free limits

The cap is on work, not people: 250 issues total, 2 teams maximum, 10 MB uploads. Hit 250 issues and new issues are blocked until you archive or upgrade.

Where the free tier breaks

A two-person startup shipping weekly will hit 250 issues within a few months. Archiving stretches the runway, but this is a countdown timer, not a permanent home. Basic at around $8-10/user/month (annual) removes the issue cap, raises teams to 5, and adds docs and the fuller AI set. Best for: early-stage software teams who live in GitHub and run cycles.

Jira — free for 10 users, built for real agile

Jira's free plan covers up to 10 users with unlimited projects and boards, 2 GB of storage and 100 automation runs a month. Its strength is measured agile: backlogs, sprints, story points, burndown charts and velocity reports ship without add-ons.

The free limits

The 10-user cap is the defining limit: hire an 11th person and the free ride ends. The 2 GB of storage and 100 automation runs a month are secondary ceilings. Advanced roadmaps and Atlassian's Rovo AI are reserved for paid tiers.

Where the free tier breaks

Headcount again. Jira's per-user rate slides down at seat thresholds, so get a real quote at your headcount instead of multiplying the list price. Standard at $7.91/user/month (annual) is the typical landing zone. Non-engineering teams will feel the weight: Jira's configuration is a job in itself, and a marketing team of five is usually better served by Trello or Asana. Best for: engineering-led startups of 3-10 running scrum with estimation.

Asana — polished, but the free plan is a personal plan

Asana's free Personal plan is capped at 2 users. That is the whole story: unlimited tasks, unlimited projects, list, board and calendar views, and 100 MB per file — for you and one other person. It has the clearest interface here, with one assignee per task so ownership is never ambiguous. Our Asana vs Monday vs ClickUp comparison covers the paid tiers.

The free limits

Two users; no timeline or Gantt views, no automations, no custom fields, no reporting dashboards. Need a third seat and there is no free option — you jump straight to Starter at $10.99/user/month (annual) with timelines, dashboards, unlimited automations and free guests.

Where the free tier breaks

At hire number three, instantly. Asana also sits above ClickUp on per-seat pricing for a narrower feature set; Advanced at $24.99/user/month adds portfolios, goals and workload only when you genuinely need them. The free plan proves the interface, but budget the paid tier from day one. Best for: two-person partnerships where clean ownership and deadlines matter most.

Todoist — free for personal throughput, not for teams

Todoist's free Beginner plan gives 5 active projects, 5 collaborators per project, natural-language task input, priorities, subtasks and 3 filter views, with no expiry. Type "send invoice every Friday at 9am" and the recurrence is parsed automatically — input speed is unmatched. For a founder juggling personal work alongside the startup, it is the fastest free inbox in this list.

The free limits

The 5-project ceiling is the main constraint — several clients plus admin areas and deliverables run it out fast. No custom reminders, calendar view, board view or task durations — the three absences that drive most upgrades.

Where the free tier breaks

The fifth active project, or the first time you need a reminder on a deadline. Pro at roughly $4-5/month (annual) unlocks 300 projects, reminders, calendar view and AI Assist; the Business plan at $8/user/month adds team workspaces. Best for: founders needing fast personal capture alongside a team PM tool.

Basecamp — one free project, unbeatable flat-rate upgrade

Basecamp's free plan is unusual: up to 20 users, 1 GB of storage and the full core toolset — message boards, to-dos, card tables, Campfire chat, schedules, docs, check-ins — but exactly one project. No per-seat games: everyone gets everything, once.

The free limits

The single-project cap is the whole ceiling. The moment you need a second workspace, a client area or a department project, the free plan is done. No Gantt, no custom fields, no dependencies and minimal reporting are permanent philosophy — Basecamp does not intend to add them.

Where the free tier breaks

At project number two. Plus at $15/user/month adds unlimited projects — and here is the part competitors cannot match: Pro Unlimited at $299/month flat gives unlimited users, unlimited projects and 5 TB storage. The crossover against per-seat pricing lands around 20-25 people; past that, a 50-person company pays the same $299 as a 20-person one. Best for: client-service startups expecting to grow past 20 people.

Our verdict by startup stage

Solo founder or 2 people

  • Trello if the plan is visual and simple — ten free boards is a lot for one person.
  • Notion if your work is really writing: specs, wikis and content pipelines with tasks attached.
  • Todoist for personal capture speed alongside a team tool.

Early team of 3-8

  • ClickUp is the default: unlimited free users and tasks, $7/seat when you outgrow the metered limits.
  • Linear or Jira if engineering-led and running real sprints.
  • Asana Starter if you value the clearest interface and can budget $10.99/seat from hire three on.

Growing past 10-20

  • Compare the per-seat total first — ClickUp stays cheap, Asana climbs fastest.
  • Basecamp's flat $299/month becomes the cheapest option in this list once you cross roughly 20-25 people.
  • Decide your paid landing tier before the team builds on a free ceiling you already see coming.

The pattern across all eight tools: free plans cap people, work, or usage — rarely all three generously. Pick the plan whose ceiling matches the constraint you will hit last, not the one with the biggest headline number.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free project management tool for a startup?

ClickUp is the best free project management tool for most startups. Its Free Forever plan allows unlimited users and unlimited tasks, plus list, board, calendar and timeline views, with no time limit. The limits that matter are 60 MB of storage, 5 Spaces, 100 automation actions per month, and one-time-use counters on advanced features like Gantt charts.

Is the ClickUp free plan really enough for a team?

Yes, for most early teams. Because there is no seat cap, a team of 10 or 20 can run indefinitely on the free plan as long as it stays inside the storage and automation limits. Upgrade to Unlimited at $7 per user per month when you need unlimited Gantt charts, dashboards, custom fields, or more storage.

Can I run a whole startup on Trello's free plan?

You can if your work fits on ten or fewer boards with a small team. Trello's free plan gives unlimited cards and collaborators but caps boards at 10 per workspace and automation at 250 Butler runs a month. Once a busy board burns through those runs, or you need timelines and custom fields, Standard at $5 per user per month is the natural step.

When should a startup upgrade from a free project management plan?

Upgrade when a platform limit blocks the work, not the other way around. The four common triggers: a new hire pushes you past the free seat cap, monthly automation runs are exhausted, file storage or upload limits stop work, or you need permissions, portfolios, or reporting for a second department. Pick your target paid tier before you commit so critical workflows are not built on a ceiling you already know you will hit.

Which pricing model is cheaper for a growing team: per-seat or flat-rate?

Per-seat pricing wins under about 15-20 people; flat-rate wins above that. A 10-person team pays $70 a month on ClickUp Unlimited or about $110 on Asana Starter. Basecamp's flat $299 a month plan becomes cheaper than per-seat plans somewhere around 20-25 people, and the gap only widens — a 50-person team costs the same $299.

Conclusion

  • ClickUp has the most generous free plan for startups — unlimited users and tasks — with storage and automation as the real ceilings.
  • Trello is the fastest free start for visual teams inside ten boards.
  • Notion wins when docs and tasks are the same job; treat the free plan as solo-first.
  • Linear and Jira are the engineering-led picks — Linear is faster to adopt, Jira goes deeper on agile for up to 10 free users.
  • Asana (2 users) and Todoist (5 projects) have the tightest free tiers — budget the upgrade from day one.
  • Basecamp gives one free project and the best flat-rate upgrade: $299/month unlimited.

Do not just pick the tool — pick the ceiling you can live under the longest. The cheapest free plan is the one your team never migrates off mid-sprint.
