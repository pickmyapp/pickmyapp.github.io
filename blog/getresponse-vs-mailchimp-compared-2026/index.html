GetResponse vs Mailchimp compared (2026)

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 25, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

There is no single cheaper tool — the winner flips with your list size and your automation needs. Mailchimp is the friendlier start: a gentler editor, 300+ integrations, and the cheapest entry plan at $13 a month. GetResponse is the stronger all-in-one: unlimited sends on paid plans, deeper automation, and webinars and courses bundled in. Pick Mailchimp for simple newsletters and light automation; pick GetResponse if you sell courses, run webinars, or send email daily at volume.

GetResponse and Mailchimp are the two email platforms most small businesses shortlist first, and for good reason: both have been around for over two decades, both cover newsletters, automation, and landing pages, and both start free. But they bill differently, gate their best features differently, and suit different kinds of business. This guide compares them on pricing at real list sizes, automation depth, ease of use, webinars, integrations, and the billing quirks that catch owners out. Prices below were checked in October 2026 — confirm them on the vendor pricing page before you buy, since both companies adjust tiers and run promotions.

What is GetResponse?

GetResponse is an all-in-one email marketing platform that bundles newsletters, automation, landing pages, sales funnels, webinars, and online courses under one login. Founded in Poland in 1998, it began as an autoresponder and has grown into a conversion-focused toolkit: the pitch is not just sending email, but turning subscribers into customers without bolting on extra tools.

Its current lineup, renamed in January 2025, runs Free, Starter, Marketer, Creator, and MAX (custom enterprise). Paid plans all include unlimited monthly sends, which is unusual in this category and matters a lot if you email often. The trade-off is that the cheaper tiers gate features hard: Starter allows only one custom automation workflow, and webinars and courses only arrive on Creator.

What is Mailchimp?

Mailchimp is the best-known email marketing platform for small businesses, owned by Intuit since 2021, built around a polished drag-and-drop builder, a huge integration library, and a gentle learning curve. It covers email, landing pages, signup forms, social posting, and SMS as an add-on, plus a first-party Shopify integration that powers abandoned-cart reminders and product recommendations.

Mailchimp's plans run Free, Essentials, Standard, and Premium, and every one of them prices by contact count with a monthly send cap tied to that count. It is the friendlier tool for a beginner sending a weekly newsletter, and the weaker one for a business that emails daily or needs deep automation on a budget. If you are weighing Mailchimp against a creator-focused rival instead, our Mailchimp vs ConvertKit comparison for creators covers that angle, and our Brevo vs Mailchimp comparison shows what send-based pricing does to the same bill.

How do GetResponse and Mailchimp pricing compare?

The headline prices mislead. Mailchimp's cheapest paid plan ($13) looks cheaper than GetResponse's ($19), but the two plans do not include the same things, and GetResponse's ladder starts at 1,000 contacts while Mailchimp's starts at 500. What does the same list actually cost?

At 500 contacts, Mailchimp wins on price: Essentials at $13 a month beats GetResponse's $19 Starter (which is priced for 1,000 contacts anyway). If your list is tiny and your emails are simple, Mailchimp is the cheaper start, and our guide to the cheapest email marketing software for small business puts that entry price in context against the whole market.

At 2,500 contacts the picture splits. GetResponse Starter ($29) undercuts Mailchimp Essentials ($45) by $16 a month — but Starter gives you a single automation workflow, so a business running both a welcome series and an abandoned-cart flow needs Marketer at $69, which costs more than Mailchimp Standard at $60. Compare the plan that includes the automation you need, not the cheapest tier on the page. At 10,000 contacts the gap narrows again: GetResponse Marketer ($114) vs Mailchimp Standard ($135) favors GetResponse, while the entry tiers ($79 vs $110) favor it too — until you need Mailchimp's predictive segmentation or its 300+ integrations.

Quick fact: A list that emails daily can hit Mailchimp's send cap while GetResponse keeps sending. At 2,500 contacts, Mailchimp Essentials allows 25,000 sends a month; a daily email to the full list needs about 75,000. GetResponse's unlimited sends remove that ceiling entirely.

Which is easier to use?

Mailchimp wins this round clearly. Its drag-and-drop builder is the most polished in the category, its template library runs to 300+ designs, and a brand kit pulls your logo, colors, and fonts straight from your website into every campaign. A beginner can send a good-looking newsletter within an hour of signing up, and the Customer Journey Builder explains itself as you build.

GetResponse is capable but busier. Its dashboard packs more tools into the same screen — funnels, webinars, courses, web push — and new users often take a week to feel at home. Its email builder is solid and its AI campaign generator can draft a first version fast, but the learning curve is steeper, and some settings hide in menus you would not expect. If nobody on your team enjoys learning software, that difference alone may settle it.

How does automation compare?

GetResponse's workflow builder is the deeper tool. Beyond welcome series and cart recovery, Marketer adds contact scoring, tagging, web-event tracking, and sales funnels that react to what subscribers actually do on your site. For an ecommerce store or a creator running a launch sequence, that depth is real. But remember the gate: Starter's single workflow means the cheapest plan cannot run a welcome series and a cart flow at the same time. Many buyers discover this only after paying.

Mailchimp's automation is friendlier and shallower. Essentials caps flows at 4 steps — enough for a welcome email plus a follow-up, not for a branching nurture sequence. Standard lifts the cap to 200 flows and adds predictive segmentation and send-time optimization, which uses Mailchimp's vast sending data to pick delivery times. For simple flows, Mailchimp is faster to build; for complex ones, GetResponse Marketer pulls ahead. If automation depth is your deciding factor, also compare MailerLite vs Mailchimp for small business, where the automation trade-offs run the other way.

What about webinars and courses?

This is GetResponse's clearest advantage. The Creator plan bundles native webinars for up to 100 attendees and an AI-assisted course builder with paid newsletters, replacing tools that typically cost $50 or more a month on their own. A coach or course creator can run the whole funnel — landing page, email sequence, webinar, checkout — inside one account.

Mailchimp offers no native webinars or courses at all. You would pair it with Zoom, Teachable, or a similar tool and wire the two together, which works fine but adds a second bill and a second login. If webinars or courses are core to your business, GetResponse Creator is the better buy even before you compare email features. If they are irrelevant, ignore this section entirely — it should not decide your pick.

How do integrations and deliverability compare?

Integrations

Mailchimp has the largest integration library in email marketing at 300+ native connections, covering Shopify, WooCommerce, Salesforce, Canva, and most form builders. If a tool you rely on offers exactly one email integration, it is usually Mailchimp. GetResponse covers roughly 150–185 integrations plus a public API — enough for mainstream stacks (Shopify, WordPress, Stripe, PayPal) but thinner for niche tools, where you may need Zapier or a webhook workaround.

Deliverability and support

Third-party tests put both platforms in a similar deliverability range, with Mailchimp a touch ahead in some comparisons — close enough that your list hygiene and sending habits matter more than the platform. On support, GetResponse offers 24/7 email and chat on every plan, while Mailchimp offers 24/7 email and chat from Essentials upward and reserves phone support for Premium. Both publish large help centers; neither replaces good list hygiene.

Extras worth knowing

Mailchimp's transactional email (order confirmations, password resets) is a paid add-on through Mandrill, starting around $20 a block, and is not included in any base plan. GetResponse reserves transactional email and SMS for its custom MAX plan. Mailchimp also sells an SMS add-on that needs approval. If transactional mail matters to you, price it separately rather than assuming it is included.

Billing traps to avoid on both platforms

Each platform has one billing quirk that surprises owners, and they run in opposite directions. Mailchimp bills you for unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts. A store that has collected thousands of unsubscribes over the years pays for people who will never open another email. The fix is archiving, not deleting: archiving removes contacts from your billable count while keeping the suppression record, so a sloppy re-import cannot resubscribe someone who opted out.

GetResponse, by contrast, bills only active subscribers — but the same address on multiple lists can count multiple times. Splitting one audience into five lists can push you into a higher pricing tier for no reason. Use tags and segments inside a single list instead of duplicating contacts across lists. Finally, both platforms raise your price automatically as your list crosses a tier boundary, so budget for the next bracket before a big lead-generation campaign, not after it.

Which should you choose: GetResponse or Mailchimp?

Choose GetResponse if

  • You send email daily or at high volume and want unlimited sends.
  • You run webinars, courses, or paid newsletters and want them in one tool.
  • You need deep automation, scoring, and sales funnels (on Marketer or Creator).
  • Your list is 2,500+ and you want the lower mid-tier price.

Choose Mailchimp if

  • You want the friendliest builder and the fastest path to a polished campaign.
  • You need the widest integration library, especially for ecommerce.
  • Your list is small and your automation is simple (welcome + cart flows).
  • Your team has no appetite for learning a busier dashboard.

Either way, start on the free plan and build one real campaign before paying. Both free plans are thin — Mailchimp's covers 250 contacts and 500 sends, GetResponse's 500 contacts and 2,500 sends — but both are enough to judge the builder. Then pay for the plan that includes your automation, not the cheapest tier on the pricing page.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, GetResponse or Mailchimp?

It depends on your list size and the features you need. At 500 contacts, Mailchimp Essentials costs $13 a month while GetResponse's paid plans start at $19 a month for 1,000 contacts. At 2,500 contacts, GetResponse Starter ($29) undercuts Mailchimp Essentials ($45), but GetResponse Marketer ($69) costs more than Mailchimp Standard ($60). Compare the plan that actually includes the automation you need, not just the cheapest tier.

Does GetResponse have a free plan?

Yes. GetResponse's free plan covers up to 500 contacts and 2,500 emails a month, with one landing page and GetResponse branding on messages. It is roomier than Mailchimp's free plan but has no real automation, so treat it as a trial rather than a permanent home for a growing list.

Which has better automation, GetResponse or Mailchimp?

GetResponse's visual workflow builder is deeper, with scoring, tagging, web-event tracking, and sales funnels — but Starter limits you to one custom workflow, and unlimited workflows require the Marketer plan. Mailchimp's Customer Journey Builder is easier to learn, but Essentials caps flows at 4 steps; you need Standard for up to 200 flows. For complex ecommerce automation, GetResponse Marketer is stronger; for simple welcome and cart flows, Mailchimp Standard is easier.

Does Mailchimp charge for unsubscribed contacts?

Yes. Mailchimp counts unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts toward your billable total, so an old list can quietly inflate your bill. The fix is to archive unsubscribed contacts: archiving removes them from your billable count while preserving the suppression record, so they cannot be accidentally re-added by a future import.

Can I migrate from Mailchimp to GetResponse?

Yes, but there is no one-click importer between the two. You export your contacts (and archive unsubscribes first so you do not pay for them twice), rebuild your templates and automations in the new tool, and re-test every flow before switching your signup forms over. Plan a quiet week for the move, and run both tools in parallel until your key flows are verified.

Conclusion

Mailchimp is the better first email tool: friendlier, better integrated, and cheaper at the smallest sizes. GetResponse is the better all-in-one: unlimited sends, deeper automation, and webinars and courses bundled into a single bill. If you mostly send a weekly newsletter, pick Mailchimp. If you email daily, sell courses, or run webinars, pick GetResponse Marketer or Creator — and check the automation limits before you pay, because the cheapest tier on either side is rarely the plan you actually need.
