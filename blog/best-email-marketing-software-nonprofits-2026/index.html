Best Email Marketing Software for Nonprofits (2026)

Quick answer

For most nonprofits, GetResponse is the best overall pick: its free plan covers 500 contacts and 2,500 sends a month, paid plans include unlimited sends, and it offers a 50 percent nonprofit discount. Choose Mailchimp instead if you want the easiest builder, the largest template library, and 300-plus integrations, and you mainly send a monthly donor newsletter within its send caps.

A nonprofit does not buy email software the way a store does. The same list has to thank donors, invite people to events, recruit and schedule volunteers, and keep supporters close enough that the next appeal does not feel like a cold call. The best email marketing software for nonprofits handles all of those jobs without quietly raising the bill as the list grows. In this guide, that test favors GetResponse overall, with Mailchimp as the simpler alternative for small, low-frequency senders. Prices below were checked in October 2026; confirm them on the vendor pricing pages before you pay.

What nonprofits actually need from email software

Email marketing for nonprofits means donor newsletters, event invitations, volunteer coordination, and fundraising appeals sent to people who already trust you, on a budget that cannot absorb surprise charges. Four jobs cover most of the work. Donor newsletters keep regular givers informed with impact stories, short updates, and a single clear next step. Event invites need reliable reminders: a save-the-date, a registration nudge, and a thank-you after the event. Volunteer coordination is more operational, with shift reminders, role briefings, and last-minute changes that must actually reach inboxes. Appeals, from year-end campaigns to emergency drives, need segmentation so loyal donors, lapsed donors, and brand-new subscribers do not all receive the same message.

That list sets the buying criteria. First, the free plan must be usable, not just present, because many small organizations start with a few hundred contacts and no marketing budget. Second, send limits matter more than headline prices: weekly emails plus event reminders and volunteer updates add up fast. Third, automation must cover at least a welcome series and a donor thank-you sequence without forcing an immediate jump to a mid-tier plan. Finally, deliverability matters because every message that lands in spam is a donation, a volunteer shift, or an event seat that never happens.

  • Donor newsletters: monthly impact updates, segmented by giving history, with one clear call to action.
  • Event invites: save-the-date, registration reminders, and post-event thank-you emails for galas, fundraisers, and community days.
  • Volunteer coordination: shift reminders, role briefings, and schedule changes sent to a separate, easily updated segment.
  • Deliverability on a tight budget: authenticated sending, clean lists, and realistic send volume so appeals reach the inbox.

Mailchimp for nonprofits: the simplest, most familiar choice

Mailchimp is the easiest nonprofit email tool to learn, with the most polished builder, more than 300 templates, and over 300 native integrations, but its free plan is small and its send caps punish frequent senders. The free plan covers 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with a maximum of 250 sends a day, one audience, one seat, and no automation flows. Email support lasts for the first 30 days only, and Mailchimp branding appears on free-plan emails. For a tiny organization sending one short monthly newsletter to under 250 supporters, that can be enough. For anything larger, or anything involving a welcome series, it is a trial, not a long-term home.

Paid plans start with Essentials at $13 per month for 500 contacts, rising to $45 at 2,500, $75 at 5,000, and $110 at 10,000. Essentials allows sends up to 10 times your contact count per month, three seats, three audiences, and flows of up to four steps. Standard, from $20 per month at 500 contacts, raises the cap to 12 times your contacts, allows up to 200 flows, and adds predictive segmentation and send-time optimization. Premium, at $350 per month with 10,000 contacts included, raises sends to 15 times your contacts. Overage fees apply on paid plans when limits are exceeded, while free-plan sending pauses at the limit.

Mailchimp also offers a 15 percent discount for verified nonprofits and charities on paid marketing plans, according to publicly reported program details. Request it before you buy a paid plan, because it is not applied retroactively. Even with that discount, note the billing quirk below: Mailchimp counts unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts toward your billable total. If your team is small, your sending is monthly, and you value templates and integrations over advanced automation, Mailchimp remains a strong, low-friction choice. GetResponse for nonprofits: the best value as you grow

GetResponse is the better nonprofit pick for organizations that send often, run more than one automation, or expect their donor list to grow, because its free plan is roomier and its paid plans include unlimited monthly sends. The free plan covers 500 contacts and 2,500 emails a month, with one landing page and GetResponse branding; premium features are available for the first 14 to 30 days, then restrictions apply. That is double the contacts and five times the sends of Mailchimp's free plan, which makes a real difference for a small charity running a newsletter plus event reminders. Neither free plan supports real automation, so treat both as starting points, not permanent solutions.

Starter costs $19 per month at 1,000 contacts, $29 at 2,500, $54 at 5,000, and $79 at 10,000, with unlimited monthly sends on all paid plans, three seats, autoresponders, landing pages, forms, and basic segmentation. The critical gate is automation: Starter includes exactly one custom automation workflow. A welcome series plus any second flow, such as a donor thank-you sequence, requires Marketer at $59 per month at 1,000 contacts, which adds unlimited workflows, advanced segmentation, and web-event tracking. Annual billing saves about 18 percent. Creator, from $69 per month at 1,000 contacts, adds webinars for up to 100 attendees and an AI course builder.

GetResponse offers a 50 percent nonprofit discount on its email marketing services, according to its own nonprofit guidance. Eligibility is verified through its verification partner, political organizations are excluded, and the discount is reported as valid for 24 months, so confirm the current terms before you commit. At that discount, GetResponse wins the value test for growing donor bases even though its list price starts higher than Mailchimp's. GetResponse charges for active subscribers only, so unsubscribes and bounces are not billed, but the same address on multiple lists can count multiple times. Use tags instead of duplicating contacts across lists.

Quotable fact: GetResponse paid plans include unlimited monthly email sends; Mailchimp caps sends at 10x (Essentials), 12x (Standard), or 15x (Premium) your contact count.

Brevo, MailerLite, and Kit: worth a look, with caveats

Brevo, MailerLite, and Kit are credible alternatives, but none beats Mailchimp and GetResponse on free-plan room, send limits, and confirmed nonprofit pricing in this guide. MailerLite is the strongest of the three on price relief alone: it publishes a 30 percent nonprofit discount, applied after documentation is reviewed during a free trial. Confirm its current free-plan limits and paid entry price on its pricing page before comparing it with the figures above, because this guide does not quote a MailerLite price it did not verify here. For Brevo, no published nonprofit discount found — confirm with the vendor. For Kit, no published nonprofit discount found — confirm with the vendor. Kit is built for creators, so it fits a nonprofit only when its email work is newsletter-first, rather than event-heavy volunteer coordination.

Shortlist three tools at most: GetResponse if you send often, Mailchimp if you want the simplest setup, and MailerLite if its 30 percent discount changes your budget math. The two billing traps every nonprofit should avoid

The two billing traps are Mailchimp charging for unsubscribed contacts and GetResponse double-counting the same address across multiple lists, and both are avoidable with one housekeeping habit each. On Mailchimp, unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts count toward the billable total. Archive unsubscribes instead of deleting them, because archiving removes them from the bill while preserving the suppression record. Make this a quarterly habit after each major appeal, when unsubscribes spike.

On GetResponse, the trap runs the other way. The platform charges for active subscribers only, but the same address on multiple lists can count multiple times. A donor who is also a volunteer and an event attendee should not live on three separate lists. Use one master list with tags for donor, volunteer, and event attendee, and build segments from those tags. Deliverability on a tight budget: what actually moves the needle

Deliverability for nonprofits comes down to authentication, list hygiene, and sending rhythm, not to buying a more expensive plan. Directional third-party testing puts Mailchimp at about 93 percent and GetResponse at about 90 percent in a 2026 Moosend comparison, but treat that gap as directional, not decisive: your list quality and sending habits will move your own inbox rate far more. Authenticate your sending domain, remove bounces promptly, and avoid long silences followed by a sudden appeal blast. A smaller, engaged list will outperform a large, stale list on every platform, and it costs less to maintain.

Pick by situation: small list, growing donor base, or event-heavy org

Small list, monthly newsletter

  • Choose Mailchimp if you want the simplest builder and template library and you send once or twice a month.
  • Stay on a free plan only while you are under its contact and send limits; budget for Essentials once you pass 250 contacts.
  • Archive unsubscribes quarterly so you never pay for contacts you cannot email.

Growing donor base or frequent sender

  • Choose GetResponse for unlimited paid sends, the roomier free plan, and the 50 percent nonprofit discount.
  • Budget for Marketer, not Starter, if you need a welcome series plus a donor thank-you sequence.
  • Use tags, not duplicate lists, so one supporter is never counted twice.

For an event-heavy organization, the deciding factor is automation and send volume, not templates. Invites, reminders, and post-event thank-yous add up to several sends per attendee per event, which is where Mailchimp's send caps bite and GetResponse's unlimited paid sends pay off. Choose GetResponse on Marketer if you run welcome, thank-you, and volunteer sequences together, or Mailchimp on Standard only if you will stay well inside its 12-times-contacts cap. Either way, confirm the nonprofit discount before the first paid charge.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best email marketing software for a small nonprofit?

For a small nonprofit, GetResponse is usually the better starting point because its free plan covers 500 contacts and 2,500 sends a month, and paid plans include unlimited sends. Mailchimp is the better pick if your team values its template library and 300-plus integrations and your list is small enough to stay within its send caps.

Does Mailchimp offer a nonprofit discount?

Mailchimp offers a 15 percent discount for verified nonprofits and charities on paid marketing plans, according to publicly reported program details. Request it before you buy a paid plan, because it is not applied retroactively, and confirm the current terms on Mailchimp's site before paying.

Does GetResponse offer a nonprofit discount?

GetResponse offers a 50 percent nonprofit discount on its email marketing services, according to its own nonprofit guidance. Eligibility is verified through its verification partner, political organizations are excluded, and the discount is reported as valid for 24 months, so confirm the current terms before you commit.

Does Mailchimp charge for unsubscribed contacts?

Yes. Mailchimp counts subscribed, unsubscribed, and non-subscribed contacts toward your billable total. Archive unsubscribes rather than just deleting them, because archiving removes them from the bill while preserving the suppression record.

Which is better for nonprofits, Mailchimp or GetResponse?

GetResponse wins on free-plan room, unlimited paid sends, and the larger nonprofit discount. Mailchimp wins on ease of use, templates, and integrations. Choose GetResponse if you send often or run several automations, and choose Mailchimp if you send a monthly newsletter and want the simplest setup.

Conclusion

For most nonprofits, GetResponse is the best email marketing software in 2026 because it combines the roomier free plan, unlimited sends on paid plans, and the largest confirmed nonprofit discount in this guide. Mailchimp remains the right choice for a small team that sends a monthly newsletter, wants the easiest setup, and will stay inside its send caps. Before you pay, confirm the nonprofit discount in writing, archive or tag your list so you are billed only for real supporters, and budget for the automation tier you actually need.
