Gumloop vs Lindy for solopreneurs: pricing compared at 1 user, 6 months, and your first hire (2026)

  • AI Automation Tools
  • Published Oct 20, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For a solopreneur, Gumloop is cheaper per unit of work and Lindy is cheaper on the sticker. Lindy Plus costs $29.99/month for 3,000 credits (after an August 2026 price cut from $49.99); Gumloop Pro costs $37/month for 20,000 credits. That works out to roughly $0.01 per Lindy credit versus $0.005 per Gumloop credit — about a 2x gap. The bigger difference is structural: Lindy bills per user, while Gumloop Pro includes unlimited seats. Add a virtual assistant or co-founder and Lindy doubles your bill; Gumloop stays flat at $37. Lindy wins on zero-learning-curve delegation (message it in Slack or iMessage); Gumloop wins on value once your automation volume grows. Beware stale pricing pages that still quote Lindy's old $49.99 Plus price.

What Gumloop and Lindy actually are

Gumloop and Lindy both sell AI agents to people without engineering teams, but they come at the problem from opposite ends.

Gumloop is a no-code AI workflow automation platform. In 2026 it shifted to a chat-first agent builder called Gumball: you describe the job in chat and it builds the agent, picks the tools, and writes its own instructions. Underneath is the older visual node canvas, plus a “company brain” that indexes your Slack, Gmail, Notion, Salesforce, or Linear, self-improving reusable skills, and 300+ native connectors plus MCP servers. Think of it as the process map you'd engineer — built by conversation instead of drag-and-drop.

Lindy is an AI employee you talk to, not a canvas you build on. You message it in Slack, over iMessage/SMS, or through a Chrome extension, and it does multi-step work across your inbox, calendar, meetings, and CRM. Its standout additions in 2026 include meeting recording and transcription (“Lindy Teammate,” added August 2026) and computer-use browser control on Pro plans and up. Think of it as the assistant you'd delegate to — with approval gates before irreversible actions.

The review split is striking. Lindy holds 4.9/5 on G2 across ~170 reviews but just 1.7/5 on Trustpilot (~39 reviews), where recurring themes are credits burning fast, agents stalling, and billing or cancellation problems. Gumloop sits at 4.8/5 on G2 but on a tiny sample (~7 reviews) — read both with the sample sizes in mind.

Gumloop pricing in 2026

Gumloop runs three plans — Free, Pro, and Enterprise — billed on credits. Any article still mentioning “Solo $37” or “Team $244” tiers is stale: those were merged into Pro in a December 2025 change.

Pro scales on a slider rather than jumping between tiers: 55k credits at $97, 105k at $194, 205k at $372, up to 435k at $801 per month. Overage is $0.005 per credit (pay-as-you-go up to 1M extra credits), and it's off by default — your agents stop at the limit instead of surprising you.

Credits are consumed across four components: chat and reasoning (model token cost, usually the largest), tool calls (1 credit minimum per successful call), compute (5 credits per session-minute of active processing), and an orchestration fee of 8% on top — 16% if you bring your own API key, though BYOK zeroes out the model-cost portion since you pay your model provider directly. Credits don't roll over on Pro (they do on Enterprise). The number-one user complaint is credit unpredictability: it's hard to know what a credit buys before you build, and heavy AI runs can burn 300+ credits in a single batch.

Three structural facts matter for the math below. First, Lindy bills per user with no annual discount, and all seats pool their credits across the workspace. Second, top-ups cost $10 per 1,000 credits, and if the pool runs dry, credit-using actions pause until you top up or the cycle renews — no surprise bill, but a dead agent mid-month. Third, monthly credits don't roll over (purchased top-up credits are the exception and never expire).

Per-action credit costs are unpublished — you learn what a task costs by running it. Third-party testing puts everyday asks at 2–250 credits, deep work at 250–1,000, and complex builds at 1,000–2,500. Voice tasks and misfired runs burn far faster; one reviewer reported 2,000+ credits gone on a single misfire. Whether a free tier exists is disputed between sources — safest to assume a 7-day trial and check the pricing page for any current free tier.

The head-to-head cost math (three scenarios)

Sticker price says Lindy. Unit economics say Gumloop. Here's the actual math for a solopreneur.

Scenario 1: Month one, just you

Lindy Plus at $29.99 buys 3,000 credits; Gumloop Pro at $37 buys 20,000. Per credit, Lindy costs roughly $0.01 versus Gumloop's $0.005 — about a 2x gap. In plain terms: Lindy is $7 cheaper on the invoice but delivers about one-seventh the usage. If your automation needs are genuinely light (inbox triage and a few routines), Lindy's 3,000 credits may be plenty and the lower sticker wins. The moment you use real volume, Gumloop's 20,000 credits dominate.

Scenario 2: Month six, automation is working

Success is the expensive case. At ~$200/month of budget, Lindy Max gives 35,000 credits; Gumloop's slider gives roughly 105,000 credits at $194. Same story on overages: Lindy top-ups run $10 per 1,000 credits ($0.01/credit) versus Gumloop's $0.005 — again about 2x. A solopreneur scaling outreach, enrichment, or content pipelines will feel Lindy's per-user credit pool tighten months before Gumloop's does. Our guide to using AI agents for lead generation without coding shows the volume these workflows reach — plan for the month-six bill, not the month-one bill.

Scenario 3: You add a VA or co-founder

This is where the pricing models diverge completely. Lindy bills per user: two people on Plus is 2 × $29.99, on Pro is 2 × $99.99. Gumloop Pro includes unlimited seats at the same $37. A solopreneur-plus-VA setup on Lindy Plus costs ~$60/month for 6,000 pooled credits; on Gumloop Pro it's still $37 for 20,000 credits. The per-user trap is the single biggest cost difference between the two platforms, and it's structural — no settings change fixes it.

One fairness note: credits aren't directly comparable units. Lindy credits power conversational agent tasks; Gumloop credits meter model, tool, and compute costs separately. And Gumloop's BYOK option can radically lower AI-heavy workloads if you already pay OpenAI or Anthropic directly — a lever Lindy doesn't offer.

Beyond price: what actually differs for a solopreneur

Price decides the invoice; fit decides whether you keep paying it.

  • Learning curve: Lindy wins for delegation — message it in Slack or iMessage and it works, with 100+ pre-built templates (meeting notes, inbox workflows) praised on Reddit. Gumloop's chat-first builder gets a first agent running in about ten minutes, but complex multi-step builds have a real learning curve on both (21 G2 mentions for Lindy).
  • Reliability ceiling: hands-on reviewers report Lindy's reliability drops beyond 3–4 chained steps, with difficult loop debugging; Gumloop users complain more about credit burn on faulty runs (you're charged for executed nodes even when a workflow fails halfway). Different failure modes, similar lesson: start simple, watch the meter.
  • Integrations: Lindy lists 1,000+ integrations plus MCP; Gumloop has 300+ native connectors plus MCP. For a solopreneur's stack (Gmail, Slack, Notion, Sheets, a CRM), both cover the essentials.
  • Support: neither is generous below Enterprise. Gumloop Pro gets email, a ticket portal, and a community forum (no live chat); Lindy Plus/Pro/Max get the help center and community only, with dedicated support reserved for Enterprise — and Trustpilot reviewers report slow resolution.

Which should a solopreneur pick

Pick Lindy if your automation needs are genuinely light and conversational: inbox triage, meeting prep, calendar management, quick research — tasks you describe in a sentence. The $29.99 entry price is real money saved, the iMessage/Slack interface means zero learning curve, and approval gates keep a cautious solopreneur in control. Just watch the meter: misfired runs and voice tasks can eat the 3,000-credit pool fast, and there's no annual discount to soften a Pro upgrade.

Pick Gumloop if you expect volume or growth: outreach sequences, enrichment pipelines, content repurposing, anything that runs hundreds of times a month. At ~2x cheaper per credit, ~6.6x more credits at the entry tier, a scaling slider instead of tier jumps, and unlimited seats on Pro, it's the platform whose pricing survives your success — including the day you add a VA. Frequently asked questions

How much does Lindy cost in 2026?

Lindy Plus costs $29.99 per user per month for 3,000 credits, following an August 2026 price cut (the old $49.99 price you may see quoted is stale). Pro is $99.99 for 15,000 credits, Max is $199.99 for 35,000 credits, and Enterprise is custom. Billing is per user with no annual discount; top-ups cost $10 per 1,000 credits. A 7-day trial is available; whether a free tier exists is disputed between sources, so check the pricing page.

How much does Gumloop cost in 2026?

Gumloop has three plans: Free ($0, 5,000 credits/month), Pro ($37/month, 20,000 credits, unlimited seats, 20% off annual), and Enterprise (custom). Pro scales on a slider up to 435,000 credits at $801/month, and overage is $0.005 per credit. Old “Solo” and “Team” tier names are stale — they were merged into Pro.

Is Gumloop cheaper than Lindy?

Per unit of usage, yes — roughly 2x cheaper: about $0.005 per Gumloop credit versus about $0.01 per Lindy credit. At the entry tiers, Gumloop Pro's $37 buys ~6.6x more credits than Lindy Plus's $29.99. The gap widens if you add a second person, because Lindy bills per user while Gumloop Pro includes unlimited seats at the same $37.

Do unused credits roll over on Gumloop or Lindy?

On both platforms, monthly plan credits expire at the end of each billing cycle and do not roll over. The exceptions: Gumloop Enterprise credits do roll over, and Lindy purchased top-up credits carry over and never expire.

Which is better for a solopreneur, Gumloop or Lindy?

Choose Lindy if your automation is light and conversational (inbox, calendar, meetings) — the $29.99 entry price and zero-learning-curve messaging interface win. Choose Gumloop if you expect real volume or plan to add a VA or co-founder — its per-credit cost, scaling slider, and unlimited seats make it cheaper as you grow.

Conclusion

The Gumloop-vs-Lindy decision is really a decision about your growth curve. Lindy's $29.99 Plus plan is the cheapest way to hand real work to an AI employee today, and for light delegation it's genuinely good value. But every structural choice in Lindy's pricing — per-user billing, no annual discount, $0.01 top-up credits — taxes growth, while every structural choice in Gumloop's — unlimited Pro seats, a scaling slider, $0.005 overages, BYOK — rewards it. Price the month-six and first-hire scenarios before you commit, ignore any page still quoting Lindy's old $49.99, and start on the plan whose meter you can actually watch: both platforms punish inattention more than they punish usage.
