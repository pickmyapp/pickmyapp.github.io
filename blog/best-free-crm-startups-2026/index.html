Best Free CRM for Startups (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Oct 9, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

The best free CRM for most startups is HubSpot — genuinely free forever, with a full contact database, deal pipeline, and email tracking at $0. Zoho CRM is the better free pick if you need workflow automation on the free tier (it is the only one that includes it), and Attio suits teams that want a modern interface with a generous 50,000-record free ceiling. The decision that matters most is not which free plan is biggest, but which paid plan you will land on — so compare the upgrade cost before you commit your data.

Every startup needs a CRM before the spreadsheet habit hardens — but the wrong free plan costs you a painful migration six months later. The best free CRM for startups is the one whose limits you will hit last and whose paid upgrade you can afford when you do. This guide compares the six free CRMs that are genuinely worth a startup's time in 2026, using limits verified in October 2026. We skip the tools that only offer trials (Pipedrive, monday CRM) and the ones whose "free" pages have quietly disappeared (more on Freshsales below). If you already know you will outgrow free quickly, read our HubSpot vs Pipedrive comparison for the paid decision.

What should a startup look for in a free CRM?

Most "best free CRM" roundups list features and stop there. The limits are what decide whether a free plan survives your first year of growth, so judge every free tier on these five constraints:

  • User cap. The binding limit for most startups. HubSpot, Zoho, and Capsule cap free users at 2-3. If you have four cofounders plus a sales hire, a "free" plan that covers two of you is not free.
  • Contact and record cap. Caps range from 250 contacts (Capsule, EngageBay) to 50,000 records (Attio). A B2B startup with a long prospect list blows through 250 contacts in a quarter; a consultancy with 80 clients never notices.
  • Automation. Almost every free plan reserves workflows, sequences, and auto-assignment for paid tiers. Zoho CRM is the notable exception — its free tier includes real workflow rules.
  • Branding and support. Free email tools usually carry the vendor's branding, and free support is typically community forums and docs only. Fine at the start, awkward when emailing your first enterprise prospect.
  • The upgrade path. This is the one founders forget. Model what the paid plan costs at your expected team size in 12 months. A free plan that upgrades to $100 per seat is a different commitment than one that upgrades to $14.

The 6 best free CRMs for startups in 2026

1. HubSpot CRM — the best free CRM overall

HubSpot's free CRM is the benchmark everything else is measured against: a real system of record with contacts, companies, deals, tasks, meeting scheduling, and email tracking, free forever with no credit card. New accounts get 2 users, up to 1,000 contacts, one deal pipeline, 2,000 HubSpot-branded email sends per month, and 200 email-tracking notifications. The interface is the most polished of the free options, and the upgrade path is the smoothest in the industry — your data never moves, you just add paid seats and hubs.

The ceiling is real, though. There is no automation, no sequences, no custom reporting, and the HubSpot branding on outgoing emails is unavoidable. Paid Sales Hub starts at $20 per seat (Starter) but the automation most growing teams want lives at Professional ($100 per seat). HubSpot fits inbound-led startups that generate leads through content and forms — pair it with our cheapest email marketing software guide if you plan to run campaigns on a budget.

2. Zoho CRM — the only free tier with real automation

Zoho CRM's free edition is the most capable free plan in this list for a team of up to three: 5,000 records, leads, contacts, deals, and — uniquely — workflow rules (up to 10 per module, 5 active). That means auto-assigning new leads, firing task reminders, and updating fields without paying a cent. The upgrade path is also one of the gentlest: Zoho CRM Standard starts around $14 per user per month, and the whole Zoho ecosystem (Mail, Books, Desk) shares one login.

The trade-off is the experience. Zoho's interface feels older and denser than HubSpot's or Attio's, setup takes longer, and the 3-user cap means your fourth hire starts costing money immediately. Technical teams that do not mind the learning curve get the most free functionality per dollar here.

3. Attio — the modern pick with the biggest free record cap

Attio's free tier covers up to 3 seats with 50,000 records, 3 reports, and 200 emails a month — the most generous data ceiling of any free CRM here. It is the newest product on this list, built around a flexible data model and a genuinely pleasant interface that startups tend to adopt quickly. Email sync is strong, and the contact enrichment feels smarter than its age suggests.

Automation and workflows start on the Plus tier, and the integration marketplace is smaller than HubSpot's. Attio is the right free pick for a small, design-conscious team that wants room to grow its database without watching a record counter.

4. Capsule — the simplest free CRM for tiny teams

Capsule's free plan covers 2 users, 250 contacts, one sales pipeline, and 5 custom fields — deliberately small, deliberately simple. There is almost no learning curve: contacts, tasks, and a pipeline, done well. For a two-founder startup that just needs to stop losing track of conversations, Capsule's free tier is the least distracting option on this list.

The 250-contact cap is the obvious wall, and there is no automation on the free tier. Capsule suits relationship-driven micro-teams, not volume prospectors. If simplicity is the priority and you will pay for growth later, it is a clean start.

5. EngageBay — the free all-in-one for sales plus marketing

EngageBay's free tier bundles CRM with marketing email and a basic helpdesk for up to 15 users — the widest free user allowance here. If your startup needs contact management, newsletters, and ticket handling in one tool with no budget, nothing else free covers that much surface area. The paid Basic plan starts around $14-15 per user per month.

The 250-contact cap is the binding constraint: once your list crosses it, you are paying regardless of headcount. The integration marketplace is also smaller than the leaders'. EngageBay fits early-stage teams that want one tool for sales, marketing, and support while the database is still small.

6. Bitrix24 — the free pick for large teams with simple needs

Bitrix24's free tier allows unlimited users and includes CRM basics alongside team chat, video calls, and task management. For a startup with a big team and basic sales needs — or one that wants internal communication bundled with its CRM — the free user math is unbeatable.

The catch is complexity: Bitrix24 packs in so many tools that new users routinely feel lost, and meaningful CRM automation sits behind paid plans. It is a collaboration suite that happens to include a CRM, not a CRM-first product. Choose it for the team-size economics, not for sales depth.

The Freshsales catch: it is not free anymore

Stale-article warning: many "best free CRM" roundups still list Freshsales with a free plan for up to 3 users. That plan is gone. Freshsales' current pricing page shows a 21-day trial followed by paid plans — Growth at $9, Pro at $39, and Enterprise at $59 per user per month billed annually. If a free tier is the reason you shortlisted Freshsales, confirm it on the vendor's page on the day you sign up. This is exactly why we re-verify limits on every guide's update date.

The Freshsales episode is a useful reminder about all free plans: they are marketing decisions, not entitlements. Zoho could tighten its workflow rules; HubSpot could lower its contact cap again (it did in 2024). Pick a free CRM for the product and the upgrade path, not just the current limit — because the limit is the thing most likely to change.

Free now, pay later: model the upgrade cost

Here is the exercise that saves startups from the classic free-CRM trap. Write down your expected team size and contact count in 12 months, then price the paid tier each free plan upgrades into:

  • HubSpot: Starter at $20 per seat covers the basics; the automation most teams eventually want (workflows, sequences, custom reports) lives at Professional, $100 per seat plus a $1,500 onboarding fee. One published pricing analysis put the median annual HubSpot contract near $27,000 — a reminder that the ceiling is high.
  • Zoho CRM: Standard at about $14 per user per month, with the gentlest escalation curve of the group. A 5-person team pays roughly $70 per month for a genuinely capable CRM.
  • Attio: Plus tier pricing is competitive and the interface keeps adoption easy, which matters when every hire must learn the tool.
  • EngageBay: paid plans start around $14-15 per user per month for the all-in-one bundle, the cheapest way to get sales plus marketing plus support in one bill.
  • Capsule and Bitrix24: cheap paid tiers, but check that the paid features actually cover what you will need — migration off a cheap tool you have outgrown costs more than a pricier tool you stay on.

The pattern: HubSpot is the cheapest start and the most expensive finish. That is not a criticism — it is a platform you can scale on for years. It is simply the reason to model the cost at 12 and 24 months before your data, automations, and team habits are all living inside one vendor's walls.

How to choose the right free CRM

Pick by your situation

  • Inbound startup, 1-2 founders: HubSpot. Free forever, best interface, smoothest upgrade path.
  • Need automation free: Zoho CRM. The only free tier with workflow rules; accept the 3-user cap and the denser UI.
  • Big prospect list, small team: Attio. 50,000 free records gives you the most headroom.
  • Sales plus marketing on $0: EngageBay. One free bundle for CRM, email, and support while your list is small.

Avoid these mistakes

  • Choosing by feature list alone. The free tier you pick is decided by user caps and record caps, not by how many features the paid plan has.
  • Trusting old roundups. Free plans disappear (see Freshsales). Re-check the vendor pricing page the week you commit.
  • Ignoring the upgrade bill. Model the paid cost at your 12-month team size before your data is locked in.
  • Skipping the trial week. Import real contacts, build your real pipeline, and run one real workflow before deciding.

One last practical note: every CRM on this list exports to CSV, and every paid CRM imports from CSV, so you are never truly locked in — but the cleanup always costs a weekend. Spending that weekend on the right tool the first time is the cheaper move. Frequently asked questions

Is HubSpot CRM really free forever?

Yes. HubSpot's free CRM has no expiration date and needs no credit card. New accounts get 2 users, 1,000 contacts, one deal pipeline, and 2,000 branded email sends per month. There is no time limit — the limits are on users, contacts, and features, so most startups run it for free for months before upgrading.

Which free CRM has the best automation?

Zoho CRM's free tier. It is the only free plan that includes real workflow rules (up to 10 per module, 5 active), so you can auto-assign leads and send task reminders without paying. HubSpot, Attio, Capsule, and EngageBay reserve automation for paid tiers. The trade-off is Zoho's steeper learning curve and a 3-user cap.

Is Freshsales still free?

No. Freshsales no longer offers a free plan. Its pricing page now shows a 21-day trial, then paid plans starting at $9 per user per month billed annually. Many older roundups still list a free Freshsales tier — that information is outdated. Shortlist it only if you are prepared to pay.

When does a free CRM stop being enough?

Most startups hit the wall in one of three ways: headcount passes the free user cap (2-3 users on HubSpot, Zoho, and Capsule), the contact database grows past the free record limit (250-5,000 depending on the tool), or the team needs email automation, sequences, or custom reporting. The practical test: if you are keeping a separate spreadsheet because the CRM "can't do that," it is time to upgrade.

What is the cheapest paid CRM after the free tier?

For a tiny team, EngageBay's paid plan and Zoho CRM Standard (both around $14-15 per user per month) are the cheapest full-featured upgrades. HubSpot Starter at $20 per seat is the cheapest way to keep your HubSpot data, and Attio Plus is competitive for teams that value its modern interface. Price the plan you will actually need, not just the cheapest seat.

Conclusion

Start with HubSpot if you want the best free CRM experience and an inbound motion — it is genuinely free forever and the easiest to grow into. Pick Zoho CRM if free automation matters more than polish, Attio if your prospect list is large, and EngageBay if you need sales, marketing, and support in one free bundle. Whatever you choose, do the upgrade-cost math for 12 months out before your data, your team habits, and your automations all live inside one vendor's walls. The free plan is a runway, not a destination — and the cheapest runway is the one that lands you on an affordable paid plan, not a painful migration.
