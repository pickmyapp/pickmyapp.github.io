Substack vs beehiiv for free newsletters (2026)

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 15, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For a completely free start, Substack wins: it costs nothing at any list size, with no monthly fee and no subscriber cap. beehiiv's free Launch plan is more generous on features — custom domain included, better analytics, unlimited sends — but it caps free subscribers at 2,500, after which paid plans start around $49/month. The catch that decides it: Substack takes 10% of paid subscription revenue forever; beehiiv takes 0%. So hobbyist writers who may never charge should start on Substack, while anyone planning to monetize should start on beehiiv.

Substack and beehiiv are the two platforms every new newsletter creator shortlists, and both let you start without paying a cent. But their free plans are built on opposite philosophies: Substack gives you unlimited scale for free and makes its money when you do, while beehiiv gives you a professional toolkit for free up to 2,500 subscribers and charges you to keep growing. This guide compares both free starting points head to head — limits, website builder, discovery, fees, and analytics — so you can pick the right one for the newsletter you actually want to run. The free plans, side by side

The single most important difference is the subscriber cap. Substack has none — your free newsletter can grow to 5,000, 50,000, or 500,000 subscribers without Substack ever charging you a monthly fee. beehiiv's free Launch plan stops at 2,500 subscribers. That is a generous ceiling for a new newsletter (most newsletters take a year or more to reach it), but it is a ceiling, and crossing it means a paid plan starting around $49 a month.

The free plan is a genuine professional foundation (good analytics, custom domain, unlimited sends), not a trial. But if you want the full monetization machine from day one, you are paying for it.

Substack's free plan in detail

Substack's pitch to free users is radical simplicity: sign up, write, publish, and your newsletter is live in minutes. There are no tiers to compare, no subscriber limits to watch, and no monthly bill — ever. You can run a free newsletter for 10 years, grow to six figures in subscribers, and Substack will not charge you a dollar unless you turn on paid subscriptions.

What you get for free is deliberately lean. The editor is clean and distraction-free. Your publication gets an archive page, subscriber management, comments, and basic analytics (opens, clicks, subscriber counts). You can launch a podcast with built-in hosting on any plan, which is a genuine edge if audio is part of your plan. What you do not get: email sequences or drip campaigns (just a single welcome email), segmentation, A/B testing, design flexibility beyond a logo and accent color, or a public API. Substack is a closed ecosystem — your newsletter lives inside it, and that is the deal.

The honest trade-off is this: Substack's free plan is the best in the industry for staying free, and the most expensive the moment you start charging. That 10% cut never shrinks, no matter how big you get. A newsletter earning $2,000 a month from paid subscriptions hands Substack $200 every month, forever. beehiiv's free plan in detail

beehiiv's Launch plan is free up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited sends and no credit card required. For a creator starting from zero, 2,500 is a lot of runway — and what you get inside that runway is more professional than Substack's free tier: a custom domain at no extra cost, deeper analytics (including subscriber sources and engagement data), and a proper website builder instead of a basic archive page.

The ceiling is the whole story, though. At 2,501 subscribers you must upgrade to a paid plan (starting around $49 a month) or stop growing. beehiiv also gates its headline growth features — the ad network, referral program, Boosts, A/B testing, automations, and API — behind paid tiers. So the free plan is best thought of as a 2,500-subscriber runway to a paid plan, not a permanent free home. If you are already thinking about paid subscriptions, our Kit vs beehiiv comparison for paid newsletters picks up where this guide leaves off.

Bottom line on limits: if your goal is "never pay for my newsletter," Substack wins outright. If your goal is "build a newsletter business," beehiiv's free plan is a better launchpad — but budget for the paid plan from the start, because you will hit 2,500 subscribers right when growth gets exciting.

Website builder and SEO

This is beehiiv's clearest free-plan win. beehiiv gives free users a real website builder: customizable templates, editable slugs, metadata controls, and a free custom domain. Your newsletter doubles as an SEO-ready website that can rank on Google and bring in subscribers while you sleep.

Substack's web presence is an archive page with minimal control — your posts are public and indexed, but you get little say over SEO elements, and a custom domain costs a $50 one-time fee. For a writer who just wants to write, that is fine. For anyone treating the newsletter as a business with organic search as a growth channel, beehiiv's free website is a material advantage.

Discovery and network effects

Substack's superpower is its built-in audience. The Notes social feed, the recommendations network (publications recommending each other), and the Substack reader app mean a new newsletter can pick up subscribers without spending a dollar on ads. For a free newsletter, this is the single biggest growth lever Substack offers — and it costs nothing at any list size.

beehiiv counters with its own recommendation network and Boosts (a system where newsletters pay each other for subscriber referrals), plus a referral program on paid tiers. These are powerful, but the full versions sit behind paid plans. On the free plan, discovery leans on your own promotion more than Substack's does. If you are starting from zero with no audience and no budget, Substack's reader network is the friendlier place to get your first hundred subscribers.

Monetization: the 10% vs 0% question

Sooner or later, the free comparison becomes a money comparison. Here is the math that matters:

  • Substack: no monthly fee, but a 10% cut of every paid subscription, plus Stripe's card processing fees (about 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction) and a small recurring-billing fee.
  • beehiiv: 0% platform fee on paid subscriptions — you keep everything except Stripe fees — but you pay a monthly plan fee (around $49/month to start).

The break-even point is roughly $490 a month in paid subscription revenue: at that level, Substack's 10% ($49) equals beehiiv's monthly plan cost. Below it, Substack is cheaper; above it, beehiiv pulls ahead fast. A newsletter earning $2,000 a month pays Substack $200 but pays beehiiv roughly $49 — a $150 monthly difference, or $1,800 a year. beehiiv also offers more ways to earn beyond subscriptions: an ad network, Boosts, and referrals (mostly on paid plans), while Substack's options center on paid subscriptions plus native sponsorships.

The honest version: if you might never charge readers, the 10% cut is irrelevant and Substack's free plan is unbeatable. If you plan to charge from month one — or believe you will within a year — beehiiv's 0% fee pays for its subscription many times over.

Analytics and automations

beehiiv wins analytics at every level. Even on the free plan you get deeper data: where subscribers came from, how they engage, and how posts perform. Substack's analytics are basic — opens, clicks, subscriber counts — enough to see that something worked, not enough to know why.

On automations, neither free plan is impressive, which matters if you want welcome sequences or drip campaigns. Substack offers essentially a single welcome email and nothing else. beehiiv's visual automation builder exists but unlocks on paid plans. If automated sequences are central to your plan (courses, onboarding, funnels), neither free tier is the answer — our cheapest email marketing software guide covers tools with stronger automation at low cost.

The verdict: which free start is right for you

Choose Substack if you are a hobbyist writer

  • You want to write and publish with zero setup and zero ongoing cost.
  • You may never charge readers, so the 10% cut does not bother you.
  • You value the built-in reader network and Notes for free discovery.
  • You want unlimited growth with no plan upgrade hanging over you.

Choose beehiiv if you are a growth-minded creator

  • You want a custom domain, real website builder, and SEO controls from day one.
  • You plan to monetize within a year — the 0% fee beats Substack's 10% fast.
  • You want deeper analytics to understand what is actually working.
  • You are comfortable upgrading to ~$49/month once you pass 2,500 subscribers.

If paid subscriptions are the whole ambition

  • Start on beehiiv anyway. The 0% platform fee compounds: at $1,000/month in paid revenue you save $100/month vs Substack, and the gap only grows. Starting on Substack and moving later means migrating paying subscribers mid-stream.

If you truly cannot decide

  • Start on Substack. It costs nothing at any size, the reader network finds your first subscribers, and both platforms export subscriber lists as CSV — so moving to beehiiv later, when revenue justifies it, is a weekend project, not a disaster.

Switching later is easier than you think

The fear that keeps creators stuck is platform lock-in, and for these two platforms it is mostly unfounded. Both let you export your full subscriber list as a CSV, and both publish import tools aimed at the other's users. Custom domains can be transferred, so your links and brand survive the move. The real cost of switching is not technical — it is the week of admin (reconnecting integrations, re-verifying your domain, telling subscribers nothing changed) and, if you move off Substack, giving up the reader-app discovery you were getting for free. Frequently asked questions

Can I start a newsletter completely free on Substack or beehiiv?

Yes, on both. Substack is free at any list size with no monthly fee. beehiiv's free Launch plan supports up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited sends and no credit card required.

What happens when beehiiv's free plan hits 2,500 subscribers?

You need to move to a paid plan, starting around $49 a month, or stop adding subscribers. Substack has no such cap — a free newsletter can grow to any size without paying the platform anything.

How much does Substack take from paid subscriptions?

Substack takes 10% of paid subscription revenue, plus Stripe card processing fees (about 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction) and a small recurring-billing fee. beehiiv takes 0% — you keep everything except Stripe fees.

Can I use my own domain for free on Substack or beehiiv?

beehiiv includes a custom domain on its free plan. Substack charges a $50 one-time fee for a custom domain. Both give you a free subdomain to start with (yoursubstack.substack.com or yoursite.beehiiv.com).

Can I move my newsletter from Substack to beehiiv (or the reverse) later?

Yes. Both platforms let you export your subscriber list as a CSV, and both publish import tools. Your custom domain can be transferred too — starting on one does not lock you in forever.

Conclusion

For a free newsletter in 2026, the choice comes down to what "free" means to you. If it means never paying anything, at any size, Substack is the answer — unlimited subscribers, no monthly fee, built-in discovery, and the simplest setup in the industry. The price is the 10% cut if you ever charge readers. If "free" means the best launchpad for a newsletter business, beehiiv wins — custom domain, real website builder, deeper analytics, and a 0% fee on paid subscriptions, with 2,500 free subscribers of runway before paid plans start around $49/month. Hobbyist writers should start on Substack; anyone with monetization in their plans should start on beehiiv. Either way, you can export your list and switch later — so the bigger mistake is waiting, not choosing.
