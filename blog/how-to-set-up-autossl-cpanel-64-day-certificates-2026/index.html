how to set up AutoSSL in cPanel for 64-day certificates (2026)

  • Web Hosting
  • Published Oct 17, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 12 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

AutoSSL is cPanel's free automatic SSL system: it requests, installs, and renews Domain Validated certificates for every domain on your account with no manual work. To set it up in WHM, go to SSL/TLS → Manage AutoSSL, choose a provider (Sectigo or Let's Encrypt), accept the terms of service, enable it for your users, and click Run AutoSSL For All Users. In cPanel, open SSL/TLS Status, select your domains, and click Run AutoSSL. This matters more than ever: Let's Encrypt cuts default certificate lifetimes from 90 days to 64 days on February 10, 2027, so reliable automatic renewal is no longer optional.

What AutoSSL is (and why the 64-day era changes everything)

AutoSSL is cPanel's built-in system for issuing and renewing free Domain Validated (DV) SSL certificates automatically. Once it is enabled, the server checks your domains every night, requests certificates from your chosen certificate authority, installs them, and renews them before they expire. You never upload a CSR, paste a certificate bundle, or set calendar reminders.

For years, AutoSSL was a convenience feature. In 2026 and 2027 it is becoming infrastructure. Let's Encrypt — the authority behind a huge share of the web's free certificates — announced on October 7, 2026 that default certificate lifetimes will drop from 90 days to 64 days on February 10, 2027, and to 45 days in February 2028. The CA/Browser Forum's Ballot SC-081v3 adds a parallel industry-wide schedule: maximum public TLS validity falls to 200 days (already in force since March 15, 2026), 100 days in March 2027, and 47 days in March 2029. The days of renewing a certificate once a year by hand are over; your renewal pipeline either runs itself or your site goes dark.

By 2029, a single domain will need roughly 8 certificate renewals per year, up from about 2 today. Any renewal step that requires a human click will not survive that cadence.

What changes with 64-day certificates

The shift to short-lived certificates changes operations, not the setup steps. Here is the timeline that affects cPanel servers:

Three practical consequences follow. First, renewal frequency doubles and doubles again, so AutoSSL's automatic nightly check becomes the only sane renewal method — nothing else on a shared cPanel server keeps up. Second, domain validation gets stricter: Let's Encrypt is cutting authorization reuse from 30 days to 10 days in February 2027, which means more renewals require a fresh proof of domain control rather than a cached one. Third, fixed renewal schedules break: Let's Encrypt now recommends renewing at roughly two-thirds of a certificate's lifetime instead of hardcoding day counts, so any custom cron jobs or wrapper scripts with values like 83, 80, or 60 days need updating. The good news is that AutoSSL already behaves this way — it watches expiry and revalidates on its own schedule.

If you want to watch your certificates as lifetimes shrink, pairing AutoSSL with a dedicated expiry monitor is cheap insurance. Our roundup of SSL expiry monitoring tools for the 64-day era covers the best options for independent alerting that catches failures AutoSSL misses.

Before you start: requirements

AutoSSL works at two levels: the server level (WHM, for resellers and VPS/dedicated owners) and the account level (cPanel, for individual site owners). You need WHM root access for the full setup below; if you only have cPanel, skip to the account-level section and ask your host about the server-level provider choice.

Before enabling AutoSSL, confirm these prerequisites:

  • DNS points at the server. Each domain's A record (and the www record) must resolve to the cPanel server's IP. AutoSSL cannot validate domains that point elsewhere.
  • Port 80 is open. Domain control validation happens over HTTP, so the server must answer on port 80 even if you redirect everything to HTTPS.
  • A current cPanel license. The Sectigo-backed provider requires a valid license; Let's Encrypt works on any licensed server with the plugin installed.
  • A hosting plan with cPanel. If you are shopping for one, our comparison of the best cheap web hosting for WordPress in 2026 notes which budget hosts include free AutoSSL and which charge extra for certificates.

Step-by-step: set up AutoSSL in WHM

Log in to WHM as root and open Home → SSL/TLS → Manage AutoSSL. The screen has five tabs: Providers, Options, Manage Users, Pending Queue, and Logs.

Step 1: Choose your provider (Providers tab)

Select the radio button for your certificate authority. The standard choices are cPanel (powered by Sectigo), the default, and Let's Encrypt™. If Let's Encrypt is not listed, install it first from Home → cPanel → Manage Plugins, or run /scripts/install_lets_encrypt_autossl_provider over SSH. Read the provider's terms of service, check the I agree to these terms of service box, and click Save. See the provider comparison below before deciding.

Step 2: Set your renewal options (Options tab)

The key setting here is Allow AutoSSL to replace invalid or expiring non-AutoSSL certificates. Enable it if you want AutoSSL to swap out certificates that were not issued by AutoSSL — for example, to migrate paid certificates to free ones automatically. Leave it off if any domain uses a paid OV or EV certificate, because a DV AutoSSL certificate would overwrite it and you would lose the higher validation level. The Options tab also holds the notification toggles that control whether account owners get emailed about successful installations and failures — keep failure notifications on, because a silent failure is how sites expire.

Step 3: Enable it for your users and run it (Manage Users tab)

The Manage Users tab lists every cPanel account. Select the accounts you want covered and choose one of three actions:

  • Enable AutoSSL — force AutoSSL on for the selected accounts.
  • Disable AutoSSL — turn it off for the selected accounts.
  • Reset to Feature List Setting — return the accounts to whatever the account's feature list specifies (the default on most servers).

To check a single user immediately, use the Check button next to their name. For the first run across the whole server, click Run AutoSSL For All Users at the top of the page. Then watch the Logs tab — click View Log on a run and look for success messages per domain. The first run can take several minutes on a busy server.

Enable AutoSSL in cPanel (site-owner level)

If you do not have WHM access, you can still trigger AutoSSL for your own domains — your host controls the provider and server-level switches, but the per-domain request is yours:

  1. Log in to cPanel and open Security → SSL/TLS Status.
  2. Tick the checkboxes next to the domains and subdomains you want secured. You can select several at once.
  3. Click Run AutoSSL.
  4. The request queues immediately and usually processes within a few minutes. Refresh the page to see per-domain results.

AutoSSL also runs by itself every night, so after the first successful run you should never need to click this again — but it is the fastest way to force a certificate when you have just pointed DNS at the server or fixed a validation problem. Advanced users with SSH access can do the same thing from the command line with /usr/local/cpanel/bin/autossl_check --user=username, or --all for every account on the server.

Troubleshooting common AutoSSL errors

Most AutoSSL failures come down to domain validation (DCV) — the certificate authority could not prove you control the domain.

DCV failures: the domain does not resolve correctly

AutoSSL's HTTP validation fetches a token file from your domain, so the domain must resolve to the cPanel server. If you recently changed DNS, migrated, or use a CDN like Cloudflare, the validation request may land on the wrong machine. Check the A record with dig yourdomain.com +short and confirm it returns the server's IP. Domains proxied through a CDN can fail if the CDN intercepts the validation path — temporarily pausing the proxy or using DNS-based validation usually resolves it.

The .well-known directory is blocked

Validation files live under /.well-known/ (Sectigo uses pki-validation, Let's Encrypt uses acme-challenge). Overly aggressive .htaccess rewrite rules, security plugins, or a forced HTTPS redirect loop can make these files unreachable, producing errors like "Local HTTP DCV error". Test with curl -v http://yourdomain.com/.well-known/pki-validation/test.txt and add a rewrite exception for .well-known if requests are being redirected or blocked.

Rate limits after repeated forced runs

Clicking Run AutoSSL over and over is the fastest way to hit Let's Encrypt's limit of 50 certificates per domain per week. The Logs tab will show rate-limit errors plainly. The fix is patience: wait for the limit window to clear and let the nightly run handle it. The same advice applies in the 64-day era — because authorization reuse is shrinking to 10 days, wasted validation attempts cost more than they used to.

"The cPanel (powered by Sectigo) provider cannot currently accept incoming requests"

This is a known recurring provider-side problem, not a misconfiguration on your server. The community-tested workarounds are: run /usr/local/cpanel/bin/autossl_check --user=username once at an off-peak time, clear the Pending Queue in WHM, or switch the provider to Let's Encrypt for the affected accounts..

Frequently asked questions

Does AutoSSL work with 64-day certificates?

Yes. AutoSSL already issues short-lived certificates (90 days from both Sectigo and Let's Encrypt) and renews them on its own schedule, so it absorbs the move to 64-day Let's Encrypt certificates on February 10, 2027 without any settings change. Just make sure AutoSSL stays enabled and validation keeps working.

Do I need to change anything when certificates shrink to 64 days?

Not inside AutoSSL itself — it renews based on expiry, not a fixed calendar. The things to fix are outside it: custom cron jobs or scripts with hardcoded renewal intervals (values like 83, 80, or 60 days), and any manual renewal process. Let's Encrypt recommends renewing at roughly two-thirds of the certificate's lifetime.

Which AutoSSL provider should I choose: Sectigo or Let's Encrypt?

Sectigo is the default and works well for typical servers. Choose Let's Encrypt if you hit Sectigo's recurring "cannot currently accept incoming requests" outages, run a high volume of domains, or want better compatibility with CDNs. Let's Encrypt's 50-certificates-per-domain-per-week rate limit is the main constraint to watch.

Why does AutoSSL keep failing with a DCV error?

DCV errors almost always mean the certificate authority could not reach the validation file on your domain. The usual causes are DNS not pointing at the cPanel server, a CDN or proxy intercepting the request, .htaccess rules blocking the .well-known directory, or a CAA record that does not authorize your provider. Check the Logs tab's View Log output for the exact failing domain.

Does AutoSSL renew automatically, or do I need a cron job?

AutoSSL renews automatically — it runs a check every night and requests new certificates before the old ones expire. No cron job or manual action is needed. You only need to intervene when validation fails, which the Logs tab and failure notification emails will tell you about.

Conclusion

Setting up AutoSSL in cPanel takes about ten minutes: pick a provider in WHM's Manage AutoSSL, set the replacement option to match your certificate strategy, enable it for your users, and run it once. From there it looks after itself — which is exactly what the 64-day certificate era demands. With Let's Encrypt moving to 64-day defaults in February 2027 and the industry heading to 47 days by 2029, the servers that stay secure will be the ones whose renewal pipeline runs without human intervention. Enable AutoSSL now, keep failure notifications on, and let the automation do the worrying.
