n8n Pricing Explained: Self-Hosted vs Cloud Costs (2026)

  • AI Automation Tools
  • Published Oct 10, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 8 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

n8n Cloud costs €24/month (€20 billed annually) for 2,500 workflow executions on Starter, €60/month (€50 annual) for 10,000 on Pro, and €800/month for 40,000 on Business — with unlimited users and unlimited active workflows on every paid plan. Self-hosting the free Community Edition costs roughly $5–10/month for a small server, with no execution limit at all. Rule of thumb: under about 2,000 executions a month with nobody comfortable running a server, Cloud is the simpler pick; above that — or when your data must stay on your own infrastructure — self-hosting is dramatically cheaper. Prices change, so verify on n8n.io/pricing before buying.

n8n is the automation platform most small businesses discover right after outgrowing Zapier's per-task bills — but its pricing confuses newcomers because n8n is really two different products: a managed cloud you pay for monthly, and a free self-hosted edition that costs you nothing in license fees at all. The question isn't which plan is "best" in the abstract; it's how many times your workflows will run each month, and whether anyone on your team can look after a server. This guide explains the execution model, walks through every cloud plan with prices checked in October 2026, and does the real math on self-hosting — worked out at three different volumes — so you can pick the cheaper path for your own business. n8n bills in euros; treat every figure below as a starting point and confirm on n8n.io/pricing.

How n8n pricing works (the execution model)

An execution is one complete run of a workflow, no matter how many steps it contains. A lead-notification workflow with three steps and a 30-step monster that enriches the lead, checks three APIs, branches twice, and writes to two systems each cost exactly one execution per run. That single fact is what makes n8n pricing so different from the alternatives: Zapier counts every successful action step as a task, and Make spends roughly one credit per module action inside a scenario — we worked through that comparison in detail in our n8n vs Make guide.

The shape of your automations decides whether the execution model is a bargain or a trap. Long, multi-step workflows — onboarding sequences, order processing, report generation — are where n8n shines, because the per-run cost stays flat no matter how complex the workflow gets. High-frequency, trivially simple workflows are the opposite: if a workflow fires on a schedule every five minutes, that is 12 runs an hour, 288 a day, and roughly 8,640 executions a month — more than triple the entire Starter allowance — for a workflow that does almost nothing.

Watch for the polling trap. Two details inflate execution counts fast: schedule triggers that poll frequently (every 5 minutes burns ~8,640 executions/month), and sub-workflows called from a parent, which can each count separately. Measure real volume during the 14-day trial before committing to a tier.

n8n Cloud plans, explained

n8n Cloud is the fully managed option: n8n runs the servers in Frankfurt, handles backups and updates, and you build workflows in your browser. Every paid plan includes unlimited users and unlimited active workflows; only the execution allowance, concurrency, and governance features change — and annual billing saves about 17%.

The Pro-to-Business cliff

Notice the gap: Business costs roughly 13 times Pro while offering only 4 times the executions. That cliff mostly gates governance features — single sign-on, Git version control, multiple environments, audit logs — not raw capacity. If you need more than 10,000 executions a month but not SSO or Git, this is the tier where self-hosting starts looking extremely attractive. Startups with fewer than 20 employees get 50% off Business, which softens the jump. AI credits, not AI pricing

Cloud plans also include a small allowance of AI Workflow Builder credits (50 on Starter, 150 on Pro) for n8n's AI-assisted workflow generation — a builder convenience, not the cost of running AI inside your workflows, which your model provider bills separately. The true cost of self-hosting n8n

The Community Edition license is $0, with no execution limit — but "free software" is not the same as "free to run." n8n ships under the Sustainable Use License (a "fair-code" license): the source is public, and running your own automations for your own business is free forever. You just can't resell n8n itself as a competing hosted service. That license has no usage cap, which is why the real cost of self-hosting is the server plus your time.

What the server costs

RAM is the number to watch: every active execution holds its data in memory, and n8n's own Docker Compose setup lists a minimum of 4 GB RAM and 2 vCPUs. In practice, third-party 2026 setups put a trial box at roughly $5–10 a month (1–2 vCPU, 2 GB RAM) and a box you'd actually run a small business on at roughly $10–20 a month (2–4 vCPU, 4–8 GB RAM). Add a dollar or two a month if you want off-site backup storage, and possibly a domain name if you don't own one yet.

What your time costs

The license is free, but the operations are yours. A standard Docker setup takes roughly an hour the first time, then you own: applying updates, keeping SSL certificates renewing, backing up the database, watching disk space fill with execution logs, and registering your own OAuth apps for each connected service — on the cloud, those are preconfigured. None of this is hard if you've run a server before, but it is real, recurring work.

All in, self-hosting costs roughly $72–240 a year in server bills plus your maintenance time, versus €288 a year for Cloud Starter or €720 a year for Cloud Pro. The gap gets wider the more you run, because your VPS price doesn't move when your executions double.

When Cloud wins, and when self-hosting wins

Cost isn't the only variable. Here's how the two options compare on the factors that actually decide which one a small business sticks with.

n8n Cloud wins when

You're not comfortable with Docker or Linux server admin — the cloud trial takes minutes and everything just runs. You need an automation live this week, or you want managed backups, automatic updates, and someone else's support team when something breaks. At modest volume (under ~2,000 executions a month), the price gap is small enough that your time is worth more than the savings.

Self-hosting wins when

Someone on the team can run a Docker container — then the economics are overwhelming: unlimited executions for roughly the price of two coffees a month. Your volume is growing past 10,000 executions a month, where the cloud's pricing cliff turns punitive. Customer data can't leave your infrastructure — with self-hosting, everything sits in your own Postgres database, while cloud data lives on n8n's EU servers in Frankfurt. And experimenting costs nothing on a VPS, so you learn fast instead of watching the execution meter.

How self-hosting actually works (the basics)

Self-hosting sounds scarier than it is. At its core, you're running n8n's official Docker image on a rented server — the same software as the cloud, with you as the operator.

What you install

The standard setup is Docker and Docker Compose on a small VPS, with n8n's official Compose file as the starting point: one container for n8n, one for Postgres, and a reverse proxy (Caddy, Traefik, or nginx) for HTTPS. Hosts like Hostinger, DigitalOcean, and Hetzner rent suitable boxes for $5–20 a month, and several offer one-click n8n templates that skip the command line entirely.

What you're responsible for

Updates — n8n ships them regularly and you apply them with a pull command. Backups — export your workflows and back up the Postgres volume on a schedule, because nobody else will. Security — keep the host OS patched, restrict the editor's exposure, and use strong credentials. Monitoring — watch disk space, since execution logs accumulate. And OAuth — each service you connect (Google, Slack, and the rest) needs its own OAuth app registered in that provider's developer console, a one-time chore per service that's genuinely the most fiddly part of the setup.

What you give up

A few features are reserved for paid licenses: single sign-on, Git version control, multiple environments, and long-term audit logging. Managed support is replaced by the community forum and documentation. But the editor, the integration nodes, and the execution engine are identical — and workflows export as JSON from the cloud and import into a self-hosted install with zero conversion.

Our verdict

Choose n8n Cloud if

  • Nobody on the team wants to run a server, and that's fine.
  • You need automations live this week with zero setup risk.
  • Your volume is under ~2,000 executions a month, where the price gap is small.
  • You want managed backups, updates, and support handled for you.

Choose self-hosting if

  • Someone on the team is comfortable with Docker and a VPS.
  • Your volume is growing toward or past 10,000 executions a month.
  • Customer data must stay on your own infrastructure.
  • You want unlimited executions so experimenting costs nothing.

The honest one-line rule: pay for Cloud when your time is worth more than the server bill, self-host when your volume is worth more than the server admin. Start with the 14-day cloud trial, measure your real execution count, then move to a $5–10 VPS the moment the math says so — the workflows come with you as plain JSON.

Frequently asked questions

Is the self-hosted n8n Community Edition really free?

Yes, for your own internal business use. The Community Edition ships under n8n's fair-code (Sustainable Use) license: no license fee, no execution limit, no workflow cap. You pay only for your server — roughly $5 to $10 a month for a small VPS — plus the time to maintain it. The one hard limit: you may not resell n8n itself as a hosted service.

How much does n8n Cloud cost per month in 2026?

Starter is €24/month (€20 billed annually) for 2,500 executions, Pro is €60/month (€50 annual) for 10,000 executions, and Business is €800/month (€667 annual) for 40,000 executions. Annual billing saves about 17%, and every paid plan includes unlimited users and unlimited active workflows. Prices checked October 2026 — verify on n8n.io/pricing before buying.

What counts as an execution in n8n?

One execution is one complete run of a workflow, no matter how many steps it has — a 3-step workflow and a 30-step workflow each cost one execution per run. Sub-workflows called from a parent workflow can count as their own executions, so modular designs inflate the number faster than the workflow count suggests.

Can I start on n8n Cloud and switch to self-hosting later?

Yes. n8n workflows export as JSON files that import directly into a self-hosted Community Edition with no conversion. You will need to reconnect your app credentials and set up OAuth apps yourself, but nothing about your workflow logic is lost — which is why starting on the cloud trial and moving down to a VPS later is a common path.

Is n8n cheaper than Zapier for a small business?

Usually, for anything beyond trivial automations. Zapier counts every action step as a task, so a 10-step workflow run costs ten tasks there and one execution on n8n. Third-party 2026 breakdowns put a workload needing n8n Pro's 10,000 executions at $300+/month on Zapier's Professional plan — roughly six times the price. Conclusion

n8n gives you an unusual choice: pay €24–60 a month for a managed cloud with a hard execution cap, or pay $5–20 a month for a server with no cap at all. The execution model makes n8n Cloud genuinely cheap for complex, multi-step workflows — one run is one execution whether the workflow has three steps or thirty. But past 10,000 executions a month, the cloud's pricing cliff turns self-hosting from a hobbyist option into the obvious business decision. Start with the cloud trial, count your real executions for a month, and let the meter tell you which option to keep. A typical small business wiring up lead capture, a welcome email series (see our Mailchimp vs ConvertKit guide for the email side), and reporting lands comfortably under 2,000 executions.
