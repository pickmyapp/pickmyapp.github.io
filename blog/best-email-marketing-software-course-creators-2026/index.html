Course creators do not need a generic email tool. They need one that sells seats, onboards students, drips lessons, and brings graduates back for the next course. The best email marketing software for course creators in 2026 is Kit for most creators, because it was built for people who sell things to an audience: flat-fee pricing, unlimited automations on paid plans, tagging by course and purchase, and built-in commerce for selling the course itself. GetResponse is the strongest alternative if you want webinars and a course builder inside the same tool, and ActiveCampaign is the pick if your sales process needs the deepest automation. This guide compares the six tools course creators actually shortlist, with real prices checked in November 2026 and the math for a 1,000- and 5,000-subscriber list. What course creators actually need from email

A course business runs on four email jobs, and a tool that does only two of them will cost you sales. First, launch email: a timed sequence that opens carts, handles objections, and closes them — usually 5 to 10 emails over a week, with different messages for clickers, non-buyers, and non-openers. Second, student onboarding: a welcome series that gets new students to actually start the course, because unfinished students never buy the next course. Third, lesson drips: scheduled lessons or reminders tied to where each student is in the course, not to the calendar. Fourth, segmentation by course and purchase: buyers of Course A should never get the launch emails for Course A again, and graduates of the beginner course are the warmest audience for the advanced one.

On top of that, three practical requirements decide most purchases. The tool must integrate with your course platform — Teachable, Thinkific, Kajabi, Podia, or Gumroad — so a purchase tags the student automatically instead of you exporting CSVs by hand. It must price fairly as your freebie list grows, because a creator's list is mostly free subscribers who may never buy. And it should let you sell directly from email, so a launch email can take a reader to payment in one click. A general email tool can do all of this, but creator-built tools do it with less setup. Kit

Kit, formerly ConvertKit, is an email marketing platform built for creators who sell things: newsletters, digital products, and courses. Launched in 2013 as ConvertKit and rebranded in 2024, it assumes your subscribers are also your customers. That is why it is the default recommendation for course creators.

The creator-specific case is strong. Kit prices by total subscriber count, not by revenue, and its free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited broadcasts and landing pages — roomy enough to grow a freebie list for a long time before paying. On the Creator plan, from $39 a month at 1,000 subscribers ($33 billed annually), you get unlimited visual automations and email sequences, tagging and segmentation, and built-in commerce: you can sell a course or subscription directly from an email, paying only Kit's 3.5% plus 30 cents per transaction, which includes card processing. A welcome series, a lesson drip, and a launch sequence can all run at once.

The catches are worth knowing. The free plan includes only one basic automation, so a real welcome series needs the Creator plan — the free tier is a list-building tool, not a course machine. Kit has no full website, just a newsletter feed and landing pages, so your course still lives on Teachable, Thinkific, or your own site, connected by integration. And Kit bills only confirmed subscribers, which is fair, but a 5,000-subscriber list costs $89 a month for Creator, so the bill grows with your freebie audience even if only a fraction ever buys.

GetResponse

GetResponse is the only tool on this list that can replace your webinar software and your course builder as well as your email tool. Its Creator plan, $69 a month at 1,000 contacts, adds webinars for up to 100 attendees, an AI course builder, and paid newsletters, on top of the email marketing in the lower plans. If you teach live, that bundling is worth real money: a standalone webinar tool typically costs $50 or more a month.

The pricing structure needs care. GetResponse's plans were renamed in January 2025: Free, Starter, Marketer, Creator, and MAX. Starter costs $19 a month at 1,000 contacts with unlimited sends, but it includes exactly one custom automation workflow — a welcome series plus any second flow requires Marketer at $59 a month. The Creator plan at $69 sits just above Marketer, so for a course creator the realistic entry point is $69, not $19. The free plan covers 500 contacts and 2,500 emails a month, with GetResponse branding and no real automation, and annual billing saves about 18%.

ActiveCampaign

ActiveCampaign is the deepest automation tool on this list, and the right pick when your sales process is a funnel rather than a broadcast. Its Starter plan costs $15 a month at 1,000 contacts on annual billing, and every plan includes multi-step automation, an AI automation builder, There is no free plan — only a 14-day trial — which means it is for creators who are already selling.

What you get is automation depth no rival matches at this price: more than 950 pre-built automation recipes, conditional logic, site and event tracking, and behavioral triggers that route a subscriber by what they clicked, watched, or bought. The limits matter, though. Starter allows only one user, limited segmentation, and five actions per automation — a welcome series of three emails with a wait and a tag between them reaches that limit quickly. Unlimited automation actions start on Plus at $49 a month. Prices also climb steeply with list size: Starter is $149 a month at 10,000 contacts, about ten times the 1,000-contact price. ActiveCampaign has no webinar hosting or course tools of its own; it is built around email, SMS, and CRM-style automation, so your course still lives elsewhere and the integration does the joining.

Mailchimp

Mailchimp is the most polished generalist on this list, and the easiest first tool for a creator starting from zero. It offers 300-plus native integrations — the largest in the category — including first-party connections to Teachable, Thinkific, and Shopify, so a course purchase can tag a student without extra software.

The pricing is where creators should look twice. The free plan now covers only 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with one seat, one audience, and no automation flows — the thinnest free plan on this list. Essentials starts at $13 a month at 500 contacts but caps automation flows at four steps; Standard at $20 a month at 500 contacts allows up to 200 flows, plus predictive segmentation and send-time optimization. Mailchimp also bills unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts toward your total — archive unsubscribes instead of just deleting them, or you keep paying for people who cannot receive your email. Mailchimp is fine for a creator whose course is one product among many, but a creator whose business is the course will outgrow its free plan fast and pay more per subscriber than on Kit or GetResponse.

MailerLite

MailerLite is the cheapest honest starting point for a creator on a budget. Its free plan covers 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month with two seats, and Comfort starts at $12 a month at 500 subscribers (about $10.80 billed annually), rising to about $19 a month at 1,000. The builder is simple, the landing pages are solid, and the automation covers a welcome series and basic drips. The trade-offs are scale: deliverability and template depth trail Mailchimp and Kit, and the free plan's automation is reported inconsistently across sources — do not assume it includes full automation without checking the current plan page. MailerLite is the right pick for a first course with a small list, and the wrong pick for a creator running multi-course launch funnels.

Which should you choose?

Choose Kit if

  • You sell courses, products, or paid subscriptions to an audience.
  • You want welcome series, lesson drips, and launch sequences running at once.
  • You want to sell directly from email without a separate checkout tool.
  • Your list is mostly free subscribers and you want a generous free plan.

Choose GetResponse if

  • You teach live and want webinars and a course builder in the same tool.
  • You send often and want unlimited sends on paid plans.
  • You are comfortable paying $69 a month for the Creator plan.

Choose ActiveCampaign if your funnel needs conditional logic, lead scoring, and 950-plus automation recipes — and you are already selling enough to justify $49 a month for Plus. Choose Mailchimp if you are starting from zero, want the most integrations, and your course is one product among many. Choose MailerLite if your list is small and your budget is tight, and Brevo if your list is large and your sends are infrequent. Whichever you pick, check the automation gate before you pay: the plan that includes your welcome series, your lesson drip, and your launch sequence is the plan you actually need, and it is rarely the cheapest one on the pricing page. Frequently asked questions

What is the best email marketing software for course creators?

Kit is the best fit for most course creators: it was built for people who sell to an audience, with unlimited automations on the Creator plan ($39 a month at 1,000 subscribers), tagging by course and purchase, and built-in commerce for selling the course from email. GetResponse is the strongest alternative if you teach live and want webinars and a course builder included; ActiveCampaign wins if you need the deepest automation.

Can I sell my course directly through email?

Yes, on the right tool. Kit's commerce feature lets you sell courses and products directly from an email at 3.5% plus 30 cents per transaction, including card processing. GetResponse's Creator plan includes an AI course builder and paid newsletters. On other tools you link from the email to your course platform's checkout — Teachable, Thinkific, or Kajabi — which works fine but adds a step.

Which email tool is cheapest for a course creator?

At the plan a creator actually needs, Brevo is cheapest if your list is large and mailed rarely ($9 a month for 5,000 emails), followed by MailerLite Comfort at about $19 a month at 1,000 subscribers. Kit Creator at $39 a month is the cheapest of the creator-focused tools with unlimited automations. Beware the headline trap: GetResponse Starter at $19 and ActiveCampaign Starter at $15 both lack the automations a launch needs.

Do I need a separate tool for webinars?

Not if you choose GetResponse: its Creator plan ($69 a month at 1,000 contacts) includes webinars for up to 100 attendees and an AI course builder. On every other tool in this guide you need a separate webinar tool, which typically costs $50 or more a month — factor that into the comparison before you decide GetResponse is expensive.

Can I automate lesson drips by email?

Yes, on any paid plan with real automation: Kit Creator, GetResponse Marketer or Creator, ActiveCampaign Plus, Mailchimp Standard, or MailerLite Comfort. The free plans mostly cannot do this — Kit's free plan allows only one basic automation, Mailchimp's free plan has no automation flows at all, and GetResponse's free plan has no real automation. Check the automation gate on the plan you are considering before you pay.

Conclusion

For most course creators, Kit is the right answer: creator-first design, unlimited automations on the Creator plan, tagging by course and purchase, and commerce built in, at $39 a month for 1,000 subscribers. GetResponse is the pick if you teach live and want webinars and a course builder bundled in. ActiveCampaign is the pick for the deepest funnels, Mailchimp for the easiest start and the most integrations, MailerLite for the tightest budget, and Brevo for the big list mailed rarely. List the four email jobs your course needs — launch, onboarding, lesson drips, and segmentation — then buy the plan that includes all four.
