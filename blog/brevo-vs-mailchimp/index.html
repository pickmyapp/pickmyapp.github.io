Brevo vs Mailchimp: Pricing and Features Compared

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 10, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most small businesses, Brevo is the cheaper and more flexible pick: you pay per email sent (from $9 a month for 5,000 emails) and can store unlimited contacts on every plan. Mailchimp charges per contact stored (from $13 a month for 500 contacts), which gets expensive as your list grows, but it wins on polish — more templates, the friendliest builder, and the largest integration marketplace. Choose Brevo for value and multi-channel reach; choose Mailchimp for ease of use and its ecosystem.

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) and Mailchimp are the two email platforms most small businesses shortlist, and they are surprisingly hard to compare on a single pricing page. That is because the headline numbers hide the real decision: Brevo bills you for the emails you send; Mailchimp bills you for the contacts you store. Depending on your list size and send frequency, the same business can pay half as much on one as on the other. This guide compares pricing plan by plan, features head to head, and worked costs at three list sizes, using prices checked against vendor pricing pages in October 2026. Both vendors change prices — verify the current figures on brevo.com and mailchimp.com before buying.

What Brevo is, in brief

Brevo is a multi-channel marketing platform, founded in France in 2012 as Sendinblue, that bundles email, SMS, WhatsApp, chat, and a built-in sales CRM under one roof. Transactional email — order confirmations, password resets, booking receipts — is included on every plan through the same sending infrastructure, via the app or an API, with no separate product to buy.

Brevo's defining trait is send-based pricing. You pick a plan by how many emails you send each month, and your contact count barely matters: every plan allows unlimited contacts (up to 100,000 stored on the free tier). For a business with a large database it emails selectively, that is the difference between paying for 100,000 contacts and paying for 5,000 sends.

What Mailchimp is, in brief

Mailchimp is the best-known email marketing platform in the US market, owned by Intuit since its $12 billion acquisition in 2021, and famous for its friendly builder and enormous template and integration ecosystem. It is a capable all-rounder: email campaigns, landing pages, signup forms, social posts, and increasingly, generative-AI writing assistance (Intuit Assist) on paid tiers.

Mailchimp's defining trait is contact-based pricing. You pick a plan by how many contacts you store, and each plan also caps how many emails you can send as a multiple of that count (10x on Essentials, 12x on Standard, 15x on Premium). It is simple to understand — and punishing if your list grows faster than your send volume.

The pricing models are opposites

Before looking at plan names, get the economics clear, because they flip the answer depending on your business shape. On Brevo, a bigger list costs nothing extra — only more sends raise your bill. On Mailchimp, a bigger list raises your bill even if you never email half of it.

That means two businesses with the same 10,000-subscriber list can face opposite winners. If you email the whole list twice a month, Brevo charges for 20,000 sends (around $32 a month on the Starter volume tier); Mailchimp charges for 10,000 contacts (around $110 to $135 a month on Essentials or Standard). If you have only 300 subscribers and email them weekly, Mailchimp's Essentials tier at $13 a month is the cheaper move. The right platform is the one whose billing unit matches your ratio of contacts to sends.

Quick fact: Mailchimp's free plan has shrunk dramatically — from 2,000 contacts in 2022 to just 250 contacts and 500 sends a month today, after a cutback in January 2026. Brevo's free plan has stayed generous by comparison: 300 emails a day with up to 100,000 contacts stored.

Where Brevo is cheaper

For any list over a couple of thousand contacts, Brevo is typically cheaper — often dramatically. At the 5,000-contact mark, Brevo's 5,000-email Starter tier is $9 a month (or $32 at the 20,000-email tier if you send weekly); Mailchimp charges $75 a month for Essentials and $100 for Standard at the same contact count. Brevo also folds transactional email and SMS into the platform, while Mailchimp pushes transactional to a separate Mandrill add-on. If you run a lean, budget-driven email program, Brevo is hard to beat.

Where Mailchimp is cheaper

Mailchimp wins on very small, frequently emailed lists. A newsletter with 250 to 500 subscribers on Essentials costs $13 a month and can send up to 5,000 emails — the same program on Brevo's Starter costs $9, so the difference is genuinely small either way, and at that size Mailchimp's simpler editor and bigger template library may matter more than $4. Mailchimp is also the better-known quantity for teams that value the Intuit ecosystem (QuickBooks, TurboTax) and its 300-plus integrations.

Free plans compared

Brevo's free plan is the most generous in this pair: 300 emails a day (about 9,000 a month), up to 100,000 contacts stored, and transactional sending via API — the only real limits are the daily cap and Brevo branding in the footer. It is a usable plan for a tiny store or a solo newsletter, not just a trial in disguise.

Mailchimp's free plan is now a genuine starter tier: 250 contacts, 500 sends a month with a 250-a-day cap, one audience, one seat, Mailchimp branding, and no automation. It works for testing the builder and running a micro list, but most businesses outgrow it within a few months. Features compared

Automation depth

Both platforms offer visual automation builders, but the gates differ. On Brevo, automation unlocks on the Business plan (from $18 a month) and scales up to multi-branch workflows with SMS and WhatsApp steps. On Mailchimp, the Customer Journey builder — its strongest automation surface, with branching up to 200 steps — sits on Standard (from $20 a month); Essentials caps automations at 4 steps. Mailchimp ships more than 100 pre-built journey templates, which beginners finish faster; Brevo's workflow builder is leaner but covers the core recipes (welcome series, abandoned cart, win-back) well.

Templates and the builder

Mailchimp wins on templates and polish. Its drag-and-drop builder is the friendliest in the category, the template library is far larger, and creative tools like the content studio and Intuit Assist generative-AI drafting are included on paid plans. Brevo's builder is clean and fast but offers fewer starting templates; some users design from scratch more often than they would on Mailchimp.

Deliverability

Both platforms land above 90% inbox placement in independent tests when SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are configured properly. Mailchimp has a slight edge for very high-volume senders because of its longer track record and dedicated sending infrastructure; Brevo offers dedicated IPs on higher tiers and solid sender-reputation tooling. For most small businesses sending under 50,000 emails a month, the deliverability difference is negligible — your authentication setup and list hygiene matter far more than which logo sends the email.

Segmentation

Both handle the basics — tags, lists, and engagement-based segments. Mailchimp's advanced segmentation (predictive segments, purchase-likelihood scoring) sits on Standard and Premium; Brevo's segmentation is strong on the Business tier with behavioral and web-tracking conditions. Neither will frustrate a small business; enterprise-grade segmentation lives at ActiveCampaign's level anyway.

SMS and multi-channel

This is one of the clearest splits. Brevo treats SMS and WhatsApp as native channels with the same credits-based billing, so you can run an abandoned-cart SMS sequence without a second vendor. Mailchimp sells SMS as a paid add-on (from $20 a month for 1,000 credits) and keeps WhatsApp out of the picture. If multi-channel is part of your plan, Brevo is the more natural fit.

CRM

Brevo includes a real sales CRM — pipelines, deals, meetings, and conversations — on all plans, which can replace a separate CRM for a small team. Mailchimp's CRM is marketing-flavored: contact profiles, notes, and audience management rather than deal tracking. If you need actual sales pipeline software alongside email, compare HubSpot vs Pipedrive; if you just need contact records, both suffice.

Our verdict

Choose Brevo if

  • Your list is growing and you want the bill to stay flat as contacts accumulate.
  • You want email, SMS, WhatsApp, and a sales CRM in one platform.
  • You send transactional email and want it included, not a separate add-on.
  • You are price-sensitive and willing to trade some template polish for value.

Choose Mailchimp if

  • Your list is small and stays small — the contact-based bill stays low.
  • You want the friendliest builder, the biggest template library, and Intuit Assist AI drafting.
  • You lean on its 300-plus integrations and the Intuit/QuickBooks ecosystem.
  • Migration cost is real: you are already deep in Mailchimp workflows.

You are not locked in on day one. Brevo's free plan (300 emails a day, up to 100,000 contacts) is a real sandbox, and Mailchimp offers a 14-day trial on Essentials and Standard. Build one automation — a welcome series is ideal — in both, and see which one your team actually finishes. The tool your team will use beats the tool that wins on paper.

Frequently asked questions

Is Brevo actually cheaper than Mailchimp?

For most small businesses, yes. Brevo charges per email sent (from $9 a month for 5,000 emails) with unlimited contacts on every plan, while Mailchimp charges per contact stored (Essentials from $13 a month for 500 contacts). The gap widens as your list grows: a 5,000-contact list costs roughly $9 to $18 a month on Brevo versus $75 to $100 a month on Mailchimp. Prices checked October 2026 and subject to change.

How many contacts does the Mailchimp free plan allow?

The Mailchimp free plan allows 250 contacts and 500 email sends per month (with a 250-per-day cap), plus one audience and one seat. Brevo's free plan is more generous: 300 emails per day (about 9,000 a month) with up to 100,000 contacts stored. Both free plans add vendor branding to your emails.

Does Mailchimp charge for unsubscribed contacts?

Yes. Mailchimp counts subscribed, unsubscribed, and non-subscribed contacts toward your billable total — only archived, cleaned, or deleted contacts are excluded. If someone unsubscribes, you must manually archive them or you keep paying for them. This is one of the most common reasons Mailchimp bills come in higher than expected.

Which has better deliverability, Brevo or Mailchimp?

Both land above 90% inbox placement in independent tests when SPF, DKIM, and DMARC are set up correctly. Mailchimp has a slight edge for very high-volume senders thanks to its longer track record and dedicated sending infrastructure. For most small businesses sending under 50,000 emails a month, the deliverability difference is negligible — your authentication and list hygiene matter more than the platform.

Can I move my email list from Mailchimp to Brevo?

Yes. Export your contact list from Mailchimp as a CSV file and import it into Brevo — the process takes a few minutes. Templates and automation workflows do not transfer, so plan an afternoon to rebuild them in Brevo's editor, and confirm your new sending domain with SPF and DKIM before you send your first campaign.

Conclusion

Start with Brevo if your list is growing, your budget is tight, or you want SMS, WhatsApp, a sales CRM, and transactional email under one bill — its send-based pricing is the fairest deal in email marketing in 2026. Pick Mailchimp if your list is small, you want the most polished builder and template library, or your stack leans on the Intuit ecosystem — at 500 contacts, Essentials is genuinely affordable. Either way, multiply your list size by your real send frequency first; that single multiplication, more than any feature comparison, tells you which platform saves you money. And remember that prices change — verify the current figures before you buy.
