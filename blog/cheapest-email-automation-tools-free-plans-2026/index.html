Cheapest Email Automation Tools With Free Plans (2026)

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Nov 4, 2026
  • Updated: November 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Sender is the cheapest email automation tool with a genuinely usable free plan in 2026: 2,500 subscribers, 15,000 emails a month, and multi-step automation included at $0. EmailOctopus is a close second (2,500 subscribers, 10,000 emails, simple sequences). Kit is best if you mainly broadcast to a big list (10,000 subscribers free, but only one automation). Skip Mailchimp's free plan for automation entirely — it includes no automation flows at all. All figures below were checked in November 2026 against vendor pricing pages and current pricing guides; confirm on the vendor's page before you pay, because free tiers in this category keep shrinking.

Almost every email tool advertises a free plan, but most free plans are built for sending newsletters, not for running automation. A welcome sequence, an abandoned-cart series, or a re-engagement flow is what turns a signup form into revenue while you sleep — and several well-known free plans either exclude automation or cap it so tightly that one welcome series uses it up. This guide compares only tools with a genuinely free tier, no time-limited trials, judged on the three limits that decide whether free automation is real: subscribers, sends, and automation depth. What counts as free email automation?

Email automation is a sequence of emails sent automatically when a subscriber does something — signs up, clicks a link, abandons a cart, or reaches a date. Three levels show up on free plans, and vendors rarely label them. A broadcast is a one-off newsletter sent by hand: every free plan includes this. An autoresponder is a single delayed email, such as one welcome message a day after signup. A multi-step workflow is a timed series with branches — three welcome emails over a week, with a different follow-up for clickers. Only the third level is real automation for most businesses, and it is the level this guide scores.

Two more details decide whether a free plan survives contact with a real list: the send allowance (a 2,500-person list emailed weekly needs about 10,000 sends a month before one automated email goes out) and the automation cap (workflows, steps per workflow, or contacts allowed in). Read all three numbers, not just the headline subscriber count.

Quick fact: MailerLite cut its free plan twice in just over a year (1,000 to 500 in September 2025, then to 250 in June 2026), and Mailchimp cut its free plan to 250 contacts in February 2026. Treat every free tier here as a snapshot, not a promise.

Sender: the strongest free automation deal

Sender's free plan covers 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month with multi-step automation workflows included, and no credit card is required. That combination is unmatched: the largest free send allowance anywhere, with automation intact. You get a drag-and-drop builder, forms and popups, segmentation, welcome and cart flows, and transactional email.

Trade-offs are modest: Sender branding until you pay, one user seat, no A/B testing, no SMS on free, dedicated IPs reserved for Professional. Paid plans start at about $8 a month and remove the branding. Sender's weakness is polish, not power — an easy trade if your priority is automating free as long as possible.

EmailOctopus: the simple, cheap runner-up

EmailOctopus's free Starter plan covers 2,500 subscribers and 10,000 emails a month, with automated sequences included and no time limit. It is deliberately simple: a clean editor, templates, forms, landing pages, segments, and time-based welcome and onboarding sequences. No CRM, no SMS, no website builder — a feature, not a gap, if you only want email done well and cheaply.

Two limits matter. Automation means sequences, not branching workflows: a timed series after signup, but no easy branches based on clicks, opens, or ecommerce events. Free reports expire after 30 days, and emails carry EmailOctopus branding. Pro starts at $10 a month billed monthly ($9 yearly) for up to 500 subscribers, removes branding, and keeps reports forever.

Kit: the biggest free list, the narrowest automation

Kit (formerly ConvertKit) offers the largest free subscriber allowance in email marketing: up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited broadcasts on its free Newsletter plan. For a creator who mainly sends a newsletter to a large audience, nothing else free comes close — a list of 8,000 costs $0 here, with unlimited forms and landing pages.

The catch is automation: only one basic automation with one sequence. You can run a single welcome sequence, but not a welcome sequence plus a course-delivery sequence plus a re-engagement flow. The visual builder, unlimited sequences, and integrations start on Creator at $39 a month for 1,000 subscribers (about $33 annually). If automation is why you are shopping, Kit free covers exactly one job. Broadcast on Kit free; pay for Creator, or pick Sender, if you automate. Brevo: generous storage, a daily send wall

Brevo's free plan stores up to 100,000 contacts, sends 300 emails a day, and allows automation for up to 2,000 contacts. That storage number looks spectacular until you do the arithmetic: 300 a day is about 9,000 sends over a 30-day month, and unused sends do not roll over. A 1,000-person newsletter must be split across four days. For a list under 300, Brevo free works fine, and it includes transactional email, which most free plans do not.

Brevo's pricing also has a trap: paid plans are priced by monthly email volume, but each email tier carries a contact cap, so the cheapest Starter tier at $9 a month (5,000 emails) may not hold a list of a few thousand contacts, forcing you into a higher tier than your sending volume suggests. Standard, from $18 a month, lifts the 2,000-contact automation cap and adds A/B testing and send-time optimization. Brevo is a good free start for tiny lists; price it carefully once you grow. MailerLite: the best small free plan for real workflows

MailerLite's free plan now covers just 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, but still includes 3 automations of up to 5 steps each. After two cuts in just over a year, it is no longer the standout free deal it once was — but for a brand-new list, three short workflows cover a welcome series, a lead-magnet follow-up, and one more flow, and MailerLite's builder is the most polished free tool here. Over 250 subscribers, campaigns, automations, and signups pause until you upgrade or trim the list.

The paid step is gentle: Comfort starts at $12 a month for up to 500 subscribers ($19 at 1,000) and raises you to 50 automations of up to 100 steps, with sends capped at 10 times your tier. That makes MailerLite the cheapest paid route to serious multi-step automation for a small list. If you value design quality, start free here and expect to pay once you pass 250 subscribers. GetResponse and Mailchimp: the cautionary pair

GetResponse's free plan covers 500 contacts and 2,500 emails a month, but automation is heavily restricted once the 14-day premium trial ends. New accounts get premium features for two weeks, after which the real limits appear: branding on every message, one landing page capped at 1,000 unique visitors, and no integrations. Guides disagree on what automation survives the trial — some report basic autoresponders only, with multi-step workflows unavailable — so do not assume a sequence will keep running. Paid Starter at $19 a month for 1,000 contacts includes unlimited sends but only one custom workflow; unlimited workflows start at Marketer ($59 a month). Pay for GetResponse for bundled webinars and courses, not free automation.

Mailchimp's free plan is the weakest here for automation: 250 contacts, 500 emails a month with a 250-a-day cap, one seat, one audience, and no automation flows at all. A weekly newsletter to a full 250-person list uses the entire monthly allowance in two sends. Essentials at $13 a month for 500 contacts adds flows capped at 4 steps; genuine branching automation with up to 200 flows only arrives on Standard at $20 a month for 500 contacts. Mailchimp also counts unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts toward your billable total, so archive unsubscribes. Choose Mailchimp for its 300-plus integrations and polished builder, not cheap automation. Our GetResponse vs Mailchimp comparison prices both in full.

What it costs at 1,000 and 2,500 subscribers

Headline limits hide the real question: what do you pay at a realistic size? Assume a weekly newsletter plus a welcome series — about 5,000 sends a month at 1,000 subscribers, 11,000 at 2,500.

  • At 1,000 subscribers: Sender $0, EmailOctopus $0, Kit $0 for broadcasts but one automation only, Brevo $0 split across days, MailerLite Comfort $19, Mailchimp Essentials $13 (4-step flows only), GetResponse Starter $19 (one workflow).
  • At 2,500 subscribers: Sender still $0, EmailOctopus still $0 but tight (10,000 allowance against about 11,000 sends), Kit still $0 for broadcasts, Brevo still $0 split across days, MailerLite Comfort $33, Mailchimp Essentials $45, GetResponse Starter $29.
  • The lesson: the cheapest automation at 1,000 subscribers is free, and at 2,500 it is still free — on Sender. The first paid plan you need is usually bought for automation depth or branding, not send volume, so pick the free plan whose automation cap matches your plans, not just the biggest subscriber number.

Quick fact: Kit free is worth $0 only while you broadcast. The moment you need a second automation, Creator pricing applies — from $39 a month at 1,000 subscribers upward. Price the automation you will need in six months, not just today's list.

What actually forces you to upgrade

Free plans end in four predictable ways. Knowing which wall you will hit first tells you which tool to start on:

  • Subscriber cap: MailerLite and Mailchimp (both 250) hit this first for any growing list. Sender and EmailOctopus give 2,500 of headroom; Kit gives 10,000.
  • Send cap: Mailchimp's 500 a month and Brevo's 300 a day are tightest. A list emailed more than twice a month outgrows Mailchimp free almost immediately.
  • Automation cap: Kit's one automation, MailerLite's three workflows, and Brevo's 2,000 automation contacts are the walls beyond a welcome series. Sender is the only free plan here with no published workflow-count cap.
  • Branding and features: every free plan here stamps the vendor's badge on your emails. Removing it, adding a teammate, or getting A/B testing all require a paid plan.

Before you commit, test any shortlisted tool: build your welcome sequence as you want it, send it to a test address, and check every step fires. Limits are often enforced at activation, not signup.

Which one should you pick?

Pick Sender if

  • You want real multi-step automation free for as long as possible.
  • Your list is under 2,500 and you email weekly or more.
  • You run welcome, cart, or re-engagement sequences, not just newsletters.

Pick Kit if

  • You mainly broadcast a newsletter to a large audience.
  • One welcome sequence is all the automation you need right now.
  • Your list is growing fast toward several thousand subscribers.

If neither fits — polished design plus several workflows on a small list — start on MailerLite free and budget $12 a month for Comfort once you pass 250 subscribers. If you run a store and need abandoned-cart flows free, Sender covers that on its free tier while EmailOctopus's simpler sequences may not stretch far enough. Avoid Mailchimp free if automation is the goal.

Frequently asked questions

Which email automation tool is completely free?

Sender is the strongest fully free option: 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month with multi-step automation included. EmailOctopus is close behind at 2,500 subscribers and 10,000 emails a month with simple sequences. Both keep their branding on your emails until you pay.

Is Mailchimp's free plan good for automation?

No. Mailchimp's free plan covers 250 contacts and 500 emails a month and includes no automation flows. Real automation starts on Essentials at $13 a month for 500 contacts, where flows are capped at 4 steps; Standard at $20 a month allows up to 200 flows.

What is the cheapest paid plan with real automation?

Brevo Starter starts at $9 a month for 5,000 emails, but watch its contact caps. For multi-step workflows at a small list size, MailerLite Comfort at $12 a month for up to 500 subscribers gives 50 automations, while Sender's paid plans start at about $8 a month.

How many emails can I send free on Brevo?

Brevo's free plan allows 300 emails per day, about 9,000 over a 30-day month. Unused sends do not roll over, and no campaign to more than 300 people can go out in one day, so a 1,000-person newsletter must be split across four days.

When should I upgrade from a free email automation plan?

Upgrade when you hit one of four walls: your subscriber count passes the free cap, your monthly sends run out, you need more automations than the free plan allows, or you need your own branding. Check your busiest month, not your average month.

Conclusion

The cheapest email automation tool with a free plan in 2026 is Sender: 2,500 subscribers, 15,000 emails a month, and multi-step automation at $0 is a combination no rival matches. EmailOctopus is the best simple alternative, Kit the best free plan for broadcast-heavy creators needing one automation, and MailerLite the cheapest paid step up at $12 a month for up to 500 subscribers. Mailchimp and GetResponse free plans look free but do not deliver usable automation. Start on the plan that fits next month's automation, test the full sequence before launch, and upgrade when a cap — not a calendar — tells you to.
