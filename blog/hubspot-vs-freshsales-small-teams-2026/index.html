HubSpot vs Freshsales for small teams (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Oct 15, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For a tiny team on a tight budget, Freshsales wins on price: Growth starts at $9 per user per month billed annually, against HubSpot Starter at around $20 per seat. But price at the tier you will actually need. HubSpot gives you the deeper free CRM and a platform you can grow marketing and service into — with a steep cliff to Professional at $90 per seat plus a $1,500 onboarding fee. Freshsales gives you built-in calling on every plan and AI lead scoring on its entry tier, with its own jump to Pro at $39 per user. Phone-first sellers on a budget: Freshsales. Marketing-and-sales in one system: HubSpot.

HubSpot and Freshsales look like direct competitors — both are sales CRMs with free entry tiers aimed at small teams — but they are built for different buyers. HubSpot is a platform play: the CRM is the free front door to an ecosystem of marketing, service and content tools sharing one contact database. Freshsales, from Freshworks, is a sales-first tool with the phone system built in and AI lead scoring on its cheapest paid plan. The choice comes down to how far the free tier takes you, what the tier you will actually need costs, and what is bundled versus bolted on. Prices below were checked in October 2026. HubSpot for small teams: generous start, steep climb

HubSpot's free CRM is the deepest free tier in this comparison. Up to 2 users get unlimited contacts, companies and deals, deal pipelines, task management, email tracking (up to 200 notifications a month), live chat and basic reporting. The limits are real — only 3 email templates, 5 documents, and minimal customization — but a two-person founding team can genuinely run on it. The 2-user cap is the hard wall; a third hire forces a paid tier.

Sales Hub Starter, currently listed around $20 per seat per month (HubSpot has run promotions near $7, so check the price in checkout), adds core sales tooling: simple automation, more email templates, additional dashboards and no HubSpot branding. For a small team that just needs contact and deal tracking with more seats, Starter is a real, usable CRM at a reasonable price.

The problem is the cliff. Email sequences, forecasting, conversation intelligence and real custom reporting all sit behind Sales Hub Professional at $90 per seat per month billed annually — plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee. A 5-seat team moves from roughly $420 a year on Starter to about $6,900 in year one. That is where most teams discover what HubSpot actually costs. Our Zoho CRM vs HubSpot breakdown compares it to a vendor with a flatter price ladder.

Quick fact: HubSpot's per-seat model is only half the story. Marketing Hub prices separately and scales by marketing-contact tier, so if you later add email marketing, you pay for seats plus a contact-based fee. Sales-only teams never see this; teams that grow into the full suite always do.

Where HubSpot's platform argument actually pays off

HubSpot's real advantage is architectural: Marketing Hub, Service Hub and Content Hub share one CRM object model, so a contact captured by a form lives in the same record a salesperson calls. A team that expects marketing and sales to share a system within a year or two is buying this integration whether it needs it today or not.

Freshsales for small teams: cheap start, watch the jump

Freshsales' Growth plan at $9 per user per month billed annually is one of the cheapest paid entries in the CRM market. It includes visual sales pipelines, 20 basic workflows, custom reports, two-way email sync with open and click tracking, built-in phone, and Freddy AI lead scoring. For a team that lives on the phone and needs a pipeline, a phone system and some lead scoring in one login, Growth is a genuinely strong offer.

Growth's limits are where you need to look. Sales sequences — automated multi-touch outreach — are not included on Growth at all; they start on Pro at 10 per user. Multiple pipelines, predictive scoring, sales goals, advanced workflows, and reportedly API access all require the jump to Pro at $39 per user per month billed annually — a 4.3x jump, and the features most sales teams call essential are precisely the ones it gates. Freshsales also lists a free plan for up to 3 users with contact management, built-in phone and live chat, though it is limited (no activity dashboard, no basic custom fields, no sequences), and one reviewer found it absent from the live pricing cards — verify it on the Freshworks pricing page before counting on it. If free is the deciding factor, our roundup of the best free CRMs for startups compares the genuinely usable free tiers side by side.

The other Freshsales-specific number is phone credits. Built-in telephony with call recording is the product's signature feature, but the calls themselves cost credits — typically a few cents per minute for local calls, with international rates varying. A team making hundreds of calls a week should price the credits, not just the seats. Add-ons stack too: branded documents run $19 per user per month and the Freddy AI Agent costs $49 per 100 sessions.

Two rows decide most evaluations. If your reps make outbound calls all day, Freshsales' native phone is a genuine structural advantage — no integration to buy, no separate calling seat. If your funnel starts with content, forms and email nurture, HubSpot's free marketing tooling and its upgrade path into Marketing Hub are the equivalent advantage.

Which is easier to set up?

Both vendors market themselves as small-team friendly, and both deliver — with different caveats. Freshsales is the quicker start for a pure sales team: import contacts, connect email, set up one pipeline, and reps can call from day one. There is less to misconfigure because the product does less.

HubSpot is the quicker start for a team that needs forms, live chat and email marketing alongside the CRM, because all of it is in one account from the beginning. But the platform's breadth becomes its onboarding cost: as you add hubs, you inherit more settings and more decisions about data hygiene. Note too that HubSpot's free and Starter tiers limit customization of workflows, reports and email templates.

The practical difference is migration, not setup. Teams that start on HubSpot's free tier and outgrow it face the Professional cliff; teams that start on Freshsales Growth and need sequences face the 4.3x jump to Pro. In both cases, the cheapest honest move is to trial the tier you expect to end on, not the tier you start on.

Hidden costs neither vendor puts in the headline

  • HubSpot's onboarding fees. Professional carries a mandatory $1,500 one-time fee; Enterprise carries $3,500. Freshsales has no equivalent fee, which makes HubSpot's mid-tier jump steeper than the per-seat number suggests.
  • HubSpot's Marketing Hub contact pricing. If you add marketing, you pay seats plus a contact-tier fee that grows with your database. Budget both axes or the bill surprises you.
  • Freshsales' phone credits. Calling costs credits — a few cents per minute for local calls, with international rates varying. Multiply by your call volume before comparing per-seat prices.
  • Freshsales' add-ons. Branded documents ($19 per user per month) and the Freddy AI Agent ($49 per 100 sessions) sit outside the plan prices.
  • HubSpot's promotional Starter pricing. HubSpot has run prolonged promotions near $7 per seat against a ~$20 list rate. Promotions end; budget the list rate for year two.
  • Freshsales' reporting ceiling. Forecasting insights are gated behind Enterprise, and reviewers rate the reporting as adequate, not excellent. If your leadership runs on custom reports, the cost of switching later is a real hidden cost.

Our verdict: which CRM for your team

Choose HubSpot if

  • Your team is 2 to 5 people and the free CRM covers you today, with marketing or service tools likely within a year.
  • Your funnel starts with content, forms and email nurture rather than cold calls.
  • You are already using HubSpot's free marketing tools and want one database, not two.
  • You need 1,000+ integrations or the deepest reporting a small team can get without enterprise software.

Choose Freshsales if

  • Your reps spend the day on the phone and want calling, email and the pipeline in one login.
  • Your budget is tight and Growth's $9 per user covers your actual needs — verify sequences are not among them.
  • You want AI lead scoring without paying for a mid-tier plan.
  • You are a solo consultant or tiny team that wants to be productive in hours, not days.

There is no wrong answer here, only a wrong price comparison. A tiny team on a budget should pick Freshsales Growth — the cheapest working CRM in this comparison, provided you do not need sequences. A growing sales team running outbound cadences should compare Freshsales Pro ($39/user) against HubSpot Professional ($90/seat plus onboarding) and pick based on whether the phone system or the platform ecosystem matters more. A team already inside HubSpot's ecosystem should stay: the integration value almost always exceeds the seat savings. Frequently asked questions

Is Freshsales cheaper than HubSpot for a small team?

At entry level, yes. Freshsales Growth costs $9 per user per month billed annually, while HubSpot Sales Hub Starter lists around $20 per seat per month (promotions near $7). But most sales teams need Freshsales Pro ($39/user/month) or HubSpot Professional ($90/seat/month) for sequences and real automation — compare the mid tiers, not the entry prices.

Does Freshsales have a free plan?

Freshsales lists a free plan for up to 3 users with contact management, built-in phone and live chat. It is limited: no activity dashboard, no basic custom fields and no sales sequences. One reviewer found it missing from the live pricing cards, so verify it on the Freshworks pricing page before relying on it.

Can I make calls from inside Freshsales?

Yes. Freshsales has a built-in phone on every plan including free, with call recording and local presence dialing. The calls themselves cost credits: local calls typically run a few cents per minute, with international rates varying by country.

Why does HubSpot get expensive so fast?

HubSpot's free CRM and Starter tier cover core contact and deal tracking, but sequences, forecasting, conversation intelligence and real custom reporting sit behind Sales Hub Professional at $90 per seat per month plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee. That cliff moves a 5-seat team from a few hundred dollars a year to roughly $6,900 in year one.

Which is better for a team that is already in the HubSpot ecosystem?

HubSpot, almost always. It shares one CRM object model across Marketing Hub, Service Hub and Content Hub, so existing forms, email and live chat keep their data and automation in one place. Moving to Freshsales to save on seats means rebuilding those workflows, which usually wipes out the savings.

Conclusion

HubSpot vs Freshsales for small teams is really a question about what your team does all day. Phone-first sellers on a budget get more from Freshsales Growth's $9 seats and built-in calling than from anything HubSpot offers at that price. Teams building a marketing-and-sales engine in one system get more from HubSpot's free tier and platform, provided they budget for the Professional cliff instead of the Starter headline. Trial the tier you expect to end on and price the seats plus the add-ons — the decision mostly makes itself.
