Mailchimp vs Klaviyo for ecommerce: pricing compared

  • Customer Support
  • Published Oct 17, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 9 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For ecommerce, Klaviyo and Mailchimp cost almost the same at small list sizes — $20/month at 500 contacts, $60 at 2,500, $100 at 5,000 — but diverge as you grow: at 10,000 contacts Mailchimp Standard is $135 vs Klaviyo $150; at 25,000 it's $270 vs $400. The real cost difference is billing philosophy: Mailchimp charges for every contact including unsubscribes, while Klaviyo bills only active profiles. Klaviyo includes full automation (abandoned cart, flows, predictive analytics) on every paid plan; Mailchimp gates real automation behind Standard ($20+/mo).

At 2,500 contacts or fewer, the two platforms cost exactly the same for email — the pricing war only starts at 10,000 contacts, where Mailchimp Standard undercuts Klaviyo by $15/month, widening to a $130/month gap at 25,000.

Three things the table doesn't show. First, Mailchimp also has an Essentials tier ($13/mo at 500 contacts, $75 at 5,000) — cheaper, but its automation is capped at 4-step flows, which rules it out for serious ecommerce. That's why this comparison uses Standard as the realistic Mailchimp baseline. Second, Mailchimp Premium is a flat $350/month from 500 through 10,000 contacts — absurd at 500 contacts, but it becomes competitive with Klaviyo only at very large lists. Third, both offer free plans (250 contacts/profiles, 500 sends a month), which are genuinely useful for testing but too small to run a store on.

Send limits and overage behavior

Price is only half the bill. Both platforms cap monthly sends as a multiple of your contact count, and both bill overages instead of cutting you off — which means a heavy campaign month can surprise you.

In practice, a 10–12× multiple is generous: a store emailing its full list 3–4 times a week uses roughly 12–16 sends per contact monthly. You'll hit the contact bracket wall long before the send wall on either platform. One quirk worth knowing on Klaviyo: crossing a profile bracket bumps your price immediately, even mid-cycle — there's no penalty for sitting near the top of your current range, but growth right after a bracket jump stings.

The hidden cost: how contacts are counted

This is the single biggest real-world cost difference, and it's buried in the fine print:

  • Mailchimp bills for every contact in your audience — including unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts. If 2,000 of your 10,000 contacts unsubscribed, you're still paying the 10,000-contact rate until you manually archive or delete them.
  • Klaviyo bills only for active profiles — contacts you can actually message. Suppressed profiles (unsubscribes, hard bounces, manual suppressions) cost nothing. One catch: once you unsuppress a profile, you can't re-suppress it for 90 days, so it stays billable for at least three months.

A store with a 10,000-contact Mailchimp list and a typical 20–30% inactive rate may be paying for 2,000–3,000 ghosts — roughly $25–$40/month at the $135 tier — that Klaviyo would charge $0 for.

List hygiene is therefore a cost lever on Mailchimp, not just a deliverability best practice. Budget 30 minutes a month to archive dead contacts, or the bracket creep will do it for you — upward.

Which is easier to leave

Switching email platforms is never free — the cost is your time rebuilding flows, templates, and segments, plus a 2–4 week deliverability warm-up on the new sender. But the two vendors differ in friction:

  • Leaving Mailchimp for Klaviyo is the well-trodden path: Klaviyo offers free migration tools and onboarding support, and its Shopify integration imports customers, products, and order history cleanly. Your Mailchimp flows don't transfer — you'll rebuild them, which is also a chance to fix the 4-step-limited sequences Essentials forced on you.
  • Leaving Klaviyo for Mailchimp is straightforward technically (CSV exports, API), but Klaviyo's Premium-tier equivalent costs less than Mailchimp Premium at most list sizes, so the financial incentive usually points the other way.
  • The lock-in nobody mentions: Klaviyo's analytics, predictive models, and segment history don't export. If you've spent a year training its predictions on your store, that institutional knowledge stays behind.

Bottom line: migration costs are measured in weeks of your time either way, not vendor fees. Pick the platform you'll still want in two years.

Deliverability notes

Both platforms maintain strong deliverability infrastructure — shared IPs with good reputations, authentication (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) setup wizards, and deliverability dashboards. The differences are at the margins:

  • Klaviyo shows a per-account deliverability score and offers dedicated IPs on higher/custom tiers — worth considering past ~100,000 contacts, where shared-IP neighbors can affect you. It also enforces stricter list hygiene by design (suppressed profiles are free, so there's no incentive to keep dead weight).
  • Mailchimp has mature deliverability tooling and a longer track record, but its shared-IP pools serve an enormous long tail of tiny senders, and its billing model (paying for unsubscribes) subtly discourages the list cleaning that protects your sender reputation.
  • Either way, your practices matter more than the platform: double opt-in, sunset unengaged contacts after 6–12 months, and warm up new sending domains gradually.

Who should pick which

Pick Klaviyo if email and SMS drive (or will drive) a meaningful share of store revenue — roughly 20%+ is the common benchmark. Its deep Shopify/BigCommerce/WooCommerce integrations, unlimited flows on every tier, and predictive analytics pay for the premium once automation is doing real work: abandoned cart sequences, post-purchase flows, win-back campaigns, and VIP segmentation. Stores doing $50k+/year online are Klaviyo's sweet spot.

Pick Mailchimp if you need polished broadcast campaigns with light automation — newsletters, promotions, basic abandoned cart — without the learning curve. It's easier to learn, has better built-in templates, and Standard undercuts Klaviyo from 10,000 contacts up. Creators and content-led stores should also compare Mailchimp vs ConvertKit for creators before deciding. Why Mailchimp

  • Cheaper at 10,000–25,000 contacts on Standard ($135–$270 vs $150–$400)
  • Easier to learn; stronger templates and campaign polish out of the box
  • Abandoned cart and product recommendations on every paid plan
  • No dedicated-IP or deliverability expertise needed to get started

Why Klaviyo

  • Full automation suite on every paid tier — no feature gating by plan
  • Bills only active profiles; suppressed contacts are free
  • Deepest ecommerce integrations (Shopify, BigCommerce, WooCommerce) with catalog feeds
  • Predictive analytics and segmentation built for revenue, not just opens

Frequently asked questions

Is Klaviyo cheaper than Mailchimp for ecommerce?

At 5,000 contacts or fewer, they cost the same for email ($20–$100/month). From 10,000 contacts up, Mailchimp Standard is cheaper ($135 vs $150 at 10k; $270 vs $400 at 25k). But Mailchimp bills for unsubscribed contacts while Klaviyo bills only active profiles, so Klaviyo is often cheaper in practice for lists with typical inactive rates.

Does Mailchimp include abandoned cart emails?

Yes — abandoned cart emails are included on all Mailchimp paid plans, including Essentials ($13/mo). However, Essentials limits automation flows to 4 steps; multi-step post-purchase and win-back sequences require Standard ($20/mo and up).

How much does Klaviyo SMS cost on top of email?

Klaviyo's Email + SMS plan adds roughly $15/month at small list sizes: $35/month at 500 contacts (vs $20 email-only), $75 at 2,500, $115 at 5,000, and $165 at 10,000. The SMS allowance is a base credit pool (1,250 credits/month); heavy SMS senders pay per-message overages.

Can I migrate from Mailchimp to Klaviyo for free?

Yes — Klaviyo provides free migration tools and onboarding support, and its Shopify integration imports customers, products, and order history. The real cost is your time: flows and templates must be rebuilt (2–4 weeks typically), plus a deliverability warm-up period on the new sender.

Which is better for a Shopify store: Mailchimp or Klaviyo?

Klaviyo, for most revenue-focused stores. Its Shopify integration is deeper (real-time catalog feeds, predictive analytics, unlimited flows on every tier), and it was built specifically for ecommerce. Mailchimp is the better pick if you mainly send polished newsletters and promotions with light automation, or if you're highly price-sensitive above 10,000 contacts.

Conclusion

Below 5,000 contacts, Mailchimp Standard and Klaviyo Email cost exactly the same — so choose on features, not price: Klaviyo if you want full automation from day one, Mailchimp if you want the easiest polished campaigns. Above 10,000 contacts, Mailchimp Standard wins on sticker price, but audit your list first: if 20–30% of your contacts are unsubscribed ghosts you're paying for, Klaviyo's active-profile billing may erase the gap entirely. And Klaviyo isn't the only rival worth pricing: our ActiveCampaign vs Klaviyo cost comparison shows the gap between those two is U-shaped — ActiveCampaign wins at 1k and 50k contacts, Klaviyo in the middle.
