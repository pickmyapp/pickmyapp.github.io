HubSpot vs Pipedrive for Small Teams (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Oct 9, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most small teams, pick HubSpot if sales and marketing will share one system — its free CRM is the cheapest start in the industry, and Starter adds the basics for about $20 per seat per month. Pick Pipedrive if your team is purely sales-led — the pipeline view is simpler, email sequences and automation arrive on the $39 Growth plan, and pricing has no marketing-contact meter. The catch reviews skip: HubSpot's free tier caps at 2 users, so a 5-person team compares paid plans either way, and automation included in Pipedrive Growth costs $100/seat on HubSpot Professional.

HubSpot and Pipedrive are the two CRMs small teams most often shortlist, and they are built from opposite ends of the problem. HubSpot is an all-in-one customer platform where sales is one hub among marketing, service, and content tools. Pipedrive is a sales-only CRM designed around a visual deal pipeline. That difference — platform versus pipeline — decides nearly everything: how pricing scales, which features you get at each tier, and which team will feel at home. This guide compares both for teams of roughly 2 to 20 people, using vendor pricing pages checked in October 2026. If a genuinely free plan is your priority, start with our best free CRM for startups guide first.

What is HubSpot CRM?

HubSpot CRM is the free core of HubSpot's Customer Platform, a system of record for contacts, companies, deals, and tickets that grows into paid sales, marketing, service, and content tools. The company built its name on inbound marketing, so the CRM is unusually generous for marketing-adjacent work: forms, live chat, and email tracking live on the free tier, and contacts, deals, and tasks never expire. Paid tiers are sold as "Hubs" — Sales Hub for outreach and pipeline management, Marketing Hub for campaigns, Service Hub for support — each priced separately.

That hub structure is both the strength and the trap. A two-person startup can genuinely run on the free CRM for months, adding hubs as it grows without ever migrating data. But the bill has two axes: Sales Hub is priced per seat, while Marketing Hub is priced by marketing-contact tier, so a team that grows its list and its headcount sees costs climb from two directions at once. HubSpot also distinguishes between Core Seats (general CRM editing across hubs), Sales Seats (sequences, forecasting, playbooks), and free unlimited view-only seats — so not every employee needs a paid seat, but the seats that do sales work cost more.

What is Pipedrive?

Pipedrive is a sales-first CRM built around a drag-and-drop visual pipeline, designed so a rep can see every deal, its value, and its next action on one screen. Founded in Estonia and popular with outbound and SMB sales teams, it uses "activity-based selling": every deal shows the next scheduled activity, so nothing sits still without someone noticing. The company says more than 100,000 sales teams use it, and its marketplace covers over 500 apps.

Plan names changed: Pipedrive rebranded its tiers to Lite, Growth, Premium, and Ultimate, replacing the old Essential, Advanced, Professional, Power, and Enterprise names. Older reviews that mention "Essential" or "Advanced" are describing the same plans — Lite is the old Essential, Growth is Advanced, Premium is Professional, and Ultimate merges the old Power and Enterprise. This guide uses the current names.

Pipedrive does not try to be a marketing suite. Lead capture exists through paid add-ons like LeadBooster and Campaigns, but email marketing, landing pages, and content tools are not core to the product. What it does exceptionally well is the daily life of a small sales team: logging calls, scheduling follow-ups, moving deals across stages, and reporting on who is doing what. If your team sells by phone, email, and meeting — not by content and campaigns — that focus keeps the tool lean.

How does HubSpot pricing compare with Pipedrive pricing?

The headline prices look close until you count what a real small team needs. HubSpot charges per seat per hub, and its free CRM caps at 2 users. Pipedrive charges per user per plan, with no free tier at all — only a 14-day trial. The features a five-person sales team relies on (email sequences, workflow automation, custom reporting) live on Pipedrive's $39 Growth plan but require HubSpot's $100-per-seat Professional plan, which also carries a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee and a 5-seat minimum.

What does a real small team pay?

Run the numbers at five users, billed annually. On HubSpot Starter that is about $100 per month (less with a promo). On Pipedrive Growth that is $195 per month. On Pipedrive Lite it is $70 per month. So HubSpot wins at entry level — with a caveat. Pipedrive Growth includes email sequences and workflow automation that HubSpot gates behind Professional: five Professional seats cost $450 per month plus the $1,500 onboarding fee. If your team will not touch automation for a year, HubSpot Starter is the cheaper path. If you need sequences on day one, Pipedrive Growth costs less than half of HubSpot Professional.

Also watch the second billing axis. HubSpot's Marketing Hub starts around $20 per seat at Starter but jumps to $890 per month at Professional, priced by marketing-contact tier. A team that grows its email list to a few thousand contacts can find its marketing bill rising independently of headcount. Pipedrive has no marketing-contact meter — but it also has no real marketing tools, so that comparison cuts both ways.

How do the features compare?

Pipeline and deal management

Pipedrive's pipeline is the product's signature feature and still the best pure deal board of the two. Deals are cards you drag across stages, each card shows its value and its next scheduled activity, and the "roten" view flags deals that have gone quiet. HubSpot's board view is solid — filters, stage automation, deal tags — but the pipeline is one object among many, and the contact record is the home screen most users live on. For a team whose day is moving deals, Pipedrive's focus shows.

Email, outreach, and automation

This is where the tiers matter most. Pipedrive Growth ($39) includes full email sync, templates, bulk email with open and click tracking, and multi-step nurture sequences. HubSpot Starter ($20) includes email tracking and simple sequences, but the real workflow automation — branching logic, enrollment triggers, lead rotation — waits for Professional ($100). Small teams that run outbound sequences should price the tools by the automation they need, not the headline seat price. On the free tier, HubSpot includes 200 email-tracking notifications a month and 2,000 branded email sends, while Pipedrive's trial is time-boxed, so nothing is free for long.

Marketing tools

HubSpot is in a different league here. Landing pages, forms, live chat, email campaigns, and ad management exist on the free and Starter tiers, and Marketing Hub adds the full campaign toolkit. Pipedrive's answer is the Campaigns add-on (priced separately per month) plus LeadBooster for chatbots and lead forms. A startup that generates leads through content and inbound forms gets that entire machine with HubSpot; on Pipedrive it pieces together third-party tools. If your lead flow starts with a blog post or a newsletter, this is usually the deciding factor. Our Mailchimp vs ConvertKit comparison covers dedicated email platforms for teams that want best-of-breed marketing instead.

Reporting and AI

Pipedrive Growth includes forecast and subscription reports plus an AI report builder that generates dashboards from text prompts; Premium adds custom reports, revenue forecasting, and an AI sales assistant. Pipedrive's newer Nova meeting-intelligence tool also generates pre-meeting briefs, call transcripts, and ready-to-approve CRM updates after calls. HubSpot Starter is limited to standard dashboards — custom reports need Professional, where you get up to 100 custom reports plus Breeze AI features across the platform. For a small team, Pipedrive's mid-tier reporting is the better value; HubSpot's AI is broader but paywalled higher.

Which is easier for a small team to set up?

Setup and learning curve

Pipedrive is the faster start. Most small teams import contacts, define their deal stages, and run a live pipeline in an afternoon — the product assumes you sell deals through stages and optimizes everything around that. HubSpot's free CRM is also quick to start, but the moment you add a second hub or configure Sales Hub automation, setup becomes a project. The distinction the seat model introduces (Core Seats versus Sales Seats) is sensible once learned but confusing in the first week, and several pricing reviewers in 2026 flagged exactly that as a budgeting headache.

Daily use and administration

Both are modern, fast, and well-designed on web and mobile — this is not a case of one pretty tool and one ugly one. Pipedrive's admin surface stays small because the product is smaller: adding a user, changing a pipeline stage, or building a report rarely requires a settings hunt. HubSpot's admin is deeper because there is more product: permissions, deal tags, contact tiers, and hub-level settings live in different corners. For a team without a dedicated operations person, Pipedrive needs less caretaking. For a team that will hire a marketing hire and a sales hire within the year, HubSpot's depth is the point.

How do the integrations compare?

HubSpot's ecosystem is roughly three times the size: the company lists more than 1,500 native integrations, including deep ties to Gmail, Outlook, Slack, Zoom, Stripe, and Shopify, plus a marketplace of third-party apps built on its API. Pipedrive's marketplace covers 500+ apps and handles the essentials most small sales teams need — email, calendar, calling, accounting, and marketing tools — but niche or regional tools are likelier to be missing, which means a Zapier or Make bridge for the odd integration. If automation connects your CRM to the rest of your stack, our Zapier alternatives roundup shows how to wire either CRM into other tools without Zapier's price.

Quick fact: HubSpot's unlimited view-only seats are free, so finance, founders, or advisors can watch the pipeline without a paid seat. Pipedrive charges for every user who logs in, including read-only viewers.

Which should you choose: HubSpot or Pipedrive?

Choose HubSpot if

  • Your leads come from content, forms, or inbound marketing that sales should see in the same system.
  • You want a free start and plan to add marketing or service tools within the next year or two.
  • Your team is 2 people today and you value a platform that scales to 20 without a data migration.
  • You want unlimited free view-only seats for founders or advisors.

Choose Pipedrive if

  • Your team sells outbound — calls, emails, meetings — and lives in the deal pipeline all day.
  • You need email sequences and workflow automation at $39 per seat, not $100.
  • You want the simplest admin overhead and no second billing axis for marketing contacts.
  • Your team is 3 to 10 people and wants predictable per-user pricing.

One practical tip: trial both against the same workflow. Import 50 real contacts into each, build your actual deal stages, and run one real sequence for a week. Pipedrive's 14-day trial and HubSpot's free tier make that comparison free, and a week of real usage tells you more than any review. Frequently asked questions

Is Pipedrive free?

No. Pipedrive has no free plan — only a 14-day free trial. Paid plans start at $14 per user per month billed annually on the Lite plan (formerly Essential), rising to $79 on Ultimate. If you need a genuinely free CRM for a small team, HubSpot's free tier or Zoho CRM's free tier are the better options.

Why did Pipedrive rename its plans?

Pipedrive rebranded its tiers to Lite, Growth, Premium, and Ultimate, replacing the old Essential, Advanced, Professional, Power, and Enterprise names. The tiers map one-to-one: Lite is the old Essential, Growth is Advanced, Premium is Professional, and Ultimate combines the old Power and Enterprise. Older reviews using the old names are describing the same plans.

Is HubSpot CRM really free for a small team?

Only for very small teams. HubSpot's free CRM is free forever but caps at 2 users and 1,000 contacts, with one deal pipeline, HubSpot branding on emails, and no automation. A team of three or more must buy paid seats, so model the cost at your real team size before assuming "free."

Which is cheaper for a 5-person team: HubSpot or Pipedrive?

HubSpot is usually cheaper at the entry level: five Starter seats cost about $100 per month, versus $195 per month for five Pipedrive Growth seats (annual billing). But Pipedrive Growth includes email sequences and sales automation that HubSpot only adds at Professional ($100 per seat per month), so the cheapest choice depends on which features your team actually needs.

Can I migrate from Pipedrive to HubSpot later?

Yes. Both tools have official import wizards that bring in contacts, companies, deals, and activity history from CSV files, and HubSpot offers a native Pipedrive integration for a direct import. The practical cost is cleanup time: field names, deal stages, and custom properties rarely map perfectly, so budget a day or two of data tidying for a small team.

Conclusion

Choose HubSpot when marketing and sales share one system — its free tier is the best on-ramp in the industry, and the platform grows with you without a migration. Choose Pipedrive when your team is sales-led and wants the cleanest pipeline per dollar, with sequences and automation at $39 a seat. Do not pick HubSpot's Professional plan for sales automation alone; at $100 per seat plus a $1,500 onboarding fee, it only makes sense when the marketing and service hubs pull their weight. And do not assume HubSpot is "free" for a five-person team — the 2-user cap means you are comparing paid plans either way, which is exactly why running the real math matters.
