Best Cheap Web Hosting for WordPress in 2026

  • Web Hosting
  • Published Oct 9, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

The cheapest reliable WordPress hosting in 2026 is Hostinger at $2.99 per month, but that rate requires a 48-month term and renews around $10.99. Bluehost ($3.99 intro, ~$9.99 renewal) is the best all-round pick for beginners with a free domain on every plan. SiteGround ($2.99 intro, ~$17.99 renewal) is the quality pick if support matters more than price. The surprise from our two-year cost math: Bluehost and DreamHost cost about the same as Hostinger over two years, because their renewal rates are lower.

WordPress powers over 40% of all websites, and for a new blog or small business site, cheap shared hosting is all you need to start. The problem is that most hosting guides rank providers by the big promotional price on the homepage and bury the renewal price in the fine print. This guide does it differently: every price below includes the renewal rate, checked in October 2026, and we show what two years actually cost. If you are still deciding between a website builder and WordPress, our Wix vs Squarespace comparison covers the no-code route first.

Hostinger: the cheapest intro price

Hostinger is the best cheap WordPress host if your priority is the lowest possible first-year cost. Its $2.99 per month intro rate is the cheapest headline price in the group, and its LiteSpeed servers deliver genuinely strong performance for the price. The hPanel control panel is widely considered the simplest in the industry, which beginners appreciate, and plans include a free domain for the first year on higher tiers, free SSL, a free CDN, and AI website tools including an AI builder.

The catches are the terms. The $2.99 rate requires a 48-month commitment paid up front, about $143.52. Backups are weekly on the entry plan rather than daily, and support is chat-only with no phone option. If you are comfortable committing for four years and chatting with support instead of calling, Hostinger is excellent value. If you might leave within two years, Bluehost and DreamHost match it on total cost with shorter commitments.

Bluehost: the beginner-friendly all-rounder

Bluehost is the safest pick for first-time WordPress users. It has been officially recommended by WordPress.org since 2005, it includes a free domain for the first year on every plan rather than just the higher tiers, and it bundles free SSL, a free CDN, and automatic WordPress installation. Its dashboard guides new users through setup, and higher plans add staging environments, AI site creation, and malware scanning.

At $3.99 per month intro with a renewal around $9.99, Bluehost has the lowest renewal price of the mainstream budget hosts, which is why it ties Hostinger on the two-year math. The main downsides are a busy checkout full of upsell add-ons, which you should decline, and performance that is solid rather than exceptional. Bluehost also offers phone support, which first-timers often prefer over chat-only help.

SiteGround: the quality pick with a price cliff

SiteGround is the best cheap host if support quality and reliability matter more to you than the lowest price. It runs on Google Cloud infrastructure, includes daily backups with 30-day history on all plans, free SSL, a free CDN, free email, and staging environments on its GrowBig plan and above. Its support consistently earns the highest satisfaction ratings in the industry, and phone support is available.

The trade-off is the renewal cliff: the $2.99 intro renews around $17.99 per month, roughly triple the renewal of Bluehost. The entry StartUp plan also allows only one website, so it does not suit anyone running multiple sites. SiteGround is the right choice for a growing business site where a fast answer from excellent support is worth paying for, but it is not the budget pick its intro price suggests.

More budget-friendly options

DreamHost

DreamHost is the quiet value pick. Its $2.89 per month intro is close to the cheapest on the market, it renews around $10.99, it includes 50 GB of storage, daily backups, and a free domain on annual plans. Independent reviewers consistently praise its uptime record. It lacks the brand recognition of the bigger names, but the two-year math puts it level with Hostinger and Bluehost.

InMotion Hosting

InMotion's WordPress plans start around $3.69 per month with 100 GB of NVMe storage and renew around $11.49. Its standout feature is the 90-day money-back guarantee on terms of six months or more, triple the industry-standard 30 days, which makes it the lowest-risk trial of the group. Support quality is well regarded, and it is a solid pick if you want a longer safety net before committing.

Features that actually matter in cheap hosting

Backups

Daily backups are the gold standard, and SiteGround and DreamHost include them. Hostinger and Bluehost offer weekly backups on entry plans, which is acceptable for a new site but means you could lose up to a week of changes if something breaks. Whatever host you choose, install a backup plugin as a second safety net.

SSL, CDN, and email

Free SSL is table stakes now: all five hosts include it. A free CDN is included by most of them and meaningfully speeds up sites for international visitors. Free email accounts are less consistent, with some hosts offering them only on higher plans or as paid add-ons, so check if you want a professional address at your domain.

Staging and migration

Staging lets you test changes before they go live. Bluehost includes staging on its entry plan, SiteGround gates full staging features to GrowBig and above, and Hostinger reserves it for higher tiers. On migrations, all five offer free migration tools or assisted transfers, so switching hosts later is easier than it used to be. For creators building an audience alongside their site, pairing solid hosting with affordable outreach matters: our cheapest email marketing guide and Mailchimp vs ConvertKit comparison cover that side.

Support and server technology

Support quality varies more than any other feature at this price. SiteGround's phone and chat support is the benchmark, Bluehost offers phone support on most plans, and Hostinger is chat-only. On raw technology, Hostinger's LiteSpeed servers and Bluehost's NVMe storage both deliver good speeds for shared hosting, while SiteGround's Google Cloud foundation and multi-level caching aim higher. For a new site, any of these is fast enough; the differences matter once traffic grows.

What to avoid in cheap hosting

  • Hosts with no free SSL or that charge extra for it. Browsers warn visitors away from non-HTTPS sites, and Google ranks them lower.
  • Vague renewal pricing. If you cannot find the renewal rate on the pricing page before checkout, assume it is high.
  • "Unlimited everything" plans with throttled CPU. Unlimited storage means little when the server slows your site under modest traffic.
  • Free hosts with ads or forced branding for any business site. They are fine for experiments, not for earning trust.
  • Locking a 48-month term for a site you may abandon. Cheap per month is not cheap if you leave in year one and cannot get a refund.

Who should pick which host?

Pick Hostinger if

  • You want the lowest possible first-year cost.
  • You are comfortable with a long term and chat-only support.
  • You value the simplest control panel and LiteSpeed speed.

Pick Bluehost if

  • You are setting up WordPress for the first time.
  • You want a free domain on every plan and phone support.
  • You care about the lowest renewal price and two-year cost.

Pick SiteGround if

  • Support quality and reliability matter more than price.
  • You want daily backups and staging as your site grows.
  • You run one site and can absorb the higher renewal.

If none of these fits, DreamHost is the quiet alternative with strong uptime and fair pricing, and InMotion's 90-day guarantee makes it the safest trial. Start cheap, and upgrade to managed WordPress hosting when your traffic or revenue justifies it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest reliable WordPress hosting in 2026?

Hostinger has the lowest intro price at $2.99 per month, but that rate requires a 48-month term and renews around $10.99. On a two-year total-cost view, Bluehost and DreamHost cost about the same, because their renewal rates are lower. For the lowest possible first-year spend, Hostinger wins; for the lowest two-year spend, compare the renewal column before buying.

Why do hosting prices jump so much after the first term?

Introductory prices are promotional rates for new customers, often two to four times lower than renewal rates. Hostinger renews around $10.99 from $2.99, and SiteGround renews around $17.99 from $2.99. Always check the renewal price on the pricing page before you commit, because that is what you will pay from year two or three onward.

Is Bluehost still officially recommended by WordPress?

Yes. Bluehost has been officially recommended by WordPress.org since 2005, one of only a few hosts with that status. For beginners who want a free domain on every plan, one-click WordPress installation, and phone support, it remains a natural choice.

Do I need shared hosting or managed WordPress hosting?

Shared hosting is the cheapest option and is fine for new blogs and small business sites. Managed WordPress hosting costs more but adds automatic updates, staging, better caching, and WordPress-specialist support. Start with shared hosting, and upgrade to managed hosting when your traffic or revenue justifies the cost.

Can I move my WordPress site to another host later?

Yes, and it is easier than most beginners expect. Hostinger, Bluehost, SiteGround, and DreamHost all offer free migration tools or assisted transfers, and plugins like Duplicator or All-in-One WP Migration can move a site between any hosts. Do not let fear of switching lock you into a host you have outgrown.

Conclusion

Cheap WordPress hosting is a solved problem in 2026, but the headline prices are misleading. Hostinger wins the first-year sprint, Bluehost wins on renewals and beginner friendliness, and SiteGround wins on support and reliability at a premium. The single most useful thing you can do before buying is the two-year math we did above: the cheapest intro price rarely means the cheapest long-term cost. Pick the host that matches your budget horizon, check the renewal price before you pay, and remember that every host on this list will migrate you for free if you change your mind.
