Mailchimp vs ConvertKit for Creators (2026)

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 9, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 9 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most creators in 2026, Kit (formerly ConvertKit) is the better fit: it is free up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited broadcasts, prices by active subscribers only, and is built around newsletters, sequences, and selling digital products. Choose Mailchimp instead if you run an e-commerce or merch store — its templates, Shopify integration, abandoned-cart emails, and product recommendations are built for selling products, not growing audiences. Mailchimp and Kit (the platform renamed from ConvertKit in 2024) are the two email tools creators compare most. One grew up serving small businesses and e-commerce stores; the other was built from day one for newsletter writers, course sellers, and indie makers. Both can send a newsletter. The differences that matter are pricing logic, what "free" actually means, and which features each platform invests in. This guide is written for newsletter writers, bloggers, course creators, and indie makers comparing the two for audience growth and monetization, with all prices checked in October 2026. If budget is your main constraint, see our cheapest email marketing software roundup for the wider field.

How do Mailchimp and Kit pricing actually compare?

The two tools do not measure usage the same way, and that matters more than the headline prices. Mailchimp bills by stored contacts — including subscribed, unsubscribed, and non-subscribed contacts — while Kit bills by unique, active subscribers only. In practice, Mailchimp charges you for the list you have, dead weight included, and Kit charges you for the audience you actually email. That single rule quietly reshapes the whole comparison.

Why the sticker prices mislead

The $13 versus $39 entry prices look like a big gap until you line up what each buys. Mailchimp's $13 buys 500 contacts, not 1,000, and it keeps billing you for people who unsubscribed years ago unless you archive them manually. Kit's $39 buys 1,000 active subscribers with no dead weight. Now look at the other end: at 10,000 subscribers, Kit's Creator plan costs $139 a month and Mailchimp Standard costs about $135. The prices converge as you grow — what differs is what each platform counts and includes.

Quick fact: If 30% of your contacts are unsubscribed, Mailchimp still bills you for all of them. On a 10,000-contact list, that is 3,000 people you are paying for and can never email again. Kit excludes them by definition.

Which grows more predictably?

Kit's pricing moves in published subscriber tiers and only counts people who can actually receive your emails, which makes budgeting simple as a newsletter grows. Mailchimp's price moves with total contacts, so a re-engagement campaign that fails — or a list import you never clean — raises your bill. Mailchimp also caps monthly sends at roughly 10x your contact count on Essentials and 12x on Standard, which is generous, but a daily newsletter to a large list can approach it. Kit's paid plans do not meter sends the same way; broadcasts are effectively unlimited.

Free plans: the biggest gap in email marketing

This is the most lopsided free-plan comparison in the email marketing world. Kit's free Newsletter plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, signup forms, and broadcasts. Mailchimp's free plan covers 250 contacts and 500 sends a month, with a daily cap of 250 sends, no multi-step automation, and no scheduling.

For a creator starting from zero, the difference is practical, not theoretical. On Kit you can grow a real newsletter to thousands of subscribers and multiple broadcasts a week before spending a dollar. On Mailchimp you will hit the ceiling within months — or weeks if a post goes viral. Kit has trimmed automation from the free plan over time (welcome sequences and multi-step automations now need the Creator plan), so the free tier is best understood as a generous runway for sending and list-building, with monetization and automation arriving when you upgrade. Built for creators vs built for e-commerce

Each platform invests its engineering in its core customer. Kit builds for the creator economy: newsletter writers, bloggers, course sellers, and indie makers. Mailchimp builds for small businesses and online stores.

What Kit gives creators

Kit's core is a visual automation builder designed around subscriber journeys: welcome sequences, tag-based branching, and unlimited email sequences on the Creator plan. Around that sit creator-native features Mailchimp does not prioritize — a built-in commerce layer for selling digital products directly to subscribers, paid-newsletter support, a newsletter referral system (on Creator Pro) that turns subscribers into promoters, subscriber scoring to spot your most engaged readers, and a creator-network recommendation feature that grows your list through cross-promotion with other Kit users. Kit also migrates your list from another platform for free on paid plans, which lowers the cost of switching.

What Mailchimp gives sellers

Mailchimp's center of gravity is commerce. Its Shopify integration, abandoned-cart emails, order notifications, and personalized product recommendations on every paid plan are aimed at stores. The Customer Journey Builder offers multi-step automations with generative-AI assistance and predictive segmentation (send-time optimization and predicted demographics) on Standard and above. If you are a creator who also runs a merch store, that combination is genuinely useful. If you sell attention — newsletters, courses, community — you are paying for a commerce engine you will barely use.

Automation and sequences

Both platforms automate, but with different depth and different paywalls. On Kit's Creator plan, visual automations and email sequences are unlimited — welcome series, onboarding flows, and evergreen sequences run without caps. On Mailchimp, automation depth is gated by plan: Essentials limits customer journeys to short flows, while Standard and Premium unlock multi-step automations and advanced branching.

In practice, creators tend to live in sequences — a welcome series that runs for every new subscriber, an onboarding flow for a course, an evergreen launch. Kit treats those as the product's core and prices them flat within the plan. Mailchimp treats automation as a premium feature tied to its e-commerce workflows. If your plan is "send a weekly newsletter plus a welcome series," Kit's Creator plan covers it completely. On Mailchimp, the same setup pushes you from Essentials to Standard.

Design and templates

Mailchimp wins the design round on paper: a large template library, a polished drag-and-drop editor, and campaign previews that look like a small agency built them. Kit's emails are deliberately plain — text-first, minimal styling. That is not a missing feature; it is a philosophy. Plain-text-style emails feel personal, land in the primary inbox more reliably, and get replies, which is why most successful newsletters look like a note from a friend.

For landing pages and signup forms, both are capable: Kit offers unlimited landing pages and forms on every plan including free, and Mailchimp bundles landing pages with its site builder. Creators who want a beautiful standalone website alongside their newsletter should also compare Wix vs Squarespace for small business, since either email tool plugs into both.

Deliverability and support

Both platforms maintain strong deliverability infrastructure — shared IPs with reputation monitoring, authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) setup, and bounce handling. Newsletter-style plain-text emails tend to have a slight practical edge in inbox placement over image-heavy templates, which quietly favors Kit's default style, though list hygiene matters more than platform choice for either.

On support, Mailchimp offers 24/7 support from the Essentials plan up and phone support on Premium. Kit offers community support on the free plan, 24/7 support on Creator, and priority support on Creator Pro. Neither has a free plan with real human support, which is standard in this industry. Which should you choose: Mailchimp or Kit?

Choose Kit if

  • You are newsletter-first: a writer, blogger, or indie creator growing an audience.
  • You want the most generous free start — up to 10,000 subscribers before paying.
  • You sell digital products, courses, or paid newsletters.
  • You refuse to pay for unsubscribed contacts inflating your bill.
  • You want unlimited sequences and automations on one plan.

Choose Mailchimp if

  • You run an e-commerce or merch store and need abandoned-cart and product emails.
  • You want polished, design-heavy campaigns from a big template library.
  • You value a mature Shopify integration and predictive segmentation.
  • You want 24/7 support on the cheapest paid tier.
  • You need an all-in-one marketing suite beyond email.

Try before you commit: Kit's free plan lets you build a real list first, and Mailchimp's free plan (or the 14-day trials on paid plans) is enough to test the builder. Frequently asked questions

Is Kit really free for up to 10,000 subscribers?

Yes, with caveats. Kit's free Newsletter plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcasts. But Kit has trimmed automation from the free plan, so welcome sequences and multi-step automations require the Creator plan, which starts at $39 per month for 1,000 subscribers.

Is Mailchimp cheaper than Kit?

The sticker price is lower — Mailchimp Essentials starts at $13 per month versus Kit Creator at $39 — but the comparison is not like-for-like. Mailchimp's $13 buys 500 contacts and it bills for unsubscribed contacts too, while Kit's $39 buys 1,000 active subscribers with dead weight excluded. At 10,000 subscribers, Kit Creator ($139/month) and Mailchimp Standard (about $135/month) cost almost the same.

Can I move my list from Mailchimp to Kit?

Yes. Export your contacts as a CSV from Mailchimp, import into Kit, and Kit will migrate your list for free on the Creator and Creator Pro plans. Archive your Mailchimp audience before importing so unsubscribed contacts do not inflate anything, and plan to rebuild automations since there is no one-click importer.

Which is better for selling digital products?

Kit, because it has a built-in commerce layer for selling digital products directly to subscribers, plus paid-newsletter support. Mailchimp is stronger for physical products and merch: its Shopify integration, abandoned-cart emails, and product recommendations are built for e-commerce stores.

Which is better for a plain newsletter?

Kit. Its emails are intentionally text-first, which reads as personal and tends to get replies and engagement. It also offers subscriber tagging, segments, a newsletter referral system on the Pro plan, and cross-promotion with other Kit creators — features built specifically for newsletter growth.

Conclusion

  • Pricing: Mailchimp bills stored contacts including unsubscribes; Kit bills active subscribers only. Entry prices ($13 vs $39) are not like-for-like, and at 10,000 subscribers both cost about $135-139 a month.
  • Free plans: Kit's free tier (up to 10,000 subscribers) is the most generous in the industry; Mailchimp's (250 contacts, 500 sends) is a trial in disguise.
  • Features: Kit is built for newsletters, sequences, and digital products; Mailchimp is built for e-commerce, design-heavy campaigns, and predictive segmentation.
  • Verdict: Creators growing an audience should pick Kit. Creators running a store should pick Mailchimp.
