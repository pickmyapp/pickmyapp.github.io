Intercom vs Crisp for Small Business (2026)

  • Customer Support
  • Published Oct 11, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Intercom and Crisp both do live chat plus a helpdesk, but they bill like different species. Intercom charges per seat (Essential: $29 a month per seat on annual billing) plus usage-based AI — Fin AI costs $0.99 per resolution — which makes it powerful and expensive. Crisp charges per workspace (Mini: $45 a month flat for 4 seats; Essentials: $95 a month for 10 seats), which makes it the affordable pick at small scale. A 2-person team pays roughly $58 a month on Intercom vs $45 flat on Crisp; a 5-person team pays about $145 a month on Intercom vs $95 flat on Crisp — before any AI usage. Choose Crisp when budget and simplicity rule. Choose Intercom when you need best-in-class automation and AI for a growing support operation — and budget for Fin accordingly.

Small businesses choosing live chat software usually shortlist Intercom and Crisp for opposite reasons: Intercom is the premium platform everyone in SaaS knows, and Crisp is the budget-friendly one that keeps showing up in "Intercom alternative" searches. The comparison is harder than it looks because the headline prices hide the real decision — Intercom bills per seat plus per AI resolution, while Crisp bills a flat fee per workspace. This guide compares pricing plan by plan with worked costs for small teams, features head to head, and the AI story on both sides, using prices checked against vendor pricing pages and current third-party pricing research in October 2026. If you are comparing helpdesk platforms more broadly, see our Zendesk vs Freshdesk guide for startups.

What Intercom is

Intercom is a customer-messaging platform built for SaaS and digital businesses, combining live chat, a shared inbox, a help center, and its Fin AI agent with product-tour, onboarding-message, and survey tooling. It is the platform most associated with in-app messaging: the chat widget lives inside your product as naturally as on your marketing site, and its automation layer — workflows, routing rules, and Fin — is the deepest in the category.

Intercom's defining trait is its seat-based, usage-layered pricing. You pay per full agent seat, and AI automation is a separate metered line item: Fin AI costs $0.99 per resolution, billed only for questions the AI fully resolves. That structure makes Intercom scale elegantly for teams that can put a dollar value on a deflected ticket — and makes bills unpredictable for teams that cannot. There is no permanent free plan, only a 14-day free trial.

What Crisp is

Crisp is an all-in-one messaging platform, founded in France in 2015, that bundles live chat, a shared inbox, a chatbot builder, a knowledge base, co-browsing, and multi-channel support (email, Messenger, Twitter/X DMs, Telegram, WhatsApp, SMS) under one flat per-workspace price. It pitches itself squarely at startups and small businesses that want one central place for every customer conversation.

Crisp's defining trait is workspace pricing: you pay per workspace (roughly per website or brand), not per agent, and conversations are unlimited. The current plans are Free (2 seats, basic chat only), Mini ($45 a month, 4 seats), Essentials ($95 a month, 10 seats), and Plus ($295 a month, 20 seats with extra seats at $10 a month). Note that Crisp repriced upward during 2026 — older reviews still quote a $25 Pro and $95 Unlimited structure that no longer reflects the current page, so ignore those numbers.

The pricing models are opposites

Before comparing plan names, understand the billing philosophy, because it decides the winner at almost every team size. On Intercom, every human who answers chats is a paid seat, and every AI-resolved question is $0.99. On Crisp, the workspace price covers your seats and unlimited human-handled conversations; only AI-automated conversations draw from a metered credit balance.

That means Intercom rewards teams with few agents and high AI deflection — a 2-person team with Fin handling half the queue can feel cheap per conversation. Crisp rewards teams with many humans and moderate AI needs — a 6-person team on Essentials pays the same $95 whether they handle 500 or 5,000 chats. The right platform is the one whose billing unit matches your staffing shape. If you pair either tool with a CRM, our HubSpot vs Pipedrive comparison covers the small-team side of that stack.

Three things stand out. First, Crisp is cheaper at every small-team size before AI enters the picture — roughly 25-35% less. Second, Crisp's flat pricing means adding a sixth or seventh teammate costs nothing until you hit the seat cap, while Intercom adds $29 a head. Third, the AI line flips the comparison in a subtler way: Fin's $0.99 per resolution is expensive per unit but genuinely capable, while Crisp's included AI credits are modest (a few hundred automated conversations a month) and its AI is widely described as basic. A team automating seriously will burn through Crisp's credits and either top up or outgrow it.

How do live chat and the shared inbox compare?

The chat widget and inbox

Both offer a polished website chat widget and a shared team inbox with assignment, private notes, and canned responses — the table stakes of the category. Intercom's Messenger is the more refined product: richer in-app messaging, better visitor data, and deeper customization of the widget's behavior and appearance. Crisp's widget is simpler but covers the essentials well, and setup is genuinely one snippet — reviewers consistently call it the faster install.

Automation and routing

Intercom's workflow builder is the category benchmark: multi-step routing, round-robin assignment, behavior-triggered messages, and branching logic are all first-class, with the full version on Advanced. Crisp offers triggers, routing and assignment rules, reminders, and automated replies — solid for a small team, but less sophisticated than Intercom's engine. If your support operation runs on automation rules rather than human triage, Intercom's depth justifies its price.

Product messaging (Intercom's moat)

This is where Intercom pulls away for software businesses: product tours, in-app surveys, onboarding checklists, and targeted in-app messages (some via the $99-a-month Proactive Support Plus add-on) turn the messenger into a growth tool, not just a support channel. Crisp has no equivalent — its campaigns feature covers basic proactive messaging, but nothing like product tours. If you sell software and want the chat widget to onboard users, this alone can decide the comparison.

How do the helpdesk, knowledge base, and ticketing compare?

Both platforms pair chat with self-service. Intercom includes a public help center on every plan, with a private multilingual help center on Advanced and up; its ticketing is inbox-native rather than a separate ticket system. Crisp puts the knowledge base on the Essentials plan and adds a full ticketing system on Plus ($295), alongside a status page — handy for SaaS teams that want public incident communication without a separate tool.

The practical difference: Intercom's help center is the more mature product with better article management and deeper Fin integration (Fin answers from your help-center content). Crisp's knowledge base is perfectly serviceable for a small team's FAQ and docs, and the bundled status page is a nice touch Intercom does not match at any price. Neither replaces a dedicated helpdesk like Zendesk or Freshdesk for ticket-heavy operations — see our startup helpdesk comparison if tickets, not chats, are your main channel.

Fin AI vs Crisp AI: the real cost of automation

AI is the line item most likely to surprise you, so model it before you sign. Intercom's Fin is widely regarded as the strongest support AI in the category — trained on your help center and past conversations, resolving questions end-to-end — and it costs $0.99 per resolution on every plan. The math is simple and brutal: 500 AI-resolved questions a month adds $495; 2,000 adds $1,980. For a team where each deflected ticket saves $5-8 of agent time, that is still a bargain. For a team with low ticket value, it can exceed the seat bill.

Crisp's AI is bundled differently: each plan ships with a fixed AI-credit balance (reported around $5 on Mini, $25 on Essentials, $75 on Plus — roughly 90, 450, and 1,350 automated conversations respectively), and human-handled conversations are unlimited regardless. The catch, per multiple 2026 reviews, is capability: Crisp's AI is described as basic, with resolution rates plateauing early and limited training options compared to Fin. The honest framing: Intercom sells you expensive, capable AI; Crisp sells you cheap, basic AI with unlimited human chatting around it. Pick based on which side of that trade your ticket mix sits.

How does multichannel coverage compare?

Crisp wins on channel breadth per dollar. Out of the box it centralizes email, Facebook Messenger, Twitter/X DMs, Telegram, WhatsApp, and SMS alongside website chat — a small business can run its entire customer communication from one inbox at $45-95 a month. Intercom covers email, WhatsApp, and SMS too, but messaging channels beyond chat are usage-priced (SMS and WhatsApp are pay-per-use), and social DMs are not its strength.

Intercom wins on depth of ecosystem: a large app marketplace, robust APIs, and native integrations with the SaaS stack (CRMs, data warehouses, billing tools). Crisp integrates with the essentials — Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Slack (including reply-from-Slack), Zapier — which covers most small businesses, but enterprise integration depth is not its game. If your stack is a small-business CRM plus email marketing, Crisp's integrations suffice; pair either with our cheapest email marketing software guide for the full customer-communication stack.

Our verdict

Choose Crisp if

  • Budget is the constraint — flat per-workspace pricing beats per-seat pricing at every small-team size.
  • You want the simplest setup: one snippet, one inbox, all channels working the same day.
  • Your team answers chats with humans and needs basic automation, not an AI-first operation.
  • You need broad channel coverage (WhatsApp, Telegram, social DMs) at a flat price.

Choose Intercom if

  • You sell software and want product tours, onboarding messages, and in-app surveys alongside support.
  • Automation is the strategy — Fin AI and the workflow engine are the best in the category.
  • You can model and afford $0.99 per AI resolution because each deflected ticket saves real agent cost.
  • You need deep integrations, SSO, HIPAA, or multi-brand support (Advanced/Expert tiers).

Start with the free options to feel the difference: Crisp's free plan (2 seats, basic chat) is a genuine sandbox, and Intercom's 14-day trial shows you the full platform. If you are a 2-to-5-person team that mostly needs humans answering chats well, Crisp's $45-95 flat pricing is hard to argue with. If you are a SaaS business where support automation is a growth lever, Intercom earns its per-seat price — just model Fin's $0.99-per-resolution line before you commit, because that is where Intercom bills surprise people.

Frequently asked questions

Is Crisp cheaper than Intercom?

At small scale, usually yes. Crisp charges per workspace, not per seat: Mini is $45 a month for 4 seats and Essentials is $95 a month for 10 seats. Intercom charges per seat from $29 a month on annual billing, so 5 agents cost about $145 a month before any AI usage. The gap narrows as you grow — Crisp Plus is $295 a month — and Intercom Fin AI at $0.99 per resolution can add up fast on either platform. Prices checked October 2026.

Does Intercom have a free plan?

No. Intercom has no permanent free plan — only a 14-day free trial. Crisp does have a free tier: 2 seats with basic live chat, the team inbox, and mobile apps, but no triggers, no knowledge base, and no AI. If you need a free live chat to start with, Crisp's free plan is the only option between these two.

How much does Intercom's Fin AI actually cost?

Fin is priced separately from seats at $0.99 per resolution — each customer question the AI fully resolves costs about a dollar. It is available on every plan including Essential. That can be cheap (200 resolutions a month adds $198) or shocking (2,000 resolutions adds $1,980), which is why you should model your expected resolution volume before committing, not after.

Can a non-technical small business set up Crisp or Intercom alone?

Crisp is the easier self-serve setup: install one JavaScript snippet and the chat widget, shared inbox, and channels work out of the box. Intercom's basic messenger setup is also straightforward, but getting value from its workflows, Fin AI training, and integrations realistically takes a few days of configuration and is the kind of project where a small agency or Intercom's onboarding help pays for itself.

Which is better for a SaaS startup, Intercom or Crisp?

Intercom, for most SaaS startups. Its product-tour, onboarding-message, and in-app-survey tooling is built for software products, and Fin AI is the strongest support AI in the category. Crisp is the better pick when budget is the constraint, when the team is non-technical, or when you need broad channel coverage (WhatsApp, Telegram, Twitter DMs) at a flat price.

Conclusion

Intercom vs Crisp is really a question about what kind of support operation you run. Crisp is the small-business value pick: $45 to $95 a month flat for a whole team, unlimited human-handled conversations, every major messaging channel in one inbox, and a free tier to start on. Intercom is the growth pick: per-seat pricing from $29 a month that climbs with your team, but the best automation engine and the best support AI in the business for teams that can put a dollar value on a deflected ticket. The mistake to avoid is buying Intercom's power and using it as a dumb chat widget — or buying Crisp's simplicity and expecting Fin-level AI. Match the tool to the operation, model the AI line item in advance, and verify the current prices: both vendors repriced during 2026, and the number in an older review may already be stale.
