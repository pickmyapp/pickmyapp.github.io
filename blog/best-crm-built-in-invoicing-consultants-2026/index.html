Quick answer

A CRM with built-in invoicing lets a consultant run the whole client lifecycle — lead, proposal, contract, tracked hours, invoice, and payment — in one tool instead of stitching together a CRM, a time tracker, and an invoicing app. For most consultants in 2026, HoneyBook (from $29/month) is the best all-in-one pick because it combines proposals, contracts, invoicing, and payments in a single client-facing flow. FreshBooks (from $23/month) wins if invoicing and time tracking matter more than deal management, while Dubsado ($335/year) is the best value for automation-heavy consultants outside the US and Canada. If you want a genuine CRM plus real accounting, Zoho CRM + Zoho Books is the cheapest serious combo at roughly $20–$55 per user per month.

What a CRM with built-in invoicing actually does

Consultants live in two worlds: selling the work and getting paid for it. A traditional CRM handles the selling — contact records, deal pipelines, follow-up reminders — while a separate accounting or invoicing tool handles the money: quotes, invoices, time tracking, payment collection, tax reports. The problem is constant double entry: the client you just closed in the CRM becomes a customer record you recreate in the invoicing tool, and the quote you sent becomes an invoice you rebuild line by line.

A CRM with built-in invoicing collapses that into one flow: the client record, proposal, contract, tracked hours, invoice, and payment all share the same data. For a solo consultant or small practice, that means no double entry when a proposal becomes an invoice, no chasing payments across tools, and one login instead of three. The tradeoff is depth — these tools are rarely as strong as a dedicated CRM on sales or a dedicated ledger on accounting. This guide compares the six tools that strike the balance best for consultants in 2026, using current starter pricing, real processing fees, and honest notes on each tool's tradeoffs.

1. FreshBooks — the invoicing-first choice

FreshBooks is the tool consultants buy when invoicing is the job and a "CRM" mostly means "a list of clients who owe me money." It is not really a CRM at all — there is no deal pipeline or lead scoring — but it is the best invoicing engine on this list, and that matters more for many consultants than a contact database.

Pricing scales by billable clients, not seats — unusual and consultant-friendly. In 2026 the standard monthly rates are Lite at $23/month (up to 5 billable clients), Plus at $43/month (up to 50 clients), and Premium at $70/month (unlimited clients), with 10% off for annual billing. Unlimited invoices, estimates, time tracking, and expenses are included on every plan, even Lite. Plus adds proposals, client retainers, e-signatures, double-entry reports, and accountant access. Extra team members cost $11/month each.

The standout feature for consultants is the time-to-invoice flow: tracked hours land on an invoice without retyping, and retainer clients can be auto-charged from a card on file. Processing runs 2.9% + $0.30 per card transaction and 1% for ACH (capped at $10). If you bill hourly or on retainer, FreshBooks is the fastest path from work done to money collected.

FreshBooks includes unlimited invoices and unlimited time tracking on every plan, starting at $23/month — but client management is shallow, so consultants who need a real sales pipeline should pair it with a dedicated CRM like the ones compared in our guide to the best CRMs for freelancers and solo consultants.

2. HoneyBook — the all-in-one client experience

HoneyBook is built around one idea: the client should be able to go from your proposal to a signed contract to a paid invoice in a single link, on their phone, without emailing you a single question. For consultants who sell packaged engagements — audits, strategy sprints, advisory retainers — that frictionless flow is genuinely valuable.

Pricing in 2026 starts with Starter at $29/month, Essentials at $49/month billed annually, and Premium at $109/month. Processing fees run about 2.9% + 25¢ for cards and 1.5% for ACH. The quote-to-invoice flow is the best in class: proposals become contracts with e-signature, which become invoices with payment schedules, all inside a polished client portal. QuickBooks sync handles the accounting handoff.

The dealbreakers: HoneyBook only serves the US and Canada, and time tracking was removed in late 2026, so hourly consultants bill outside the tool. Automations only unlock on Essentials and above. If you are a US-based consultant selling fixed-fee packages, HoneyBook is worth it. If you bill hourly or work internationally, skip it.

3. Dubsado — the automation powerhouse

Dubsado is HoneyBook's deeper, geekier rival. It covers the same territory — forms, proposals, contracts, invoices, payments, scheduling, client portal — but with a workflow automation builder that can route leads through conditional branching, trigger payment reminders, and onboard clients with almost no manual steps. Consultants who run a repeatable process (discovery call → proposal → contract → kickoff → delivery → final invoice) can automate the entire administrative spine of their practice.

Pricing is simple and global: Starter at $335/year (about $28/month) and Premier at $525/year (about $44/month), with 3 extra users included free on Premier. Processing runs through your own Stripe or PayPal account — 2.9% + $0.30 for cards, 0.8% for ACH. Unlike HoneyBook, Dubsado works worldwide.

The honest cost is the learning curve. Budget 10–20 hours to build your workflows properly (or pay a Dubsado specialist $500–$2,000). The mobile app is basic, and the built-in time tracking is just a timer — not project-level time billing. Choose Dubsado if automation and international availability beat polish for you.

4. Bonsai — the freelancer-friendly tax bundle

Bonsai is the closest thing to an operating system for a freelance consultant: CRM-lite, proposals, contracts, time tracking, invoicing, expenses, and — uniquely — built-in tax tools that estimate quarterly taxes from your income. If you are a US or UK solo consultant who dreads tax season, that bundling is the reason to look at Bonsai.

Pricing runs Basic at $15/month and Essentials at $25/month per seat (monthly billing). Time tracking is included on all tiers, invoicing covers hourly and fixed-fee billing, and retainer/subscription invoicing handles recurring advisory work. Card processing is 2.9% + $0.30 via Stripe, with ACH around 1%. QuickBooks and Xero sync starts on the Premium tier.

Bonsai's headline feature — automatic tax estimation and write-off tracking built into the same tool that sends your invoices — addresses the single biggest admin pain consultants report: separating personal and business money and guessing quarterly payments.

The weakness is scale: per-seat pricing and a freelancer-first design make Bonsai awkward for firms with more than a few people, and the CRM side is thinner than HoneyBook's or Dubsado's. For solo consultants who want contracts, time tracking, invoicing, and tax estimates in one subscription, it is the strongest value on this list.

5. Zoho CRM + Zoho Books — the best-value combo

Every tool above makes you compromise: invoicing-first tools have weak CRMs, and all-in-one freelancer suites are not real CRMs. The Zoho combination is the answer if you refuse that compromise. Zoho CRM is a genuine, full-featured CRM with pipelines, automation, and reporting; Zoho Books is a genuine double-entry accounting app with invoicing, expenses, bank reconciliation, and tax handling. The native integration syncs contacts to customers and CRM deals/quotes to Books invoices, so the quote-to-invoice flow survives without manual re-entry.

Pricing is where this combo shines. Zoho CRM starts with a free tier for up to 3 users, then Standard at roughly $20/user/month and Professional at about $35/user/month (annual billing) — quotes and invoicing inside CRM start at Professional. Zoho Books is priced per organization: a free tier covers up to 1,000 invoices per year, Standard is $20/month, Professional is $50/month. A solo consultant can run a real CRM plus real accounting for as little as $35–$55 per month — dramatically cheaper than Keap or HubSpot at comparable depth.

The tradeoff is polish and setup time. Neither product feels as effortless as HoneyBook or FreshBooks, and wiring the CRM↔Books integration plus the payment gateway (Stripe, PayPal, and others) takes an afternoon. But for consultants who want proper pipeline management and proper books without enterprise prices, nothing else on this list comes close on value. For a deeper look, see our Zoho CRM vs. HubSpot comparison for small business.

6. Keap — the premium suite (usually overkill)

Keap (formerly Infusionsoft) is the heavyweight here: a single platform combining CRM, marketing automation, email and SMS, appointment scheduling, quotes, invoicing, and payments, with an automation builder that is genuinely best in class. Consultants who sell high-ticket engagements and need sophisticated follow-up sequences get tools none of the cheaper options offer.

But the price is steep for most consultants. Keap restructured in 2026 to a single all-inclusive plan starting at $299/month monthly or $249/month annually, covering 1,500 contacts and 2 users, with a mandatory one-time onboarding fee starting at $500. Extra users are $39/month each, and costs climb as your contact list grows. Year-one cost for a solo consultant is roughly $3,500–$4,100 before processing fees.

Keap earns its price only if you use the automation: nurture sequences that convert leads while you sleep and automated re-engagement campaigns. If your practice runs on referrals and you just need to track clients and get paid, Keap is expensive overkill. If you run a marketing-driven practice selling $10k+ engagements, it is the most capable tool here.

How to choose: built-in vs. bolted-on

Three questions separate the right tool from an expensive mistake.

1. Is invoicing truly included, or is it an add-on? Every tool in the table above includes invoicing on its starter tier — that is why they are here. But watch the fine print elsewhere: HubSpot's CRM is free, yet real invoicing and payment collection live in Revenue Hub (formerly Commerce Hub), where the free tools cover basics for two users and Professional costs $85/seat/month ($57 bundled with Sales Hub). If you are weighing that route, read our HubSpot vs. Freshsales comparison for small teams before committing.

2. What do the payment fees really cost you? A 1.5% difference in processing fees sounds trivial until you run $100,000 through the tool in a year — then it is $1,500. Dubsado's 0.8% ACH fee beats HoneyBook's 1.5%; if your clients pay by bank transfer on $8k retainers, that gap alone covers Dubsado's subscription several times over. Always model fees on your actual average invoice, not the subscription sticker price.

Processing fees dwarf subscription costs for most consultants: on $100,000 of annual card billing, the difference between a 2.9% and a 1.5% fee structure is $1,400 a year — several times the cost of any subscription on this list.

3. Does time tracking connect to billing? If you bill hourly, this is the feature that pays for the tool. FreshBooks and Bonsai put tracked hours directly on invoices, and Zoho handles it through Zoho Projects. HoneyBook dropped time tracking entirely, and Dubsado's timer is too basic for serious hourly billing — budget for a separate tracker if you choose either.

Frequently asked questions

Which CRM has the best invoicing for consultants?

HoneyBook has the smoothest client-facing flow — proposal, contract, invoice, and payment in one link — making it the best all-in-one pick for US and Canadian consultants. FreshBooks has the best invoicing engine itself, with unlimited invoices and time tracking from $23/month, but it is not a real CRM.

Can I use HoneyBook or Dubsado outside the United States?

HoneyBook only serves the US and Canada. Dubsado, Bonsai, FreshBooks, Zoho, and Keap all work internationally, so consultants with global clients should pick one of those.

How much do payment processing fees really cost?

Card processing typically runs 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction, while ACH bank transfers cost 0.8–1.5%. On $100,000 of annual billing, a 1.5-point fee difference costs you $1,500 a year — far more than any subscription on this list — so model fees against your actual invoice volume.

Do I still need separate accounting software?

Not always. Bonsai includes tax estimation and write-off tracking, Zoho Books is a full double-entry ledger, and FreshBooks covers bookkeeping basics. Consultants with complex taxes or inventory will still want an accountant or a dedicated ledger like QuickBooks or Xero, which most of these tools sync with.

Is there a free CRM with invoicing for consultants?

Yes — Zoho CRM's free tier (3 users) plus Zoho Books' free tier (up to 1,000 invoices per year) is the most capable $0 stack. Wave also offers free invoicing, though it lacks CRM features. Free tiers work well until you need time billing, retainers, or automation.

Conclusion

There is no single best CRM with built-in invoicing for every consultant — only a best one for how you sell and bill. HoneyBook is the best all-in-one for US-based consultants selling fixed-fee packages. FreshBooks is the best pure invoicing tool with real time tracking for hourly and retainer billing. Dubsado is the best value for automation-driven consultants with international clients. Bonsai is the best affordable bundle for solo consultants who want tax help included. Zoho CRM + Zoho Books is the best-value genuine CRM-plus-accounting combo. And Keap is the right call only for marketing-driven practices selling high-ticket engagements. Start from your billing model, model the payment fees on your real invoice volume, and the right tool usually picks itself.
