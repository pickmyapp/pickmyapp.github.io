Quick answer

You can run real business dashboards for $0. For Google-centric businesses, Looker Studio is free forever with unlimited viewers. Why "free" dashboard tools all stop at the same place

Every BI vendor hands out a free tier as a sample: it gets you building, and the moment your dashboards matter — more viewers, more data, more reliability — you start paying. Knowing where each free tier stops is the whole game, because "free" here is never a trick, but it is always a fence.

Every dashboard tool has three costs, and only the first is usually free: the software license, the infrastructure (whose server runs it), and your time (setup and maintenance). Open-source tools like Metabase and Superset are free on cost one, but costs two and three are yours. Cloud tools like Looker Studio and Zoho Analytics absorb the first two and ration you with row limits and user caps instead. The honest question is not "which is free?" — all seven below are genuinely free to start. It is "which free tier's fence am I least likely to hit?"

Looker Studio

Looker Studio (Google's BI tool, formerly Data Studio) is the default answer for most small businesses: free forever, with unlimited viewers, unlimited reports, and native connectors to Google Analytics 4, Google Ads, Search Console, BigQuery, and Google Sheets. You share dashboards with a link, the way you share a Doc.

The gotchas are architectural. Looker Studio has no data layer of its own — it visualizes data where it sits, so blending multiple sources is where dashboards start drifting on definitions. Performance degrades on poorly structured data, and while the gallery lists hundreds of connectors, many are paid partner connectors.

Pick it if: your data lives in GA4, Google Ads, or Sheets and you need shareable dashboards this week with zero setup.

Metabase

Metabase's open-source edition is free and self-hosted, with unlimited users, questions, and dashboards. You run it as a JAR file or Docker container, point it at your Postgres, MySQL, or BigQuery database, and non-technical staff get a point-and-click query builder while analysts get a full SQL editor. For a small business with an app database, it is the shortest path from "data in Postgres" to "dashboards everyone can read" without a per-seat BI license.

The fence is two-sided. First, you host it: budget roughly $5–$20/month for a small VPS plus your time for updates, backups, and security. Second, the enterprise features — SSO, row-level permissions, official support — live in paid cloud plans (Starter at $100/month for 5 users). There are also no native SaaS-app connectors: if your data is in Stripe or HubSpot rather than a database, Metabase is the wrong tool. One license note: Metabase is AGPL-licensed, fine for internal use but restrictive if you want to embed it in a commercial product.

Pick it if: you have a database and one person comfortable running a Docker container.

Apache Superset

Apache Superset started at Airbnb and is now a top-level Apache Software Foundation project. It is completely free under Apache 2.0, with 40+ visualization types, a no-code chart builder, a full SQL Lab IDE, and enterprise governance baked in: row-level security, role-based access control, SSO, and result caching all ship in the free edition.

The fence is expertise. Superset expects engineering capacity to deploy, configure, and maintain it, and there is no built-in data-transformation layer, so teams usually pair it with something like dbt for consistent metric definitions. The interface is less polished for non-technical users than Metabase. Managed exits exist: Preset Cloud offers a free Starter tier for up to 5 users.

Apache Superset is the only tool on this list that ships enterprise security — row-level security, SSO, role-based access — in its free, open-source edition, with zero licensing cost.

Pick it if: you have SQL-skilled people and someone who can own a server. It is the most "real BI platform" you can get for free.

Tableau Public

Tableau Public gives you the full Tableau Desktop authoring experience for free — the same visual analytics companies pay $75/user/month for in Creator licenses. For learning Tableau, building a portfolio, or visualizing public datasets, it is legitimately excellent.

The catch is absolute: everything you save in Tableau Public is published to the public web. You cannot save workbooks locally or privately — the only way to save is to publish to your public profile, and the packaged workbook includes the underlying dataset, which anyone can download. It connects mainly to flat files (Excel, CSV, Google Sheets, JSON), not live corporate databases, and data does not refresh after publishing.

When you publish to Tableau Public, you are uploading your entire workbook including every row of its data — anyone who finds the visualization can download the packaged file and see it all. Never use it for sales figures, customer lists, financials, or anything with personal data.

Pick it if: you are learning Tableau or visualizing already-public data. For business dashboards, use any other tool on this list.

Google Sheets

Before reaching for a BI platform, check whether you already have one. Google Sheets — free with any Google account — supports pivot tables, a solid chart library, sparklines, and Apps Script automation. A weekly KPI view from a pivot table plus a few charts is a dashboard, and for datasets under a few thousand rows it builds faster than anything else here.

The fence is scale and governance. Manual refreshes become a maintenance burden, two people can quietly define the same metric two ways, and performance degrades as sheets grow — if your reporting is already sluggish, our guide to speeding up slow spreadsheets covers the usual culprits.

Pick it if: you need a simple KPI dashboard this afternoon and your data fits in a spreadsheet.

Zoho Analytics

Zoho Analytics is one of the few serious BI vendors with a permanent free plan rather than just a trial: 2 users, 10,000 rows, 5 workspaces, and unlimited reports and dashboards, with an AI assistant (Ask Zia) and mobile apps. That makes it the most complete free cloud BI tier aimed at actual businesses rather than hobbyists.

The fence is small but clearly marked: only 2 business-app connectors, no live database connections, and the 10,000-row ceiling arrives fast with order history. Paid plans start at $30/month for half a million rows. Teams already on Zoho's apps get extra value from native integrations — our Zoho CRM vs HubSpot comparison for small teams covers the ecosystem decision behind that.

Pick it if: you are a 1–2 person operation with modest data that wants real BI without hosting anything.

Power BI Desktop

Power BI Desktop is a free download with the full authoring power of the platform: a strong data-modeling engine, hundreds of connectors, DAX formulas, and polished visuals. For a single analyst on their own machine, it is arguably the most capable free tool here.

The fence is sharing. Reports stay on your PC. To publish to the Power BI Service so teammates can view dashboards online, both the publisher and every viewer need a Pro license at $14/user/month (after the April 2025 price increase), and Pro caps datasets at 1 GB. It is free to build and $14 per person per month to collaborate — a personal analysis tool, not a team dashboarding tool, at the free tier. For the paid side of this choice, see our Power BI vs Tableau guide for small businesses.

Pick it if: you are a Microsoft 365 shop and one person needs deep local analysis.

Which free tool should you pick?

Run this decision tree and you will land on the right free tier in about a minute:

  • Data in Google Analytics, Google Ads, or Sheets? → Looker Studio. Least friction, zero setup.
  • Data in a database and someone technical? → Metabase. Self-service BI without a per-seat license — but you own hosting, updates, and backups.
  • SQL-skilled team wanting maximum power for free? → Apache Superset. The strongest free BI platform, with real governance, at the cost of a learning curve.
  • Need one KPI view this week? → Google Sheets. Do not over-engineer a pivot table.
  • 1–2 person team wanting cloud BI? → Zoho Analytics free. Real BI with a 10,000-row ceiling; the $30/month plan is the cheapest paid exit.
  • Microsoft 365 shop analyzing solo? → Power BI Desktop. Free to build; the sharing decision is $14 per person per month.
  • Tempted by Tableau Public for business data? → Stop. It is a learning and portfolio tool. Business data there becomes publicly downloadable.

Quick-start path: a live dashboard this week

Do not evaluate all seven — pick your lane from the tree above and run this sequence.

Day 1: Connect one data source

Not three sources, not a warehouse migration — one. Looker Studio: your GA4 property or main Sheet. Metabase or Superset: a read-only view of one trusted table. Sheets: decide which single sheet is the source of truth.

Days 2–3: Build one dashboard, five charts or fewer

Answer five questions: revenue this month, revenue vs. last month, top 3 products, traffic or lead volume, and one number the owner checks daily. Then define each metric once, in writing — what it includes and excludes — because two charts disagreeing about "revenue" is the number-one cause of dashboard distrust.

Day 4: Share it with one stakeholder and ask one question

"What number on here would you act on?" If the answer is "none," cut charts until every remaining one drives a decision.

Day 5: Schedule the refresh

Live connectors refresh themselves. File-based setups need a routine: a weekly CSV export into a fixed folder, an Apps Script import, or a scheduled Metabase dashboard email every Monday. A dashboard nobody refreshes is a screenshot with extra steps.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best completely free dashboard tool for a small business?

For most small businesses, Looker Studio: free forever, unlimited viewers, and native Google Analytics, Google Ads, and Sheets connectors with zero setup. If your data lives in a database instead, Metabase's free open-source edition (unlimited users, self-hosted) is the best free option. Google Sheets with pivot charts is enough for very small datasets.

Is there a free version of Power BI for small business?

Yes — Power BI Desktop is a free download with the full report-building feature set. The catch is sharing: reports stay on your PC unless you publish to the Power BI Service, which requires a Pro license at $14/user/month for both the publisher and every viewer. It is free for solo analysis, paid for team dashboards.

Can I use Tableau Public for my company's private data?

No — never. Tableau Public is free because everything you save is published to the public web: your workbook and its underlying data become visible and downloadable by anyone. There is no private saving. It is designed for learning Tableau and visualizing public datasets, not for business intelligence.

Is Metabase really free?

The open-source edition is genuinely free: unlimited users, questions, and dashboards, self-hosted via JAR or Docker. The costs are the server it runs on (roughly $5–$20/month for a small VPS) and your time maintaining it. Paid Metabase Cloud plans (from $100/month for 5 users) add hosting, SSO, row-level permissions, and support.

What are the limits of Zoho Analytics' free plan?

The free plan allows 2 users, 10,000 rows, 5 workspaces, and unlimited reports and dashboards, with only 2 business-app connectors and no live database connections. It is a real BI product for micro-teams, but the 10,000-row ceiling arrives quickly with transaction data — paid plans start at $30/month for half a million rows.

Conclusion

There is no trick to free dashboard tools — only fences, and now you know where each one stands. Looker Studio is the best free default for Google-centric businesses. Metabase and Apache Superset are genuinely free BI platforms if you can host them, with Metabase favoring ease and Superset favoring power. Google Sheets is the fastest free dashboard for tiny datasets. Zoho Analytics' free plan is the most complete free cloud BI tier for micro-teams. Power BI Desktop is free until sharing enters the picture. And Tableau Public is free with one absolute condition: your data becomes public. Pick the lane that matches where your data lives, start with one source and five charts, and write your metric definitions down before anyone asks why two dashboards disagree.
