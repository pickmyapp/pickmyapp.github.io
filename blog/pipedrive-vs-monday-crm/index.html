Pipedrive vs Monday CRM Compared

  • CRM Software
  • Published Oct 10, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For a dedicated sales team, start with Pipedrive: its activity-based selling system ties calls, emails and tasks to every deal so nothing slips, and its pipeline is the clearest deal-tracking view in this price range. Pick Monday CRM instead when sales is only part of the job — its boards bend to any workflow, so you can run sales, marketing and project delivery in one workspace, and its per-seat prices run lower at every tier ($12–$28 vs $14–$99). Pipedrive and Monday CRM both promise to organize your customer relationships, but they were built for different jobs. Pipedrive is a pipeline-first CRM designed by salespeople: every screen nudges you toward the next sales activity. Monday CRM is a CRM built on top of a work-management platform: you get boards you can bend into any process, from sales to onboarding to project delivery. For a small business choosing between them in 2026, the decision comes down to whether you need a focused selling tool or a flexible team workspace — and what each one really costs per seat once seat minimums and add-ons are counted. Prices below were checked against the vendors' pricing pages and recent independent reviews in October 2026. CRM pricing changes, so verify before you buy.

Two different philosophies

Pipedrive is built around one idea: activity-based selling. Founded in Estonia in 2010 and now owned by Vista Equity Partners, Pipedrive treats every deal as a chain of scheduled activities — a call, an email, a meeting, a follow-up — and refuses to let a deal sit without a next step. The visual pipeline is the heart of the product: you drag deals between stages, and the software reminds you which activities are due today. It does one job, and it is deliberately uninterested in doing others.

Monday CRM is built around a different idea: one flexible board for everything. monday.com (founded in Israel in 2012) started as work-management software; its CRM is one product on that platform. Deals are items on boards, stages are status columns, and you can view the same data as a kanban, a table, a calendar, or a dashboard. The strength is that sales, marketing, onboarding and delivery can all live in one workspace. The trade-off, noted in G2 user reviews, is that the sales-specific features are shallower than in a dedicated CRM like Pipedrive.

The one-line version: Pipedrive tells you what to do next. Monday CRM lets you build how you do everything. If your team's job is closing deals, the first is the better starting point; if sales is one workflow among many, the second is.

Where Monday CRM is cheaper

On list price alone, Monday CRM undercuts Pipedrive at every tier: $12 vs $14 at entry, $17 vs about $29 in the middle, and $28 vs about $49 at the upper tier. A team that can live on Standard gets a usable CRM with automations and email integration for less per seat than Pipedrive's Advanced. The gap widens as teams grow, which matters more than the headline numbers suggest — see the worked examples below.

Where Pipedrive costs more than it looks

Pipedrive's add-ons are the quiet budget risk. LeadBooster (chat, web forms, prospecting) runs about $32.50 a month per account, Campaigns (email marketing) from about $13.33 a month, and Web Visitors from about $41 a month — flat fees that stack on top of every seat's license. Monday's equivalent catch is seat buckets: a 4-person team must buy the 5-seat bucket, and a solo founder pays for three seats they do not have. Neither vendor's headline price is the whole bill; model your actual team size and add-ons before comparing.

Sales features compared

Pipeline management

Pipedrive's pipeline is its crown jewel: a drag-and-drop board where every deal shows its value, probability and next activity, plus multiple customizable pipelines for different products or regions. It also flags "rotting" deals that sit idle too long — a small feature that catches real money slipping through the cracks. Monday CRM offers kanban board views with custom stages, which are fine for straightforward pipelines, but you design the stages yourself and there is no built-in rotting logic. For pure deal tracking, Pipedrive's view is simply better thought through.

Activities and follow-ups

This is where the philosophies diverge most. Pipedrive ties calls, emails, tasks and meetings to each deal with due dates, and the next activity is always one glance away. Sync with Google or Outlook calendar is standard, and overdue activities are impossible to miss. Monday CRM tracks tasks and has an Emails & Activities app, but the same discipline requires you to build automations or dashboards yourself. If your reps follow up because the CRM tells them to, Pipedrive wins this round decisively.

Email and calling

Pipedrive syncs email both ways (Advanced and up), tracks opens and clicks, offers templates and group email, and its Campaigns add-on handles newsletters and segmented lists. One-click calling with call tracking is built in on Professional. Monday CRM integrates Gmail and Outlook, sends email sequences on Pro, and has mass-email options — but there is no native calling; you add a telephony app from the marketplace. If you are also comparing lead-focused tools, our HubSpot vs Pipedrive for small teams guide covers the free-tier angle.

Lead capture and prospecting

Pipedrive ships web forms, live chat and a prospecting database (Prospector) for finding new leads by criteria — mostly behind the LeadBooster add-on. Monday CRM has web forms and lead capture boards, but no prospecting database and no native chat. Both handle imports from spreadsheets and other CRMs without much friction.

Automation and customization

Pipedrive's automations are sales-shaped: when a deal moves to a stage, assign an owner and create the follow-up activities; when an email is opened, alert the rep; when a deal is won, trigger the handoff. Its AI sales assistant (Professional and up) estimates win probability and suggests which deals need attention. Limits scale by plan — from a handful of active automations on Essential to effectively unlimited on Enterprise.

Monday CRM's automation builder is visual and no-code ("when status changes to Won, notify the delivery team and create onboarding tasks on another board"), and its real superpower is cross-board workflows that span departments. AI blocks can summarize threads, classify leads and draft emails. The catch is the monthly action quotas: 250 actions a month on Standard is easy to burn through with a chatty team, while Pro's 25,000 gives real headroom. If you need heavier automation than either CRM offers, a dedicated tool is the better answer — see our n8n pricing: self-hosted vs cloud breakdown for one option.

Rule of thumb: if your automations are sales-heavy, Pipedrive's triggers fit better. If they span multiple teams — sales to delivery to finance — Monday's cross-board automations are more powerful.

Integrations and ecosystem

Monday's own comparison puts the marketplace counts at about 500 apps for Pipedrive and 200+ for Monday CRM. Pipedrive's catalog leans sales-tool heavy: calling, e-signatures, email, prospecting. Monday's leans toward productivity: docs, files, project views, and collaboration tools that live in the same workspace. Both connect through Zapier and Make for anything missing, and both have open APIs.

One genuine Monday advantage: docs, whiteboards, dashboards and file storage are native to the platform, so you buy fewer extra tools. Pipedrive assumes your sales stack lives elsewhere and just needs to connect. Monday's project-management DNA also means it overlaps with standalone PM tools — if that is the comparison you are really making, see our Asana vs Monday vs ClickUp for small teams guide.

Ease of use for a sales team

G2's user community scores the two almost identically on ease of use — Monday CRM a touch above Pipedrive, per figures cited on Monday's own comparison page — but the scores hide a real difference. Monday wins first impressions: colorful, playful boards that anyone can click around in. Pipedrive wins the second week: reps know exactly what to do each morning because the pipeline and the activity list tell them.

The setup burden runs in opposite directions. Pipedrive needs little configuration — import contacts, define pipeline stages, start selling. Monday needs someone to design the sales process first: boards, columns, statuses, views, automations. That is more powerful and more work, and it means Monday setups need an owner to stay clean. If your reps are non-technical and turnover is a factor, Pipedrive's simplicity is an asset, not a limitation.

Total cost at 3, 10 and 25 seats

List prices hide the seat rules, so here is what three common team sizes actually pay per month with annual billing (October 2026 prices; verify before buying):

  • 3 seats. Monday Standard: 3 × $17 = $51 (and even Basic at 3 × $12 = $36 beats Pipedrive's entry price, though Basic lacks automations). Pipedrive's usable tier, Advanced: 3 × $29 = $87. Monday wins clearly — and a two-person team can even use Monday's free tier while Pipedrive charges from day one.
  • 10 seats. Monday Standard: 10 × $17 = $170; Pro: 10 × $28 = $280. Pipedrive Advanced: 10 × $29 = $290; Professional: 10 × $49 = $490. Monday's mid tier costs roughly a third of Pipedrive's — the gap is now several thousand dollars a year.
  • 25 seats. Monday Pro: 25 × $28 = $700. Pipedrive Professional: 25 × $49 = $1,225. The annual difference is $6,300 — enough to hire a part-time hire or fund a marketing budget. Add Pipedrive add-ons (Campaigns, LeadBooster) and the gap widens further.

But watch the buckets: Monday's 3, 5, 10, 25 and 40-seat buckets mean awkward team sizes overpay — a 4-person team buys 5 seats, a 12-person team buys 15. Pipedrive charges for actual seats, so its math is cleaner even when the total is higher.

If these numbers sting, remember you do not have to pay for a CRM at all in the beginning — our best free CRM for startups guide shows how far a $0 stack can carry a team of two or three.

Our verdict

Choose Pipedrive if

  • Your team's main job is selling, and you want the CRM to enforce follow-up discipline.
  • You like activity-based selling: calls, emails and tasks tied to every deal.
  • You need sales forecasting, revenue reporting and built-in calling out of the box.
  • You want the simplest possible onboarding for new reps.

Choose Monday CRM if

  • Sales is one of several workflows you want in a single workspace.
  • You want to design your own boards, dashboards and cross-team automations.
  • Budget per seat matters and you have three or more people.
  • You already use monday.com for projects, so the learning curve is zero.

You are not locked in on day one. Pipedrive and Monday CRM both offer trials, so run a two-week shootout: import real contacts, build one pipeline each way, and have a rep work real deals in both. The tool your team actually opens every morning beats the one that wins on paper.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper for a small team, Pipedrive or Monday CRM?

Monday CRM lists lower per-seat prices at every comparable tier: $12 vs $14 at entry, $17 vs about $29 in the middle, and $28 vs about $49 at the upper tier (annual billing, checked October 2026). But Monday requires a minimum of three seats in fixed bucket sizes, while Pipedrive charges for your actual seats — and Pipedrive's flat add-ons (LeadBooster, Campaigns, Web Visitors) can raise its real bill.

Is Pipedrive or Monday CRM better for a dedicated sales team?

Pipedrive is the better fit for a dedicated sales team. Its activity-based selling system ties calls, emails and tasks to every deal so nothing slips, and it has deeper sales-specific features: deal rotting alerts, revenue forecasting, an AI sales assistant, and built-in calling. Monday CRM's sales features are broader but shallower by comparison.

Can Monday CRM fully replace Pipedrive?

For a simple sales pipeline, yes — especially if you already use monday.com for projects, or you want sales, marketing and delivery work in one workspace. It is a weaker replacement if your process depends on activity-based selling, deal rotting alerts, sales forecasting depth, or built-in calling, all of which Pipedrive handles more naturally.

Do Pipedrive or Monday CRM offer a free plan?

Pipedrive has no free plan, only a 14-day trial on every tier. Monday CRM has a free plan limited to two seats with very basic features, and paid plans start with a three-seat minimum. A two-person team can genuinely run on Monday's free tier; on Pipedrive you pay from day one.

Can I migrate from Pipedrive to Monday CRM (or back)?

There is no one-click importer between them, but both tools import contacts and deals from CSV files, so the data moves across without much pain. You will rebuild pipelines, custom fields and automations by hand on the new platform, so run both systems in parallel for a few weeks and validate every stage before switching off the old one.

Conclusion

Pipedrive remains the sharper selling tool: its pipeline, activity reminders and sales-specific features are exactly what a small sales team needs and nothing they do not. Monday CRM is the broader platform: lower per-seat prices, endlessly customizable boards, and a workspace your whole company can share. Choose Pipedrive when closing deals is the job; choose Monday CRM when sales is one workflow among many. Either way, check the current pricing page before you commit — both vendors adjust their tiers regularly.
