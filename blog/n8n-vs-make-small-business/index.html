n8n vs Make: Which Is Better for Small Business in 2026?

  • AI Automation Tools
  • Published Oct 9, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 9 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most small businesses, start with Make: its Core plan is $9 a month for 10,000 credits, the visual builder is friendlier for non-technical teams, and there is no server to maintain. Pick n8n instead when someone on the team is comfortable with APIs, your workflows have many steps, or you want to self-host — n8n counts one workflow run as one execution, so long multi-step automations cost far less per run than on any per-action platform.

n8n and Make are the two names that come up in almost every conversation about leaving Zapier. Both are visual automation builders: you connect apps on a canvas instead of writing code, and data flows from one step to the next. But they charge for usage in opposite ways, assume different levels of technical comfort, and one can run on your own server while the other cannot. This guide compares them from a small-business perspective — a team of roughly 1 to 20 people automating lead capture, invoicing, email, and reporting — using prices checked against the vendor pricing pages in October 2026. If Zapier is still in the running, see our cheaper Zapier alternatives for the wider picture.

What n8n is, in brief

n8n is a node-based workflow automation platform, founded in Berlin in 2019, that can run on n8n's own cloud or on a server you control. The "n" stands for "node": every automation is a workflow built from nodes, each performing one action or transformation. The source code is public under a "fair-code" license, which means the free Community Edition is genuinely free for your own internal business use — you just cannot resell the software as a competing service.

What sets n8n apart for a small business is its per-workflow pricing model. A workflow run counts as a single execution whether it has three steps or thirty, and every paid cloud plan includes unlimited users and unlimited active workflows — only the execution allowance changes between tiers. Add the Code node (JavaScript or Python), dedicated AI agent nodes, and the option to connect to models hosted on your own infrastructure, and you have a tool that behaves like developer software with a visual skin on top.

What Make is, in brief

Make is a cloud-only visual automation platform, formerly known as Integromat, where you build "scenarios" by linking modules on a canvas. It started in the Czech Republic and has been owned by Celonis since 2020. There is no self-hosting option and no server to think about: you sign up, connect your apps, and build.

Make's scenarios feel like flowcharts. Routers split the flow into conditional branches, iterators and aggregators process lists of items, and error-handler modules decide what happens when a step fails. The company says its catalog covers more than 3,000 apps. Since August 2025, Make bills in credits rather than operations: one credit is spent for roughly every module action inside a scenario.

The billing unit decides everything

Forget the plan names for a moment. The single most important difference between these two tools is what you are charged for. On n8n, one execution equals one complete workflow run, regardless of step count. On Make, one credit equals roughly one module action inside a scenario. A 12-step automation that runs once costs n8n one execution and Make about twelve credits.

This has a practical consequence that surprises people: a Make scenario with polling triggers, routers, and a handful of actions can burn through credits faster than its price tag suggests, while an n8n workflow of the same shape stays flat at one execution per run. Conversely, a business running thousands of tiny two-step automations gets more units per dollar from Make than from n8n's execution model. The right tool is the one whose billing unit matches the shape of your automations.

Quick fact: Make cut the annual-billed prices of its three paid tiers by roughly 25% in September 2026 — Core from $12 to $9, Pro from $21 to $16, and Teams from $38 to $29 — which makes the entry tier noticeably cheaper than most third-party comparison articles published earlier in the year suggest.

Where Make is cheaper

For a small business running a handful of simple automations a few hundred times a month, Make is hard to beat. Core costs $9 a month with annual billing — about half the effective price of n8n Cloud's Starter tier — and includes 10,000 credits, four times n8n Starter's 2,500 executions. If your scenarios average two or three modules each, those 10,000 credits cover roughly 3,000 to 5,000 runs. The free plan is also a usable sandbox: 1,000 credits a month is enough to prove a concept, though the 15-minute minimum interval between runs means anything time-sensitive belongs on Core.

Where n8n pulls ahead

n8n's advantage appears when workflows grow long. A lead pipeline with enrichment, duplicate checks, CRM writes, and notifications might contain ten or more steps; each run consumes one n8n execution but roughly ten Make credits. At 2,000 such runs a month, n8n Starter's 2,500-execution allowance still covers you at €24 a month, while Make needs about 20,000 credits — double the base allowance on any paid tier, meaning a larger credit pack. Self-hosting is the extreme case: the Community Edition has no execution cap at all, so a $5-to-$10-a-month server can run unlimited workflows once you accept responsibility for updates, backups, and uptime.

A real cost example

Consider a small agency that automates new-client onboarding: a form submission triggers client research, a CRM record is created, an invoice draft is generated, and the client gets a welcome email — eight steps total. At 400 new clients a month, n8n counts 400 executions, comfortably inside Starter's 2,500. Make burns about 3,200 credits, comfortably inside Core's 10,000. Both work, and Make costs less per month.

Now imagine the agency triples its client flow to 1,200 a month. n8n still counts 1,200 executions and stays on Starter. Make needs roughly 9,600 credits — nearly the entire Core allowance, with little room for retries or errors. One more growth step and n8n is the cheaper cloud option, or self-hosting becomes the cheapest of all. Model your own step count and monthly volume before comparing sticker prices; the billing unit will tell you which curve rises faster.

Ease of use for a small team

First week

Make is the easier tool to pick up. Modules are labeled by app and action ("Gmail: Send an email"), you map fields by clicking rather than writing expressions, and the template library hands you working scenarios to adapt. n8n is usable without code for common tasks, but it assumes you understand how data items move between nodes and are comfortable writing small expressions in JavaScript. A non-technical owner can learn both; Make takes days, n8n takes a week or two.

Building and debugging

Both platforms show the data flowing through each step, which is what makes visual builders worth paying for. Make displays each module's output after a test run, so a failed field mapping is easy to spot. n8n shows input and output per node and lets you pin sample data, which means you can rerun a single node without triggering the whole workflow — a real time-saver when the workflow talks to a slow or rate-limited API. Make's error-handler modules (ignore, resume, rollback, break) are easier to configure visually; n8n answers with per-node retries and error workflows that can page your team through Slack or email.

Templates and community

Both have large template libraries and active forums. Make's templates are generally easier for beginners to adapt. n8n workflows export as JSON that can be pasted straight into another editor, which is handy when someone in the community has already solved your exact problem. If your automations mostly touch a project management tool or a CRM, check the template libraries first — whichever platform already has a good template for your stack has a genuine head start.

AI features and code access

n8n is the stronger platform for custom AI work. It ships dedicated AI agent nodes, an HTTP Request node that can call virtually any API, a Code node running JavaScript or Python, and the ability to connect to language models hosted on your own infrastructure — which matters if customer data cannot leave your region. Make's code module exists but is metered by compute time, and its AI modules cover the common providers for simpler steps such as summarizing text or classifying a support ticket.

The honest line: for "send this text to an AI model and use the answer," Make is quicker to set up. For agents that call tools in loops, retrieval pipelines over your own documents, or private models, n8n is the better foundation. If you are just starting with AI in your operations, our AI writing tools comparison covers the content side of the same question.

Integrations and apps

Make claims more than 3,000 apps in its catalog, including many niche business tools. n8n lists several hundred built-in nodes for popular services, plus community-built nodes you can install on self-hosted setups. For the mainstream stack most small businesses run — Google Workspace, Slack, HubSpot, Stripe, Shopify, and a budget email tool — both cover the essentials.

When an app is missing from both, you fall back to the generic HTTP module and webhooks. n8n has a small but meaningful edge here: you can paste a cURL command to generate a request instantly, which removes a lot of trial and error with unfamiliar APIs. Make lets developers build custom apps on its platform, though that is overkill for most teams under twenty people.

Our verdict

Choose n8n if

  • Someone on your team is comfortable with APIs and basic JavaScript.
  • Your workflows have many steps or run thousands of times a month.
  • You want to self-host for data control or the lowest cost at scale.
  • You are building custom AI agents or using privately hosted models.

Choose Make if

  • You want the friendliest builder and zero infrastructure to manage.
  • Your automations are simple and run a few hundred to a few thousand times a month.
  • You need niche apps that n8n does not cover natively.
  • You want the lowest paid entry price in the cloud.

You are not locked in on day one. Make has a permanent free plan and n8n offers a cloud trial, so build the same small workflow — say, new leads from a form into your CRM with a notification — in both and see which one your team actually finishes. The tool your team will maintain beats the tool that wins on paper.

Frequently asked questions

Is n8n really free for a small business?

The n8n Community Edition is free to install on your own server for internal business use, with no execution limit — but you pay roughly $5 to $10 a month for a small server plus the time to maintain it. The managed n8n Cloud starts at €24 a month billed monthly (€20 billed annually) for 2,500 workflow executions.

How much does Make cost per month in 2026?

Make's Free plan costs $0 for 1,000 credits a month. Paid plans start at Core, $9 a month billed annually ($10.59 monthly) for 10,000 credits, then Pro at $16 a month annual and Teams at $29 a month annual. One credit is spent for roughly every module action in a scenario. Prices checked October 2026.

Is Make or n8n better for a non-technical team?

Make is the better fit for a non-technical team. Its modules are labeled by app and action, fields are mapped by clicking, and templates give you working starting points. n8n assumes comfort with JSON, expressions, and API credentials, so the first projects take noticeably longer.

Can I migrate my automations between Make and n8n?

There is no automatic importer between the two platforms. You rebuild each scenario as a workflow (or the reverse), reconnect your app credentials, and test everything again. Rebuild the automations one at a time, starting with the simplest, so the old tool keeps running while you migrate.

Which tool is better for AI-powered workflows?

n8n is the stronger choice for custom AI work: it has dedicated AI agent nodes, a Code node for JavaScript or Python, and it can call models you host yourself, which helps with data privacy. Make covers the simpler cases well, such as sending text to an AI model as one step in a scenario.

Conclusion

Start with Make if your automations are simple, your team is non-technical, and you want the lowest monthly bill with no server involved — at $9 a month it is the cheapest credible cloud automation platform in 2026. Move to or start with n8n when your workflows get long, your volume climbs, or you want to self-host and own your data; its per-execution billing and free Community Edition make it the better long-term bet for growing operations. Either way, model your own step count and monthly runs first — the billing unit, not the plan name, decides which tool saves you money.
