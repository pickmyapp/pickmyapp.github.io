OnePageCRM vs Capsule for solo consultants (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Nov 8, 2026
  • Updated: November 2026
  • 12 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most solo consultants, OnePageCRM is the better buy: it starts at $9.95 a user a month billed annually ($119.40 a year) against Capsule's $18 a month ($216 a year), and its Action Stream keeps your next follow-up in front of you so deals stop going quiet. Capsule wins if you want a genuinely free starting point — its free plan covers 2 users and 250 contacts — or if you manage client delivery projects in the same tool as your sales. Both are deliberately simple CRMs; the right choice depends on whether your biggest problem is forgetting follow-ups or keeping sales and delivery together.

The verdict up front

OnePageCRM and Capsule solve the same problem in opposite ways. OnePageCRM assumes your deals stall because nobody scheduled the next step, so it builds the whole tool around a single prioritized list of next actions. Capsule assumes your problem is chaos — contacts, deals, and client work scattered across spreadsheets and inboxes — so it gives you one tidy place for contacts, opportunities, and projects.

On price alone, OnePageCRM wins clearly. Its Professional plan is $9.95 a user a month billed annually, while Capsule's cheapest paid plan, Starter, is $18 a user a month billed annually. Over a year that is $119.40 against $216 — a $96.60 gap for one solo user. The gap widens when you need more: OnePageCRM Business is $19.95 a month ($239.40 a year) while the Capsule plan with automation, Growth, is $36 a month ($432 a year).

But the cheaper tool is not automatically the better one. Capsule's free plan is a genuine $0 start — 2 users, 250 contacts, one sales pipeline — and that matters if you are testing whether you will actually use a CRM. And if your consulting work involves delivering projects after the sale, Capsule's built-in project management means you may never need a second tool. Prices were checked November 8, 2026; confirm them on each vendor's pricing page before you buy.

Short version: pick OnePageCRM if follow-ups are where your money leaks. Pick Capsule if you want the simplest possible CRM, a free start, or sales and delivery in one place.

What each tool actually is: Action Stream vs pipelines and projects

OnePageCRM is built around one idea: the Action Stream. Every contact has a scheduled next action attached to it — a call, an email, a task — and all of those actions sit in one prioritized list that tells you what to do today, in what order. Open the app in the morning and the work is already sequenced: call this prospect, email that client, follow up on the proposal that went quiet last Tuesday.

That design reflects a specific belief about why solo consultants lose deals. It is rarely because the proposal was weak. It is because the follow-up never happened — the prospect went quiet, the consultant got busy with delivery work, and nobody chased. The Action Stream is a machine for preventing exactly that.

Capsule takes the opposite approach. It is deliberately simple: contacts, opportunities, and projects in one clean interface, with a single sales pipeline on the entry plans and a visual board for moving deals along. Where OnePageCRM pushes you toward the next action, Capsule waits for you to decide what matters. It also bundles project management into the same tool, so a deal that closes can become a delivery project without switching apps — a real advantage if your engagements run for weeks.

The practical difference shows up on a busy Monday. In OnePageCRM, you open the Action Stream and work the list. In Capsule, you open your pipeline, see where each deal sits, and decide what to do next. OnePageCRM is better for consultants whose problem is follow-up; Capsule is better for consultants whose problem is keeping sales, contacts, and delivery in one calm place. Neither tries to be a full sales platform: both are built for people who found the big CRMs bloated and went looking for something they would actually open every day.

Email and follow-up features compared

OnePageCRM includes email on its plans, with email tracking from the Business plan ($19.95 a month) upward — you can see when a prospect opens your proposal email, which is genuinely useful when deciding whether to chase or wait. It also has built-in calling and notes, and a strong mobile app, which matters if you sell away from your desk: between meetings, on a train, in a client's lobby. The Action Stream works just as well on a phone as on a laptop.

Capsule includes email integration with Gmail and Outlook even on the free plan, with two-way email sync so messages sent from your inbox attach to the right contact automatically. That is a generous free-tier feature. The catch is that the deeper email features — two-way sync and automation around email — only arrive at the Growth plan, $36 a month. On Starter, you get the basics: email integration, tasks, and follow-up reminders.

Support is one place where the comparison is uneven, and honesty requires flagging it. OnePageCRM's own comparison page states that Capsule offers no phone or live chat support on any plan. That claim comes from a competitor's page, so treat it as OnePageCRM's assertion rather than a neutral fact — but verify it on Capsule's own support pages before you buy, because solo consultants have nobody else to call when the CRM breaks on a deadline day. If responsive help matters to you, test both vendors' support during your trial: send a real question and time the answer.

The follow-up verdict: OnePageCRM's email tracking and mobile app give it the edge for consultants who live in their inbox and on the move. Capsule's free-plan email integration is the better deal if you are starting from zero and want your Gmail or Outlook history attached to contacts without paying anything.

Automation and limits: where each tool stops being enough

Automation is where the price gap between these tools becomes a feature gap. OnePageCRM's Professional plan includes 15 Autoflows — automated sequences, such as sending a follow-up email a set number of days after a call, or moving a deal when an action is completed. Fifteen is enough for a solo consultant's core routines: a new-lead welcome sequence, a proposal follow-up, a post-project check-in. Business adds more pipelines (10, against Professional's single pipeline) and email tracking.

Capsule keeps automation behind its Growth plan at $36 a month. Starter, at $18, has no automation at all — just one pipeline, basic reporting, and manual everything. That is the single most important trade-off in this comparison: Capsule's entry paid plan is nearly twice the price of OnePageCRM's, and still has no automation, while OnePageCRM's cheapest plan includes 15 Autoflows. Growth also adds the full AI suite and dashboards; Advanced at $54 adds 120,000 contacts and 50 pipelines, which no solo consultant needs.

Pipeline limits tell a similar story. OnePageCRM Professional allows 1 sales pipeline; Business allows 10. Capsule Starter allows 1 pipeline; Growth allows multiple; Advanced allows 50. For a solo consultant, one pipeline is usually fine — most run a single flow from first call to signed contract — but if you sell two distinct services with different sales stages, the second pipeline matters, and on both tools it costs the mid-tier price.

Other limits worth knowing: Capsule's free plan allows 10 MB of file storage, which fills quickly if you attach proposals and contracts. OnePageCRM has unlimited contacts on every plan. Who should pick which: three solo consultant scenarios

Scenario 1: the pipeline-heavy consultant. You run discovery calls every week, send proposals, and juggle ten live opportunities at once. Your problem is volume and prioritization: which deal needs attention today, and which one is quietly dying. Pick OnePageCRM. The Action Stream turns a messy pipeline into a daily to-do list, and at $9.95 a month it is the cheapest way to get that discipline.

Scenario 2: the follow-up-heavy consultant. You win work through relationships, not volume — a small number of high-value clients, long sales cycles, referrals that arrive months after the first conversation. Your problem is remembering to stay in touch without being pushy. Pick OnePageCRM again: scheduled next actions on every contact mean nobody falls through the cracks over a six-month sales cycle.

Scenario 3: the project-delivery consultant. You sell a project, then spend six weeks delivering it — workshops, audits, implementation — and you want the deal, the client notes, and the delivery tasks in one tool, not three. Pick Capsule. Its built-in project management is the feature OnePageCRM does not try to match, and for a consultant who lives in delivery as much as in sales, one tool for both halves of the work is worth the higher price. Start on the free plan if your contact list is under 250; move to Starter when you outgrow it, and to Growth only when you genuinely need automation. If you run your whole practice through Gmail, also read our Streak CRM vs HubSpot for Gmail users comparison — a Gmail-native CRM is a third option worth considering.

One scenario that favors neither: if you sell to real estate clients, our best simple CRM for real estate agents guide covers tools built for that workflow instead.

Switching and migration: how to trial both without the mess

Both tools import contacts from a CSV file, so moving in is straightforward: export your contacts from a spreadsheet or your current CRM, map the columns (name, email, company, phone, notes), and import. Check the export options before you commit months of notes to either one, because a CRM you cannot leave is a CRM you will regret.

The smart way to choose is to run both trials back-to-back, not at the same time. Start with OnePageCRM's 21-day free trial: it is full access, so you get the real product, including the Action Stream and Autoflows. Import your real contacts — not a test list — and run your actual week through it: log your calls, schedule your follow-ups, send a proposal. If by day 14 the Action Stream has caught a follow-up you would have missed, you have your answer.

Then run Capsule's 14-day paid trial (and spend a few days on the free plan first, since it costs nothing and shows you the core interface). Import the same contacts, set up your pipeline, and try managing one live client project inside it. Ask yourself two questions at the end: did I open it every day without being reminded, and did it replace a second tool?

One practical warning: do not run both trials simultaneously. You will split your attention, log half your activity in each, and conclude that neither works. Back-to-back trials with real data give you a clean comparison in about five weeks. Confirm the current trial terms on each vendor's pricing page, because trial lengths change.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, OnePageCRM or Capsule?

OnePageCRM is cheaper at every comparable level. Professional is $9.95 a user a month billed annually ($119.40 a year) against Capsule Starter at $18 a month ($216 a year) — a $96.60 annual gap for one solo user. At the automation tier, OnePageCRM Business is $239.40 a year against Capsule Growth at $432 a year. Prices checked November 8, 2026; confirm on the vendor pricing pages.

Does Capsule have a free plan?

Yes. Capsule's free plan covers 2 users, 250 contacts, 1 sales pipeline, email integration with Gmail and Outlook, tasks, follow-up reminders, project management, and 10 MB of file storage. It is a genuine $0 start, but most consultants outgrow the 250-contact ceiling quickly. OnePageCRM has no free plan, only a 21-day free trial.

Does OnePageCRM have automation?

Yes, from the cheapest paid plan. Professional ($9.95 a month) includes 15 Autoflows — automated sequences such as follow-up emails after a call. Capsule only offers automation from its Growth plan at $36 a month; Starter at $18 has none.

What is the OnePageCRM Action Stream?

The Action Stream is OnePageCRM's core feature: every contact has a scheduled next action (call, email, or task), and all of those actions appear in one prioritized list that tells you what to do today. It is designed to stop deals going quiet because nobody scheduled the follow-up.

Can I switch between OnePageCRM and Capsule easily?

Yes. Both tools import contacts from a CSV file. The best approach is to trial them back-to-back with your real contacts — OnePageCRM's 21-day full-access trial first, then Capsule's 14-day paid trial (after trying its free plan). Check each tool's export options before committing months of notes.

Conclusion

OnePageCRM is the better choice for most solo consultants: it costs less, includes automation from its cheapest plan, and its Action Stream attacks the exact problem — forgotten follow-ups — that costs independent consultants the most money. Capsule earns its higher price in two cases: you want a free start with a real $0 plan, or you deliver client projects and want sales and delivery in one simple tool. Do not buy on the feature list — run the trials back-to-back with your real contacts and pick the one you open every morning without being reminded.
