Trello Alternatives: 6 Kanban Tools Compared

  • Project Management
  • Published Oct 10, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

If you want the verdict in ten seconds: pick ClickUp for the most features per dollar, Monday.com for the prettiest boards and dashboards for a non-technical team, Jira if you build software, Asana for the most polished general project manager, Notion if your work is half documents, and Todoist if it is just you. All six are compared below with pricing checked in October 2026.

Trello taught millions of people what kanban is: columns, cards, drag-and-drop. It is still a fine tool. But its free plan is strict in the ways that matter to growing teams — 10 boards per workspace, 250 automation runs a month, 10MB file uploads — and the cards themselves stay flat: no task dependencies, no native time tracking, no timeline views without paying $10 per user a month. The six tools below keep the board you love and add what Trello holds back, at prices checked against vendor pricing pages in October 2026. Vendors change prices often, so treat every figure as "checked this month," not gospel.

Why look beyond Trello

A kanban tool, in the plainest sense, is a visual task board: work items move left to right through columns like To Do, Doing, and Done. Trello is the purest version of that idea, which is exactly why it stops being enough. The free plan gives you unlimited cards but caps the container — 10 boards per workspace — and meters Trello's built-in Butler automation at 250 workspace command runs per month, which a single "move finished cards to Done" rule can chew through in a busy week.

The paid tiers remove those caps but add per-user cost: Standard is $5 per user per month billed annually for unlimited boards and 1,000 automation runs; Premium is $10 per user per month for the extra views (calendar, timeline, table, dashboard, map), unlimited automation, and admin controls. The deeper problem is structural, not financial. Trello cards cannot depend on each other, you cannot see your whole project on a timeline without Premium, and there is no built-in way to track time or workload. If your work has outgrown flat cards, the honest question is not "which Trello plan" but "which tool."

Quick fact: Trello and Jira are both owned by Atlassian, so moving between them is the path of least resistance for one specific audience — software teams, where Jira's free tier (up to 10 users) often beats Trello's Premium on both price and features.

The six alternatives, reviewed

1. Asana — the polished general project manager

What it is: Asana is the most complete general-purpose project manager in this list: boards, lists, timelines, goals, portfolios, and reporting dashboards, all wrapped in one of the cleanest interfaces in the category. If you want to see the same work as a kanban board on Monday and a Gantt chart on Tuesday, Asana is built for that.

Strengths: Timeline and Gantt views arrive at the Starter tier, not the top one; automation rules are unlimited from Starter; paid plans include unlimited free guests, which keeps client collaboration cheap. Our full Asana vs Monday vs ClickUp comparison puts all three side by side.

Limits: The free Personal plan is capped at 2 users — it is a plan for a pair, not a team. Portfolios, workload management, and native time tracking need Advanced at $24.99 per user per month, which is the priciest mid-tier in this guide.

Price: Free for up to 2 users; Starter $10.99/user/mo billed annually ($13.49 monthly); Advanced $24.99 annual.

2. Monday.com — the visual favorite for non-technical teams

What it is: Monday.com boards are spreadsheets that grew a visual brain: columns for status, people, dates, and numbers, wrapped in color and dashboards. It is the tool non-technical teams tend to "get" fastest, and marketing and operations teams are its natural audience.

Strengths: Unlimited free viewers on paid plans, guest access from the Standard tier, and dashboards that turn board data into reports without formulas. It can even double as a lightweight sales board — though if selling is the actual job, our Pipedrive vs Monday CRM comparison is the honest next read.

Limits: Every paid plan has a 3-seat minimum, so a solo user pays $27 a month at entry — the most expensive solo seat in this guide. Worse, Basic ($9/seat) includes zero automations and zero integrations; the real starting point is Standard at $12 per seat per month, with 250 automation actions.

Price: Free for 2 seats (3 boards, 200 items); Basic $9/seat/mo annual ($12 monthly, min 3 seats); Standard $12 annual ($14 monthly).

3. ClickUp — the most features per dollar

What it is: ClickUp's pitch is the "everything app": tasks, documents, whiteboards, goals, chat, and time tracking in one workspace. It is the answer for teams that look at Trello's feature list and think "yes, but also."

Strengths: The Free Forever plan allows unlimited members with kanban, docs, and calendar views — the most generous free team tier here (capped at 60MB storage and 100 automation runs a month). Paid Unlimited is $7 per user per month billed annually, undercutting Asana Starter and Monday Standard, and native time tracking is included from that tier.

Limits: Feature depth is also the complaint: ClickUp can feel overwhelming, and it rewards having one person own the setup. Automation is metered in executions (1,000 a month on Unlimited), and the AI assistant is a paid add-on, not included.

Price: Free Forever $0; Unlimited $7/user/mo annual ($10 monthly); Business $12 annual ($19 monthly).

4. Notion — the docs-and-tasks combo

What it is: Notion is a wiki and database tool where a kanban board is simply one view of a database. If your team's work lives in documents — briefs, meeting notes, product specs — and tasks grow out of those documents, Notion collapses two tools into one.

Strengths: Free solo use is genuinely unlimited on pages and blocks; Plus ($10) removes the team collaboration limits, raises file uploads to unlimited, and extends history to 30 days. Synced databases mean one task can appear in a board, a calendar, and a doc at once. If lead tracking also lives in this workspace, see our best free CRMs for startups before building a sales pipeline in a wiki.

Limits: Kanban is a view, not the core product: there are no sprints, no workload planning, and no native time tracking. Team collaboration on the free plan is deliberately limited (block trial, 5MB files, 7-day history), so real teams pay from day one.

Price: Free for solo use; Plus $10/user/mo annual ($12 monthly); Business $20 annual with full AI.

5. Jira — the software team's answer

What it is: Jira is Atlassian's agile issue tracker: boards, backlogs, sprints, releases, and JQL — a query language that answers questions about your work that other tools cannot. For engineering teams, it is the default; for everyone else, it is usually too much.

Strengths: Free for up to 10 users with boards, backlog, and reports; the deepest agile tooling available (sprints, burndown charts, release tracks on Premium); and a marketplace of thousands of integrations. Since Atlassian also owns Trello, imports are the least painful in this list.

Limits: Jira inverts Trello's model — unlimited boards, limited people — and configuration is genuinely a job in itself. It is heavy, clicky, and overkill for marketing or operations teams. Per-user pricing slides with team size (about $7.91 for Standard and $14.54 for Premium at 300 seats), so run your own headcount through Atlassian's calculator rather than extrapolating.

Price: Free up to 10 users; Standard ~$7.91/user/mo annual; Premium ~$14.54 annual.

6. Todoist — the solo user's speed tool

What it is: Todoist is a personal task manager with a board view, famous for one thing: you type "call the dentist tomorrow at 9am #errands" and it becomes a scheduled, labeled task. For freelancers and solo operators, capture speed beats every feature on this list.

Strengths: The fastest natural-language input of any tool tested, identical apps on every platform with near-instant sync, and a genuinely useful free plan. Pro at $5 a month billed annually ($60 a year) is the cheapest paid upgrade in this guide.

Limits: The free Beginner plan caps you at 5 projects and 5 collaborators per project. There are no Gantt charts, timelines, workload views, or dependencies, and team collaboration stays basic — shared dashboards and role permissions do not exist. This is a personal tool that teams can borrow, not a team tool.

Price: Beginner $0; Pro $5/mo annual ($60/yr, $7 monthly); Business $8/user/mo annual ($96/yr).

How to choose

Match the tool to the shape of your team, not to the feature list. Here is the short decision guide:

  • Just you (freelancer, solo founder): Start with Todoist free; upgrade to Pro at $5 a month if you outgrow the 5-project limit. Notion free is the alternative if you also live in documents.
  • Small team (3–10) that wants more than Trello for the least money: ClickUp — free is genuinely usable, and Unlimited at $7 per user per month is the cheapest serious paid tier in this guide.
  • Non-technical team that wants beautiful boards and dashboards: Monday.com Standard at $12 per seat per month — but remember the 3-seat minimum, which makes it $36 a month before you add anyone.
  • Team that wants docs and tasks in one place: Notion Plus at $10 per user per month billed annually.
  • Software team running sprints: Jira — free up to 10 users, and Premium only when you need the planning and SLA features.
  • Team that wants structure (timelines, portfolios) with the most polished UI: Asana Starter at $10.99 per user per month; budget Advanced at $24.99 if portfolios matter.
  • Happy with Trello's simplicity but hitting limits: Trello Standard at $5 per user per month is still the cheapest fix — unlimited boards, 1,000 automation runs — before you switch ecosystems.

Whatever you shortlist, run the free tier for two weeks with your real boards before paying. The tool your team actually opens beats the tool that wins on paper. If your shortlist is the big three, our Asana vs Monday vs ClickUp deep dive goes further than the summary above.

Moving your boards out of Trello

Switching tools is less painful than it looks if you do it in order:

  • Export first. In Trello, open a board's menu, then Print/Export/Share, and export as JSON (keeps the most detail) or CSV. Do this before canceling anything — some exports get harder once a workspace drops to free.
  • Import with the built-in tools. Asana, Monday.com, ClickUp, and Notion all ship Trello importers. Run one test board before bulk-importing, and check that labels and due dates landed where you expect.
  • Rebuild automations, don't transfer them. Butler rules do not export. Use the move as an audit: most teams keep only about half their old rules once forced to justify them. If your new stack needs automation across tools, our n8n pricing breakdown compares self-hosted and cloud costs for that job.
  • Archive, don't migrate, old work. Exported history is worth keeping, but completed boards rarely get opened again. Import active work; leave the archive in a read-only Trello workspace.
  • Run both for two weeks. Keep Trello read-only while the team settles in. If nobody misses it after two weeks, you picked correctly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free Trello alternative?

For a team, ClickUp's Free Forever plan: unlimited members and kanban boards (with a 60MB storage cap and 100 automation runs a month), compared with Trello's 10-board free limit. For solo use, Todoist's free plan or Notion's free plan work well. Asana's free plan is capped at 2 users, so it only suits a pair.

Why do teams switch from Trello?

The usual triggers are the free plan's 10-board limit, the 250 monthly automation runs, and missing views: timeline, Gantt, and table views need Premium at $10 per user per month. Trello cards also lack native task dependencies and time tracking, so teams that outgrow flat boards pay per user for features rivals include cheaper.

Can I import my Trello boards into another tool?

Yes. Asana, Monday.com, ClickUp, and Notion all offer Trello importers that bring over boards, lists, and cards. Export from Trello first (board menu, then Print/Export/Share), test the import on one board, and expect to re-upload some attachments and rebuild any Butler automation rules.

Which Trello alternative is cheapest for a small team?

ClickUp's Unlimited plan at $7 per user per month billed annually undercuts Asana Starter ($10.99) and Monday.com Standard ($12 per seat with a 3-seat minimum, so $36 a month minimum). For a solo user, Todoist Pro at $5 a month billed annually is the cheapest paid upgrade here. Prices change, so verify before buying.

Is Notion a good replacement for Trello?

It is a good replacement if your work is half documents and half tasks: Notion Plus at $10 per user per month billed annually turns databases into kanban boards alongside wikis and docs. It is a weaker pick if you need sprints, workload planning, or heavy automation — a dedicated project tool like Jira, Asana, or ClickUp fits those needs better.

Conclusion

Trello is still the simplest kanban board you can hand a new team — but once you need more than 10 boards, real automation, or views beyond the board itself, its per-user paid tiers stop being the cheapest answer. For most small teams the value pick is ClickUp: unlimited members free and $7 per user per month for the full paid tier. Non-technical teams that live in dashboards should look at Monday.com, document-heavy teams at Notion, and software teams at Jira, which is free up to 10 users. Start on a free plan with your real boards, give it two weeks, and only then reach for your wallet. Prices shift — verify on the vendor's pricing page before you buy.
