Kit vs Beehiiv for Paid Newsletters: Which Pays Creators More? (2026)

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 14, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

beehiiv pays creators more on paid subscriptions: it takes a 0% platform fee, so you keep everything after Stripe's processing cost. Kit's flat plans start cheaper at small lists but scale steeply with subscribers, and it takes a commerce transaction fee on sales. Choose beehiiv if the newsletter is your business and you want growth and ad tools built in. Choose Kit if you want the strongest automations and tagging, or you sell courses and digital products alongside your newsletter.

Kit and beehiiv are the two platforms creators most often shortlist when they want to charge readers for a newsletter. They look similar on the surface — both sell you email, landing pages, and paid subscriptions — but they come from opposite worlds and take opposite approaches to your money. Kit grew out of ConvertKit, an email marketing tool for creators who also run courses and digital products. beehiiv was built by former Morning Brew staff as a newsletter-first publishing platform with growth and advertising baked in.

This guide is for creators who intend to charge for their newsletter: freelance writers, indie journalists, niche analysts, and course creators adding a paid tier. We compare what each platform costs at realistic list sizes, what each one takes from your subscription revenue, and which extra income tools each offers. Pricing figures were checked against vendor pricing pages and current third-party reviews in mid-October 2026. If you are still weighing free tools before you start charging, see our guide to the best free email marketing tools first.

What is Kit?

Kit (formerly ConvertKit, rebranded in October 2024) is an email marketing platform built for creators who sell things: newsletters, courses, digital products, and coaching. Its core strength is the automation system — visual workflows, tagging, and subscriber segments that let you run a whole creator business from one list. If you have ever compared it against a classic email tool, our Mailchimp vs ConvertKit comparison covers the legacy differences in depth.

Kit's pricing has three tiers. The Newsletter plan is free for up to 10,000 subscribers — the most generous free tier in the category — with unlimited broadcasts, landing pages, and basic commerce, but only one automation, Kit branding, and no live support. The Creator plan starts at $39 per month ($33 per month billed annually) for up to 1,000 subscribers and unlocks unlimited automations and sequences, integrations, two team seats, and no branding. The Creator Pro plan starts at $79 per month ($66 billed annually) and adds the newsletter referral system, Sponsor Network access, advanced reporting and subscriber scoring, and unlimited team seats. Prices scale upward with list size: at 10,000 subscribers Creator costs roughly $139 per month. A September 2025 repricing roughly doubled the entry prices from the old ConvertKit days, which is why older reviews quote much lower numbers.

For paid newsletters specifically, two things about Kit matter. First, it charges a commerce transaction fee on sales — older published rates cite 3.5% + $0.30 per transaction, and a September 2026 review reports the fee may have been reduced, so confirm the current figure on Kit's help docs before you commit. Second, the features that grow a paid newsletter — the referral system and paid recommendations — mostly sit in Creator Pro, the most expensive tier.

What is beehiiv?

beehiiv is a newsletter publishing platform founded in 2021 by former Morning Brew employees Tyler Denk, Benjamin Hargett, and Jake Hurd. It treats the newsletter as the entire business: the editor, website, referral programs, an ad network, and paid subscriptions all live in one dashboard. Its headline promise is a 0% platform fee on paid subscriptions — you keep everything you earn minus Stripe's standard processing fee of about 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.

beehiiv's pricing also has three tiers. Launch is free for up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited sends, but it does not include paid subscriptions, email automations, or the Ad Network. Scale starts at $49 per month ($43 billed annually) at the 1,000-subscriber band and unlocks monetization: paid subscriptions, Boosts, and the Ad Network. Max starts at $109 per month ($96 billed annually) and adds branding removal, up to 10 publications, and priority support. Like Kit, prices step up with list size — a list just past 2,500 subscribers lands around $77 per month on Scale billed annually.

Quick fact: Substack, the third big option, takes a flat 10% of subscription revenue with no tiers and no way to reduce it. Both Kit and beehiiv beat Substack on platform fees — but only if their fixed plan costs fit your list size.

Pricing at 1k, 10k, and 50k subscribers

The single biggest pricing difference is what the bill is based on. Both charge by list size, not by revenue. But Kit's per-subscriber ladder climbs faster than beehiiv's, and Kit's prices were repriced upward in September 2025. Here is the monthly-billing comparison at three realistic stages.

How to read this table

Kit wins the entry round on the free plan: 10,000 free subscribers is unmatched, so a creator still growing a free audience before switching on paid subscriptions pays Kit nothing. beehiiv's Launch free tier caps at 2,500 and blocks paid subscriptions entirely, so you must upgrade to Scale ($49) the moment you start charging.

Past that point, beehiiv's mid-tier is cheaper at every scale: Scale costs $109 versus Kit Creator's $139 at 10,000 subscribers, and $249 versus roughly $379 at 50,000. The catch is that beehiiv's Launch tier cannot monetize at all, while Kit's free tier can sell paid subscriptions (with the commerce fee). If you want beehiiv's referral and branding-free experience, Max at $219 per 10,000 subs costs more than Kit Creator but less than Kit Creator Pro.

Quick fact: Kit counts every subscriber on the bill, including unengaged ones. Before each tier breakpoint, a 90-day-inactive list cleanup can cut the monthly bill by a third or more.

Why beehiiv wins the fee question

beehiiv takes nothing from subscription revenue — the 0% platform fee is its signature policy and it has held it through every repricing. On a $10 subscription, you lose only Stripe's roughly $0.59 per payment. Kit takes a commerce transaction fee on each sale on top of Stripe, which at older published rates of 3.5% + $0.30 means roughly $0.95 per $10 payment instead of $0.59 — an extra $36 per month per 100 paying subscribers that compounds as you grow. (One September 2026 review reports Kit's fee may have been lowered; verify the current rate on Kit's help docs.)

Put differently: at 500 paying subscribers, that fee gap is roughly $180 per month — more than either platform's plan cost at that size.

Features for paid newsletters compared

Email and automation depth

Kit is the clear winner here. Its visual automation builder, tagging, segments, and sequences are built for creators who run funnels: welcome sequences, upsells from free to paid tiers, win-back campaigns for lapsed subscribers. beehiiv has email automations, but reviews consistently describe them as simpler and less flexible. If your paid newsletter is really a product business with lead magnets and nurture sequences, Kit's tooling pays for itself. See our ConvertKit alternatives guide for the wider field.

Editor and publishing

beehiiv's editor is built for publishing: clean long-form writing, a website for your newsletter, and paid post management (which posts are free, which are paywalled) without extra tools. Kit's editor is serviceable but basic — it is an email tool first, and its website features feel like an add-on. beehiiv's Max plan supports up to 10 publications under one account, handy if you run multiple newsletters. Also compare MailerLite vs Kit for free-email strengths.

Deliverability and analytics

Both platforms report strong deliverability. Kit publishes a reported 99.8% delivery rate and offers advanced reporting and subscriber engagement scoring on Creator Pro. beehiiv provides solid analytics plus advertiser-grade metrics from its Ad Network, but its deepest analytics live on the higher tiers. For a solo creator, both give you open and click data that is more than enough.

Growth and monetization tools

This is beehiiv's home turf, and it is the least copied part of its product. Three systems matter:

  • Recommendation network: beehiiv newsletters recommend each other, earning paid recommendation revenue for every subscriber you send to another publication. This is a built-in growth flywheel Kit's free plan charges back to Kit (on Kit's free tier, the recommendation slot earnings go to Kit, not you).
  • Boosts: paid cross-promotion between publications — you can pay to have your newsletter recommended inside other newsletters, or get paid to recommend theirs.
  • Ad Network: sponsors are matched to your newsletter automatically, with performance-based campaigns. Kit's answer is the Sponsor Network, but it lives on Creator Pro — the $79-per-month-and-up tier.

Kit's growth tools are its Creator Network (cross-promotion among Kit creators) and the newsletter referral system on Creator Pro, which rewards readers for referring friends. They are solid, but the ad revenue layer is beehiiv's exclusive edge. A creator whose income model is "subscriptions plus ads" has only one real option here. A creator whose model is "subscriptions plus courses and affiliates" leans Kit, because Kit's commerce and automation stack is built exactly for that mix.

Migration

Both platforms want your business enough to move you for free. beehiiv migrates subscribers, content, and paid subscriptions from other platforms at no cost. Kit offers free migrations for new paid customers, typically those bringing over 5,000 subscribers. Coming from Substack is a well-trodden path on both: beehiiv even markets directly to Substack refugees fleeing the 10% fee.

Which pays more: verdict by creator type

Choose beehiiv if

  • The newsletter IS your business — subscriptions and ads are the income, not a funnel for something else.
  • You want the 0% platform fee so revenue share never eats into growth.
  • You plan to use the Ad Network, Boosts, or paid recommendations as income streams.
  • You want cheaper mid-scale pricing ($109 vs $139 at 10,000 subscribers).
  • You run multiple newsletters and want them under one account (Max plan).

Choose Kit if

  • You sell courses, digital products, or coaching alongside the newsletter.
  • You need advanced automations, tagging, and segments to run upsells and win-back campaigns.
  • You want to start free and monetize later — the free plan covers 10,000 subscribers.
  • You are a team of three or more and need seats (Creator Pro includes unlimited users).
  • You already know ConvertKit's automation model and want to stay in it.

On pure revenue share, beehiiv keeps more money in your pocket at every paid-subscriber count — 0% platform fee beats any transaction fee, and the gap widens as you grow. The one scenario where Kit costs you less overall is a large free audience that converts poorly: Kit's plan bill scales with total subscribers, so a 50,000-subscriber list with few paying readers costs you $379 per month on Kit Creator with almost nothing coming back, while beehiiv Scale costs $249 and you at least get the growth tools to fix the conversion problem. Either way, audit your list before each tier breakpoint.

You do not have to guess. beehiiv's Launch plan is free to 2,500 subscribers for testing the editor and growth tools, and Kit's Newsletter plan is free to 10,000 with paid plans offering a 14-day trial. Run the same free content on both for a month, then move the paid tier to the one whose fee math favors your price point.

Frequently asked questions

Does beehiiv take a cut of my paid subscriptions?

No. beehiiv charges a 0% platform fee on paid subscription revenue. You pay only the flat monthly plan (starting at $49 per month on Scale, monthly billing) plus Stripe's standard processing fee of about 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.

Can I sell paid newsletters on Kit's free plan?

Kit's free Newsletter plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers and includes basic commerce, so you can start selling paid subscriptions without paying a monthly plan fee. Kit charges a commerce transaction fee on sales, and paid plans add unlimited automations, remove Kit branding, and unlock growth tools.

Which is cheaper: Kit or beehiiv at 10,000 subscribers?

At 10,000 subscribers, Kit Creator costs about $139 per month and Kit Creator Pro about $189 per month, while beehiiv Scale costs $109 per month and beehiiv Max $219 per month (all monthly-billing prices checked October 2026). Kit is cheaper only if you do not need Pro's referral and analytics tools.

Can I migrate my paid newsletter from Substack or Kit to beehiiv?

Yes. Both beehiiv and Kit offer free migration services for creators moving from other platforms. beehiiv's team migrates your subscribers, content, and paid subscriptions, and Kit handles free migrations for new paid customers, typically with over 5,000 subscribers.

Is beehiiv better than Kit for a solo creator just starting out?

beehiiv is better if the newsletter itself is your whole business: the Launch plan is free to 2,500 subscribers, paid tiers cost less at scale, and the 0% platform fee plus Ad Network and Boosts give you built-in monetization tools. Kit is better if you want advanced automations, tagging, and landing pages alongside your newsletter, or you run courses and digital products too.

Conclusion

For paid newsletters, beehiiv pays creators more: the 0% platform fee means your revenue share math is the best in the category, its mid-scale pricing undercuts Kit, and the Ad Network plus Boosts add income streams Kit cannot match. Choose Kit instead when the newsletter is one arm of a bigger creator business — courses, digital products, complex automations — or when you want the longest free runway before paying anything. Check the current commerce fee on Kit's help docs and confirm both vendors' plan prices before you subscribe, then pick the platform whose fee structure matches your price point.
