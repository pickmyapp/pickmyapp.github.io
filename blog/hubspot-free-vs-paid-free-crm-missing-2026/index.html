HubSpot free vs paid: what the free CRM is missing (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Oct 21, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

HubSpot's free CRM is genuinely free — no time limit — and includes contact and company management, one deal pipeline, email tracking, meeting scheduling, live chat, forms, 2,000 marketing emails per month, and basic reporting. What it's missing: since September 2024, new free accounts are capped at 2 users and 1,000 contacts; HubSpot branding appears on your emails, forms, and chat widget; there are no workflows or sales sequences, no custom reports, no landing pages, and only 3 email templates. Starter (from roughly $20 per seat per month, less on annual billing) removes the branding and adds simple automation, email sequences, and calling. Real multi-step automation (workflows) starts at Professional, where prices jump to roughly $800–$1,300/month before onboarding.

What the free CRM actually includes

HubSpot's free tier is not a trial — it's a permanent, usable CRM. There's no expiry date, no credit card requirement, and the core database is real: unlimited companies and unlimited deals alongside your contacts, with a deal pipeline you can drag through stages. For a freelancer, a two-person startup, or a side project, the free tier handles the actual work of tracking who you're talking to and where each conversation stands.

The free marketing tools are the reason many small teams start here. You get 2,000 marketing emails per month, forms and pop-up forms for your website, a live-chat widget, meeting-scheduling links, and basic dashboards. Email tracking inside Gmail or Outlook tells you when a prospect opens your message. That's a legitimate starter growth stack at zero dollars, and it explains why HubSpot owns the "free CRM" search result.

One under-appreciated free feature: the basic reporting dashboard and email analytics. Most free CRMs make you pay for any reporting at all; HubSpot gives you the fundamentals — enough to answer "what closed this month?" — before charging you a cent. The hard limits of Free (including the 2024 cuts)

The free tier's limits are specific and mostly undocumented in the marketing copy. Here's the current picture as of 2026:

  • 2 users, 1,000 contacts. Since September 2024, new free accounts are capped at 2 users and 1,000 contacts (older grandfathered accounts may hold higher limits). Contact management beyond that — plus a 1-million-record cap across other standard objects — is the single most common reason teams outgrow Free.
  • 1 deal pipeline. Free includes exactly one pipeline. One sales process is fine at the start; the moment you run separate processes for services vs. products, or new business vs. renewals, you're stuck — or splitting everything into custom stages inside a single pipeline, which gets ugly fast.
  • 2,000 marketing emails per month. Generous for a free tier, but it counts every marketing send, and there's no flexibility: blow past it and your campaigns stop until the next month unless you upgrade.
  • 3 email templates. That's it — three. Any sales motion that needs more than three reusable templates is already paying for them elsewhere.
  • HubSpot branding everywhere. Every marketing email, every form, every chat widget carries HubSpot's badge. For a brand-conscious business, this alone can be the upgrade trigger — and it can't be removed on any free plan, ever.

One caution from buyer reviews: multiple users report that HubSpot locks access to your own records after 30 days of inactivity on the free tier, effectively nudging you toward an upgrade. Free is genuinely free, but it is also a funnel — HubSpot is patient about it, and the patience has teeth.

What Free is missing: the feature gaps

Beyond the numeric caps, Free is missing whole categories of CRM work:

  • No workflows or sales sequences. This is the big one. Free has no marketing automation workflows and no sales sequences — you cannot auto-send a follow-up email when a deal moves stages, enroll leads in a drip, or trigger tasks from behavior. Manual follow-up is your automation strategy.
  • No custom reports. You get basic dashboards, but building your own reports — revenue by rep, conversion by source, cohort analysis — requires a paid plan.
  • No landing pages, no knowledge base. These live in paid hubs. Free covers the CRM core, not the surrounding marketing and service surface.
  • No calling minutes or advanced scheduling. Free lacks the calling tools and the deeper meeting-routing features sales teams rely on.
  • Community support only. No chat or phone support on Free — you're searching the knowledge base and forums when something breaks.

The pattern: Free handles recording your business (contacts, deals, activity) but not automating it. The day your team spends more than an hour a day doing CRM work manually — copying data, sending follow-ups, building spreadsheets because the reports can't — the missing automation is costing you more than Starter would.

What Starter adds (and what it still doesn't)

Starter is sold per hub — Sales Hub Starter, Marketing Hub Starter, Service Hub Starter — at roughly $20 per seat per month on monthly billing, with meaningful discounts on annual billing (around $15/seat). There's also a Starter bundle combining Marketing + Sales + Service Starter at a discount, which is the usual recommendation for very small teams. Here's what the money buys:

  • HubSpot branding removed. Emails, forms, and chat widgets come clean. For most businesses, this is the feature that pays for itself in professionalism.
  • Simple automation and email sequences. Sales Hub Starter adds email sequences (around 500 enrolled contacts) and simple automation — follow-ups that fire themselves. This is "automation" in the useful-but-basic sense: linear sequences, not branching logic.
  • 5x email send limits. Marketing Starter multiplies your contact-tier send limit, so the 2,000/month ceiling stops being a growth blocker.
  • Calling (500 minutes per user per month) and goals tracking. The sales basics get properly usable.

What Starter doesn't add is the stuff people assume comes with paying: true multi-step workflows (that's Professional), lead scoring, A/B testing, custom reporting, and the knowledge base. The most common buyer complaint about HubSpot pricing is really a Starter complaint: you pay to remove the training wheels and discover the bike still needs a bigger frame. The Professional price cliff

This is the part of the HubSpot pricing page that ends upgrade conversations. Professional starts around $800–$1,300 per month depending on the hub — Marketing Hub Professional from about $890/month, Sales Hub Professional from $100/seat with a 5-seat minimum — and it carries mandatory onboarding fees ($1,500–$3,000, not optional) plus annual contracts. Most mid-sized teams buying Marketing + Sales Professional land at $1,300–$1,600/month before contact overage fees.

What justifies it: full workflow automation (multi-step, behavioral triggers), lead scoring, A/B testing, smart content, custom reporting, forecasting, ABM tools, and the features that make HubSpot an actual platform rather than a contact database. The three columns that matter: branding removal happens at Starter, real automation starts at Professional, custom objects and SSO require Enterprise.

Industry data keeps the scale honest: across hundreds of purchases, the median HubSpot annual contract lands around $27,000, with negotiated discounts averaging 18%. HubSpot's pricing is designed to be negotiated at the Professional tier and above — list price is the opening bid, not the final number.

Notice the traps. A 3-person team can't stay on Free — the 2-user cap forces the upgrade, and that's not an accident. The honest sequence for a growing team: stay on Free until exactly two things are true — you've hit a hard cap (users, contacts, sends) and the missing automation is visibly costing staff time. Who should stay free — and who should pay

Stay on Free if: you're a solo operator or two-person team under 1,000 contacts; your sales cycle is simple enough for one pipeline; you send under 2,000 marketing emails a month; and HubSpot branding on your materials doesn't bother you or your customers. At this size, Free is genuinely one of the best deals in SaaS — our best free CRM for startups ranking says as much.

Buy Starter if: a specific paid feature is blocking a workflow — usually branding removal, sequences, or the send limit. Don't buy it aspirationally; buy it when the free cap has a name and a date.

Skip Starter for Professional if: you need branching workflows, lead scoring, custom reporting, or A/B testing on day one. Starter will disappoint you — it doesn't contain those features — and you'll pay twice. Get quotes from two or three HubSpot alternatives at the Professional price point before signing; the onboarding fee alone ($1,500+) buys a lot of someone else's software.

The meta-rule: HubSpot's funnel is engineered to carry you from Free to Starter on frustration and from Starter to Professional on aspiration. Make each upgrade answer a concrete, named problem — and re-audit yearly, because the caps that forced you up (users, contacts, sends) are the same caps that keep pricing you up.

Frequently asked questions

Is HubSpot CRM really free forever?

Yes. HubSpot's free CRM has no time limit and no credit card requirement — contact and company management, one deal pipeline, email tracking, meeting scheduling, live chat, forms, 2,000 marketing emails per month, and basic reporting are all free indefinitely. The catches are the caps: since September 2024 new free accounts are limited to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, HubSpot branding appears on all outgoing materials, and there is no workflow automation. Multiple buyer reviews also report locked record access after 30 days of account inactivity.

How many users and contacts can the free HubSpot CRM have?

New free accounts (created after September 2024) are capped at 2 users and 1,000 contacts, with a 1-million-record limit across other standard objects. Older accounts created before that date may retain higher grandfathered limits. A third user forces an upgrade to a paid plan — that's the most common trigger for the first purchase.

Can you remove HubSpot branding on the free plan?

No. HubSpot branding on marketing emails, forms, and the chat widget can only be removed by upgrading — branding removal starts at the Starter tier (roughly $20/seat/month). For brand-conscious businesses, this is the single most common reason to pay, and there is no free workaround.

What's the cheapest way to upgrade from free HubSpot?

Buy Starter on annual billing for only the seats that need it — annual rates run roughly $15/seat/month versus about $20/seat on monthly billing. The Starter bundle (Marketing + Sales + Service Starter together) is discounted versus buying hubs individually. Don't upgrade preemptively: wait until a specific cap or missing feature is blocking a real workflow, because Starter adds branding removal, simple automation, and sequences — but not true workflows.

When should you choose Professional instead of Starter?

Choose Professional when you need multi-step branching workflows, lead scoring, A/B testing, custom reporting, smart content, or full sales forecasting — Starter contains none of these. The price jump is steep (roughly $800–$1,300/month per hub plus mandatory $1,500–$3,000 onboarding and annual contracts), so get competing quotes at that price point first. Many teams discover that what they actually wanted was in Professional all along, and paying for Starter first was the expensive detour.

Conclusion

HubSpot's free CRM is the rare free tier that earns its reputation — real software, no expiry, genuinely useful for one- or two-person teams. What's missing is equally real: the 2-user and 1,000-contact caps, branding you can't remove, and an automation gap that widens the moment your team does. Starter fixes the surface problems for ~$20/seat/month; Professional fixes the deep ones for ~$1,000+/month plus onboarding. The right move is the boring one: stay free until a named cap blocks you, buy only the seats that need Starter, and skip straight to Professional — with competing quotes in hand — the day you need real workflows.
