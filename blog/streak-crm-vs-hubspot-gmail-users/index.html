Streak CRM vs HubSpot for Gmail users (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Nov 7, 2026
  • Updated: November 2026
  • 13 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Streak and HubSpot both serve Gmail users, but they are built in opposite directions. Streak is a CRM that lives inside Gmail: your pipelines sit in your inbox as columns, and you never open another app. HubSpot is a full CRM platform that connects to Gmail through a Chrome extension, so Gmail becomes one window into a bigger system. The price gap decides it: Streak's CRM starts at Pro, $49 per user per month on annual billing, because its free tier is email tools only, with no pipelines. HubSpot's free CRM is a real CRM for up to two users at $0, and Starter lists at $20 a seat a month. Pick Streak if your whole working day happens in Gmail; pick HubSpot if you need a CRM that grows past Gmail.

Streak vs HubSpot: the short answer

If your entire working day happens inside Gmail, Streak feels like magic for the first month. You open your inbox and your deals are right there, in columns, next to the emails that created them. No tab to switch to, no login to remember, no second interface to learn. For a freelancer or a two-person team that lives in email, that is a genuine advantage — and the only one Streak has, because HubSpot beats it on free plan, automation, reporting, integrations, and price per seat.

The honest frame: Streak is a CRM inside Gmail; HubSpot is a CRM that connects to Gmail. Streak wins if Gmail is the whole job. HubSpot wins if Gmail is one part of a bigger sales motion — calls, meetings, marketing, reporting, and a team that will outgrow the inbox. One more thing: Streak used to have a free CRM tier. It does not anymore. In March 2024 Streak ended its free CRM plan, and the free tier that remains is email productivity tools — tracking, templates, mail merge — with no pipelines and no contact management. Any guide that still calls Streak "free" is quoting a product that stopped existing two years ago.

Gmail integration: Streak lives in Gmail, HubSpot visits it

Streak is a Chrome extension that turns Gmail itself into your CRM. Install it, and pipelines appear as columns inside your inbox. Each deal is a "box" — a row holding the contact, the emails, the notes, and the files for one opportunity, living in the same thread as the conversation. You move a deal from "Contacted" to "Proposal sent" without leaving the email you are reading. There is no separate Streak app to open for daily work; the inbox is the app.

HubSpot takes the opposite approach. The CRM lives at hubspot.com, and Gmail connects to it through the HubSpot Sales Chrome extension and a connected inbox. From inside Gmail you can track opens, use templates, schedule sends, and log emails to contacts — but the pipeline, reporting, and automation all live in HubSpot's own interface. Gmail is a window; HubSpot is the house. That sounds like a disadvantage until your team grows past email: the moment you need a call log, a meeting scheduler, or a forecast, HubSpot already has it, and Streak needs a separate tool. There is also a hard limit: Streak works only with Gmail and Google Workspace. If your company runs on Outlook, Streak is not an option at all — there is no Outlook version, and your pipelines do not export to another CRM in one click. HubSpot connects to Gmail and Outlook alike. On the Gmail experience itself, both track opens, offer templates, and log emails automatically. Streak's edge is that the pipeline is visible while you read email; HubSpot's is that its sidebar also shows the contact's history and deal stage.

Pipelines and automation: simple boards vs a real sales engine

Streak's pipelines are simple by design. You create a pipeline, define its stages, and drag boxes between them — inside Gmail. A pipeline can hold up to 25,000 rows on Pro, and you can run as many pipelines as you like. The limits show up where a growing team eventually needs more: workflow automation is locked behind Pro+ at $69 per user per month, advanced reporting and dashboards are also Pro+, and API access and webhooks need Pro+ too. Pro at $49 gives you the CRM itself — pipelines, contacts, mail merge, basic reports — but the automation that makes a CRM save time costs another $20 a user.

HubSpot's pipeline is a different class of tool. Even the free plan includes a visual deal pipeline with contact and company records, tasks, and email logging. Starter at $20 a seat adds sequences (up to 500 sequence emails per user per day, per 2026 pricing trackers), 50 workflows, and meeting scheduling. Professional at $90 a seat adds forecasting, 300 workflows, playbooks, and custom reporting. The automation is the product, and it starts cheap. The trade-off is complexity: HubSpot's settings are famously deep, and a team that wants a simple board can feel lost in a platform built for marketing, sales, and service at once. Reporting is where the gap is widest: Streak Pro's reporting is basic pipeline totals and stage summaries, while HubSpot's free plan already shows deal totals and activity, Starter adds real dashboards, and Professional adds custom reports and forecasting.

Email tracking, mail merge, and sequences

Email is where Streak's free tier still shines. Even without paying, Streak gives you open tracking, email templates (called snippets), mail merge up to 50 emails a day, and thread management inside Gmail. That is a genuinely useful free tool — it is just not a CRM. The paid plans raise the mail merge ceiling to 1,500 emails a day on Pro and add a small monthly allowance of AI Co-Pilot credits, which draft and summarize emails. For a team that sends a lot of similar outreach from Gmail, mail merge at 1,500 a day justifies Pro on its own.

HubSpot's email tools are broader and more gated. The free plan includes email tracking, templates, and scheduling through the Gmail extension, plus live chat on your website. Sequences — the automated series of follow-up emails and tasks that chase a lead over days — start on Starter, not on free. That is the single most important free-versus-paid line in HubSpot: the free CRM stores and tracks, but it does not follow up for you. If a lead needs three touches over two weeks without anyone remembering, you need a paid HubSpot seat, Starter at $20 or Professional at $90. Where HubSpot pulls ahead is the rest of the toolkit: Starter includes a meeting scheduler, and the platform adds marketing email, forms, and a help desk as you buy more Hubs. Streak has none of those — it is a CRM and an email tool, and everything else is an integration. One practical note: open tracking is increasingly unreliable, because Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates open rates. Treat opens as a rough signal; reply rates and meetings booked are the numbers that matter.

Free plans: Streak's is email tools, HubSpot's is a CRM

The free-plan comparison is the most lopsided part of this article, and the part most older guides get wrong — because they were written before March 2024, when Streak's free CRM still existed. Streak free in 2026: email tracking, snippets (templates), mail merge up to 50 emails a day, and thread management, all inside Gmail. No pipelines, no deal tracking, no contact management, no automations, no reports, no integrations. It is a permanently free Gmail add-on for email productivity. It is not a free CRM: the pipeline view simply is not there until you pay $49 a user.

HubSpot free in 2026: a real CRM for up to two users and 1,000 contacts, with contact and company records, a deal pipeline, tasks, email logging and tracking, and live chat. No sequences, workflows, forecasting, or custom reporting — but the core job of a CRM works on the free plan, indefinitely, with no credit card. The catch is the one every HubSpot user learns: the third user forces a paid seat, and the jump from Starter to Professional is steep — $90 a seat plus a $1,500 onboarding fee. For the full breakdown of what sits behind each tier, see our guide to HubSpot free vs paid: what the free CRM is missing.

The practical consequence: a two-person team can run on HubSpot free for $0 and get a real pipeline, while the same team on Streak pays $98 a month for Pro. A solo freelancer can run on Streak Solo at $15 a month, or on HubSpot free for $0. Our guide to the best free CRM for startups ranks the $0 options across the market — and Streak's free tier, honestly, does not make that list as a CRM.

Who should pick which

Pick Streak if: your whole working day happens in Gmail, your team is one to three people, your sales process is a simple board with five or six stages, and you will never need Outlook, marketing automation, or a manager's forecast. The in-inbox pipeline is genuinely pleasant, setup takes an afternoon, and Pro at $49 a user is a fair price for a tool you will live in all day. Solo at $15 a month is the cheapest honest way for a single freelancer to get a Gmail CRM — just know that adding a teammate means Pro pricing.

Pick HubSpot if: you want a free CRM for two users, you need sequences and workflows without paying $69 a seat, you might ever use Outlook, you want reporting and forecasting, or your team will grow past five people. Starter at $20 a seat is the cheapest paid CRM in this comparison by a distance. Go in with your eyes open on the Professional jump ($90 a seat plus $1,500 onboarding) and the two-user free limit. For the wider comparison, our HubSpot vs Pipedrive for small teams guide covers a rival with no onboarding fee, and our Attio vs HubSpot for startups comparison covers the startup that beats HubSpot on data-model flexibility.

Pick neither yet if: you are a solo operator with ten deals and a spreadsheet that works. A CRM you do not use is worse than a spreadsheet you do use. When the deals stop fitting in your head, run both free for two weeks with your real inbox and pipeline, and keep the one you enjoy using daily. Frequently asked questions

Is Streak CRM really free?

No — not as a CRM. Streak ended its free CRM tier in March 2024. The free tier that remains is email productivity tools only: open tracking, templates, and mail merge up to 50 emails a day, with no pipelines and no contact management. The cheapest Streak plan with CRM features is Pro at $49 per user per month on annual billing ($59 monthly); Solo at $15 a month is for individual freelancers.

Is Streak or HubSpot better for Gmail users?

Streak is better if your whole working day happens inside Gmail: its pipelines live in your inbox as columns, so you never leave Gmail. HubSpot is better if Gmail is one part of a bigger sales motion: it connects to Gmail through a Chrome extension, but the CRM, automation, and reporting live in HubSpot's own platform — and its free CRM covers two users for $0 while Streak's CRM starts at $49 a user.

Does HubSpot work with Gmail?

Yes. HubSpot connects to Gmail through its Sales Chrome extension and a connected inbox: you get email tracking, templates, snippets, and scheduling from inside Gmail, and emails log to contact records automatically. Sequences and workflows need a paid Starter seat ($20 a seat at list). HubSpot also works with Outlook, while Streak is Gmail-only.

How much does Streak cost for a 5-person team?

Streak Pro costs $245 a month for five users on annual billing ($49 per user, $2,940 a year). If the team needs workflow automations, advanced reports, or API access, those sit on Pro+ at $69 a user — $345 a month for five. By comparison, five HubSpot Starter seats cost $100 a month at list price ($35 on the $7 new-customer promo).

Can I migrate from Streak to HubSpot?

Yes, but it is a manual job, not a one-click import. Streak exports contacts and pipeline data as CSV, and HubSpot imports CSV, but pipeline stages, email history, and automations do not transfer cleanly — expect to rebuild stages and sequences by hand. Budget a day for a small pipeline and a week for a large one.

Conclusion

Streak vs HubSpot is not a close call on value — it is a close call on workflow. Streak wins the Gmail experience outright: no other CRM puts your pipeline inside your inbox, and for a one-to-three-person team that lives in email, that is worth real money. But Streak's CRM starts at $49 a user a month, its free tier has no pipelines, and automations cost $69 a user. HubSpot gives two users a real CRM for $0, charges $20 a seat for Starter with sequences included, and works with Gmail and Outlook alike. Pick Streak if Gmail is the whole job. Pick HubSpot if Gmail is one part of a sales motion that will grow.
