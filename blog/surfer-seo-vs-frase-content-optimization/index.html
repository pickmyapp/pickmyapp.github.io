Surfer SEO vs Frase for Content Optimization (2026)

  • AI Writing Tools
  • Published Oct 11, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Surfer SEO and Frase solve the same problem — content that ranks on Google — from opposite ends. Surfer is the optimization specialist: an NLP-driven Content Score, a SERP Analyzer, and Content Audit tools that sharpen drafts and refresh old pages. Frase is the research-to-draft generalist: it analyzes the top 20 ranking pages, auto-generates a structured content brief, and includes an AI writer to draft from it. Freelancers publishing under 10 articles a month get more for their money from Frase Starter ($49/mo, $39 annual), where briefs and AI writing are bundled. Teams and agencies optimizing at volume or refreshing big back catalogs get more from Surfer (entry around $49-99/mo after its 2026 repricing), whose scoring depth and audit features scale better.

Every SEO content tool promises the same thing: write what Google wants to rank. But Surfer SEO and Frase approach that promise from opposite directions, and picking the wrong one costs you a subscription you will barely open. This guide compares them on the things that actually matter for content marketers, freelancers, and small teams — pricing after both tools repriced in 2026, the content-editor workflow, scoring quality, SERP analysis depth, AI writing add-ons, and how each handles the new reality of AI search. Prices were checked against vendor pricing pages and current third-party reviews in October 2026. If you are choosing the tool that will draft your content in the first place, read our roundup of the best AI writing tools for small business first.

What Surfer SEO does

Surfer SEO is a content-optimization platform built around data-driven scoring: it analyzes the pages already ranking for your keyword and tells you exactly how your draft should be structured, worded, and worded again to compete with them. Its signature surface is the Content Editor, a writing interface with a live Content Score that rises as you add the terms, headings, and structural elements the top-ranking pages share.

Surfer's strength is precision. It uses natural-language processing to identify which terms and topics correlate with rankings for a specific query, then breaks them into a guideline: target word count, heading structure, image count, and a list of terms to include with recommended frequencies. Beyond single articles, it offers a Content Audit feature that re-scores your published pages against the current search results and tells you what to update — valuable for sites with a back catalog that has decayed. Its Grow Flow feature suggests ongoing optimization tasks, and higher tiers include internal-linking suggestions and AI-visibility tracking. Surfer does not try to be a full SEO suite: rank tracking is limited and most teams pair it with a tool like Ahrefs or Semrush for keyword research and backlink data.

What Frase does

Frase is an AI-powered content platform that takes you from keyword research to a finished draft: it analyzes the top 20 pages ranking for your target query, extracts their headings, questions, and topics, and auto-generates a structured content brief that you or its built-in AI writer turn into an article. Its signature workflow is research-first rather than score-first.

Frase's defining feature is the automated brief. Instead of showing you a list of terms to sprinkle into a draft, it assembles an outline — suggested headings, the questions searchers are actually asking (via its Question Finder, which surfaces "People Also Ask" data), topic clusters, and stats worth citing. You then write against that brief inside Frase's editor, which scores your draft against the top results as you go, and its AI writer can generate sections directly from the brief. Frase also tracks how often your content gets cited in ChatGPT and Perplexity answers, a metric aimed squarely at the AI-search era. The tool positions itself as the complete content-research-and-drafting workspace for teams that want one subscription covering briefs, writing, and optimization.

The core difference: scores vs briefs

The fastest way to tell which tool fits your process is to look at where each one starts. Surfer starts with your draft and optimizes it. Frase starts with the search results and plans your draft.

A Surfer workflow looks like this: you paste a keyword into the Content Editor, Surfer generates guidelines from the current rankings, and you write or paste a draft into the editor while watching the Content Score climb. The tool is reactive — it judges what you produce against the data and nudges you toward the winning pattern. Writers who already know how to write but want their content to rank find this natural, because the writing comes first and the data refines it.

A Frase workflow looks like this: you enter a keyword, Frase returns an auto-generated brief with headings, questions, and topics pulled from the top 20 results, and you build the article from that brief — either writing it yourself in Frase's editor or letting the AI writer draft sections. The tool is proactive — it does the research phase for you. Writers and editors who spend too long staring at competitor articles find this faster, because the "what should this article cover" question is answered before a word is written.

Neither workflow is inherently better; they match different bottlenecks. If your bottleneck is ranking quality — you write plenty but underperform — Surfer's scoring discipline helps. If your bottleneck is throughput — research and outlining eat your hours — Frase's briefs help. Many freelancers discover that what they actually want is a faster brief, while many agencies discover that what they want is a sharper score across a big portfolio.

What the same content program costs on each

Take a freelance writer publishing 8 articles a month for clients. Frase Starter at $39 a month (annual) covers 10 articles — one subscription, briefs, AI writing, and optimization included. Surfer's entry plan at roughly $49-99 a month covers around 120 documents a year (10 a month) with the core editor. At this volume, Frase is usually the cheaper and more complete single tool, because the brief and the AI writer come in the same box.

Now take a three-person content team publishing 30 articles a month and auditing a 500-page back catalog. Frase Professional at $103 a month (annual) covers 40 articles and 3 seats — it fits, but the 250-audit-page monthly limit constrains big refresh programs. Surfer's mid tiers, while pricier per month, are built for exactly this: higher document allowances, deeper scoring, and audit workflows. For teams, the per-seat, per-document math increasingly favors Surfer as volume grows — a pattern several 2026 hands-on comparisons have noted.

How does content scoring compare?

Surfer's Content Score: the deeper model

Surfer's scoring is the more sophisticated of the two. It derives its Content Score from NLP analysis of the top-ranking pages for your exact query — not just keyword density, but which terms and phrases correlate with ranking, broken down by recommended frequency ranges, plus structural signals like word count, heading counts, paragraph length, and image usage. The guidelines adapt per query rather than applying a generic template, and the score updates live as you write. Reviewers consistently rate Surfer's score as the more trustworthy signal of "does this draft look like what ranks," which is why it remains the reference point the category is measured against.

Frase's topic score: simpler but actionable

Frase scores your draft against the top 20 pages it extracted, showing topic coverage — which concepts from the brief appear in your text and which are missing. It is less granular than Surfer's NLP model: reviewers describe it as a coverage checklist rather than a deep statistical model. That simplicity is a feature for beginners, because "you are missing these 6 topics" is easier to act on than a term-frequency table. For experienced SEOs who want to understand exactly why a page scores 72 instead of 85, Surfer gives more to work with.

The honest takeaway: if scoring rigor is the purchase criterion, Surfer wins. If you want a score that is "good enough" attached to a faster research workflow, Frase's score is adequate — and many freelancers never feel the difference.

How does SERP analysis compare?

Frase: deep extraction of the top 20

Frase's SERP analysis is one of its strongest features. It pulls the top 20 ranking pages for your query and extracts their full content structure — headings, subtopics, questions, and the statistics they cite — then turns that extraction into the automated brief. You see what the ranking pages actually cover, not just a keyword list. The Question Finder layers in "People Also Ask" style queries so your article answers the exact questions searchers type. For writers building an outline from scratch, this is the single most time-saving feature in either tool.

Surfer: the SERP Analyzer and audit engine

Surfer's SERP Analyzer examines the ranking pages' content structure, common backlink patterns, and on-page factors, and pairs it with keyword research and the Content Audit tool. Where Frase's extraction feeds a brief, Surfer's analysis feeds scoring guidelines and refresh recommendations — it is oriented toward "what should change in this existing page" as much as "what should the new page say." One cost to watch: at least one 2026 review reports the SERP Analyzer as a $29 add-on on Surfer's entry plan, so verify what is bundled at your tier. Both tools integrate with Google Docs and WordPress, though the integrations sit on higher tiers.

How do the AI writing add-ons compare?

This is where the two tools diverge most sharply in philosophy. Frase treats AI writing as part of the core product: the AI writer drafts sections directly from the generated brief on every plan, since plans differ by volume and team size rather than by capability. For a freelancer who wants one subscription that goes from keyword to draft, that bundling is the appeal — and it pairs naturally with a dedicated drafting tool like the ones in our AI writing tools roundup.

Surfer treats AI writing as a premium layer. Its higher tiers (the Scale AI tier at roughly $219 a month in older reviews) include Surfer AI article generation, and reviewers note the output is serviceable but behind dedicated AI writing tools — one hands-on 2026 test described it as "a well-organized skeleton" needing a full rewrite of the introduction. Surfer's historical strength was its integration with Jasper, and many teams still pair Surfer's scoring with a separate writer like Jasper or Copy.ai (see our Jasper vs Copy.ai comparison). Rule of thumb: if you want the brief and the draft in one tool, Frase. If you want the sharpest optimization of text written elsewhere, Surfer.

What about AI-search (GEO) visibility tracking?

In 2026, "did it rank on Google" is only half the question — the other half is "did ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overviews cite it." Both tools have responded. Frase tracks an inclusion-rate metric showing how often your content appears in AI-generated answers, building on its 2025 AI-visibility launch. Surfer tracks AI Overview and brand visibility, with weekly refreshes on its Standard tier and daily refreshes on Pro (one 2026 review quotes a standalone AI Tracker at $95 a month as an add-on for 25 prompts). These are genuinely useful for content teams in 2026, but treat them as higher-tier features on both platforms, not entry-level ones. Freelancers vs teams: our verdict

Choose Frase if

  • You are a freelancer or solo marketer publishing up to 10 articles a month and want briefs, AI writing, and optimization in one subscription.
  • Your bottleneck is research and outlining — the auto-generated briefs from the top 20 results save you the most time.
  • You want a 7-day free trial with no credit card to test everything before paying.
  • You value one tool covering the whole research-to-draft workflow over the deepest scoring model.

Choose Surfer if

  • You are a content team or agency optimizing at volume, refreshing a large back catalog, or managing multiple domains.
  • Scoring rigor matters to you — Surfer's NLP-driven Content Score and Content Audit are the category's sharpest instruments.
  • You already write well and want data to sharpen drafts, rather than needing briefs to start them.
  • You want the deepest AI-visibility and internal-linking tooling at team scale.

Budget reality check: at solo volume the two tools cost roughly the same per month now, so decide on workflow, not price. Frase wins when research is your bottleneck; Surfer wins when ranking precision is. Whichever you pick, remember the uncomfortable truth every honest review lands on: a perfect optimization score does not guarantee a top ranking. Backlinks, site authority, and genuinely original insight still decide the close races — the tool just makes sure your content is not losing on structure.

Frequently asked questions

Is Frase cheaper than Surfer SEO?

At entry level they now start close together: Frase Starter is $49 a month ($39 billed annually) and Surfer entry plans are reported around $49 to $99 a month after its 2026 repricing. Frase tends to be cheaper for solo users because briefs and AI writing are bundled; Surfer gets more expensive as you add team seats and AI-visibility tracking. Prices checked October 2026 and subject to change.

Does Surfer SEO have a free trial?

Not a conventional one. Surfer offers a 7-day money-back guarantee on paid plans, so you pay first and ask for a refund if it is not for you. The free Keyword Surfer Chrome extension shows search volume data on Google results without paying. Frase is more generous here: a 7-day free trial with full feature access and no credit card required.

Which is better for optimizing existing content, Surfer or Frase?

Surfer is stronger for optimizing existing pages. Its Content Audit feature re-scores published pages against current top-ranking results and flags exactly what to add, cut, or restructure, which is ideal for refreshing a back catalog. Frase can audit pages too, but its audit limits are tight on the entry plan (50 pages a month on Starter) and the tool is oriented more toward new content.

Can Frase write the article for me, or does it only optimize?

Both, within limits. Frase generates a full content brief from SERP research (headings, questions, topics to cover) and its built-in AI writer can draft sections from that brief. Surfer is primarily an optimizer: its higher tiers include Surfer AI article generation, but the core product scores and guides content you or your writers produce. Neither replaces a human editor for accuracy and original insight.

Do Surfer and Frase help content get cited by AI search like ChatGPT?

Both added AI-visibility tracking in 2025-2026. Frase monitors how often your content appears in ChatGPT and Perplexity answers (an inclusion-rate metric). Surfer tracks AI Overview and brand visibility, refreshed weekly on the Standard tier and daily on Pro. Tracking citations is included or bundled on higher tiers; it is not a free-plan feature on either tool.

Conclusion

Surfer SEO and Frase are both excellent at what they were built for — the mistake is buying one for the other's job. Choose Frase when your scarcest resource is research time: its automated briefs and bundled AI writing compress the keyword-to-draft pipeline, and at $39 a month on annual billing the Starter plan is the best single-tool value for freelancers. Choose Surfer when your scarcest resource is ranking precision at scale: its NLP-driven Content Score, Content Audit, and deeper SERP tooling are the sharpest instruments in the category, and they pay off fastest for teams managing many pages. Start with Frase's no-card free trial to feel the brief workflow, and check Surfer's current pricing page before you commit — its 2026 repricing means the number in older reviews may be stale. Either way, treat the tool as a structure-checker, not a strategy: original reporting and real expertise are still what win the queries worth winning.
