Zoho CRM vs HubSpot for Small Business: Which Is Worth It? (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Oct 13, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 12 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most businesses with 1 to 20 people, Zoho CRM wins on total cost of ownership and HubSpot wins on ease of use. Both have real free plans: HubSpot covers up to 2 users, Zoho covers up to 3. Once you pay, Zoho's Standard plan ($14/user/month billed annually) undercuts HubSpot's Starter ($15/seat/month), and Zoho's priciest CRM tier ($52/user/month) costs less than one HubSpot Professional seat ($90/month) — before HubSpot's mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee. Pick HubSpot only if your team needs zero learning curve and native marketing tools, and your budget survives the upgrade cliff.

Zoho CRM and HubSpot are the two systems small businesses shortlist most often, and they are priced for two different kinds of buyer. HubSpot sells simplicity: a polished free CRM that your team will actually open every day, with the bill arriving later. Zoho sells value: a deeper, more customizable CRM where the per-user price stays modest even as your team grows. This guide compares them through a small-business lens — real pricing checked in October 2026, free plans, setup effort, email integration, and what it costs to leave — with a verdict keyed to your company size and budget rather than enterprise feature parity. If you are starting from zero users, our guide to the best free CRMs for startups is a useful companion.

What is Zoho CRM?

Zoho CRM is a sales-focused customer relationship manager sold per user per month, and one piece of Zoho's suite of more than 45 business apps. Zoho Corporation, headquartered in Chennai, India, has been building business software since the mid-1990s, and the CRM is its flagship: lead and deal management, workflow automation, custom modules, an AI assistant called Zia, and integrations across the rest of the Zoho ecosystem.

The product's reputation is consistent across review sites. On G2 it holds about 4.2 out of 5 from more than 10,000 reviews: users praise the affordability and the depth of customization, and criticize the learning curve and uneven support response times. The median Zoho CRM contract lands around $540 per year, which tells you who buys it — small teams watching every dollar.

What is HubSpot?

HubSpot is an all-in-one customer platform built around a free CRM, with paid "hubs" for marketing, sales, service, content, and operations sold per seat. Founded in Cambridge, Massachusetts, HubSpot now serves more than 288,000 companies, and its G2 rating of about 4.4 out of 5 from nearly 35,000 reviews reflects what users consistently say: the interface is the easiest in the category, adoption is high, and the bill climbs fast once you outgrow the entry tiers.

HubSpot's pricing is seat-based: most people only need a Core Seat for editing records, while Sales Seats unlock tools like sequences and forecasting — and view-only seats are free and unlimited. Our GoHighLevel vs HubSpot breakdown digs further into the platform pricing.

The entry tiers are close — $14 versus $15 a month. The gap opens at the mid tier and never closes. A 5-person team pays about $1,380 a year for Zoho CRM Professional. That 5× difference is the single most important number in this comparison.

Quick fact: Zoho's most expensive CRM tier, Ultimate at $52/user/month, still costs less than a single HubSpot Professional seat at $90/month.

HubSpot's upgrade cliff: where the real cost hides

HubSpot's Starter tier looks affordable because it is — the cliff is the step after it. The features that make a CRM genuinely useful for a growing sales team — email sequences, sales automation, forecasting, playbooks, custom reporting — live on Professional, where the per-seat price jumps 6× and a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee appears. A three-seat Professional team pays roughly $4,740 in year one before any add-ons; a ten-seat team pays about $12,300.

Marketing costs escalate separately: Marketing Hub Professional starts around $890 per month for 3 seats, and the full Professional Customer Platform runs about $1,300 per month for 5 seats — and contact-based pricing means your marketing bill grows with your database, not just your team. Our HubSpot vs Pipedrive guide covers a cheaper sales-first alternative.

How to keep a HubSpot bill down

  • Use free view-only seats for anyone who only reads reports — they cost nothing and are unlimited.
  • Buy Core Seats, not Sales Seats, for team members who edit records but never run sequences or forecasting.
  • Stay on Starter as long as possible. It includes basic automation and calling, which covers many small teams' real needs.
  • Watch the marketing contact count. It is the quietest driver of HubSpot invoices.

Zoho One: the bundle that changes the math

Zoho's trump card is not the CRM alone — it is Zoho One, the bundle of 45+ apps (CRM, Books, Desk, Projects, People, Analytics, Campaigns, Creator, and more) for about $37 per employee per month billed annually, with a flexible $90/user/month option if you only want to license some people. Every employee gets every app under one invoice.

Compare that with HubSpot's full Professional Customer Platform at roughly $1,300 per month for 5 seats — about $260 per seat per month for the comparable all-in stack. The rule of thumb from pricing analysts: once you regularly use three or more Zoho apps, the One bundle beats buying them separately, and it beats HubSpot's bundle by an order of magnitude. A business running CRM, accounting, helpdesk, and HR on Zoho One pays $37 per head; assembling HubSpot + QuickBooks + a helpdesk + an HR tool typically costs three to five times more.

Caveats: the $37 plan requires licensing every employee on payroll, quality varies across the 45 apps, and support response times are a recurring complaint. But for a 10-to-20-person business that needs several tools, nothing in this price range comes close.

Which is easier to set up and use?

HubSpot wins this clearly, and it is the main reason teams pay the premium. The interface guides new users through checklists and pre-built dashboards, automation is drag-and-drop, and most teams are operational in 2 to 6 weeks without outside help. High adoption keeps data clean — practitioners call this HubSpot's "UI tax": you pay more, but people actually use the thing.

Zoho is the mirror image: more configurable but denser, with menu sprawl and inconsistent terminology slowing new users, and multi-app rollouts typically taking 2 to 4 months. Teams that invest in training adopt it much faster — but that is still time spent learning instead of selling. This is Zoho's "training tax." If you are a freelancer or solo consultant who needs something running this week, our CRM guide for freelancers leans toward the simplest options.

Customization depth

The tradeoff for HubSpot's simplicity is shallower customization: fewer custom modules and stricter limits on reshaping the system. Zoho is developer-friendly, with up to hundreds of custom modules on Enterprise and Ultimate tiers, custom functions, and an API ceiling that grows with the plan. If your sales process is unusual — manufacturing quotes, territory rules, custom approval chains — Zoho bends further. If your process is standard, HubSpot's opinionated structure is a feature, not a bug.

Email integration and marketing tools

Both CRMs sync with Gmail and Outlook for sending and tracking sales emails. The difference is marketing automation. HubSpot's Marketing Hub lives in the same database as the CRM — campaigns, contact history, deal stages, and ad tracking share one record, with visual journey builders and A/B testing built in.

Zoho handles email marketing through Zoho Campaigns, a separate add-on with its own pricing (it scales with your contact list) and its own console. It connects to the CRM, but power users describe it as an integration rather than one system. If email marketing is central to your business — newsletters, drip campaigns, list growth — HubSpot's native approach saves real friction, and our cheapest email marketing software roundup can help you price the alternatives. If email is just follow-ups and quotes, Zoho's built-in tools are enough.

What does migrating involve?

Moving between these two CRMs — or from spreadsheets into either — is a data project, not a button click. Contacts, companies, and deals export as CSV from both systems and import cleanly into the other. What does not transfer: workflow and automation rules, email templates, custom reports and dashboards, and scoring models. Those get rebuilt by hand.

Plan on a weekend of cleanup and import for a few thousand records with simple fields. For a large database with custom fields, deduplication, and active automations, budget several weeks and run the old and new systems in parallel for at least one sales cycle. Either way, clean your data before you move it — migrating dirty data just gives you an expensive dirty CRM.

Which should you choose: Zoho CRM or HubSpot?

Choose Zoho CRM if

  • Your budget is the deciding factor — it wins on total cost at every paid tier.
  • You have 3 to 20 users and want room to grow without a 6× price jump.
  • You need deep customization, custom modules, or unusual sales processes.
  • You will also use accounting, helpdesk, or HR tools — the Zoho One bundle is unmatched value.
  • Someone on the team can own setup and training.

Choose HubSpot if

  • Ease of use and fast adoption matter more than the invoice.
  • Marketing automation is core to how you win customers — native beats add-on.
  • Your team is non-technical and you want to be live in weeks, not months.
  • You can afford the upgrade cliff: budget roughly $7,000+ in year one once you need Professional features.
  • You want one vendor for CRM, marketing, and service with a single shared record.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zoho CRM really cheaper than HubSpot?

Yes, at nearly every paid tier. Zoho CRM Professional costs $23 per user per month billed annually, while HubSpot Sales Hub Professional costs $90 per seat per month plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee. For a 5-person team, year one on Zoho Professional is about $1,380; the same team on HubSpot Professional pays about $6,900 in year one.

Is HubSpot's free CRM actually free?

Yes. HubSpot's free CRM is free forever for up to 2 users, with contact management, one deal pipeline, email integration, and meeting scheduling. The limits that push you to paid plans are advanced automation, email sequences, extra pipelines, and removing HubSpot branding.

What is the HubSpot onboarding fee, and can I skip it?

Onboarding is mandatory on HubSpot's Professional and Enterprise tiers: $1,500 one-time for Sales Hub Professional, $3,000 for Marketing Hub Professional, and $3,500 for Sales Hub Enterprise. You cannot skip it by signing up yourself. Starter plans have no onboarding fee, and Zoho CRM charges no onboarding fees at any tier.

Can I migrate from HubSpot to Zoho CRM, or the other way around?

Yes, both ways, but expect real work rather than a one-click move. Contacts, companies, and deals export as CSV and import cleanly; workflows, automation rules, email templates, and custom reports have to be rebuilt. Budget a weekend for a few thousand records and several weeks for a large database with heavy customization.

Which is better for email marketing: Zoho or HubSpot?

HubSpot, because Marketing Hub is built into the same database as the CRM, so campaigns, contact history, and pipeline share one record. Zoho handles email marketing through Zoho Campaigns, a separate add-on with its own pricing and console, which works fine but feels less unified out of the box.

Conclusion

For a 1-to-20-person business choosing on total cost of ownership, Zoho CRM is the better buy: lower per-user prices at every tier, no onboarding fees, and the Zoho One bundle that replaces half your software stack for $37 per employee per month. HubSpot earns its premium when ease of use is worth paying for — non-technical teams, marketing-led growth, rollouts that must succeed in weeks. Start free with either, and budget HubSpot's upgrade cliff before you hit it, not after.
