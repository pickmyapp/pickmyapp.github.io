Cloudways vs SiteGround for Agencies

  • Web Hosting
  • Published Oct 11, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Cloudways is the better agency workhorse: from $11/month on DigitalOcean with flat hourly billing (no renewal shock), unlimited client sites per server, one-click staging, and server cloning. SiteGround is the better client-facing platform: white-label Site Tools, granular client access, and custom hosting plans let clients manage their own sites without seeing SiteGround branding. Real prices: SiteGround GrowBig renews at $29.99/month (intro $4.99) and GoGeek at $44.99/month (intro $7.99), both 12-month prepaid. Summary: pick Cloudways for cost-per-client economics and control; pick SiteGround when clients log in themselves. Prices checked October 2026.

Agencies don't buy hosting the way bloggers do. You need to put ten or thirty client sites somewhere, keep them fast, stage updates before they touch production, give some clients access without giving them the keys to everything, and ideally resell the hosting at a margin. Cloudways and SiteGround both pitch agencies hard, but they're different products underneath: Cloudways is managed cloud infrastructure with a flat price, SiteGround is managed shared hosting with a white-label client layer. This guide compares them on the agency dimensions that matter — client management, white-label, staging, real pricing — with numbers checked in October 2026. For single-site pricing context, see our best cheap WordPress hosting guide.

What each host is built for

Cloudways (owned by DigitalOcean since 2022) is managed cloud hosting: you pick a cloud provider — DigitalOcean, Vultr, Linode, AWS, or Google Cloud — pick a server size, and Cloudways manages the stack on top. The product line in 2026 splits into Flexible (you control the server, hourly billing, scale vertically) and Autonomous (auto-scaling WordPress with Cloudflare Enterprise bundled). Agencies live on Flexible: unlimited applications per server, staging, cloning, team access, and SSH/SFTP per app. Billing is hourly with a monthly cap, so you pay for what you run with no contracts.

SiteGround is premium managed shared hosting with an agency layer on top: white-label Site Tools, granular client access, custom hosting plans you define and resell, and private DNS. Each plan is a shared environment with visit caps (roughly 100,000 monthly visits on GrowBig, 400,000 on GoGeek) and automatic CPU/RAM scaling when a site spikes. The agency pitch is the client experience: your client logs into a dashboard with your branding, manages their site, and never sees SiteGround. The catch is the billing model: cheap intro prices for a 12-month prepay, then renewals at 5–6x.

The headline most comparisons quote — "$4.99 a month for GrowBig!" — is a 12-month prepaid intro. The price you will actually pay in year two and beyond is $29.99 a month for GrowBig and $44.99 for GoGeek. Cloudways has no such trap: the $11/month DigitalOcean entry price is the ongoing price, billed hourly with a monthly cap, and a 2GB server runs about $26 a month. When you model agency costs, always model the renewal rate — it's the only number that matters past month twelve.

Client management and white-label

This is SiteGround's home turf and Cloudways' weakest category. SiteGround's GrowBig and GoGeek plans include white-label Site Tools: you grant clients granular access to their sites under your branding, build custom hosting plans to resell at your own prices, and use free private DNS. A client can log in, manage files, databases, and backups, and never see the word "SiteGround." That's a genuine reseller workflow, and it's why agencies that want clients self-serving under their own brand pick SiteGround.

Cloudways has no true white-label client portal. What it offers instead is team collaboration: add team members (or clients) with role-based access to servers and applications, plus per-app SSH/SFTP credentials. It's workable for an agency that manages everything itself and gives a client read-only-ish access, but there's no branded client dashboard and no custom-plan resale mechanics. If your business model is "clients log into my hosting brand," SiteGround wins outright. If your model is "I manage the hosting, clients never touch it," Cloudways' gap here costs you nothing.

Staging, cloning, and dev workflows

Both platforms offer one-click staging: SiteGround includes it on GrowBig and up, Cloudways on all Flexible plans. For agencies, the workflows beyond basic staging matter more. Cloudways adds server cloning — duplicate an entire server's configuration when onboarding similar clients — plus unlimited applications per server, which makes it cheap to keep a staging server or a template server alongside production. SSH access per application, Git integration, and WP-CLI access come standard.

SiteGround's dev tooling is solid but shared-hosting-shaped: staging, Git on GoGeek and up, WP-CLI, and a WordPress Migrator plugin that moves unlimited sites for free. The one cost to note: SiteGround charges $30 per site for done-for-you migrations, while Cloudways includes a free migration plugin that agencies run themselves. For an agency migrating ten client sites, that's a $300 line item on SiteGround versus an afternoon of your own time on Cloudways.

Performance positioning

Cloudways' performance story is the Lightning Stack: NGINX, Varnish, Memcached, Redis, and PHP-FPM preconfigured, with Object Cache Pro and a free CDN included. The Cloudflare Enterprise CDN is available as a paid add-on with tiered caching, Brotli compression, and image optimization. Because you pick the underlying cloud (DigitalOcean, Vultr, AWS, GCP), you can size resources to the workload — a WooCommerce store gets a bigger server, a brochure site shares a small one. Independent tests in 2026 put Cloudways' uptime at 99.99% with strong response times on DigitalOcean plans.

SiteGround's performance story is its SuperCacher stack plus Google Cloud infrastructure underneath, with server-level caching and a free CDN on every plan. GrowBig and GoGeek handle their stated visit caps (roughly 100,000 and 400,000 monthly visits) with automatic CPU/RAM scaling absorbing traffic spikes. In practice, both hosts are fast for WordPress in 2026 — the difference is control. Cloudways lets you tune the stack and scale the server; SiteGround manages it for you and caps what the plan absorbs. Agencies comfortable with server basics get more headroom from Cloudways; agencies that want performance handled for them get a simpler life on SiteGround.

Support for agencies

Support quality is the reason many agencies picked SiteGround historically, and it's the category to watch most carefully in 2026. SiteGround's priority support (GrowBig and up) is still well-regarded for WordPress-specific help, though independent reviews note the company has been trimming costs — paid migrations, priority support moved up-tier, and heavier upselling than in past years. Cloudways offers 24/7 live chat and tickets; reviewers report fast chat response (under 90 seconds in some tests) but note that help with problems inside your site costs extra — Cloudways supports the platform, not your WordPress debugging, unless you pay for the add-on.

Quick fact: SiteGround's cheap intro prices require 12-month prepayment, and renewal is per month at $29.99 (GrowBig) or $44.99 (GoGeek) — a 5–6x jump. Cloudways' entry price is its ongoing price: no prepay, no renewal shock, billed hourly with a monthly cap.

Cost per client: the agency math

Run the numbers for a 15-client agency. On Cloudways: one $26/month DigitalOcean 2GB server can comfortably carry several low-traffic WordPress sites; a realistic setup is two or three servers totaling $60–$90 a month for 15 sites — roughly $4–$6 per client per month in hosting cost, resold to clients at $30–$50 a month as care-plan hosting. On SiteGround: one GrowBig account at $29.99/month renewal hosts unlimited sites, so 15 sites cost about $2 per site per month — even cheaper per site, until you hit the ~100,000-visit cap and need GoGeek ($44.99) or Cloud ($100+).

The honest read: SiteGround is cheaper per site at small scale on shared plans, while Cloudways is cheaper at portfolio scale and for heavier sites — and Cloudways' flat pricing makes the margin predictable year after year, while SiteGround's renewal jump means your year-one margins collapse in year two unless you priced against the renewal rate from day one. Either way, the hosting line item is small next to the care-plan revenue; the decision should turn on workflow, not $10 a month. Our verdict

Choose Cloudways if

  • You manage hosting for clients yourself and want flat, predictable pricing with no renewal shock.
  • You run many sites and want unlimited applications per server plus server cloning.
  • You want control over the stack — cloud provider, server size, caching — and you're comfortable with it.
  • Your sites are heavy (WooCommerce, membership) and need room to scale vertically.

Choose SiteGround if

  • Clients log in themselves — white-label Site Tools and granular client access are the product.
  • You want to build custom hosting plans and resell hosting under your own brand.
  • You want performance and maintenance handled for you on a managed shared platform.
  • You price against the renewal rate ($29.99/$44.99) from day one, not the intro.

Start with a trial: Cloudways offers a 3-day free trial with no credit card, and SiteGround's 30-day money-back window covers the shared plans. Spin up two client sites on each, run a staging push on both, and hand a test client login to a colleague to see which dashboard they'd rather use. The right host is the one whose client workflow matches how your agency actually operates.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper for agencies: Cloudways or SiteGround?

Cloudways is cheaper at steady state: from $11/month on DigitalOcean with hourly billing and no renewal shock, hosting unlimited client sites per server. SiteGround's GrowBig renews at $29.99/month and GoGeek at $44.99/month after cheap first-term intros ($4.99 and $7.99). SiteGround's intro prices are 12-month prepaid, so the real comparison is always against the renewal rate.

Does Cloudways have white-label for agencies?

Not true white-label in the SiteGround sense. Cloudways gives agencies team-member access with role controls, but there is no client-facing branded portal. SiteGround's GrowBig and GoGeek plans include white-label Site Tools with granular client access, so clients can manage their sites without seeing SiteGround branding.

Can I host unlimited client sites on Cloudways?

Yes, on Flexible plans there is no fixed limit: you can host as many applications as the server's resources can handle. That makes Cloudways economical for agencies with many small client sites — one $26/month DigitalOcean 2GB server can comfortably carry a handful of low-traffic WordPress sites.

Which has better staging: Cloudways or SiteGround?

Both offer one-click staging environments. SiteGround includes staging on GrowBig and up; Cloudways includes it on all Flexible plans. For agencies, Cloudways also adds server cloning, which lets you duplicate an entire server setup when onboarding similar clients — something SiteGround's shared plans don't offer.

Is Cloudways good for WordPress agencies in 2026?

Yes, for agencies comfortable with a less hand-holding panel. The Lightning Stack (NGINX, Redis, Varnish, Object Cache Pro) delivers strong WordPress performance, billing is flat with no renewal trap, and you pick the cloud provider. The tradeoff: no built-in email hosting (a paid Rackspace add-on), no free domain, and clients don't get a white-labeled dashboard.

Conclusion

For most agencies, Cloudways is the better workhorse: flat pricing from $11 a month, unlimited sites per server, staging and cloning built for multi-client workflows, and no renewal ambush in year two. SiteGround wins when the client logs in — white-label Site Tools, granular access, and custom resellable plans make it the better client-facing platform, at $29.99–$44.99 a month once the intro ends. Price both against their steady-state numbers, trial each with two real client sites, and pick the one whose workflow matches your agency. Either way, build your care-plan pricing on the renewal rate, not the intro.
