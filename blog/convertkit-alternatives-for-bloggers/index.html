ConvertKit Alternatives for Bloggers: 7 Email Tools Compared (2026)

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 12, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

The best ConvertKit alternative depends on your kind of blog. Newsletter writers fit Beehiiv (free to 2,500 subscribers, 0% platform cut) or Substack (free at any size, but it takes 10% of subscription revenue). Affiliate bloggers fit MailerLite (50 automations from $12 a month) or Brevo (unlimited contacts, from $9 a month). Bloggers who sell digital products fit Ghost (0% revenue cut, from $15 a month managed) or Flodesk (beautiful design, now priced by list size). ConvertKit (now called Kit) is still a strong tool, but it is no longer the obvious default for every blogger. The Creator plan now starts at $39 a month ($33 billed annually) for 1,000 subscribers, and the free plan, while generous at up to 10,000 subscribers, allows only one basic automation. Depending on what your blog actually does, one of seven alternatives is probably a better fit: MailerLite, Brevo, Mailchimp, Beehiiv, Substack, Ghost, or Flodesk. This guide compares real prices (checked October 2026), free plans, automations, and migration ease — matched to three blogger archetypes.

Why bloggers switch from ConvertKit

Kit's free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited broadcasts, so small blogs often start there and stay. The friction appears in three places. First, the price curve. Creator starts at $39 a month for 1,000 subscribers and climbs to $59 at 3,000, $89 at 5,000, and $139 at 10,000 — with no gradual ramp past the free tier. Second, design. Kit's plain-text aesthetic converts well, but travel, food, and lifestyle blogs wanting polished visual emails find it limiting. Third, publishing. Kit is an email tool with a website attached; bloggers who want a real publication with SEO and paid memberships tend to look at Beehiiv, Substack, or Ghost instead.

None of that means Kit is bad — its visual automation builder is still the gold standard for creators. But if you are choosing fresh, the question is not "which tool beats ConvertKit" — it is "which tool fits the kind of blog I run."

Quick fact: ConvertKit rebranded to Kit in 2024; older reviews quoting a $29 entry price are stale — prices rose roughly 35% in late 2025.

Which alternative fits your kind of blog

Most "ConvertKit vs X" articles rank tools head-to-head. That misses the point: a newsletter writer and an affiliate marketer need almost opposite things.

The newsletter blogger

You publish essays, analysis, or a weekly digest and your list is the product. You care about writing experience, a public archive that ranks on Google, paid subscriptions, and built-in discovery. Your natural alternatives are Substack, Beehiiv, and Ghost.

The affiliate blogger

You write reviews, roundups, and guides that earn commissions, and email is a conversion engine: lead magnets, welcome sequences, product funnels. You care about automation depth, click-based segmentation, and low cost per subscriber. Your natural alternatives are MailerLite, Brevo, and Mailchimp.

The digital-product blogger

You sell courses, templates, or presets, and your list feeds a checkout. You care about commerce built into the same database, beautiful emails, and keeping as much of the revenue as possible. Your natural alternatives are Ghost, Flodesk, and (for automations) MailerLite.

The 7 ConvertKit alternatives

Real pricing at the entry tier, automation depth, and who each suits. All prices in USD, checked in October 2026. More context in our email marketing guides.

1. MailerLite — the best all-round value for affiliate bloggers

MailerLite is a friendly, design-capable email platform that undercuts Kit at nearly every list size. Note the 2026 changes: the free plan tightened to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, and paid plans were renamed in June — Comfort (from $12 a month, $10.80 annually) and Power (from $25 a month, $22.50 annually). Comfort includes 50 automations with up to 100 steps each, segmentation, templates, an AI writing assistant, and logo removal.

For an affiliate blogger, visual automations plus click-based segmentation plus genuinely low prices is hard to beat. Weak spots: no built-in audience discovery (you bring your own traffic), and the free plan is now only a testing ground. If budget is the top priority, see our ranking of the cheapest email marketing software for small business.

2. Brevo — best for big affiliate lists that mail infrequently

Brevo (formerly Sendinblue) prices by emails sent, not contacts stored — and contacts are unlimited on every plan. The free plan gives 300 emails a day with unlimited contacts. Paid plans start at Starter $9 a month (5,000 emails, no daily cap) and Standard $18 a month, which adds marketing automation, A/B testing, and reporting.

Brevo also bundles SMS, WhatsApp, a built-in CRM, and transactional email in one vendor, and as an EU company it simplifies GDPR compliance. Trade-offs: automation depth trails Kit, removing the Brevo logo costs an add-on that can exceed the Starter plan itself, and automation only starts at Standard. For a direct head-to-head, read our Brevo vs Mailchimp comparison.

3. Mailchimp — the safe, familiar choice with a sting in the tail

Mailchimp is the most recognized email brand in the world, and for general-purpose blogging it is still the easiest tool to learn. The free plan now covers just 250 contacts with 500 monthly sends (250-per-day cap), no scheduling, and no automation beyond a single welcome email — really only a trial. Essentials starts at $13 a month for 500 contacts; Standard starts at $20 a month and adds the Journey Builder for multi-branch automations and behavioral targeting.

The catch: Mailchimp bills for every contact it stores — including unsubscribed contacts — unless you manually archive them. A decaying affiliate list gets more expensive every year without you doing anything. There is also no meaningful annual discount, and transactional email is a paid Mandrill add-on. Our Mailchimp vs ConvertKit for creators piece goes deeper on which creator type suits which.

4. Beehiiv — the growth machine for newsletter bloggers

Beehiiv is a newsletter publishing platform built by the team behind Morning Brew's growth — the most feature-complete Substack competitor in 2026. The free plan covers up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited sends, a website, and the recommendation network. Paid plans start at Lite $59 a month ($49 billed annually) for up to 1,000 subscribers and Pro at $109 ($95 annually), scaling with list size. Beehiiv takes a 0% platform fee on paid subscriptions — only Stripe processing. It also runs an Ad Network, paid Boosts, and a referral program, so a newsletter can earn before charging for subscriptions.

For newsletter bloggers, Beehiiv wins on growth tooling and analytics (subscriber attribution, engagement scoring, revenue by stream). Weak spots: monetization and full automations require paid plans, branding removal needs Pro, and automation depth trails Kit for complex affiliate funnels. Substack is cheaper if you never charge; once you earn a few hundred a month, Beehiiv pulls ahead.

5. Substack — the free home for writers who want to monetize later

Substack is free at any list size — no subscriber cap, no monthly fee — and that is the entire pitch. Publishing, email, podcasts, and the Reader app are included. You pay only when readers pay you: Substack keeps 10% of each paid subscription payment, plus Stripe's processing (about 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction). A custom domain costs a one-time $50 — so a newsletter earning $5,000 a month pays Substack $500 a month, every month.

For newsletter bloggers still finding their voice, Substack's discovery surface — the Reader app, Notes, recommendations — is unmatched, and its editor is the simplest here. Trade-offs: no A/B testing, weak automation (drip campaigns only), emails send from an @substack.com address, and monetization is locked to Substack's subscription rails. Writers who just want to write should start here.

6. Ghost — the independent platform for product sellers who keep 100%

Ghost is open-source publishing software: a fast, modern CMS with newsletters, memberships, and paid subscriptions built in — and a 0% platform fee. Self-host for free (plus roughly $5 to $20 a month for a server and your own email delivery), or use Ghost Pro managed hosting: Starter is $15 a month billed annually ($18 monthly) for roughly 1,000 members, with Publisher tiers above at around $29 annually. Paid subscriptions run through your own Stripe account — Ghost never touches your revenue.

For digital-product bloggers, that math is the headline: at $10,000 a month in membership revenue, Ghost costs about $29 a month while Substack costs $1,000. You also get a real website with proper SEO, themes, and full data ownership — no platform lock-in. Trade-offs: no built-in discovery network — you bring your own audience — and self-hosting means you own updates and deliverability.

7. Flodesk — beautiful emails, now priced by list size

Flodesk is the design-first choice for lifestyle, travel, food, and creative bloggers — but its famous flat-rate pricing is gone. On December 2, 2025, Flodesk retired flat-rate unlimited pricing for new accounts; new customers pay contact-tiered prices. At 1,000 subscribers, Lite costs $25 a month ($19 annually) with one workflow automation, and Pro costs $28 a month ($25 annually) with unlimited workflows, branding removal, and a product checkout. At 10,000 subscribers, Pro is $79 a month ($72 annually).

The templates are the best-looking in this lineup, and the checkout lets you sell digital products straight from your emails. Trade-offs: no A/B testing at any tier and Zapier is the main integration story. For more ways to start email for nothing, see our best free email marketing tools for 2026.

Migration and deliverability basics

Every platform here accepts CSV imports, and most offer a migration assistant. For a clean move: export with tags intact, suppress unengaged and bounced addresses, and warm up gradually. Send first to your most engaged segment — blasting 10,000 cold contacts on day one from a fresh domain is the fastest way to land in spam.

Three deliverability basics decide most inbox placement: authenticate your domain (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), keep bounces under about 2%, and send consistently. If you switch from a platform that counts unsubscribed contacts (Mailchimp) to one that does not (MailerLite, Flodesk), drop the dead weight before you import — it directly lowers your first bill.

Our verdict by blog type

Newsletter bloggers

  • Start free: Substack — no caps, best discovery, pay nothing until readers pay you.
  • Grow and monetize: Beehiiv — recommendation network, ad network, 0% platform fees once revenue passes a few hundred a month.
  • Own everything: Ghost — your domain, your Stripe, 0% cut, from $15 a month managed.

Affiliate and product bloggers

  • Best automation value: MailerLite — 50 deep automations from $12 a month, click-based segmentation for funnels.
  • Biggest list, lowest bill: Brevo — unlimited contacts and volume-based pricing from $9 a month.
  • Design-led brands: Flodesk — the prettiest emails here, Pro at $28 a month, checkout built in.
  • Familiarity: Mailchimp — huge template and app library, but audit unsubscribed contacts first.

You do not have to pick perfectly on day one. Shortlist two, import a segment, and send a real campaign from each before you commit your whole list — most of these tools offer free tiers or trials.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free ConvertKit alternative for bloggers?

Substack is the best genuinely free option: publishing and email are free at any list size, and you pay only when readers pay you (10% of subscription revenue plus Stripe fees). Beehiiv's free plan covers up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited sends. Kit itself is free for up to 10,000 subscribers, with just one basic automation.

Is Beehiiv or Substack better for a paid newsletter?

Substack is cheaper until you earn roughly $420 to $500 a month from paid subscriptions: free to use, but it keeps 10% of every payment plus Stripe processing. Beehiiv's Lite plan starts at $59 a month ($49 billed annually) for up to 1,000 subscribers and takes a 0% platform cut. Past that crossover, Beehiiv keeps getting cheaper.

Can I migrate my subscribers from ConvertKit to another platform?

Yes. Export your list and tags as a CSV from Kit and import it into MailerLite, Mailchimp, Brevo, Beehiiv, or Ghost; all accept CSV imports, and most provide a migration assistant or import guide. Warm up gradually: send to your most engaged subscribers first, and avoid blasting a cold list from a brand-new sending domain.

Which ConvertKit alternative has the best automations for affiliate bloggers?

MailerLite is the strongest balance of automation depth and price: its Comfort plan ($12 a month) includes 50 automations with up to 100 steps each and click-based segmentation that suits affiliate funnels. Brevo adds automation from its $18-a-month Standard plan and prices by email volume, which helps large lists that mail infrequently.

Is Flodesk still flat-rate pricing in 2026?

No. Flodesk retired flat-rate unlimited pricing for new accounts on December 2, 2025; only accounts active before that date are grandfathered. New customers pay contact-tiered prices: Lite from $25 a month ($19 annually) and Pro from $28 a month ($25 annually) at 1,000 subscribers. The free plan covers forms and landing pages only.

Conclusion

There is no single best ConvertKit alternative — there is a best one for your kind of blog. Newsletter writers should shortlist Substack (free start), Beehiiv (growth and monetization), and Ghost (independence at 0% fees). Affiliate bloggers: MailerLite's $12-a-month automations or Brevo's volume pricing, with Mailchimp as the familiar fallback. Product-selling bloggers should look hardest at Ghost and Flodesk. Check prices at your actual list size, and trial two finalists before committing.
