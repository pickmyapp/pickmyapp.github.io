Cycurion Habit is a new AI security operations center (SOC) that watches how every person, device, and workload in your business normally behaves, then investigates whatever breaks the pattern. It launched on November 4, 2026, from Cycurion, Inc., a Nasdaq-listed cybersecurity company. For a small team with no security staff, the pitch is simple: instead of buying more alarms, you rent a team that already knows what your normal looks like — and only bothers you when something genuinely does not fit. This guide explains what Habit is, how behavioral baselining works in plain language, what it costs (there is no public price), how it compares with the tools small teams already buy, and the five questions to ask before booking a demo. If you are weighing a managed SOC against simpler managed detection, our Anthropic cyber mission explainer covers another recent launch aimed at the same buyers.

What is Cycurion Habit?

Cycurion Habit is an AI-driven security operations center that builds a behavioral baseline for every identity, device, and workload in your environment, then hunts for activity that deviates from that baseline. Cycurion announced it on November 4, 2026, via GlobeNewswire, and it was covered the same day by Help Net Security. Habit is the first release in a new "predictive intelligence" portfolio, with Panoptic and Fathom to follow.

The name is the product idea. Most security tools work from signatures: they recognize attacks someone has already seen and written a rule for. Habit works from behavior instead. As Cycurion's chief product officer Danny White put it at launch: "Security is a behavior problem, so we named the product after one. Signatures catch what someone has already seen. Habits catch what is different." For a small business, the practical difference is this: a stolen password used at 3 a.m. from a new country passes every signature check, because the login itself is valid — but a behavioral system flags it because the pattern is wrong, even though no rule was broken.

Cycurion itself is a real, publicly traded company, which matters when a vendor asks you to trust it with your security. Founded in 2017 in McLean, Virginia, it went public in February 2025, listing on Nasdaq as CYCU. It already operates a 24/7 SOC and claims more than 2,000 engagements across 30-plus agencies. It is also a small company — roughly 74 employees as of November 2026 — with a second-quarter 2026 GAAP net loss of $4.0 million on $3.8 million in revenue, though it signed a $54.6 million, 10-year contract with a top-five global consulting firm in October 2026, the largest in its history. None of that makes Habit good or bad — it is context for the "can they deliver this?" question, which we come back to below.

How "learning what normal looks like" actually works

The phrase "learns what normal looks like" is marketing for user and entity behavior analytics, or UEBA — a term Gartner coined in 2015. The same idea already lives inside tools your business may own, so understanding it in plain language is the key to judging Habit.

The baseline

Habit connects to the security tools you already run, or deploys Cycurion's own Habit agent, and watches ordinary activity for a learning period: sign-in times, devices, places, systems touched, data moved, hours kept. From that it builds a per-person and per-device profile — a fingerprint of normal — plus peer-group profiles, so it knows what a typical marketing employee does versus a typical finance employee. New behavior is scored against both the individual baseline and the peer group.

The flags

The classic examples make it concrete. A marketing employee who has never opened the finance database suddenly downloads from it at 3 a.m. A laptop that only ever connected from your office signs in from two countries within an hour. A user who normally sends a few megabytes of email a day suddenly moves gigabytes. Each single action might be allowed; the pattern is what looks wrong. When something deviates, Habit's AI triage investigates first, then 24/7 human analysts verify the threat, fix it, and confirm the fix held before closing the case.

The red team

Habit also includes built-in offensive analysis: an on-demand AI red team that attacks your own environment first, mapped to the MITRE ATT&CK framework, to surface weaknesses before a real attacker finds them, then retests to confirm each fix. Traditional penetration testing is bought once or twice a year; an always-on AI red team is a genuinely different proposition — if it works as described. As with everything else in this launch, there is no independent test of it yet.

What is included

According to the launch announcement, Habit bundles several things small teams usually buy separately. Unlimited incident response with no hourly or per-incident fees is the headline: when something goes wrong, the response is included rather than metered. It also includes integration with your existing endpoint protection or the Habit agent, email security, backup for Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, and AWS S3, and a security support inbox — one service instead of four separate renewals.

Two caveats belong in any honest reading of that list. First, "unlimited incident response" is the most disruptive pricing claim in the launch — and the least examined. No coverage has asked what counts as an "incident," whether there is a fair-use cap, or how a 74-employee company staffs unlimited response for new clients. Treat it as a demo-call question, not a fact. Second, the press release itself warns that some capabilities are under development and "subject to change." Habit is day one; what you see in a demo may not be exactly what ships.

How much does Habit cost?

There is no public price for Habit. As of November 5, 2026, no list price appears anywhere — the product page is demo-or-quote only. That is normal for a SOC service aimed at managed-security clients and agencies, but it makes Habit hard to compare with the tools small teams usually shortlist, most of which publish prices. Context, not speed: how Habit differs

Every AI SOC vendor tells a speed story: faster triage, faster response, fewer minutes to containment. Habit's pitch is the opposite. Cycurion's chairman and CEO, L. Kevin Kelly, put it this way at launch: "Everyone is trying to answer the alarm faster. We want to know which alarm matters before the attacker makes a move… The answer is context, not speed." That is a real differentiator, because alert fatigue — too many alarms, most of them noise — is the problem small teams actually feel. A system that sends you five verified signals beats one that sends you five hundred fast ones.

The counterweight is that the technique is not unique to Habit. Microsoft Sentinel builds behavioral profiles with an anomaly score for each investigation, Splunk UEBA is built into Splunk Enterprise Security, and CrowdStrike and Exabeam ship behavior analytics too. The honest framing is not "the only tool that learns normal" but "a managed service that leads with baselining, bundles unlimited response and an AI red team, and puts human analysts between the AI and you." Whether that bundle is worth a quote-only price depends on what you would otherwise pay for its parts — a question only a demo quote can answer.

What nobody has verified yet

This is the section most launch coverage skips. As of November 5, 2026, there are no independent customer reviews of Habit, no third-party tests, no public pricing, and no named small-business customers. All capability claims come from the vendor; independent coverage so far is one trade-press write-up and one investor-analysis piece, and everything else is syndication of the same release. That does not make Habit a bad product — every product is unreviewed on day one — but no one outside Cycurion can yet confirm that the baselining works, that the AI red team finds real weaknesses, or that "unlimited" incident response holds up under load.

If you take a demo, ask these five questions and write down the answers:

  • What is the price per endpoint or per user at our size, and what is the contract minimum?
  • What counts as an "incident" under unlimited incident response, and is there a fair-use limit?
  • How long is the baseline learning period before alerts are trustworthy, and what is the false-positive rate in the first month?
  • What happens to our baseline if we change tools — do we start over?
  • Which of the announced capabilities are shipping today, and which are still under development?

What it means for a small team

Habit is pitched as scaling "from small and midsize businesses to demanding public-sector environments," but read the launch language carefully: its target market is existing managed-security clients, public-sector agencies, and commercial organizations, and its heritage is federal contracting. A 10-person company should ask openly whether it is the intended buyer or an afterthought — and whether the minimum contract size fits a team with no security staff.

The practical playbook for a small team is unchanged by this launch. Start with the priced floor: managed endpoint detection with a 24/7 SOC, such as Huntress, is the default shortlist for teams of this size, and Blumira's published per-employee pricing gives you a number to compare against any quote. Use the 50-endpoint worked example above to convert every quote to the same unit, then take the Habit demo with the five questions in hand and judge whether the baselining bundle and unlimited response are worth a premium. Frequently asked questions

What is Cycurion Habit, and is it real?

Yes, it is real. Cycurion Habit is an AI security operations center launched on November 4, 2026, by Cycurion, Inc., a Nasdaq-listed cybersecurity company (CYCU) based in McLean, Virginia. It builds a behavioral baseline for every identity, device, and workload, then investigates activity that breaks the pattern, with 24/7 human analysts verifying threats. It is brand-new, unreviewed, and unpriced as of November 5, 2026.

How much does Habit cost?

There is no public price. Habit is sold on a demo-or-quote basis, and the launch announcement gives no list price. The headline pricing claim is unlimited incident response with no hourly or per-incident fees. For comparison, Huntress Managed EDR lists at $8.99 per endpoint per month, Blumira starts at $12 per employee per month, and staffing your own 24/7 SOC costs roughly $621,000 a year.

How is Habit different from Huntress, Arctic Wolf, or CrowdStrike?

Habit leads with behavioral baselining — learning what normal looks like, then hunting deviations — while most rivals lead with faster alert triage. It also bundles unlimited incident response and a built-in AI red team. The counterweight: rivals already ship behavior analytics (Microsoft Sentinel, Splunk UEBA, CrowdStrike), and Habit has no independent reviews, tests, or named small-business customers yet.

What does "learns what normal looks like" actually mean?

It means the system watches ordinary activity — sign-in times, devices, locations, systems touched, data volumes — and builds a per-user and per-device profile of normal, plus peer-group profiles for comparison. New activity is scored against those baselines. Classic flags are a 3 a.m. database access by someone who never touches that database, or a sign-in from two countries within an hour. It needs a learning period first, and legitimate changes (a new role, travel) generate false positives a human must clear.

Do I need a SOC if I only have 10 people?

Probably not an in-house one — about $621,000 a year for five analysts working around the clock. The real choice for a 10-person team is a managed service with published pricing, such as Huntress or Blumira, versus a quote-only SOC service such as Habit or Arctic Wolf. Conclusion

Cycurion Habit is a real product from a real public company, launched November 4, 2026, with an interesting idea at its center: stop chasing alarms faster and start knowing which alarm matters. Behavioral baselining, human-verified triage, unlimited incident response, and a built-in AI red team are a strong bundle on paper. But it is day one — no public price, no independent reviews, no third-party tests, and some capabilities still under development. For a small team, the right move is to price the alternatives first using the 50-endpoint worked example, then take the demo with five questions ready. If Habit's quote beats the priced options on value, it is worth a trial.
