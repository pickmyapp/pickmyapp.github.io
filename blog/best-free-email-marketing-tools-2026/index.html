Almost every email marketing platform offers a free plan, but "free" means wildly different things in 2026 than it did a few years ago. Mailchimp's free plan has shrunk from 2,000 contacts to 250. MailerLite cut its free tier to 250 subscribers during its 2025–2026 pricing overhaul. Meanwhile, less-famous tools like Sender and Brevo are quietly offering free tiers that outgun the big names. This guide compares the genuinely free plans — no credit card, no time limit — with the real subscriber limits, send caps, automation access, and catches verified against vendor pricing pages in October 2026. One caution that applies to every entry: free tiers shrink over time, so verify the current numbers on each vendor's pricing page before you commit.

What "free" really means in 2026

A genuinely free email marketing plan lets you send real campaigns to a real audience indefinitely, without a credit card or an expiry date. That definition matters because many tools blur the line: a 30-day free trial is not a free plan. Moosend is the cautionary example here — it appears in many "best free email tools" roundups, but as of 2026 it only offers a 30-day trial (unlimited sends up to 1,000 contacts), not a permanent free tier. This guide only includes tools with an ongoing $0 plan.

The real question on any free plan is the billing unit. Platforms meter either contacts or sends. Brevo and Mailjet meter sends, so you can store an enormous database for free. Mailchimp, MailerLite, and Sender meter contacts or subscribers, so a growing list eventually pushes you off the free tier even if you rarely email it. Your list shape decides which model is friendlier.

Sender — most generous free tier

Sender's free plan is the most generous ongoing free tier we could verify in October 2026: up to 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month, with no daily sending limit. Automation, landing pages, signup forms, popups, and even transactional email are included, which is unusual — transactional sending is typically a paid feature elsewhere. Independent verification roundups in September 2026 consistently rank Sender first on free-plan volume.

The tradeoff is brand and ecosystem. Sender is far less known than Mailchimp or Brevo, so its template library, third-party integrations, and community how-to content are thinner. The emails carry Sender branding until you pay. For a small business or newsletter that just needs volume without a bill — say, a weekly newsletter to 2,000 subscribers — Sender is the honest best value at zero cost. The jump to paid is also cheap when the free tier runs out, which matters if you want to stay on one platform.

Brevo — best for a big contact database

Brevo's free plan gives you 300 emails a day (about 9,000 a month) with up to 100,000 contacts stored, plus transactional email via API and automation for up to 2,000 contacts. That contact ceiling is the standout: no other major free plan lets you keep a six-figure database for free. Brevo also folds SMS, WhatsApp, chat, and a built-in sales CRM into the same platform, so the free plan doubles as a trial of a full marketing stack.

The daily cap is the real constraint. 300 emails a day means you cannot blast a 5,000-person list in one go — you would need the paid Starter plan (from $9 a month for 5,000 emails) or schedule sends across days. Brevo branding sits in the footer of free-plan emails, and removing it costs extra. Brevo is the right free pick when your list is large but your send frequency is modest: a monthly newsletter to a big database, a loyalty list you email selectively, or a startup that wants contacts accumulating while it is pre-revenue. MailerLite — good editor, smaller free tier

MailerLite's free plan covers up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with 2 seats, 3 active automations (up to 5 steps each), one website, one landing page, 3 signup forms, and A/B testing on every plan. Note the timing: MailerLite renamed its paid plans in June 2026 — Comfort (from $12/month) and Power (from $25/month) — and cut the free tier from the old 1,000-subscriber limit during the 2025–2026 overhaul. Older articles quoting 500 or 1,000 free subscribers are out of date.

What MailerLite still does better than most is the editor. Its drag-and-drop builder is clean, fast, and easy for non-technical users to pick up, and the free plan includes A/B testing and automation that some competitors gate behind paywalls. The free tier is now effectively a trial in everything but name — great for testing MailerLite or running a launch list, not a long-term home for a growing newsletter. When you outgrow it, MailerLite's paid plans stay near the cheap end of the market, which is a reasonable upgrade path. Mailjet — free marketing plus transactional email

Mailjet's free plan includes 6,000 emails a month for up to 1,000 contacts, with a 200-email daily cap, plus API access, SMTP relay, and webhooks from day one. That last detail is why Mailjet earns a place here: it is an email specialist that treats transactional email (order confirmations, password resets) and marketing email as one platform. Most free plans in this guide handle only newsletters; Mailjet's free tier can run your store's receipts and your campaigns together.

The limits shape the fit. The 200-a-day cap means Mailjet's free plan suits small, steady senders — a small store sending receipts plus a weekly newsletter — rather than big campaign blasts. Automation is basic on the free tier, and Mailjet is an email specialist, so look elsewhere if you need SMS or a CRM in the same tool. Paid plans start at $9 a month (Starter, 8,000 emails and 2,000 contacts) and scale on send volume with unlimited contacts from the Essential tier up.

Mailchimp — still usable for micro lists

Mailchimp's free plan allows 250 contacts and 500 email sends a month, with a 250-per-day cap, one audience, and one seat. Automation was removed from the free tier in mid-2025, and the subscriber cap was cut again in early 2026 — down from 2,000 contacts and 10,000 sends just a few years ago. It is now one of the least generous free plans among major platforms, and it carries Mailchimp branding on every email.

So why include it at all? Because Mailchimp's builder, template library, and 300-plus integrations are still the friendliest in the category, and for a true micro list — a personal newsletter with 200 subscribers, a test list while you learn — the free plan works fine. Just don't mistake it for a long-term free home; most businesses outgrow it within months and then face contact-based paid pricing (Essentials from $13 a month for 500 contacts) that climbs fast. Benchmark Email — solid free tier with real support

Benchmark Email's free plan covers up to 500 contacts and 3,500 emails a month, with free templates, signup forms, autoresponders, and list-based automations — forever, with no time limit. The plan has been running since Benchmark's family-business beginnings in 2004, and it is still the plan's personality that stands out: even free accounts get onboarding help and fast, knowledgeable chat support, which is rare at this price point.

The limits are modest next to Sender or Brevo, and Benchmark's integration catalog is smaller than the big names'. But for a small business that values being able to ask a human a question without paying — a restaurant building its first list, a nonprofit with 400 supporters — the support quality is a genuine differentiator that the raw numbers don't capture.

Zoho Campaigns — best inside the Zoho ecosystem

Zoho Campaigns' Forever Free plan supports up to 2,000 contacts and 6,000 emails a month, with up to five users, a drag-and-drop editor, templates, and basic reporting. The obvious audience is businesses already on Zoho CRM or other Zoho apps: the free plan plugs straight into the rest of the suite, and the multi-user allowance is generous.

The catch is that automation and advanced segmentation are paid-only, so the free plan is a sending tool, not an automation tool. Outside the Zoho ecosystem there is less reason to pick it over Sender or Brevo, but inside the ecosystem it is the frictionless choice. What free plans don't give you

Every $0 plan trades something for the price. Vendor branding on your emails is universal on this list — only paid plans remove it. Daily sending caps (Brevo's 300 a day, Mailjet's 200 a day, Mailchimp's 250 a day) mean you schedule big blasts across days or pay up. Reporting is stripped to basics, dedicated support is usually limited to docs and community forums after a short window, and advanced automation is gated or absent on several plans.

The deeper catch is that free tiers shrink. Mailchimp and MailerLite both cut their free plans within the past year. A free plan that fits today may not fit next year, which is why the upgrade path matters as much as the free limits: check what the paid tiers cost at your projected list size before you invest months building templates and automations in a platform. And when you start wiring email into a bigger stack — forms, CRM, webhooks — cheap automation tools like n8n or Make can fill gaps the free email tier leaves open.

Our verdict

Start with Sender if

  • You want the most sending volume for free — 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month.
  • You want automation, landing pages, and forms included at $0.
  • You are fine with a smaller brand and thinner integration catalog.

Start with Brevo if

  • Your contact database is big and you email it selectively.
  • You want transactional email, SMS, and a CRM in the same platform.
  • You can live with a 300-a-day sending cap on the free tier.

Neither Sender nor Brevo is the "best" tool in any absolute sense — Mailchimp still has the friendliest builder and the biggest template library, MailerLite's editor is superb, and Mailjet is the only free plan here that treats transactional email as a first-class citizen. But in 2026, the free-tier math is unambiguous: for volume, pick Sender; for contact storage, pick Brevo; for everything else, verify the current limits and pick the free plan whose billing unit matches your list shape.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best truly free email marketing tool in 2026?

Sender offers the most generous ongoing free tier we could verify in October 2026: up to 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month with automation included. Brevo is the pick for a big stored database (up to 100,000 contacts with 300 emails a day). Both are genuinely free — no credit card and no time limit.

How many free emails can I send with Mailchimp's free plan?

Mailchimp's free plan allows 250 contacts and 500 email sends per month, with a 250-per-day cap and one audience. It no longer includes automation on the free tier. It is now one of the least generous free plans among major email platforms, after cuts in 2025 and early 2026.

Is MailerLite still free for 1,000 subscribers?

No. MailerLite cut its free plan during its 2025–2026 pricing overhaul: it now covers up to 250 subscribers and 2,500 emails a month, with the MailerLite logo on emails. Paid plans were renamed Comfort (from $12/month) and Power (from $25/month). Articles quoting the old 1,000-subscriber limit are out of date.

Do free email marketing plans include automation?

Some do. Sender and Brevo include automation on their free plans, and MailerLite allows 3 active workflows with up to 5 steps each. Mailchimp removed automation from its free tier in mid-2025, so you need a paid plan for even basic welcome emails there.

What is the catch with free email marketing tools?

Every free plan trades something for the $0 price: vendor branding on your emails, daily sending caps (Brevo's 300 a day, Mailjet's 200 a day), stripped reporting, and support limited to docs or forums. Limits also shrink over time — Mailchimp and MailerLite both cut their free tiers within the past year, so re-check the vendor's pricing page before you commit.

Conclusion

If you want a free email marketing tool in 2026, start with Sender for the most sending volume or Brevo for the biggest contact database — both are genuinely free with automation included. MailerLite, Mailjet, Benchmark, and Zoho Campaigns each serve a narrower fit well: MailerLite for the editor, Mailjet for transactional email, Benchmark for human support, Zoho for the Zoho ecosystem. Mailchimp's free plan is now only for micro lists. Whichever you pick, check the current limits on the vendor's pricing page first — free tiers shrink, and the number you read today may not be the number you sign up with.
