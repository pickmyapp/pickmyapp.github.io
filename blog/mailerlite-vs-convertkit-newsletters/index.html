MailerLite vs ConvertKit for Newsletters in 2026: Which Should You Pick?

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 13, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 9 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

MailerLite wins on price at every list size — about 40–50% cheaper than Kit (ConvertKit) at 1,000, 5,000, and 10,000 subscribers — and includes paid newsletters, RSS campaigns, and landing pages on its $12/month Comfort plan. Kit wins if you monetize through its Creator Network, paid recommendations, sponsor network, or digital product sales with its built-in commerce (3.5% + $0.30 per transaction). Both support paid newsletters. Choose MailerLite for a cost-efficient newsletter business; choose Kit if creator monetization beyond the newsletter itself is your growth plan.

MailerLite and Kit (the platform formerly known as ConvertKit) are the two email tools newsletter publishers most often shortlist. Both serve independent writers and small businesses, both offer free plans, and both now support paid newsletters — but they are built for different business models. MailerLite is a lean, budget-first email platform. Kit is a creator platform that wants to be your store, your growth network, and your newsletter host in one. This comparison is for newsletter publishers specifically, not email marketers in general: we compare price at real list sizes, newsletter features (paid newsletters, RSS-to-email, landing pages, editors), deliverability, and which tool fits which kind of newsletter. Prices were checked against vendor pricing pages and third-party breakdowns in October 2026. If you are comparing wider than newsletters, see our Brevo vs Mailchimp comparison.

What is MailerLite?

MailerLite is an independent email marketing platform for small businesses and creators, priced by active subscriber count. In June 2026 it renamed its paid plans — Growing Business became Comfort and Advanced became Power — and raised prices across tiers, but it remains the cheapest major option at most list sizes. Every paid plan includes the full feature set; you pay more only for a bigger list or more sending. It offers websites with blogs, landing pages, digital products, and paid newsletters, all on a drag-and-drop builder that consistently gets high ease-of-use ratings. Only active subscribers count toward your bill — unsubscribed and bounced contacts do not inflate it.

What is ConvertKit (Kit)?

Kit — rebranded from ConvertKit — is a creator-focused email platform built to turn audiences into income. Its headline attraction for newsletter writers is the free plan: up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited broadcasts, landing pages, forms, and tagging. But automation is paywalled — you get only one automation/sequence on the free plan, and visual automations, sequences, and A/B testing require the Creator plan. Kit's differentiators are its monetization layer: Kit Commerce (sell digital products and paid newsletters), paid recommendations through SparkLoop, a sponsor network, and a creator-to-creator referral network that surfaces your newsletter to other creators' audiences. In September 2025 Kit raised Creator pricing roughly 35%, so guides quoting a $29 entry price are stale.

Paid newsletters and monetization

Both tools now let you charge for your newsletter, but the models differ in cost and reach.

On MailerLite, paid newsletters and recurring subscriptions are available from the Comfort plan ($12/month at 500 subscribers). You also get digital product sales — 5 products on Comfort, unlimited on Power — so a newsletter plus a few ebooks or templates fits the same subscription. The limitation is growth tooling: referrals come through a SparkLoop add-on rather than a native network, and there is no built-in sponsor marketplace.

On Kit, paid newsletters run through Kit Commerce, which works on every plan including free, at 3.5% + $0.30 per transaction (card processing included). Kit layers on paid recommendations — other creators pay you $1 to $4+ per subscriber you send them, minus Kit's 20% service fee — plus a sponsor network and tip jars. If your growth strategy is "borrow audiences from other creators," Kit's network is a real advantage that MailerLite cannot match. Just remember the subscription itself costs more: at 10,000 subscribers, Kit's $139/month versus MailerLite's $89/month is a $600 annual head start you have to earn back through those monetization channels.

Quick fact: Kit's 3.5% + $0.30 commerce fee is roughly a third of Substack's 10% take — one reason creators move established paid lists off Substack onto Kit.

Deliverability: can your newsletter reach the inbox?

Deliverability matters more than any feature, because a newsletter nobody receives is a diary. Kit publishes a 99.8% delivery rate and invests visibly in deliverability tooling: ISP-level analytics, sender scoring, spam complaint analytics, and automatic cleaning of risky addresses, with more of this on paid plans. Its deliberately plain, text-first email style also avoids the spam triggers that heavily designed HTML emails often hit. MailerLite does not publish a delivery rate, but it maintains a strong reputation across independent tests — EmailToolTester's long-running deliverability tests have historically scored MailerLite and ConvertKit within a few points of each other, both in the mid-70s for main-inbox placement. The honest bottom line: for a clean, permission-based, engaged list, the practical difference between the two is small. Your list hygiene and content matter far more than the logo on the sending platform.

Ease of use and migration

MailerLite is widely rated the easier tool: the drag-and-drop editor, template library, and straightforward settings make a first newsletter quick to ship. Kit's interface is simple too — arguably simpler, since the editor is intentionally minimal — but its power sits in tags, segments, and the automation builder, which take longer to learn. For pure newsletter publishing, both are a weekend's work.

Switching is realistic in either direction: both offer free migration services with CSV import and preserved subscriber fields, tags, and groups, typically completed in a few days. What does not migrate is workflow logic — automations, forms, and landing pages get rebuilt by hand. Verdict by newsletter type

Choose MailerLite if

  • You want the lowest bill at every list size (about $600/year saved at 10k subscribers).
  • You run a free newsletter with automations — the free plan includes 3 automations; Kit's free plan allows only 1.
  • You need RSS-to-email for a blog, or a design-forward newsletter with rich templates.
  • Your list is growing and you want the bill to stay lean while it proves it can earn.

Choose Kit if

  • You monetize through paid newsletters, digital products, or sponsorships — Commerce, the sponsor network, and paid recommendations are native.
  • You want the largest possible free runway: 10,000 subscribers at $0 with unlimited broadcasts.
  • Audience cross-promotion with other creators is your growth strategy.
  • You need granular tagging and creator-oriented integrations (WordPress, Teachable, Gumroad).

Neither choice is permanent. A newsletter that starts on MailerLite's free plan and later needs Kit's monetization network can migrate in a few days — and many publishers do exactly that once revenue justifies the higher bill.

Frequently asked questions

Is MailerLite cheaper than ConvertKit?

Yes, at every list size. At 1,000 subscribers MailerLite Comfort is $19/month versus Kit Creator at $39/month; at 5,000 it is $49 versus $89; at 10,000 it is $89 versus $139. That is roughly a 40-50% gap, or about $600 a year at 10,000 subscribers. Both count only active subscribers.

Can I run a paid newsletter on MailerLite?

Yes. MailerLite supports paid newsletters and recurring subscriptions on its Comfort plan (from $12/month) and Power plan, plus digital product sales with 5 products on Comfort and unlimited on Power. Kit also offers paid newsletters through Kit Commerce, charging 3.5% + $0.30 per transaction.

Which has better deliverability, MailerLite or ConvertKit?

Kit (ConvertKit) publishes a 99.8% delivery rate and invests in ISP-level deliverability analytics and sender scoring, especially on paid plans. MailerLite does not publish a delivery rate but has a strong reputation and consistent performance in independent tests. Both score well; the practical difference for a clean, engaged list is small.

Does MailerLite or ConvertKit have RSS-to-email?

MailerLite includes RSS campaigns and auto-resend on its paid Comfort and Power plans, so blog posts can be emailed automatically when published. Kit's newsletter workflow centers on its editor, newsletter site, and publishing flow rather than RSS-to-email — if automatic blog-to-email is essential, MailerLite is the easier fit.

Can I switch from MailerLite to ConvertKit (or the reverse)?

Yes. Both offer free list migration services with CSV import, subscriber fields, and tags/groups preserved. Migration typically takes a few days. The real work is rebuilding automations, forms, and landing pages, since there is no one-click migration of workflow logic between the two.

Conclusion

For a newsletter publisher, MailerLite vs ConvertKit is a budget-versus-business-model decision, not a features contest. MailerLite costs roughly half as much at every list size, includes paid newsletters and RSS-to-email from $12/month, and is the better pick for a newsletter that needs to stay lean. Kit costs more, but its free plan stretches to 10,000 subscribers, and its commerce layer, creator recommendations, and sponsor network make it the better pick for a newsletter built to be monetized from day one. Pick the tool that matches how your newsletter earns — and re-check the pricing pages, because both vendors have repriced within the last two years.
