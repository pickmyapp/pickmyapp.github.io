Attio vs HubSpot for startups (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Nov 6, 2026
  • Updated: November 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Attio is the better CRM for a sales-led startup of 2–10 people whose customers do not fit HubSpot's contact-and-deal mold: its free plan covers 3 seats and 50,000 records, Plus costs $35 a seat a month with custom objects included, and there is no onboarding fee. HubSpot is the better CRM if marketing will generate most of your pipeline: Starter costs just $7 a seat a month on annual billing and includes sequences and 15 pipelines, but Professional jumps to $90 a seat plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee, and custom objects are Enterprise-only. The decision is not about price — it is about whether your pipeline starts in marketing or in sales.

Pricing side by side (October 2026)

Prices below come from Attio's and HubSpot's pricing pages, checked in October 2026 by multiple 2026 comparisons. HubSpot runs first-year promotions that move Starter's price around — confirm the live page before you sign.

Read that table twice, because it contains the whole argument. HubSpot Starter at $7 a seat looks like a typo. It is not — and for two founders sending follow-ups out of a shared pipeline, it is plenty of CRM. It includes sequences (5,000 per account) and up to 15 pipelines. Attio Plus at $35 a seat is five times the price, and the reason is not seats or storage — it is the data model. Attio lets you track investors, partners, candidates, or anything else alongside deals, with custom objects from the entry paid plan. HubSpot locks custom objects behind Enterprise at $150 a seat. If your customers fit HubSpot's contact-company-deal mold, you will never notice. If they do not, you will notice on day one.

Attio also advertises a startup program with up to 80% off for eligible companies — worth checking before you compare list prices, because an 80% discount changes every number in this article.

The cheapest row is HubSpot's and so is the most expensive. That is the pattern of HubSpot pricing: the entry is nearly free and the ceiling is steep, with a mandatory onboarding fee at every serious tier. Attio's curve is flatter and has no onboarding fee at all — but it starts five times higher, because the cheapest Attio that does real work is $35 a seat, not $7.

For a founding team of three, the free plans decide it. Attio's free plan covers three seats, 50,000 records, and three objects — a three-person startup can run on it for $0. HubSpot's free CRM covers only two users, so the third founder forces a paid seat: three Starter seats are listed at $60 a month (less in the first year with new-customer discounts). For a three-person team, Attio's free plan is hard to beat on cost.

For a ten-person sales team on the mid tier, billed annually: Attio Pro is 10 × $79 × 12 = $9,480 a year. HubSpot Professional is 10 × $100 × 12 = $12,000 a year on monthly billing, or $10,800 on annual billing, plus the one-time $1,500 onboarding fee — $12,300 in year one. At the mid tier, Attio wins on price. At the entry tier, HubSpot wins by a distance. The tier you land on matters more than the vendor.

Free plans: Attio's is generous, HubSpot's is a funnel

Both vendors publish a $0 plan, and they are not the same kind of free.

Attio free: up to 3 seats, 50,000 records, 3 objects, 3 reports. That is a working CRM for a tiny team, not a demo. The limits that bite are the object count (three custom record types) and the report count, and both are honest limits — you hit them when the business outgrows them, not on the first afternoon. The catch is the one every Attio user should know before they build on the free plan: Plus caps at ten seats.

HubSpot free: up to 2 users, 1,000 contacts, one deal pipeline, email logging, tasks, and contact/company records. It is the most widely used free CRM in the world for a reason — it is genuinely useful, and it is the front door to the whole HubSpot suite. But it is also a funnel by design. The limits that bite are the contact count and the missing automation: sequences, workflows, and real reporting all sit on paid tiers, and the jump from Starter ($7–$20 a seat) to Professional ($90–$100 a seat) is a 5x price cliff that surprises new buyers. HubSpot also charges extra not for seats but for marketing contacts: once your database grows past the included allowance, HubSpot automatically bumps you to the next contact tier — roughly $250 a month more for up to 5,000 contacts, and more above that — and the tier upgrade locks until your renewal date. It is one of the most common bill-shock moments for HubSpot customers.

The real difference: the data model

Attio takes a different approach from HubSpot, and it is the reason startups pick it. Instead of fixed contact, company, and deal records, Attio lets you model your own data. A recruiting firm tracks candidates and client companies. A marketplace tracks buyers and sellers, both as customers. A fund tracks portfolio companies, LPs, and founders. In HubSpot, all of that is a workaround — custom properties on a contact, or a deal stage that means something it does not say. In Attio, it is a custom object, and custom objects start on Plus at $35 a seat: five objects on Plus, twelve on Pro, unlimited on Enterprise.

HubSpot reserves custom objects for Enterprise at $150 a seat plus a $3,500 onboarding fee. That is not a pricing detail; it is a product philosophy. HubSpot's data model is the standard B2B sales model, and it is excellent at it. If your business is a standard B2B sales motion — leads come in, reps work deals, deals close — HubSpot's model fits and its breadth (marketing, sales, service, content, and data tools on one platform, sharing one contact database) is a genuine advantage. A team that wants email marketing, forms, a help desk, and a CRM can buy them together instead of stitching four tools.

Attio's other structural advantage is how it handles email. It is built for teams that live in email: sync is deep, enrichment is native, and the AI features (Ask Attio, sequences, call intelligence, agent workflows) consume plan credits rather than demanding a separate AI add-on. The limitation is the mirror image of HubSpot's strength: Attio is not a marketing automation suite. If marketing will generate most of your pipeline — landing pages, nurture campaigns, lead scoring, forms — HubSpot wins, and Starter at $7 a seat is hard to argue with.

The 10-seat trap (and HubSpot's onboarding fee)

Two traps, one per vendor, and both are easy to miss until they cost you real money.

Attio's trap is the Plus seat cap. Plus stops at ten seats. Ten people on Plus cost $4,200 a year; the eleventh person forces the whole team onto Pro, which takes the annual bill to $10,428 a year — roughly two and a half times the bill for one extra salesperson, plus features like advanced permissions and SSO that you may not have wanted yet. If your hiring plan crosses ten salespeople in the next year, budget for Pro from the start, or negotiate the jump before you sign. The seat cap is the single most important number in Attio's pricing page for a growing startup.

HubSpot's trap is the onboarding fee plus the contact tiers. Professional costs $90 a seat a month on annual billing plus a mandatory one-time $1,500 onboarding fee — HubSpot charges it regardless of whether you use their onboarding services. Enterprise is $150 a seat plus $3,500. And the marketing-contact pricing means your bill grows with your database, not just your team: past the included contacts, each tier adds roughly $250 a month and locks until renewal. Budget for both before you compare the seat prices.

Migration is the third cost, and here Attio has the edge: it migrates through a service called Import2, free on paid plans and run by an admin. HubSpot migrations are doable — CSV import, or a paid migration service — but moving pipelines, sequences, and automations between CRMs is never one click, and neither vendor publishes a one-click importer for the other. If you are choosing between two general CRMs rather than two startup CRMs, our Freshsales vs Pipedrive comparison covers the same kind of tier-name trap in the $195-a-month range.

Verdict: which startup should pick which

Pick Attio if: you run a sales team of two to ten people, your records need their own shapes (investors, partners, candidates, buyers and sellers), nobody wants to learn HubSpot's settings menus, and you want custom objects and a modern data model without paying Enterprise prices. The free plan covers a three-person founding team at $0, Plus at $35 a seat is the honest price of that flexibility, and there is no onboarding fee. Watch the ten-seat cap.

Pick HubSpot if: marketing will work in the same tool as sales — landing pages, email campaigns, lead scoring, forms, and a help desk on one contact database — and your sales motion fits the standard contact-company-deal model. Starter at $7 a seat on annual billing is the cheapest paid CRM in this comparison by a distance, and the free plan is the best-known starting point in the category. Go in with your eyes open on the Professional jump ($90 a seat plus $1,500 onboarding) and the marketing-contact tiers.

Pick neither yet if: you are two founders with a spreadsheet and ten deals. Both free plans cover you, but a CRM you do not use is worse than a spreadsheet you do use — the tool earns its place when the deals stop fitting in your head, not before. When they stop fitting, the choice above is the right one to make, and the cheapest way to make it is a two-week trial of both with your real pipeline imported. Frequently asked questions

Is Attio cheaper than HubSpot?

At the entry tier, no — HubSpot Starter is $7 a seat a month on annual billing, while Attio Plus is $35 a seat. At the mid tier, yes — Attio Pro is $79 a seat with no onboarding fee, while HubSpot Professional is $90 a seat plus a mandatory $1,500 onboarding fee. Five seats in year one: HubSpot Starter $420, Attio Plus $2,100, Attio Pro $4,740, HubSpot Professional $6,900.

Does Attio have a free plan?

Yes. Attio's free plan covers up to 3 seats, 50,000 records, 3 objects, and 3 reports. It is a working CRM for a tiny team, not a demo — a three-person founding team can run on it for $0.

Does HubSpot have a free CRM?

Yes. HubSpot's free CRM covers up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts, with contact and company records, a deal pipeline, email logging, and tasks. Paid plans add automation, sequences, reporting, and more users.

What are custom objects, and why do they matter?

Custom objects let a CRM track record types beyond contacts, companies, and deals — investors, candidates, buyers and sellers, portfolio companies. Attio includes custom objects from Plus ($35 a seat): 5 on Plus, 12 on Pro. HubSpot reserves custom objects for Enterprise ($150 a seat), so startups with an unusual data model pay far more for the same flexibility.

What is the Attio 10-seat trap?

Attio Plus caps at 10 seats. Ten people on Plus cost $4,200 a year; the eleventh person forces the whole team onto Pro, taking the bill to about $10,428 a year — roughly 2.5x for one extra salesperson. If your hiring plan crosses ten salespeople soon, budget for Pro from the start.

Conclusion

Attio vs HubSpot is not a price comparison — it is a question about how your startup sells. If your pipeline starts in marketing, HubSpot wins, and Starter at $7 a seat is the cheapest serious CRM you can buy. If your pipeline starts in sales, your customers do not fit the contact-and-deal mold, and you want a flexible data model without an Enterprise price tag, Attio wins — and its free plan covers a three-person founding team at $0. Either way, watch the traps: Attio's ten-seat cap and HubSpot's $1,500 onboarding fee plus marketing-contact tiers. Trial both with your real pipeline for two weeks, and let the deals, not the feature list, decide.
