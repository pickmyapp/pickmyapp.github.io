Insightly vs Zoho CRM compared (2026)

  • CRM
  • Published Nov 12, 2026
  • Updated: November 2026
  • 12 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Insightly and Zoho CRM solve the same problem at very different prices. Insightly charges $29, $49 and $99 per user a month on annual billing, with no free plan and no published monthly prices. Zoho charges $14, $23, $40 and $52 on annual billing, publishes monthly prices for every tier, and offers a free plan for up to 3 users. For a 5-person team at the tier where automation actually lives, Insightly Professional costs $245 a month ($2,940 a year) against Zoho Professional at $115 a month ($1,380 a year) — about 2.1 times as much. Insightly's real edge is native project management in every plan: a won deal becomes a project in the same record. Zoho's edge is price and breadth — workflow rules even on the free plan and 900-plus marketplace extensions. Pick Insightly if the deal-to-project handoff is your daily work; pick Zoho if price per seat is the deciding factor.

Pricing at a glance — the two menus

The two vendors price in completely different ways, and the difference starts before the first dollar. Insightly publishes annual prices only — $29, $49 and $99 per user a month — and does not publish monthly prices at all; month-to-month billing is sales-quoted. Zoho publishes both annual and monthly prices for every paid tier, and keeps a free plan for up to 3 users.

FreeNone — 14-day trial only$0, up to 3 users, 5,000 records

Working tierProfessional $49Professional $23 / $35
Full-featuredEnterprise $99Enterprise $40 / $50
Top tier—Ultimate $52 / $65

Insightly's Plus at $29 covers lead and contact management, pipelines, native project management and advanced reports — but no workflow automation, no lead routing and no AI Copilot. Those start at Professional ($49), which adds lead assignment, workflow automation (50 workflow processes, per a third-party read of Insightly's plan-limits page), AI Copilot at 75 queries per seat a month, and custom objects. Enterprise ($99) adds Copilot at 100 queries a seat a month, sandboxes, products and quotes, audit logging and validation rules. Record limits run 100,000 on Plus, 250,000 on Professional and 500,000 on Enterprise, with mass-email caps of 2,500, 5,000 and 10,000 a day.

Zoho's Standard at $14 covers email integration with Gmail and Outlook, built-in calling, sales forecasting, lead scoring and the Zoho Marketplace with 900-plus extensions. Professional at $23 adds Blueprint process management, CPQ, inventory management, portals and unlimited custom reports. Enterprise at $40 adds the full Zia AI suite, CommandCenter, territory management, approval processes and a sandbox. Ultimate at $52 adds advanced BI and augmented analytics. Zoho's free plan is genuinely usable for a 3-person team — 5,000 records, 1 GB of file storage, workflow rules included — though it allows only the default pipeline and no custom reports. Paid trials run 15 days.

One warning about Insightly's published prices: its own pages contradict each other on fees. The add-on table labels guided onboarding ($1,500) and Premier Support & Success (20% of the licence, $3,000 minimum a year) as "required", while the FAQ says you "can upgrade" to Premier. Whether a small team must pay those fees is unresolved — get the terms in writing in the quote, and do not budget on the licence price alone until you have.

The 5-user math: what a small team actually pays

Headline per-seat prices mislead because the two products put the features a team actually needs on different tiers. The honest comparison prices the same 5-person team at the entry tier and at the tier where automation lives.

Insightly Plus (entry)$145$1,740

Zoho Professional, annual (working tier)$115$1,380

At the entry tier, Zoho costs less than half of Insightly — $840 a year against $1,740. At the working tier the gap widens in absolute terms: Insightly Professional is $2,940 a year against Zoho Professional at $1,380, about 2.1 times as much per seat. Even Zoho Professional plus Zoho Projects Premium for delivery work, at $1,620 a year, is well below Insightly Professional alone.

The other number that matters is the monthly-billing penalty, because it only exists on one side. Zoho Standard on monthly billing is $20 a seat against $14 annual — $100 a month for 5 users instead of $70 — and Professional monthly is $35 against $23. Insightly publishes no monthly price at all, so a team that cannot commit to a year has no published figure to budget against and must negotiate. For a 5-person team that wants to try before it commits, Zoho's free plan plus a 15-day trial is a much lower-risk start than Insightly's 14-day trial with no free tier behind it. Project management: Insightly's headline edge

This is the section that decides the comparison for a specific kind of team, and it is the one feature where Insightly is not just cheaper or dearer but different. Native project management is included in every Insightly plan, including Plus. When an opportunity is won, it converts into a project in the same record, with tasks and milestones attached — no second app, no integration, no re-keying the client details. For a consultancy, an agency, a contractor or any team whose sale ends and whose delivery begins in the same breath, that handoff is the daily work, and Insightly does it without a seam.

Zoho CRM has no project management at all. Delivery runs in Zoho Projects, a second app to license and learn. That is a real cost in money and in friction: a second login, a second interface, a second place where the client record lives. The counter-argument is that Zoho Projects is a proper project tool rather than a CRM's built-in extra — its Premium tier adds time logs, timesheets and project budgets, which Insightly's pricing pages do not list — and at $4 a user a month on annual billing it is cheap. A 5-person team on Zoho Professional plus Projects Premium pays $135 a month, still $110 a month less than Insightly Professional alone.

So the question is not "which has project management" but "how much is the integrated handoff worth to you". If your team sells a project and then delivers it, and the person who sold it is the person who delivers it, Insightly's single record is worth real money — it removes the handoff entirely. If your sales team and your delivery team are different people, or if delivery is tracked in a tool you already use, Zoho's two-app setup costs you little and saves you $1,320 a year at the working tier. Automation and AI: where each one gates the good stuff

The automation gap between the two is bigger than the price gap suggests, because the two vendors put the same capability on different tiers. Zoho includes workflow rules in every edition — including the free plan. Guided Blueprint processes, which walk a deal through defined stages with approvals, start at Professional ($23). Insightly gates all workflow automation behind Professional ($49): a team on Plus gets no automation at all, and lead routing waits for the same tier.

That means the cheapest team that automates anything pays $49 a seat on Insightly and $0 on Zoho — the free plan automates, the $14 Standard automates, and the $23 Professional adds the guided processes. For a small team whose whole reason for buying a CRM is to stop doing repetitive follow-ups by hand, Insightly's entry tier is not a real option. It is a waiting room for the $49 tier.

AI follows the same pattern, with one honest complication. Zoho's Zia AI is the deeper story — predictive scoring, anomaly detection, forecasting — but sources conflict on which tier unlocks the full version. SalesHive and Zoho's own comparison pages say Zia features start at Professional ($23); other sources say full Zia is an Enterprise ($40) feature. Insightly's AI Copilot is simpler to describe and easier to cap: summaries and record Q&A from Professional, at 75 queries per seat a month, rising to 100 on Enterprise. A team that leans on AI heavily will hit that cap; Zoho publishes no equivalent per-seat query limit for Zia. Our Zoho CRM vs HubSpot comparison covers Zoho against the other big free-tier CRM.

Ease of use, integrations and email

Reviewer verdicts on ease of use are mixed, not a clean win either way. G2 rates Insightly higher for ease of setup (8.3 against Zoho's 8.0), while Capterra sub-scores put Zoho ahead on ease of use (4.2 against 4.0). CRM.org calls Insightly easier to learn and Zoho harder to master. The fair summary is that Insightly is simpler out of the box and Zoho is more configurable with a steeper learning curve — reviewer-dependent, not vendor-verified, and the right choice depends on whether your team wants a tool that works on day one or a tool it can reshape over a year.

Integrations are Zoho's clearest win after price. The Zoho Marketplace lists 900-plus extensions, and the wider Zoho suite — Campaigns, Desk, Books and the rest — gives a team one vendor for the whole back office. Insightly includes Google and Microsoft 365, QuickBooks, Xero and PandaDoc natively, but deeper multi-app automation needs AppConnect, a separate product at $249, $799 or $1,899 a month by task volume, with a $3,000 required technical setup fee. That is a real number for a small team: the integration layer alone can cost more than the CRM seats.

Email is the one place Insightly's numbers look better on paper. Its mass-email caps run 2,500 a day on Plus, 5,000 on Professional and 10,000 on Enterprise, against Zoho's 250, 500, 1,000 and 2,000 per organisation a day. But the two caps are not the same thing: Zoho's limits are for sales activity inside the CRM, and campaigns belong in Zoho Campaigns. What is verifiable is that Zoho's email integration, built-in calling and lead scoring all arrive at Standard ($14), while Insightly's comparable email tooling sits on the same tiers as everything else.

Who should pick which — and the honest catches

Pick Insightly if your team sells projects and delivers them, and the won-deal-to-project handoff is your daily work; if you want one record from first call to final delivery without a second app; if your team is small enough that $245 a month for 5 users on Professional is affordable; and if you have the Insightly onboarding and Premier fee question answered in writing before you sign.

Pick Zoho if price per seat is the deciding factor — at the working tier it costs less than half; if you want a free plan to start on and monthly billing as an option; if you automate from day one, since workflow rules exist even on the free plan; if you want the 900-plus-extension marketplace and the wider Zoho suite; or if your delivery team already works in a project tool and does not need the CRM to do that job.

The catches, stated plainly. On Insightly: no free plan, no published monthly prices, automation gated behind the $49 tier, and the unresolved onboarding/Premier fee question that can add $4,500 to a 5-user first year. On Zoho: no native project management, so delivery needs a second app; the Zia AI tier split is unclear from official pages; and the learning curve is steeper — a team that wants a tool that works on day one will spend more time setting Zoho up. Prices were checked November 12, 2026; confirm them on insightly.com and zoho.com before you buy.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Insightly or Zoho CRM?

Zoho, by a wide margin. Insightly charges $29, $49 and $99 per user a month on annual billing, with no free plan. Zoho charges $14, $23, $40 and $52 on annual billing, plus a free plan for up to 3 users. For a 5-person team at the automation tier, Insightly Professional costs $245 a month ($2,940 a year) against Zoho Professional at $115 a month ($1,380 a year) — about 2.1 times as much.

Does Insightly have a free plan?

No. Insightly offers a 14-day free trial with no credit card required, but there is no free-forever plan — older articles claiming a free plan for 2 users are outdated; the free plan was discontinued. Zoho CRM does have a free plan for up to 3 users, with 5,000 records and workflow rules included.

Does Zoho CRM have project management?

No — Zoho CRM has no built-in project management. Delivery work runs in Zoho Projects, a separate app: free for up to 5 users and 3 projects, Premium at $4 a user a month on annual billing. Insightly includes native project management in every plan, so a won deal converts to a project in the same record. For a 5-person team, Zoho Professional plus Projects Premium costs $135 a month, still well below Insightly Professional at $245.

Which is better for a 5-person sales team?

It depends on the work. If the team sells projects and delivers them, Insightly's integrated deal-to-project handoff is worth real money. If the team mainly sells and tracks deals, Zoho Professional at $115 a month gives the same automation tier for less than half the price, with a free plan to start on and monthly billing as an option.

Can I migrate from Insightly to Zoho CRM (or back)?

Both products export contacts, deals and activities to CSV, but neither vendor publishes a one-click importer for the other and migration fidelity was not tested for this article. Budget a manual migration and a data check if you switch,

Conclusion

Insightly vs Zoho CRM is not a close call on price — Zoho wins at every tier, and by more than double at the tier where automation lives. It is a close call on the work: Insightly's native project management removes the deal-to-delivery handoff entirely, and for a team whose sale is a project, that is worth paying for. The decision rule is simple. If the won deal becomes a project in your team's hands, try Insightly Professional — after getting the onboarding and Premier fee question answered in writing. If the team sells and someone else delivers, or if price per seat decides it, Zoho Professional at $115 a month for 5 users is the better buy, with a free plan to start on and workflow automation from day one.
