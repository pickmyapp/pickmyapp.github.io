ActiveCampaign vs Klaviyo for ecommerce: which costs less as your list grows (2026)

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 20, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For ecommerce, the ActiveCampaign-vs-Klaviyo cost gap is U-shaped, not a straight line. At 1,000 contacts, ActiveCampaign Starter ($15/mo, annual billing) undercuts Klaviyo ($30/mo) — though Starter's automation is capped at 5 actions per flow, so like-for-like automation is Plus at $49. Around 25,000 contacts the two reach near-parity (~$389–$400/mo). At 50,000 contacts, ActiveCampaign pulls ahead again (~$400 vs Klaviyo's $720). Both vendors also changed billing models in 2025: Klaviyo now bills on all active profiles (not just emailed ones), and ActiveCampaign now bills all contacts including unsubscribed. The real bill includes add-ons — SMS, CRM pipelines, reviews, analytics — which can add ~$355/mo to ActiveCampaign and stack similarly on Klaviyo.

Start here: both changed how they count billable contacts in 2025

Any comparison using pre-2025 pricing logic is wrong, because both vendors changed the definition of a billable contact within the last two years.

  • Klaviyo (February 2025): switched from billing contacts you actually emailed to billing active profiles — every contact you can reach, whether you emailed them or not. Stores with bloated lists saw bills jump overnight. Cross a tier mid-cycle and you auto-upgrade for the next billing cycle, with a 90-day suppression lock complicating quick list-shrinking.
  • ActiveCampaign (November 2025): new accounts are now billed for all contacts, including unsubscribed, bounced, and unconfirmed (legacy accounts were grandfathered). List hygiene is no longer just a deliverability practice — it's a billing practice.

The practical consequence: the cheapest platform for your store depends on your list composition, not just its size. A store with 20,000 contacts and 8,000 stale ones pays for all 20,000 on both platforms now. Before comparing sticker prices, clean the list — it's the only discount both vendors still offer freely. Our guide to the cheapest email marketing software for small business covers the hygiene-first approach in detail.

A mid-size store easily adds $150–$600/mo in add-ons before sending a single extra email.

The tier you need matters more than the contact count. Starter's automation is capped at 5 actions per automation with no branching — it's an email tool with decorative automation, missing landing pages, CRM pipelines, attribution, and predictive sending. Plus is the first tier that functions as a real automation tool (unlimited automations, branching, predictive sending, CRM). Pro adds attribution and predictive content; Enterprise adds custom reporting and SSO. So the honest like-for-like comparison against Klaviyo starts at Plus, not Starter.

ActiveCampaign's own stack tax: CRM pipelines (~$68/mo), sales engagement (~$111/mo), SMS (~$16.83/mo per 1,000 credits), custom reporting (~$159/mo) — stacked, roughly $355/mo on top of the base plan. Plus send caps (10× contacts on Starter/Plus, overages ~$0.005/email) and single-seat limits until Pro.

Three takeaways. First, ActiveCampaign wins the extremes: tiny lists on Starter (if you can live with the 5-action cap) and large lists where Klaviyo's per-profile pricing accelerates. The U-shape is the insight most comparisons miss. Generic “ActiveCampaign is cheaper” verdicts are true at 50k and misleading at 10k; generic “Klaviyo is expensive” verdicts ignore that it's the cheapest serious option in the 5k–10k band. Price your actual list size, not the narrative.

Features that change the math

Price bands decide the shortlist; these features decide the winner.

  • Shopify depth: Klaviyo is purpose-built for ecommerce — native Shopify sync of products, purchase history, and browse behavior with product-level segmentation, and 60+ prebuilt ecommerce flows (cart, browse abandonment, back-in-stock, price-drop) set up in under an hour. ActiveCampaign's Deep Data syncs Shopify/WooCommerce orders and carts but is consistently described as shallower. ActiveCampaign's own comparison page concedes Klaviyo is “purpose-built for ecommerce.”
  • The CRM gap: ActiveCampaign includes a real sales CRM (pipelines, lead scoring, sales automation) natively. Klaviyo calls itself a “B2C CRM” but has no deal pipelines or lead scoring — any sales process beyond checkout needs a separate CRM. For stores with a high-touch or wholesale motion, that's a second subscription Klaviyo forces and ActiveCampaign doesn't.
  • SMS: Klaviyo unifies email, SMS, push, and WhatsApp on one profile with shared segmentation (~$0.009–$0.015 per US SMS). ActiveCampaign's SMS is a paid add-on, MMS is US+AU only, and WhatsApp is a separate plan family — though its cross-channel orchestration inside a single automation is deeper.
  • Deliverability: EmailToolTester's independent 2026 testing rated Klaviyo 5-star vs ActiveCampaign 4-star (average inbox placement 83.1% across 15 ESPs). Klaviyo's deliverability monitoring and reputation-repair tooling is more visible; both need branded sending domains. Practically, both land fine — list hygiene matters more than the platform choice.

What switching actually costs

The price tables assume you're starting fresh. If you're migrating, budget the switching cost honestly:

  • Contacts transfer; flows don't. CSV import/export works both ways with field mapping, but automations must be rebuilt manually — nothing transfers. Both vendors' template libraries (60+ Klaviyo flows, 1,000+ ActiveCampaign recipes) speed the rebuild; ActiveCampaign's AI Brand Kit imports your site's branding for template recreation.
  • History starts cold. Engagement and purchase event history doesn't transfer, so Klaviyo's predictive models (CLV, churn risk) and ActiveCampaign's predictive features retrain from zero. Plan a 14–21 day deliverability warm-up on the new sending domain, engaged segments first.
  • Don't nuke your suppression list. Unsubscribe and opt-in history must be exported and re-imported separately — a plain CSV import can re-add previously unsubscribed contacts, which is a CAN-SPAM/GDPR problem, not just an embarrassment. Keep the old account live until sends, unsubscribes, and reporting reconcile.

Direction matters. Moving to Klaviyo, its ecommerce event model means rebuilding flows around store metrics — fast, since Klaviyo auto-pulls Shopify data. Moving to ActiveCampaign means rebuilding around a different data model (lists/tags/segments vs profiles/events) plus re-configuring CRM pipelines. Either way, budget a focused week, not a weekend.

Which should your store pick

Pick Klaviyo if you're a Shopify-native store in the ~2k–15k contact band doing classic ecommerce email: cart recovery, browse abandonment, post-purchase flows, and SMS. It's cheaper there, the Shopify integration is the deepest in the industry, the 60+ prebuilt flows get you live in an afternoon, and deliverability tooling is best-in-class. Watch the bill as you scale past 25k — and clean the list quarterly, because every active profile bills.

Pick ActiveCampaign if you're very small and price-sensitive (Starter at $15, with eyes open about the 5-action automation cap), very large (the 50k band is ~$320/mo cheaper), or you need the CRM: wholesale, high-touch sales, or lead scoring alongside email. The Plus tier is the real starting line for automation, and the all-contacts billing change means list hygiene is now a budget line item. Either way, model the add-ons before you commit — the stack tax is where both platforms make their margin.

Frequently asked questions

Is ActiveCampaign cheaper than Klaviyo for ecommerce?

It depends on list size — the gap is U-shaped. ActiveCampaign is cheaper at the extremes: Starter is $15/mo vs Klaviyo's $30 at 1,000 contacts (though Starter's automation is capped at 5 actions per flow), and around $400/mo vs Klaviyo's $720 at 50,000 contacts. Klaviyo is cheaper in the middle: $150 vs ActiveCampaign Plus $189 at 10,000 contacts. Around 25,000 contacts they're near parity (~$389–$400/mo).

Do Klaviyo and ActiveCampaign charge for unsubscribed contacts?

As of 2025, effectively yes on both. Klaviyo has billed on all active profiles — every contact you can reach — since February 2025, not just contacts you emailed. ActiveCampaign has billed all contacts including unsubscribed, bounced, and unconfirmed on new accounts since November 2025. List hygiene is now a billing practice on both platforms.

Does ActiveCampaign have a free plan?

No — ActiveCampaign offers a 14-day free trial only. Klaviyo has a free tier (250 active profiles, 500 emails/month, Klaviyo branding, no A/B testing). If free-to-start matters, Klaviyo wins; if you outgrow free quickly, compare Plus vs Klaviyo's paid bands instead.

Can I migrate my email flows from Klaviyo to ActiveCampaign?

Contacts transfer via CSV with field mapping, but flows and automations must be rebuilt manually — nothing transfers automatically. Historical engagement data doesn't transfer either, so predictive features retrain from zero, and you'll need a 14–21 day deliverability warm-up. Migrate your suppression/unsubscribe list separately to avoid re-adding unsubscribed contacts (a CAN-SPAM/GDPR risk).

Which is better for Shopify stores, ActiveCampaign or Klaviyo?

Klaviyo, for most Shopify stores: its native Shopify integration syncs products, purchase history, and browse behavior with product-level segmentation, and 60+ prebuilt ecommerce flows get you live fast. ActiveCampaign's Deep Data integration is functional but shallower — ActiveCampaign's own comparison page concedes Klaviyo is purpose-built for ecommerce. Choose ActiveCampaign instead if you need its built-in sales CRM or you're at a list size where its pricing wins.

Conclusion

ActiveCampaign vs Klaviyo isn't a “which is cheaper” question — it's a “which is cheaper at your list size, with your add-ons, in your direction of travel” question. The U-shaped gap means the right answer flips twice as you grow: ActiveCampaign at the start and at scale, Klaviyo in the busy middle where most growing stores actually live. Both 2025 billing changes punish bloated lists, both stack taxes hide in the add-ons, and switching costs a focused week plus a cold start on predictive data. Price the bands, count the add-ons, clean the list — then pick the platform whose strengths match the next 18 months of your store, not just this month's invoice.
