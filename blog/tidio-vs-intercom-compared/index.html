Tidio vs Intercom Compared: The Honest Pick for Small Stores in 2026

  • Customer Support
  • Published Oct 13, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 12 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Tidio is the better pick for most small ecommerce stores. It starts free, its Growth plan costs from about $49 a month billed annually with up to 10 agents on one flat workspace fee, and its Lyro AI agent costs roughly $0.70 per AI conversation. Intercom wins when you scale: per-seat pricing from $29 a month, deeper workflows, and the Fin AI agent at $0.99 per resolution. A five-person team on 2,000 chats a month can pay over $1,100 a month on Intercom once Fin fees land — versus a few hundred on Tidio.

Tidio and Intercom are the two names that come up most when a small store owner searches for live chat with an AI agent. Both promise the same outcome — a chatbot that answers the repetitive questions so your team only handles the hard ones — but they are built for different buyers: Tidio is priced for small businesses with one flat workspace fee, while Intercom is a platform for scaling support operations with per-seat pricing and the Fin AI agent billed per resolution. This guide compares real 2026 pricing (checked October 2026), the AI agents, setup, and ecommerce fit, ending with a verdict by budget and ticket volume. Also see our Intercom vs Crisp comparison.

What is Tidio?

Tidio is a customer communication platform aimed squarely at small businesses: live chat, email, and social messages (Facebook Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp) in one shared inbox, with a built-in AI agent called Lyro and a visual automation builder called Flows. Founded in Poland in 2013, it grew up serving online stores, which shows in everything from its one-click Shopify app to its pricing.

The pricing model is the key thing to understand. Tidio bills on billable conversations — a conversation counts when a human agent replies — not on seats. Every self-service plan covers up to 10 agents on one flat fee, so adding a teammate costs nothing extra. It holds a 4.6 rating on G2 from nearly 2,000 reviews and a 4.8 on the Shopify App Store, unusually strong for this category.

What is Intercom?

Intercom is a customer messaging platform built for scaling and SaaS teams: a messenger-first inbox with ticketing, a help center, product tours, behavioral targeting, and the Fin AI agent that resolves conversations end to end. Founded in 2011, it has spent over a decade moving upmarket, and in 2026 it rebranded its corporate identity around Fin, its AI agent — a sign of where the company's priorities now sit.

Intercom bills on seats plus usage. Every plan charges per teammate per month, and the Fin AI agent bills separately at $0.99 per resolution on top. There is no permanent free plan — only a 14-day trial — so there is a hard floor to what Intercom costs from day one. What does the same store pay on each?

The seat-versus-workspace difference is where small teams feel it. Take a store with two support agents handling 200 chats a month, with no AI yet:

  • Tidio: Growth from $49.17 a month billed annually covers 250 conversations and both agents. Total: about $49/mo.
  • Intercom: Essential at $29 per seat billed annually, times two agents. Total: $58/mo — before any AI or add-ons.

Close at this size — but adding a third agent costs Tidio nothing and Intercom another $29 a month. A five-person team on Intercom Essential pays $145 a month in seats alone before Fin touches a single conversation, while the same team on Tidio Growth still pays the one workspace fee.

Now add AI at real volume. A team handling 2,000 chats a month with the AI agent resolving 60% of them (1,200 resolutions) faces very different bills:

  • Tidio: Growth plan (scales with conversation volume, from $49.17/mo) plus Lyro. Lyro's published tiers run $210 a month for 300 AI conversations and $525 for 750, settling near $0.70 per AI conversation at volume — so 1,200 AI conversations lands near $840 a month. Total: roughly $900/mo.
  • Intercom: Essential at $29 per seat billed annually, times five seats ($145), plus Fin at $0.99 per resolution: 1,200 × $0.99 = $1,188. Total: about $1,333/mo.

The pattern: Intercom is competitive at very low volume, and Tidio pulls ahead as AI-handled volume grows. Lyro costs roughly $0.70 per AI conversation in tiered quotas, while Fin's $0.99 per resolution has no cap — so the better Fin performs, the bigger the invoice. One caution for Tidio buyers: every self-service plan caps at 10 agents, and the jump to Plus (from $300 a month plus usage) is a real pricing cliff for a team hiring its eleventh agent.

Quick fact: Tidio includes 50 Lyro AI conversations as a one-time free allowance — enough to test whether the AI understands your store before you pay for a Lyro quota.

Lyro is simpler and cheaper per conversation; Fin is deeper

Lyro's setup is genuinely quick: connect your store URL, select the pages it should learn from, and it starts answering shipping, returns, and stock questions in natural sentences. The catch is that the quota is separate from your base plan — the base plan covers human-handled conversations, and every AI conversation draws from the Lyro quota you pay for on top.

Fin is the more sophisticated agent: it resolves conversations end to end across more channels and takes actions inside your systems. But Fin is never sold standalone — it requires the Intercom helpdesk underneath — and per-resolution pricing means your bill grows precisely because the product is working. At 500 resolutions a month that is $495 in Fin fees before a single seat is paid for. Budget Fin as a share of conversation volume, not a fixed line item.

Setup and ease of use

This is the least controversial part of the comparison. Tidio wins on setup speed by a wide margin. Install the Shopify app (about three minutes), paste the widget code on any other site, point Lyro at your pages, and you are answering chats the same day. The visual Flows builder covers common automations — greetings, discount offers, abandoned-cart nudges — without technical skill.

Intercom needs a real implementation: connecting product data, curating the knowledge base Fin learns from, and configuring routing takes days to weeks, and teams usually assign an owner. The payoff is a more sophisticated system — behavioral triggers, in-app product tours, and multi-team routing that Tidio's simpler rules engine cannot match. If your team has no one technical and no appetite for a project, that gap matters more than any feature list.

Ecommerce features: where Tidio pulls ahead

Tidio was built for stores, and it shows in the details that save agents time every day:

  • Native Shopify and WooCommerce integrations that surface order history, cart contents, and purchase behavior inside the chat window — agents check order status or apply discount codes without leaving the conversation.
  • Abandoned-cart triggers that message shoppers who stall at checkout — a revenue feature, not just a support one.
  • Product recommendations via Lyro, which answers catalog questions like "do you have this in blue?" directly.
  • Multichannel inbox covering the channels stores actually use: web chat, email, Messenger, Instagram, and WhatsApp.

Intercom supports Shopify too, but its commerce features are thinner. Its strength is in-app messaging for SaaS products — onboarding tours, feature announcements, usage-based triggers — which is a different job. Where Intercom wins: scale and depth

Past a certain size, Intercom is the better platform, and the reasons are structural.

  • No seat cap and real governance. Tidio's self-service plans stop at 10 agents with a steep jump to Plus. Intercom scales to hundreds of seats with roles, permissions, SSO (Expert plan), SLAs, and audit-friendly reporting.
  • Workflow automation depth. Intercom's workflow builder handles multi-step routing, assignment rules, and escalation paths that Tidio's macros and simple rules cannot express.
  • Integration breadth. Third-party comparisons put Intercom at roughly 450 integrations against Tidio's 120 or so, with deeper hooks into SaaS stacks like Stripe, Salesforce, and product analytics tools.
  • Reporting. Team performance, conversation analytics, and Fin's resolution reporting are built for managers running support as a measured function, not just an inbox.

Tidio is the better answer for businesses still building support discipline; Intercom is the better answer when support is already a serious system — multiple teams, defined processes, and a budget that treats the platform as infrastructure. If your "support team" is two people and a shared login, you are not the second buyer yet.

Verdict by budget and ticket volume

Choose Tidio if

  • Your budget is under ~$150 a month with fewer than 2,000 chats a month.
  • You run a Shopify or WooCommerce store and want order-aware chat fast.
  • Your team is 1 to 10 people and you want flat, predictable workspace pricing.
  • You want a working AI chatbot this week, not a quarter-long implementation.

Choose Intercom if

  • Your support team is growing past 10 agents or is already a formal operation.
  • You sell SaaS and need in-app messaging, product tours, and behavioral triggers.
  • You need advanced routing, roles, SSO, SLAs, and management-grade reporting.
  • Your budget treats support software as infrastructure, not an expense to minimize.

Tidio for the small store, Intercom for the scaling support team. Start on Tidio's free plan to prove the channel works, move to Growth when volume justifies it, and only consider Intercom when team size, workflow complexity, or reporting needs exceed what a flat-fee workspace can offer. Frequently asked questions

Is Tidio really cheaper than Intercom?

For small teams, yes. Tidio's Growth plan starts around $49 per month billed annually for up to 10 agents on one flat workspace fee. Intercom's Essential plan costs $29 per seat per month billed annually, so two agents cost $58 before AI fees — and Fin's $0.99-per-resolution billing widens the gap as volume grows.

How do Lyro and Fin AI differ in pricing?

Lyro is sold as a separate monthly quota starting at $39 per month for 50 AI conversations ($32.50 billed annually) — roughly $0.70 to $0.78 per AI conversation. Fin bills per outcome at $0.99 per resolution with a 50-resolution monthly minimum, on top of seat pricing. The key difference: Lyro charges per AI conversation handled, Fin only when a conversation counts as resolved.

Which is better for a Shopify store, Tidio or Intercom?

Tidio is the better fit for most Shopify stores. Its native Shopify and WooCommerce integrations show order history, cart contents, and purchase behavior inside the chat window, plus abandoned-cart triggers and product recommendations through Lyro. Intercom supports Shopify but is designed around in-app messaging for SaaS products rather than retail support.

Can Tidio scale with a growing support team?

To a point. All Tidio self-service plans cap at 10 agents, and the jump to Plus (from $300 per month plus usage) is steep. Intercom has no seat cap and adds roles, SSO, SLAs, and advanced workflows on higher tiers, so teams growing past 10 agents or needing real governance usually outgrow Tidio.

Does Intercom have a free plan?

No — only a 14-day free trial. Tidio has a genuine free plan with 50 billable conversations per month plus live chat and ticketing, making it the only one of the two a micro-business can run at zero cost.

Conclusion

For the small store owner this guide is written for, Tidio is the right default: lower starting cost, a real free plan, flat workspace pricing that does not punish you for adding teammates, and an AI agent running the same afternoon you sign up. Intercom earns its premium when support becomes a scaled operation — more than 10 agents, complex routing, SaaS-style in-app messaging, and reporting that management reads. Match the tool to the operation you have today, not the one you hope to have in three years.
