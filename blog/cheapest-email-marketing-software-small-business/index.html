Cheapest Email Marketing Software for Small Business (2026)

  • Updated: October 2026
  • 9 min read

Quick answer

The cheapest real options in 2026 are Sender (free to 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month), Brevo (Starter from $9/month for 5,000 emails, priced by sends not contacts), and MailerLite (free to 500 subscribers; paid from $10/month). The lowest sticker price is Zoho Campaigns at about $4/month, but its feature set is thin. "Cheapest" depends on your list size and sending frequency — run the math below at your actual numbers before picking. Prices checked in October 2026.

Every email marketing tool advertises a low starting price. Few of them tell you what you will pay once your list grows — or how differently each one counts what you pay for. This guide compares the cheapest email marketing software for small business on real 2026 pricing, at real list sizes, so you can see what "cheap" actually costs. We priced each tool at 1,000, 5,000, and 10,000 subscribers, ranked the free plans by what they genuinely let you do, and flagged the pricing traps that turn a $9 plan into a $90 one. If you are a creator comparing the two biggest names instead, read our Mailchimp vs ConvertKit for creators guide.

How email pricing actually works

There are two pricing models, and understanding them is half the battle. Contact-based pricing (Mailchimp, MailerLite, Kit, Moosend) charges by how many contacts you store, and your sends are bundled in. Send-based pricing (Brevo) charges by how many emails you send, and contacts are effectively unlimited on any plan. Neither is universally cheaper — they reward opposite behaviors.

Quick fact: If you send one newsletter a month to 20,000 contacts, send-based Brevo can cost $9/month while contact-based Mailchimp would charge over $100. If you send daily to 500 contacts, contact-based MailerLite at $10/month beats Brevo's send credits easily.

Contact-based: simple, scales with your list

Contact-based plans are easy to budget: you know your list size, you know your price. The catch is that the price rises whether you email often or not. Some tools also count people you will never email again. Mailchimp bills for unsubscribed and non-subscribed contacts, so a neglected list inflates your invoice. MailerLite and Kit, by contrast, count only active subscribers. Always check the counting rule — it is the most overlooked line on any pricing page.

Send-based: cheap for big lists that send rarely

Send-based pricing is the budget sleeper. Brevo lets you store unlimited contacts on every plan and charges for email volume: the free plan covers 300 emails a day, Starter is $9 a month for 5,000 emails, and higher tiers scale from there. If your business sends a monthly newsletter or occasional promotions to a large list, this model is dramatically cheaper than contact-based billing. The tradeoff: daily senders and heavy automation flows burn through credits, and Brevo gates its full automation builder behind the $18/month Business plan.

The best free plans, honestly ranked

Free plans are the cheapest way to start, but they are not equal. Ranked by what a small business can genuinely do before paying:

1. Sender — the roomiest free tier

Sender's free plan allows up to 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month, with automation, popups, and templates included. For a small business that sends weekly, this can carry you for a year or more of growth. Paid plans start at $10 a month.

2. Kit — the biggest free audience

Kit's free Newsletter plan allows up to 10,000 subscribers with unlimited landing pages, forms, and broadcasts. It is creator-focused rather than business-focused, and automation has been trimmed from the free tier, but no other free plan lets you reach this many inboxes for free.

3. EmailOctopus — simple and generous

Free for up to 2,500 subscribers with a deliberately simple newsletter workflow. Paid plans start around $9 a month on annual billing. Best if you want the cheapest path from free to paid without relearning a complex tool.

4. MailerLite — small but complete

Free for 500 subscribers and 12,000 emails a month, with landing pages and automation included. The ceiling arrives sooner, but the tool you learn on free is the same polished tool you grow into from $10 a month.

5. Brevo — unlimited contacts, metered sends

Free with unlimited contacts but 300 emails a day. Ideal if your list is large and your sending is light — a monthly newsletter to 50,000 contacts costs nothing but the daily cap forces scheduling discipline.

Quick fact: Mailchimp's free plan — 250 contacts and 500 sends a month — is the stingiest free tier among major tools. It is fine for testing the interface, but treat it as a trial, not a plan.

The cheapest picks, reviewed

Brevo — cheapest for large lists that send rarely

Brevo's send-based model makes it the cheapest paid option in the most common small-business scenario: a list of several thousand contacts emailed a few times a month. Starter at $9 a month covers 5,000 emails with unlimited contacts, and the platform adds SMS and a basic CRM on higher tiers. Watch for the automation paywall: the visual automation builder's full version needs the $18/month Business plan. If your marketing is newsletters and promotions rather than complex flows, Brevo is the value king.

Sender — cheapest all-rounder on free

Sender's combination of a genuinely roomy free plan and a $10/month paid entry makes it the cheapest all-rounder for businesses that want to stay free longest. Automation and popups are included from the start, so the free plan behaves like a paid plan on pricier tools. The tradeoff is a smaller template library and fewer integrations than Mailchimp or MailerLite.

MailerLite — best value for the money

MailerLite is not the absolute cheapest, but reviewers consistently rank it the best overall small-business pick because it balances price, usability, and features. From $10 a month you get a clean drag-and-drop editor, automation, landing pages, and solid deliverability. If you want the tool you are least likely to outgrow or regret, start here.

Moosend — cheapest with real automation

Moosend's free plan covers 1,000 subscribers with unlimited emails, and paid plans start at $9 a month with the full automation builder included — no automation paywall. For businesses whose welcome series and abandoned-cart flows are the point, Moosend delivers automation depth at a price where competitors charge for basics.

Zoho Campaigns — lowest sticker price

At roughly $4 a month, Zoho Campaigns has the cheapest entry price in the industry. It makes sense if your business already lives in the Zoho ecosystem. Standalone, the feature set and template quality lag the tools above, and the price advantage narrows as your list grows.

What about Mailchimp?

Mailchimp is the most famous name and one of the most expensive once you leave the free plan: Essentials starts at $13 a month for 500 contacts and reaches about $135 a month for 10,000 on Standard. You pay for a polished all-in-one suite — templates, e-commerce integrations, predictive segmentation — not for sending email cheaply. Businesses that need the suite should compare it properly; businesses that just need email should not. Our Mailchimp vs Kit guide breaks down when its price is justified.

What the sticker price hides

Four traps turn cheap plans expensive. Check each before you commit:

  • Who counts toward your limit. Mailchimp bills for unsubscribed contacts; MailerLite and Kit do not. A list with years of churn can cost 30% more on the wrong tool. Archive or clean dead contacts before you pay for a higher tier.
  • Send caps vs contact caps. Some plans cap monthly sends at a multiple of your contact count. A daily newsletter to a big list can hit the ceiling fast, forcing an upgrade that has nothing to do with list growth.
  • Automation paywalls. The entry price often excludes the automation builder — full workflows may need the next tier up. Moosend and MailerLite include automation early; Brevo and Mailchimp gate it higher.
  • Overage billing. Exceeding a tier mid-cycle usually triggers automatic charges rather than paused sending. Set list-size alerts, and know your plan's overage policy.

How to pick the cheapest for your business

Best overall value

  • MailerLite — from $10/month. The best balance of price, ease of use, and features for a typical small business sending weekly.

Best free plan

  • Sender — free to 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month, with automation included. Stay free the longest.

Cheapest for big lists

  • Brevo — from $9/month, priced by sends. Cheapest when your list is large but you send a few times a month.

Cheapest with real automation

  • Moosend — from $9/month with the full automation builder. Cheapest path to welcome series and cart flows.

Follow this decision path. First, count your contacts and your monthly sends. Under 2,500 contacts? Start free on Sender or EmailOctopus. Second, name your one must-have workflow. If it is automation, shortlist Moosend and MailerLite; if it is a simple newsletter, Brevo wins on price. Third, project one year ahead. Price your list at double its current size on two finalists — the tool that is cheapest now is not always cheapest at 2x. If email sits inside a bigger sales process, pair your pick with a free CRM for startups or compare HubSpot vs Pipedrive for small teams before the list gets messy. And if you connect email to the rest of your stack, cheaper Zapier alternatives can automate the glue for less.

Frequently asked questions

What is the absolute cheapest email marketing tool?

Zoho Campaigns has the lowest sticker price at roughly $4 per month, but the cheapest plan is rarely the best value. Sender's free plan (2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month) and Brevo's $9 per month Starter plan usually deliver more for less, because they include automation and generous sending allowances that the rock-bottom tools gate or omit.

Is send-based or contact-based pricing cheaper?

It depends on how often you send. Send-based pricing like Brevo's ($9 per month for 5,000 emails) is cheaper when your list is large but you send infrequently — say one newsletter a month to 20,000 contacts. Contact-based pricing like MailerLite's ($10 per month) is cheaper when your list is small and you send often, because frequent sending to a big list burns through send-based credits fast.

Are free email marketing plans really free?

Yes, the genuine ones have no credit card requirement, but every free plan has a real ceiling: Sender allows 2,500 subscribers and 15,000 emails a month, Brevo allows 300 emails a day, and MailerLite allows 500 subscribers and 12,000 emails a month. Mailchimp's free plan is the stingiest at 250 contacts and 500 sends. Most free plans also include the vendor's branding on your emails.

How much does email marketing cost at 10,000 subscribers?

On the cheapest tools, surprisingly little: Brevo can cost as little as $9 per month if you send under 5,000 emails a month, Sender and MailerLite sit in the $10 to $15 range, and EmailOctopus starts around $24 per month. Mailchimp Standard at 10,000 contacts costs about $135 per month — roughly ten times more — which shows how much pricing models differ.

Can I switch email tools later without losing my list?

Yes. Export your contacts as a CSV from your old tool and import them into the new one — every major platform supports this. Your automations and templates will not transfer, so plan to rebuild them. Some tools, like Kit, offer free assisted migration on paid plans, which can save hours.

Conclusion

  • Cheapest overall: Brevo ($9/month, send-based) and Sender (free to 2,500 subscribers) cover most small businesses for the least money.
  • Best value: MailerLite from $10/month — the tool you are least likely to regret or outgrow.
  • Cheapest automation: Moosend from $9/month with the full automation builder included.
  • Rule of thumb: price your list at double its size on two finalists before committing, and always check who counts toward the limit — unsubscribed contacts can inflate your bill by 30% on the wrong tool.
