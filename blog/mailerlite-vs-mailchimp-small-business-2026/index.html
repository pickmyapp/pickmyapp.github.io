mailerlite vs mailchimp for small business (2026)

  • Email Marketing
  • Published Oct 17, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 11 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most small businesses, MailerLite wins on price — and it's not close. At 10,000 subscribers, MailerLite's Comfort plan costs $89/mo ($80.10/mo billed annually) versus $135/mo for Mailchimp Standard, a $552/year gap that widens to $1,572/year at 25,000 subscribers ($179 vs $310/mo). MailerLite is also simpler for non-marketers, includes automation on every plan, and only bills you for active subscribers. Mailchimp's case rests on deeper automation, predictive audiences, and a broader all-in-one marketing suite (email, SMS, landing pages, social posting) under Intuit — worth it if you'll use those extras, but expensive if you won't.

Head-to-head pricing: what you'll pay at 4 list sizes

Note on plan names: on June 16, 2026, MailerLite renamed its plans — Growing Business became Comfort and Advanced became Power — and raised prices across every tier. Comparisons quoting the old Growing Business prices (e.g. $73/mo at 10,000 subscribers) are out of date. The figures below are the current Comfort prices versus Mailchimp Standard, both billed monthly in USD.

At 25,000 subscribers, MailerLite Comfort ($179/mo) costs 42% less than Mailchimp Standard ($310/mo) — a difference of $1,572 every year before any annual-billing discount.

Two caveats keep this honest. First, if your needs are genuinely basic, Mailchimp Essentials (the cheapest paid tier) is cheaper than Standard — $13/mo at 500, $45/mo at 2,500, $110/mo at 10,000 — but it drops advanced automation, predictive audiences, and behavioral targeting, which is precisely what makes Mailchimp competitive. Second, Mailchimp's list price isn't always what new customers pay: it routinely offers first-year introductory pricing at roughly 50% off (about $22.50/mo at 2,500 subscribers and $55/mo at 10,000 in year one). From year two onward, you pay the full rate — which is why the table above uses regular pricing.

Annual-billing math: MailerLite's 10% vs Mailchimp's intro pricing

Annual billing changes the math on both sides, in opposite ways:

  • MailerLite gives a flat 10% annual discount on both plans, with no time limit. Comfort drops to $10.80, $29.70, $80.10, and $161.10/mo equivalent at 500 / 2,500 / 10,000 / 25,000 subscribers; Power (unlimited sending) drops to $22.50, $44.10, $116.10, and $215.10/mo. A 10,000-subscriber list on Comfort costs $961.20/year annually.
  • Mailchimp offers no annual discount at all on regular pricing. Its only discount is the first-year introductory offer for new customers — effectively a 50%-off trial year, not a standing benefit. At 10,000 subscribers that means roughly $660 in year one, then $1,620/year from year two onward.

Over two years at 10,000 subscribers, MailerLite Comfort on annual billing costs about $1,922. Mailchimp Standard — even with its half-price first year — costs about $2,280. The gap grows every year after that because Mailchimp's full rate applies indefinitely while MailerLite's 10% annual discount renews.

Send limits: how many emails you actually get

The sticker price is only half the story — both platforms cap how much you can send, and the caps differ in ways that matter for frequent senders:

  • MailerLite Comfort: 10 times your tier ceiling per month (at the 10,000-subscriber tier, that's 100,000 emails a month). MailerLite Power: unlimited sending at every list size — no surprise bills in months when you email more.
  • Mailchimp Standard: 12 times your total contact count per month (120,000 emails at 10,000 contacts). Mailchimp Essentials: 10 times contacts. Premium goes to 15 times.

For most small businesses sending a weekly newsletter plus a few automations, neither cap is a problem — a 10,000-subscriber list sending four campaigns a month uses 40,000 emails, well under both limits. The cap bites only if you email daily or run high-volume e-commerce flows. MailerLite Power's unlimited sending is the cleanest answer if you're a frequent sender; it's $129/mo at 10,000 subscribers ($116.10 annual), still under Mailchimp Standard's $135.

The hidden cost: how each platform counts subscribers

This is the single most misunderstood line in the comparison, and it quietly adds hundreds of dollars a year to many Mailchimp bills.

MailerLite only counts active, unique subscribers — one person, one count, even across multiple segments. Unsubscribed contacts and bounced addresses don't inflate your bill.

Mailchimp counts every contact in your audience — subscribed, unsubscribed, and non-subscribed — and counts duplicates across separate audiences separately. If your account shows 12,000 contacts but 3,500 are unsubscribed or duplicated across lists, Mailchimp still bills you at the 12,000-contact tier. Agencies report that cleaning a list (deduplicating and removing unsubscribed contacts) before migrating to MailerLite routinely drops the billable count by 20–30% — which at 10,000 subscribers can mean dropping an entire pricing band.

Mailchimp's contact-counting policy is its most criticized billing practice: unsubscribed contacts — people who explicitly don't want your emails — still count toward what you pay.

Ease of use for non-marketers

MailerLite is the simpler tool, full stop. Its drag-and-drop editor is clean and modern, the interface has few menus, and the most common beginner tasks — building a signup form, sending a first campaign, turning on a welcome email — take minutes. G2's 2026 awards (Ease of Use, Buyer's Choice) reflect what users consistently say: it's the least intimidating email platform in the category.

Mailchimp has moved in the opposite direction since its 2021 acquisition by Intuit. It's now a broad marketing platform — email plus SMS, landing pages, websites, social posting, a CRM layer — and the interface shows the weight of all those features. There's more to learn, more to click through, and the email builder itself feels dated next to MailerLite's. If you're a non-marketer who just wants to send a newsletter and run a few automations, MailerLite gets you there faster. If you'll genuinely use the wider suite (say, you run a store and want email, SMS, and social in one place), Mailchimp's complexity starts to earn its keep.

Automation depth

MailerLite includes multi-step automation on every plan — including the free plan's 3 automations. Welcome sequences, abandoned-cart flows, and re-engagement campaigns work out of the box, and Power adds AI-optimized send times and multivariate testing (up to 8 variations).

Mailchimp gates automation by tier: Essentials lacks the advanced customer journeys, while Standard adds multi-step journeys, behavioral targeting, predictive audiences (who's likely to buy, who's at risk of churning), and multivariate testing. For a small business that wants set-and-forget sophistication — a welcome series that branches based on clicks, win-back flows timed by purchase probability — Mailchimp Standard is the deeper tool. The question is whether you'll use that depth: most small businesses run three to five automations, which MailerLite handles at every price tier.

Landing pages and websites

MailerLite ships a website builder and landing pages on every plan, free included (1 page and 1 site on free; unlimited on paid plans). For a small business without a website, this alone can replace a separate site builder for a simple presence and lead capture.

Mailchimp offers landing pages and a website builder too, but they're positioned as part of the broader platform suite — and notably absent from the free plan. It's capable, but you're paying Standard money ($20/mo minimum at 500 contacts) to get what MailerLite includes free. If landing pages are core to your strategy, also see our MailerLite vs ConvertKit and MailerLite vs Kit at 25k subscribers breakdowns for how the alternatives price the same features.

Deliverability reputation

Both platforms are established senders with the infrastructure to land in inboxes — neither has a deliverability scandal to its name, and both authenticate with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC. The practical differences:

  • MailerLite is widely praised for responsive support (24/7 live chat, even on paid starter plans) and offers deliverability consulting on Enterprise plans. Its smaller, more focused platform means fewer shared-IP reputation surprises from unrelated senders.
  • Mailchimp's scale is a double-edged sword: enormous sending infrastructure, but its free plan historically attracted low-quality senders, which has periodically dragged on shared-IP reputation. On paid plans with a dedicated sending reputation, this matters far less.

For a small business on either paid plan, deliverability will be determined far more by your list hygiene, authentication setup, and engagement rates than by the platform. Neither is a reason to choose or reject either tool.

Why MailerLite

  • Cheapest at every list size above 500 subscribers — $552/year less than Mailchimp Standard at 10k, $1,572/year less at 25k
  • Standing 10% annual discount; Mailchimp has no annual discount
  • Only active subscribers are billed — unsubscribed and duplicate contacts don't inflate your price
  • Simpler for non-marketers; automation, landing pages, and a website included on every plan, free included
  • Power plan offers unlimited sending with no overage surprises

Why Mailchimp

  • Deeper automation: customer journeys, behavioral targeting, and predictive audiences on Standard
  • Broader all-in-one suite — email, SMS, landing pages, websites, social posting, and a CRM layer under one login
  • Half-price first-year introductory pricing softens the entry cost for new customers
  • E-commerce integrations and reporting are more mature, especially for Shopify and WooCommerce stores
  • Stronger brand recognition and a larger template and integration ecosystem

Frequently asked questions

Is MailerLite really cheaper than Mailchimp in 2026?

Yes. After MailerLite's June 2026 repricing (Growing Business became Comfort), Comfort still costs less than Mailchimp Standard at every common list size: $12 vs $20 at 500 subscribers, $33 vs $60 at 2,500, $89 vs $135 at 10,000, and $179 vs $310 at 25,000 per month. The gap is $552/year at 10k and $1,572/year at 25k before annual discounts.

Does Mailchimp count unsubscribed contacts toward billing?

Yes. Mailchimp counts subscribed, unsubscribed, and non-subscribed contacts in your audience — and counts duplicates across separate audiences separately. MailerLite only counts active, unique subscribers, so unsubscribed contacts and duplicates never inflate your bill. This alone can drop you a full pricing tier when migrating.

Which free plan is better, MailerLite or Mailchimp?

MailerLite's: 250 subscribers with 2,500 monthly emails, up to 3 automations, 3 forms, 1 landing page, and 1 website, with no credit card required. Mailchimp's free plan allows 250 contacts but only 500 monthly emails with a 250-per-day cap, no multi-step automation, and no landing pages.

How many emails can I send per month on each plan?

MailerLite Comfort allows 10 times your tier ceiling per month (100,000 emails at the 10k tier); Power offers unlimited sending. Mailchimp Standard allows 12 times your contact count (120,000 at 10k contacts); Essentials allows 10 times. A weekly newsletter plus automations uses roughly 40,000–60,000 emails at 10k subscribers, comfortably under both.

Is Mailchimp worth the extra cost over MailerLite?

Only if you use what you're paying for. Mailchimp Standard's extras — predictive audiences, behavioral targeting, and an all-in-one suite covering SMS, social, and CRM — justify the premium for stores and teams running sophisticated marketing. If you mainly need newsletters, automations, and landing pages, MailerLite does the same work for roughly half the price.

Conclusion

For most small businesses, MailerLite is the clear winner in 2026: it's cheaper at every list size, simpler to use, includes automation and landing pages on every plan, and its billing only counts active subscribers. Mailchimp Standard earns its higher price only for teams that will genuinely use its deeper automation, predictive audiences, and all-in-one marketing suite — and even then, the first-year intro discount masks a year-two bill that's roughly double. Run your actual contact count (after removing unsubscribed and duplicate contacts, which Mailchimp bills and MailerLite doesn't) through both pricing pages before you commit, and start on MailerLite's free plan to feel the difference yourself.
