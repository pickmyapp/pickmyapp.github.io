Activepieces vs n8n: Open-Source Automation Compared (2026)

  • AI Automation Tools
  • Published Oct 12, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Activepieces and n8n both let you run powerful automation outside the big SaaS vendors, and both charge per workflow run rather than per step. The split: Activepieces is MIT-licensed, has the friendlier builder and a free cloud tier, so it wins for non-technical teams and anyone embedding automation inside a product. n8n has the deeper integration catalog, stronger error handling and a far larger community, so it wins for technical teams and complex production workflows. Prices checked October 2026: Activepieces Cloud starts free, n8n Cloud starts at €24/month.

Activepieces and n8n are the two most serious answers when a business wants automation it can run itself, free of a vendor’s servers and per-action meter. Both give you a visual builder for multi-step workflows, and both can run on your own infrastructure. But “open source” means two different things here: Activepieces ships under the MIT license, the most permissive license in software, while n8n uses its own Sustainable Use License, a fair-code license with strings attached. This guide compares the two for small teams of roughly 1 to 20 people, using prices checked against both vendors’ official pricing pages in October 2026. If you are also weighing hosted-only tools, start with our cheaper Zapier alternatives for the wider picture.

What is Activepieces?

Activepieces is an MIT-licensed workflow automation platform, founded in 2022, built around modular integrations called “pieces.” Each piece is an open-source TypeScript module connecting a third-party app or service, and roughly 60% of them are community-contributed.

The builder is deliberately simple: a vertical, top-down flow editor — a trigger at the top, then steps in order, like a checklist. Fields are configured in plain language, and a chat-to-automation feature drafts a flow from a written description. The platform also ships built-in Tables (lightweight databases), an agent builder, and native Model Context Protocol support so AI agents can call your flows as tools. For mainstream apps the piece library covers what most small businesses need, and anything with an API is reachable through the HTTP piece or a webhook.

What is n8n?

n8n is a node-based workflow automation platform, founded in Berlin in 2019, where every automation is a workflow made of connected nodes. Each node performs one action or transformation, and data flows between them on a free-form canvas. The project lists 400-plus official nodes, with community-built nodes extending the catalog further.

Where n8n shows its developer roots is control. A Code node runs JavaScript or Python at any point in a workflow, an HTTP Request node can call virtually any API, and LangChain-native AI agent nodes support retrieval, memory, and tool-calling loops. The community is an order of magnitude larger than Activepieces’ — roughly 179,000 versus 21,000 GitHub stars — so most problems you hit have already been solved publicly.

The license difference that actually matters

Most comparisons bury this in a footnote. It deserves its own section, because it is the single biggest structural difference between the two tools.

Activepieces is MIT-licensed. You can use it, modify it, embed it inside your own product, and even resell it, with no restrictions beyond keeping the copyright notice. If you are an agency reselling automation to clients, or a SaaS company embedding a workflow builder in your product, the MIT license gives you a clean legal path — Activepieces even sells a white-label Embed plan (from $36,000 a year) on that foundation. The self-hosted Community Edition is not a trial: it runs unlimited flows on your own server.

n8n is source-available under its Sustainable Use License, a “fair-code” license. You can self-host it and use it for your own internal business automation without paying a cent, but you cannot offer n8n itself as a hosted service to third parties or build a competing automation product on top of it. For most small businesses automating their own operations, this never bites. For agencies, managed-service providers, and software companies embedding automation in something they sell, it is a genuine legal grey area Activepieces simply does not have.

Quick fact: “Fair-code” means the code is public and free for your own use, but commercial redistribution is restricted. True open source, like the MIT license, imposes no such restrictions.

Pricing compared, plan by plan

Here is where Activepieces and n8n look similar at first and diverge fast. Both charge per run, not per step: Activepieces spends one credit per flow run however many steps it has, and n8n counts one execution per workflow run however many nodes it has. Both therefore dodge the per-action pricing that makes Zapier expensive at scale — but they price the run itself differently.

Where Activepieces is cheaper

The free tier is the headline. Activepieces gives you a usable cloud plan at $0 — 1,000 flow runs a month, unlimited flows, up to 5 users — and Plus costs $20 a month for 10,000 credits. n8n has no free cloud tier, and Starter is €24 a month for 2,500 executions. If your volume is modest and you would rather not touch a server, Activepieces is the cheaper managed path at every rung.

Where n8n pulls level

Self-host, and the difference evaporates: both Community editions run unlimited workflows on your own server, so the bill is just a small virtual server and your maintenance time. Our n8n pricing guide: self-hosted vs cloud costs walks through the plan-by-plan math and the real maintenance burden.

A real cost example

Take a client follow-up flow with eight steps: a form submission enriches the lead, writes to the CRM, creates a task, and sends a welcome email. At 400 leads a month, Activepieces burns 400 credits — inside the free plan’s 1,000 — so the managed cost is $0. On n8n, those 400 runs are 400 executions, but with no free cloud tier you either pay €24 a month for Starter or self-host.

Scale to 4,000 leads a month. Activepieces charges one credit per run: 4,000 credits fits inside Plus at $20 a month. n8n counts 4,000 executions, which exceeds Starter’s 2,500 allowance and pushes you to Pro at €60 a month. The honest rule: for managed cloud at low-to-medium volume, Activepieces is cheaper; self-host, and the decision is about features, not money.

Ease of use for a small team

First week

Activepieces is the easier tool to pick up. The vertical step-by-step builder reads like a recipe, configuration is described in plain language, and the chat-to-automation feature gives you a working draft from a sentence like “when someone fills in the contact form, add them to the CRM and notify the sales channel.” A non-technical owner can build a real workflow on day one.

n8n is usable without code for common tasks, but its canvas assumes you understand how data items move between nodes and are comfortable writing small expressions. If your team thinks in flowcharts, Activepieces is faster; if your team thinks in backend systems, n8n will feel like home. If you are comparing the two hosted-style builders as well, our n8n vs Make comparison covers the learning curve in more depth.

Debugging and reliability

This is where n8n’s maturity shows: per-node retry settings, pinned test data for rerunning one node, and dedicated error workflows that alert your team when something fails. Activepieces covers the basics — retries and run logs — but its error handling is thinner and its smaller community offers fewer solved edge cases. For mission-critical workflows that run while you sleep, n8n is the safer bet.

Pieces vs nodes: integrations

n8n has the broader catalog: 400-plus official nodes plus community-built ones, with deeper coverage of databases, infrastructure tools, and niche SaaS. Activepieces lists 300-plus pieces, about 60% community-contributed, and cites 760-plus integrations across its platform when every connector variant is counted.

Both cover the mainstream stack most small businesses run — Google Workspace, Slack, HubSpot, Stripe, Shopify, Notion — and both fall back to HTTP modules and webhooks when an app is missing. n8n’s HTTP node has a small edge: you can paste a cURL command to generate a request instantly. Activepieces pieces, being open-source TypeScript, are easy to inspect and extend. For mainstream apps the piece catalog is enough; for obscure or custom integrations, n8n’s longer catalog and larger community win.

AI and agent workflows

Activepieces is the friendlier AI builder: native AI pieces for major model providers, a visual agent builder, Model Context Protocol support so agents can call your flows as tools, and the option to connect your own AI provider keys on Plus and up — which drops every AI step from up to 20 credits to one. For an AI layer on top of your existing tools, see our explainer on IrisGo, the AI workflow assistant.

n8n is the deeper AI foundation for developers: LangChain-native AI agent nodes, a Code node for custom logic around models, and the ability to point at models hosted on your own infrastructure, which matters if customer data cannot leave your region. For agents that call tools in loops, retrieval pipelines over your own documents, or private models behind your firewall, n8n’s primitives go further.

Self-hosting effort compared

Self-hosting is easy on both. Activepieces deploys from a single Docker Compose file with three containers (app, PostgreSQL, Redis); n8n runs in two containers or via npm. Expect five to ten minutes of setup, a $5-to-$10-a-month virtual server, and ongoing responsibility for updates, backups, SSL, and uptime.

The trade-off is identical for both: self-hosting is the cheapest option at any real volume, but it turns a monthly bill into an ownership job. If nobody on your team wants that job, Activepieces’ managed cloud is the cheaper off-ramp. Regulated teams should note that Activepieces’ paid tiers also offer air-gapped self-hosting with SSO, custom roles, and audit logs.

Our verdict

Choose Activepieces if

  • You want the easiest open-source automation a non-technical team can actually run.
  • You are embedding automation inside a product or reselling it to clients (MIT license, no legal grey area).
  • You want a free managed cloud tier and the cheapest paid cloud entry at $20 a month.
  • You are building AI-powered flows and want native agent and MCP support out of the box.

Choose n8n if

  • Your team is technical and wants deep control: code at any node, custom agent logic, self-hosted models.
  • Your workflows are complex or mission-critical and need serious error handling and retries.
  • You need the widest integration catalog and the largest community solving edge cases.
  • You plan to self-host anyway, which makes the price difference between them vanish.

You are not locked in on day one. Activepieces has a permanent free cloud plan and n8n offers a cloud trial, so build the same small workflow in both and see which one your team actually finishes. Frequently asked questions

Is Activepieces really open source?

Yes. Activepieces is licensed under the MIT license, which means you can use, modify, embed, and even resell it with no restrictions, and its self-hosted Community Edition runs unlimited flows on your own server. n8n is source-available rather than open source: its Sustainable Use License allows internal business use and self-hosting, but blocks using it as the basis of a competing hosted service.

Is Activepieces cheaper than n8n for a small team?

On managed cloud, usually. Activepieces has a free plan with 1,000 credits a month and a Plus plan at $20 a month for 10,000 credits, where one credit is one flow run regardless of step count. n8n Cloud has no free tier: Starter costs €24 a month (€20 billed annually) for 2,500 executions. Self-hosted, both cost only your server and maintenance time. Prices checked October 2026.

Which is easier to use for a non-technical team?

Activepieces is the easier tool to start with. Its builder is a vertical, top-down flow editor with plain-language configuration, and a chat-to-automation feature that drafts flows from a description. n8n’s node canvas assumes you understand JSON data, expressions, and API credentials, so non-technical teams need a longer learning period.

Can I move my workflows between Activepieces and n8n?

There is no automatic importer between the two platforms. You rebuild each flow or workflow in the other tool, reconnect your app credentials, and test everything before switching over. Keep the old tool running until the rebuilt workflows are verified, and migrate the simplest automations first.

Which is better for AI agent workflows?

Both have genuine AI features. Activepieces ships native AI pieces, an agent builder, Model Context Protocol support, and lets you bring your own AI provider keys on Plus and up, which drops each AI step to one credit. n8n offers LangChain-native AI agent nodes and a Code node, which gives developers deeper control over custom agent behavior.

Conclusion

Activepieces and n8n both solve the same problem — self-run automation billed per run instead of per action — but serve different people. Activepieces wins for non-technical teams, agencies embedding automation in products, and anyone who wants a free cloud tier and a $20 entry plan under the MIT license. n8n wins for technical teams building complex, mission-critical workflows, with a larger catalog, stronger error handling, and a far larger community. Self-host, and the price gap disappears: then the choice is about who builds the workflows, not what they cost.
