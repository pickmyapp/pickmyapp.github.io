Fleeep explained: the free AI notes app that topped Product Hunt on November 7, and what it means for creators and small teams (2026)

Quick answer

Fleeep is a free notes app for people who make things — designers, writers, video makers and small teams — built solo by product designer Lydia since August 2026. It launched on Product Hunt on November 7, 2026, where it topped the day's ranking, and the maker reported over 120 sign-ups in its first day. It is capture-first, not write-first: one calm place for screenshots, voice notes, saved links, palettes and ideas, with a read-later reader and voice notes that transcribe themselves. It costs $0 at launch, works on phone and computer, and works offline. The honest catch: it is days old, has no independent reviews yet, and it does not do meeting transcription or long-form AI drafting — so it complements tools like Otter and Jasper rather than replacing them.

What Fleeep actually is

Fleeep is a notes and inspiration app with a simple pitch: "one calm place for everything that inspires you." Most of us keep ideas in five places at once — screenshots, a notes app, saved posts, bookmarks and open tabs — and Fleeep puts them in one place that works on your phone and your computer, online or off. The official site's tagline is "Every idea, close at hand."

The maker is Lydia, a product designer building the app on her own. She started in August 2026, and a small group of testers used it in the week before launch; their feedback shaped three features that shipped on day one. That tells you what kind of tool this is: one person's answer to a problem she had herself, not a venture-backed platform with a roadmap committee.

The launch ran on Product Hunt on November 7, 2026, where Fleeep topped the day's ranking, according to the independent launch guide that covered it — a small caveat belongs here, because Product Hunt's own product page does not display a rank badge in its text, so the #1 claim rests on that launch coverage. What is directly verifiable is the maker's own thank-you post the next day: "fleeep.app launched on Product Hunt yesterday and over 120 of you have signed up since." One hundred and twenty sign-ups is a modest number, and that modesty is part of the story — this is a week-old tool from a solo maker, not a breakout hit, and the article that pretends otherwise is selling you something.

Who is it for? The Product Hunt tagline says it plainly: "The notes app for people who make things." If you collect references for client work, save articles to read later, record voice notes while walking, or keep a mood board of colours and screenshots for a project, that is the job Fleeep is built for. If you need meeting transcription, marketing copy or a team workspace, it is not — and the maker does not claim it is.

Capture-first, not write-first — the frame that matters

Nearly every 2026 roundup of AI writing tools ranks the same ten products by drafting quality — ChatGPT, Claude, Jasper, Copy.ai, Grammarly, Notion AI and the rest — and none of them covers capture-first tools at all. That is the gap Fleeep sits in, and it is worth understanding before you compare it with anything, because capture and writing are different jobs. Writing tools generate text: marketing copy, blog drafts, emails. Capture tools save things before they disappear: a screenshot, a voice note recorded on a walk, a link you want to read later, a colour you spotted on a poster. Fleeep is firmly in the second camp.

Capture types. One tap saves a note, a list, a photo, a link, a palette, a quote, a voice note or a sketch — no folder to choose first, which is the whole point: the friction that kills capture is the decision about where something goes, and Fleeep removes it.

Voice notes that write themselves out. Every voice note is transcribed automatically, "so you can search what you said." That is the AI in "AI notes app" — transcription and search, not generation. There is no AI chat that writes for you and no long-form drafting mode.

Palettes and boards. Pull colours out of any picture, copy the codes, and check contrast — a palette is a mood-board-style collection built around a feeling, a project or a look, and the feature that most obviously comes from a product designer's own workflow. Gather things into boards, shown as a tidy grid or spread on a canvas, and share a board with a link.

The read-later reader. Save a link and read it in a calm, clean reader, even offline. That reader matters more than it looks: Pocket, the read-it-later app many writers used for research capture, shut down on July 8, 2025, and the "save an article, read it calmly, highlight one line, turn it into a note" loop has been split across paid tools ever since.

What Fleeep costs — and the free-stack math

Fleeep costs $0. Product Hunt lists its price at 0 USD, the maker says "Fleeep is free," and the official site calls it "a free notes and inspiration app." No paid tier, no usage limits and no credit-card requirement are mentioned anywhere on the launch page or the official site as read on November 9, 2026. Say "free at launch," though, not "free forever": the tool is a week old and the maker has said nothing about a future paid plan, and a free launch is not a promise.

The interesting comparison is Fleeep versus the paid stack that does the same three jobs. Voice transcription is Otter.ai Pro at $8.33 a user a month billed annually; a read-later app with highlighting is Readwise Reader at $7.99 a month. Together that is $16.32 a month — $195.84 a year — for capture plus transcription plus read-later, and Fleeep does all three for $0. The honest caveat: it does not do meeting transcription with a bot, it does not sync highlights into Notion or Obsidian the way Readwise does, and it does not do long-form drafting like Jasper Pro at $69 a month ($59 billed annually).

Fleeep vs Notion, Otter, Readwise and Jasper

The mistake is to ask whether Fleeep is "better than Notion" as if they were the same kind of tool. They are not, and the comparison that helps is the one that names the job each tool is for.

Fleeep vs Notion. Notion is a workspace: pages, docs, wikis and databases that organise a team's knowledge. Fleeep is an inbox: things land there fast, before they are organised anywhere. A creator can reasonably use both — capture in Fleeep, organise in Notion — and the overlap is smaller than the names suggest. One pricing caution: full Notion AI now requires the $20-a-member-a-month Business plan — the standalone $10 AI add-on was retired in May 2025, so a Plus team that wants AI is buying an upgrade, not an add-on. Our best AI writing tools for small business guide covers Notion AI's current pricing in detail.

Fleeep vs Otter.ai. This is the distinction most "AI notes app" articles blur, and it matters. Otter joins your Zoom, Teams or Meet call as a visible bot and transcribes everyone in the meeting; its Pro plan caps at 1,200 transcription minutes a month. Fleeep transcribes voice notes you record yourself — an idea spoken on a walk, a thought dictated after a call. One is a meeting tool, the other is a thinking tool. If your problem is "I need a record of what was said in Tuesday's client call," Fleeep does not solve it. If your problem is "I have ideas while I am away from my desk," Otter does not solve it either.

Fleeep vs Readwise Reader. The closest overlap: both save links and let you read them calmly later. Readwise is the mature product — full-text search, text-to-speech, highlights that sync to Notion and Obsidian, RSS and newsletters — at $7.99 a month with no free plan. Fleeep's reader is simpler and free, and its edge is that the saved article sits next to your screenshots, voice notes and palettes instead of in a separate app. If you already live in Readwise and your highlights feed a second brain, Fleeep is a downgrade. If you never paid for read-later because Pocket was free and its shutdown left you with browser bookmarks, Fleeep is the free way back in.

Who should try it — and who should skip it

Try it this week if you are a creator or solo operator whose ideas live in five places and you want one inbox for them; if you record voice notes and never listen back because they are not searchable; if you save screenshots and links for client work and lose them; if you liked Pocket and never replaced it; or if you are a small team that wants a shared board of references with a link, not another workspace to administer.

Skip it for now if you need meeting notes with a bot (Otter, or Notion AI meeting notes on the Business plan), long-form drafting with brand voice and templates (Jasper), a team workspace with permissions and an audit trail (Notion Business), or a highlight-sync pipeline into Obsidian (Readwise). None of those are on Fleeep's pages, and waiting for a week-old solo project to grow them is a plan, not a decision.

The middle path is the sensible one for most readers: use Fleeep for capture, and keep the specialist where the specialist earns its money. A creator who captures in Fleeep, drafts in a free ChatGPT or Claude tier, and organises in a free Notion workspace pays $0 and loses nothing that matters — and if Fleeep later adds a paid tier, the export feature means the library leaves with you. The honest risks of a week-old app

A launch-week article that only praises the tool is not a review, so here is the other side. Fleeep launched days ago. There are no independent reviews, no published roadmap, and no stated paid plan. Every source for this article is the maker's own launch post, the official site, or one beginner guide — a walkthrough, not a test. Nobody has independently verified that the transcription is accurate, that search works well at a few hundred notes, or that the offline sync is reliable.

No limits are published either — no storage cap, no voice-note length limit — so check fleeep.app if anything in this guide looks different from what you see on screen.

Three questions to ask before you move your notes in: does the export include everything — voice transcripts, palette codes, board links — or only text notes? Does search stay fast at the size your library will reach in six months? And what happens to the free plan if a paid tier arrives — do existing users keep what they have? If the answers are good, the cost of trying is zero and the cost of leaving is an export file. That is a reasonable bet for a capture tool, and a bad bet only if you treat a week-old app as a permanent home on day one.

Frequently asked questions

What is Fleeep, and is it really free?

Say “free at launch” rather than “free forever” — no future pricing has been announced.

Is Fleeep better than Notion for notes?

They do different jobs. Fleeep is a capture inbox: screenshots, voice notes, saved links and palettes land there fast, with no folder to choose first. Notion is a workspace: pages, docs, wikis and databases that organise a team's knowledge. Many creators can use both — capture in Fleeep, organise in Notion. Fleeep does not replace Notion's databases, publishing or team permissions, and Notion does not do Fleeep's voice-note transcription or palette extraction.

Can Fleeep replace Otter.ai for meeting notes?

No. Otter joins your Zoom, Teams or Meet call as a visible bot and transcribes everyone in the meeting; Pro allows 1,200 transcription minutes a month. Fleeep transcribes voice notes you record yourself — an idea spoken on a walk, a thought dictated after a call. One is a meeting tool, the other is a thinking tool, and neither does the other's job.

Does Fleeep work on phone and computer? Does it work offline?

Yes to both, per the launch page and fleeep.app: Fleeep works on your phone and your computer, works offline, and syncs when you are back online. Account creation takes about a minute, and no credit card is required.

Does Fleeep sell my data or use my notes to train AI?

No, per the official site: “We don't sell your data or use your notes to train AI.” There are no ads, private notes can be locked with a passcode, and a full library export and backup is available so your notes can leave with you.

Conclusion

Fleeep is a small, honest launch: a free capture-first notes app from a solo maker, built around the unglamorous job of saving ideas before they disappear. Its case is the price — $0 for capture, voice transcription and read-later, the same three jobs that cost about $16 a month elsewhere — and its limit is the same as its charm: it is days old, it does no meeting transcription and no long-form drafting, and nobody has independently tested it yet. Try it as a capture inbox, and check fleeep.app before you decide.
