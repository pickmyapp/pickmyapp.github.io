Best Project Management Software for Agencies (2026)

  • Updated: November 2026

Quick answer

For most agencies, Asana Advanced is the safest pick: portfolios, workload views and approvals are built for client work. ClickUp Business is the best value at $12 per user per month, with native time tracking included. Monday Pro wins when clients see your boards, Basecamp wins on flat pricing once you pass 10 people, and Notion is a client portal, not a project engine. An agency does not run one project. It runs twenty, for ten different clients, with freelancers rotating in and out and every hour either billable or burned. The tool that works for a product team falls apart the moment a client asks where their project is. This guide compares the five tools that fit agency work — Asana, ClickUp, Monday.com, Basecamp and Notion — on client visibility, time tracking, workload, and real cost at 5, 10 and 30 seats.

What agencies need that in-house teams don't

Agencies should judge tools by four jobs in-house teams rarely face. Client visibility: clients want to see progress without joining your internal chaos, so guest access must be cheap and permissions must hide other clients. Billable time: agencies sell hours, so if time tracking lives in a separate app, hours leak and invoicing becomes a Friday reconciliation job. Workload across clients: one designer on four retainers is the normal agency shape, and you need one view of a person's total load. Cost that scales: agencies grow in lumps, and per-seat pricing multiplies fast.

Quick fact: the plan an agency actually needs can cost two to three times the advertised "from" price, because time tracking, client seats and workload views are gated behind higher tiers.

Asana: the safest pick for structured agency delivery

Asana is a work-management platform built around projects, portfolios and goals — the most complete agency system here. Its case rests on Advanced: portfolios that roll many client projects into one view, workload views that show who is overbooked, approvals and proofing for creative review, and native time tracking that compares estimated and actual effort.

Pricing: the free Personal plan covers only 2 users. Starter is $10.99 per user per month billed annually ($13.49 monthly); Advanced is $24.99 ($30.49 monthly), with a $5.99 Timesheets add-on. Asana's big advantage is that free guests are unlimited, so clients join their projects at no cost. The downside is price — $750 a month at 30 seats — and seats are sold in bands at larger sizes. Asana is the pick for agencies of 10 to 50 people running structured client work. See our Basecamp vs Asana guide for remote teams.

ClickUp: the best value all-rounder

ClickUp is a work-execution platform that packs the most agency features into the lowest price. Tasks carry assignees, statuses, due dates and custom fields; there are 15+ views including Gantt, timeline and workload; and native time tracking starts on the Unlimited plan. The Business tier — workload management, goal folders, custom permissions, Google SSO — costs $12 per user per month billed annually: half the price of Asana Advanced.

Pricing, checked November 2026: Free Forever ($0, unlimited tasks and members, 60 MB storage cap), Unlimited $7 per user per month billed annually ($10 monthly), Business $12 ($19 monthly), Enterprise by quote. ClickUp Brain costs $9 per user per month billed annually. Watch the guest-seat trap: Business includes 10 permission-controlled guest seats plus 5 per member, and guests beyond that pool are billed as full seats. The other risk is feature density — agree on a small set of Spaces, statuses and fields up front. A plan migration is in progress, with price updates from January 2027, so re-check clickup.com/pricing. Our ClickUp pricing breakdown covers the tiers and AI add-ons.

Monday.com: the best tool to show a client

Monday.com is a visual work platform built on colourful, column-based boards — and the easiest of these five tools to put in front of a client. A client who opens a Monday board understands it in seconds: status colours, timelines, owners and due dates, with no training.

Pricing for Monday Work Management, checked November 2026: Free ($0, up to 2 seats), Basic $9 per seat per month billed annually ($12 monthly), Standard $12 ($14 monthly), Pro $19 ($24 monthly), Enterprise by quote. Two catches: first, paid plans require a minimum of 3 seats, and above that seats are sold in multiples of 5 — a team of 7 pays for 10 seats. Second, time tracking is a Pro feature, and automations jump from 250 actions a month on Standard to 25,000 on Pro. Basic has no automations or integrations, so Standard ($12) is the real entry price and Pro ($19) the real agency tier. Monday is the pick for client-facing agencies — marketing, design and PR — where the board itself is part of the service. See our Asana vs Monday vs ClickUp comparison.

Basecamp: flat pricing and radical simplicity

Basecamp is the opposite of the other tools here: fewer features, calmer design, and a flat monthly price with no per-user fees. One workspace holds message boards, to-do lists, card tables, chat, schedules and docs — no Gantt chart, no workload view, no automation builder. That is the point: one calm place for clients and staff instead of a powerful system nobody fully learns.

Pricing, from Basecamp's pricing page read November 6, 2026: Freelancer $29 a month (up to 5 users, 3 projects), Pro $79 (up to 10 users, 8 projects), Pro Plus $99 (up to 15 users, 12 projects), Pro Unlimited $199 a month billed annually ($329 monthly) for unlimited users, unlimited projects and 1 TB of storage. Every plan is flat, and Basecamp repriced during 2026, so confirm the tiers on basecamp.com/pricing. The economics are the story: at 30 people, Basecamp costs $199 a month against $750 for Asana Advanced. The trade-off is capability: no workload view, no portfolio roll-up, thin reporting. Basecamp fits agencies with a small number of large retainers and simple projects.

Notion: the client portal, not the project engine

Notion is a docs, wiki and database workspace — and for agencies its job is the client-facing layer, not the delivery engine. It is the best tool on this list for a client portal: a branded hub per client with scope, timeline, deliverables, meeting notes and assets in one place.

Pricing, checked October 2026: Free ($0, but workspaces with 2+ members are capped at 1,000 blocks), Plus $10 per user per month billed annually ($12 monthly), Business $20 ($24 monthly), Enterprise by quote. Full Notion AI is bundled into Business only; the old $10 AI add-on retired in May 2025. The hard limit: Notion has no native time tracking at all, and sprints and workload views must be built from databases and templates. Run delivery in Asana or ClickUp, and give each client a Notion portal. Our Notion vs ClickUp comparison for startups explains the docs-versus-delivery split in depth. Notion is the pick for any agency that wants a polished client portal and wiki — as a second tool, alongside a real project platform.

Client portals and visibility: what to check before you buy

Client access is where agency tools quietly diverge. Asana's unlimited free guests are the most generous model. ClickUp's guest pool is generous on paper but billable beyond it. Monday offers free viewers and guests on paid plans, but private boards require Pro. Basecamp includes everyone in the flat price. Notion's guest limits vary by plan. In your trial, ask: can a client see only their own project, see status and dates without learning your workflow, and approve work without a paid seat? Approvals matter most for creative agencies: Asana has them built in on Advanced, ClickUp has proofing on Business, and Monday and Basecamp handle approvals through status columns.

Time tracking and billable hours

Time tracking splits the five tools into three groups. Native and included: ClickUp (from the $7 Unlimited plan) and Asana (on Advanced, with a $5.99 Timesheets add-on). Native but gated: Monday (Pro only) and Basecamp (higher flat tiers). Not native at all: Notion, which needs an integration such as Toggl or Clockify.

One practical note: native time tracking only works if the whole team uses it, so pick the tool whose timer is one click from the task.

Verdicts by agency size and type

Small agency, 3–10 people

  • Best overall: ClickUp Business. $60 a month for 5 people buys time tracking, workload, Gantt and automations.
  • Best if clients log in often: Monday Standard or Pro. Budget for Pro if you need time tracking.
  • Best if you hate complexity: Basecamp Pro. $79 flat for up to 10 people, clients included.

Mid-size agency, 10–30 people

  • Best overall: Asana Advanced. Portfolios and workload views earn their keep past 10 people and 10 clients.
  • Best value: ClickUp Business. $120 a month at 10 seats against Asana's $250.
  • Best flat price: Basecamp Pro Unlimited. $199 a month flat, whatever your headcount.

Large agency, 30+ people

  • Best overall: Asana Advanced. Governance, portfolios and approvals scale.
  • Best on cost: Basecamp Pro Unlimited. The bill stops at $199 a month while per-seat tools pass $500.

By agency type

  • Creative: Asana Advanced for approvals; Monday Pro for client-facing boards.
  • Marketing: ClickUp Business — lowest price for time tracking and workload.
  • Consulting: Asana Advanced for portfolios and goals.
  • Small shops: Basecamp — flat pricing, clients included.

Whatever you shortlist, trial it for a week on real client work: invite two clients as guests, log time, and check the workload view on Friday.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best project management software for agencies?

Asana Advanced is the best all-round pick: portfolios, workload, approvals and native time tracking. ClickUp Business is the best value at $12 per user per month, Monday Pro wins when clients view your boards, and Basecamp wins once your team passes 10 people.

How much does project management software cost for an agency?

At 10 seats: ClickUp Business $120 a month, Monday Pro $190, Asana Advanced $250, Notion Business $200, Basecamp $199 flat. At 30 seats: ClickUp $360, Asana $750, Basecamp still $199.

Which project management tool has the best client portal for agencies?

Asana has the most generous client access, with unlimited free guests and portfolios that show every client's status in one view. Monday's boards are the easiest for clients to understand. Basecamp includes all clients in its flat price. Notion builds the prettiest portal, but works best as a companion to a real project tool.

Do agencies need time tracking in their project management software?

Yes, if the agency bills by the hour or prices retainers from past effort. Native time tracking — in ClickUp from the $7 Unlimited plan, in Asana on Advanced, in Monday on Pro — removes a separate subscription. Notion has no native time tracking and needs a tool such as Toggl or Clockify.

Is Basecamp or Asana better for a small agency?

Basecamp is better for a small agency that wants flat, predictable pricing: Pro Unlimited is $199 a month flat for unlimited users. Asana is better for structured delivery and approvals — but those features sit on Advanced at $24.99 per user per month, so a 10-person agency pays $250 a month against Basecamp's $79 Pro plan.

Conclusion

There is no single best project management tool for agencies — there is a best tool for your headcount and your clients. Asana Advanced is the safest all-round system for structured client work at scale. ClickUp Business is the best value, delivering the full agency feature set for roughly half Asana's price. Monday Pro is the right call when clients see your boards every week. Basecamp is the smartest financial choice once you pass 10 people. Notion belongs in every agency's stack as the client portal — just not as the project engine. Price the tier you actually need, trial it with two real clients for a week, and buy the one your team still likes on Friday.
