What Rerun actually is

Rerun is not another chatbot and it is not another automation tool in the Zapier sense. The pitch, from its launch page at rerun.build, is simple: the AI subscription you already pay for — ChatGPT Plus or Pro, Claude Pro or Max — is smart, but it only works when you are sitting in front of it. Rerun gives that subscription a body and a shift pattern.

Each AI coworker you hire gets three things: its own private computer, its own access to your apps, and a job description. You brief it like a new hire — "every morning at 7 a.m., check the inbox for unpaid invoices, send a polite reminder to anyone more than 14 days overdue, and log the result in the spreadsheet" — and it does the steps itself, on its own machine, at the time you chose. You do not open ChatGPT, paste anything, or watch it type.

It launched on Product Hunt on November 5, 2026, finishing #3 Product of the Day with 244 upvotes, in the same week as several other persistent-agent launches, including Perplexity Teammates for Max subscribers. The timing is not a coincidence: 2026 is the year the big AI labs all shipped delegation features — Claude Cowork from Anthropic and ChatGPT Work from OpenAI — and Rerun is the independent answer to the same question, built around a subscription you already own.

For a small team, the idea is simpler than the enterprise version: you are taking a $20-a-month subscription that currently answers questions and turning it into something that does the boring recurring work nobody on a five-person team has time for — chasing unpaid invoices, replying to routine emails, keeping the CRM updated, running the same report every Monday morning.

How Rerun works, step by step

The setup follows four steps, and the whole point is that none of them requires code.

1. Create a free account. Sign up at rerun.build. The free tier lets you start before paying anything.

2. Connect a model. You have three options, and this is the part that makes Rerun unusual: sign in with your Claude (Pro or Max) or ChatGPT (Plus or Pro) account and let coworkers run on the subscription you already pay for; bring your own API keys from Anthropic, OpenAI, or Google Gemini and pay per token; or use the Rerun credits included with a paid plan, so coworkers work on day one with nothing connected. If you plug in your own subscription, those runs cost you nothing extra on Rerun's side — Rerun says it never counts them.

3. Hire your first coworker. A coworker is a named role with a job description, tools, and a shared workspace. You give it access to the apps it needs — email, spreadsheets, your CRM — and it gets its own private machine inside Rerun, walled off from every other customer. It does not share a browser session with your other coworkers or with you.

4. Set the schedule or trigger. Work happens on a schedule ("every weekday at 7 a.m.") or on a trigger (a new email, a new row, a new form submission). You can also organize work into "Boxes" — shared workspaces where a coworker and your human team collaborate on the same job. The whole team can be invited into the workspace for free; Rerun charges per coworker, never per person.

The guardrail that matters most is the approval step: a coworker asks before risky actions. Sending an email to a customer, moving money, deleting data — the coworker pauses and waits for a human. That is the difference between an assistant and a liability, and the feature to test first on any trial.

What the coworkers can actually do

The launch materials describe coworkers doing the recurring business work that eats a small team's mornings: chasing unpaid invoices, replying to customer emails, updating spreadsheets, running the same task every morning at 7 a.m. without anyone opening anything. The pattern is always the same — a clear trigger, clear steps, a clear output — and that pattern is the honest limit of the tool.

Good first jobs for a Rerun coworker look like this:

  • Invoice chasing: each morning, find invoices more than 14 days overdue, send a polite reminder from a template, and log who was contacted.
  • Inbox triage: sort incoming customer email, draft replies to the routine questions, and flag the ones that need a human.
  • Spreadsheet upkeep: take new form submissions or new orders and put them in the right sheet, in the right format, every time.
  • Recurring reports: pull the week's numbers, write the summary, and drop it in the shared workspace every Monday.

There is also a practical limit worth stating plainly: a coworker is only as reliable as the apps it can reach and the instructions it was given. If your invoicing lives in three different tools and your "unpaid" definition lives in your head, the coworker will do the wrong thing confidently. Writing the job description precisely, connecting the right apps, and defining what counts as done is real work — and it decides whether Rerun saves time or creates a new chore. One early user quoted on the launch page says they set it up once in July and it has been running since; the setup is the price of that promise.

Rerun vs Claude Cowork, ChatGPT Work, and Perplexity Teammates

Rerun did not launch into an empty market: the same week brought Perplexity Teammates for Max subscribers, and the two big labs have spent 2026 shipping their own delegation features. Here is how they line up for a small team.

Claude Cowork is Anthropic's delegation feature, launched in January 2026 and folded into every Claude conversation in September 2026. You describe the result, point Claude at files and tools, and it plans and executes the steps, keeping a detailed record. It is included in Claude Pro ($20/month) and Max, and runs in the cloud without your computer being on. Its limit is that it lives inside Claude — one assistant, one conversation, no separate coworkers with their own machines and schedules.

ChatGPT Work is OpenAI's equivalent, launched July 9, 2026. You open it from the Work button, choose a project, plugins, and a template, and hand it a complete objective. It is included with ChatGPT Plus ($20/month). Its strength is event-triggered tasks and the largest plugin catalog. Its limit is the same shape as Cowork's: a mode inside ChatGPT, not a team of persistent coworkers.

Perplexity Teammates, launched the same week, is Perplexity's version for Max subscribers — the same "use the subscription you already pay for" idea, tied to one vendor's plan.

Rerun's difference is structural. It is vendor-neutral — Claude or ChatGPT or your own API keys — it gives each coworker its own private computer and app sessions, it runs on schedules and triggers without anyone opening a chat window, and it is open-source under AGPL v3, so you can self-host it. It also charges per coworker, never per person. The trade-off is that Rerun is a week-old product from a small company (Reunit SA), while Cowork and Work come from Anthropic and OpenAI with their support, uptime, and roadmaps. For one AI assistant inside the chat app you already use, Cowork or Work is simpler. For several persistent workers running on a schedule across both Claude and ChatGPT, Rerun is the only one of the four built for that shape.

Who Rerun is actually for

Rerun fits a specific team, and it is worth checking whether that is you before signing up.

You will get value from Rerun if: you already pay for Claude Pro/Max or ChatGPT Plus/Pro; you have two or three recurring jobs that follow the same steps every time; someone on the team can write a precise job description and connect the apps; and you are comfortable with an AI coworker drafting customer-facing messages that a human reviews before they go out.

The self-hosting angle matters for teams that handle client data and do not want it passing through a startup's servers. Rerun is open-source (AGPL v3) and self-hostable — but self-hosting is not free: you run the machines and do the updates, so budget the server and the maintenance honestly.

Risks, limits, and what to check before you trust it

Three things to check before a Rerun coworker touches anything customer-facing.

1. Credential safety. A coworker inside your apps needs access to them, and access is the risk. Rerun's model — each coworker on its own private machine with its own app sessions, walled off from other customers — is a good design, but it is still a young product holding the keys to your inbox. Use scoped access where the app allows it, never hand over an admin account, and read the security page before connecting anything that holds customer data. Our guide to 1Password's AI agent sign-in tool covers the approved way to let an agent log in without ever seeing the password.

2. The approval trap. "Asks before risky actions" only protects you if you define risky correctly. A coworker that asks about everything becomes a notification chore; one that asks about nothing becomes a liability. Start with approvals on for anything that sends, spends, or deletes, and loosen only after a few weeks of clean runs.

3. The subscription dependency. Rerun's value rests on the subscription you bring. If Anthropic or OpenAI change what Pro/Plus plans can do through third-party tools — rate limits, API access, terms of service — Rerun's economics change with them. Keep the job description and the checklist somewhere you own, so the work survives even if the tool does not.

Finally, the launch is one day old. The 2,000+ businesses quoted on rerun.build, the Product Hunt ranking, and the $29 price are all launch-week facts — check the pricing page before you commit a real workflow, and start with one coworker on one low-risk job (invoice reminders, not customer refunds) for the first month.

How to start with Rerun this week

If the shape fits, test Rerun with one coworker on one job. Pick a recurring task with a clear trigger and a visible output — invoice reminders are the classic first job. Write the job description like a brief, not a prompt: the trigger, what "done" looks like, what the coworker must never do, and what it must ask about first. Connect only the apps that job needs, with the narrowest permissions the app allows. Run it supervised for two weeks, approvals on for anything outbound, with a human spot-checking the output daily. Then decide on the numbers: if one coworker saves two hours a week, five coworkers at $29 a month is a straightforward yes. If it needs daily rescue, the job was not ready to delegate — fix the job description before blaming the tool.
