Hostinger vs Bluehost Compared Honestly

  • Web Hosting
  • Published Oct 10, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

Hostinger is cheaper than Bluehost at every comparable tier — about $2.99 a month to start versus $2.95, but the real gap is at renewal, where Hostinger's $10.99 beats Bluehost's $11.99 to $21.99 depending on the plan. Hostinger is also faster in independent speed tests thanks to its LiteSpeed servers. Bluehost's real advantage is familiarity: a WordPress.org recommendation since 2005, guided onboarding, and 24/7 phone support. If you want the lowest long-term cost, pick Hostinger; if you want the most hand-holding, Bluehost is defensible.

Hostinger and Bluehost are the two names that dominate every "cheap web hosting" search, and both advertise roughly the same thing: hosting for about three dollars a month with a free domain and a free SSL certificate. That similarity is why this comparison matters — the difference is not in the headline price, it is in what you pay in year two and three, how fast your site loads, and how the company treats you at checkout. This guide compares them from a small-business perspective — a business site or blog doing up to a few tens of thousands of visits a month — using prices checked in October 2026. For the wider budget-hosting picture, see our best cheap web hosting for WordPress roundup.

The honest summary

Strip away the marketing and the contest looks like this. Hostinger is the cheaper host long-term: Premium starts at $2.99 a month and renews near $10.99, while Bluehost's Basic starts at $2.95 and renews at $11.99 — and on higher tiers the gap widens further. Hostinger is also faster in independent tests because it runs LiteSpeed servers while Bluehost runs a more standard stack.

Bluehost's advantage is brand familiarity, not value. It has carried the official WordPress.org recommendation since 2005, its onboarding wizard walks first-timers through setup, and it is the only one of the two with 24/7 phone support. But you pay for that comfort twice: at checkout, where Bluehost pushes add-ons aggressively, and at renewal, where its prices jump just as hard as anyone else's. Review aggregators measured Hostinger at 4.3 stars versus Bluehost at 3.8, with pricing-transparency complaints dragging Bluehost down.

The one-line version: Hostinger wins on price and speed; Bluehost wins on hand-holding.

Pricing: the intro-vs-renewal trap

Budget hosting has one honest number and it is not the one in the banner ad. The intro price buys only your first commitment period — and that period requires paying the whole term upfront. The renewal price is what your site actually costs to keep alive. Here is both, side by side, with prices checked in October 2026. Hosts adjust these constantly and figures vary slightly between sources, so verify at checkout.

Now the honest math. That "$2.99 a month" Hostinger plan is really $143.52 paid upfront for four years. Bluehost's "$2.95" Basic is $106.20 for three years. Then consider the second term: four more years of Hostinger Premium at the renewal rate costs over $525, and three more years of Bluehost Basic costs about $430. The renewal rate is the real price of your website; the intro rate is a discount for committing early.

The plans explained

Hostinger Premium: the cheapest honest entry

Premium is the plan most small businesses need on Hostinger: up to 100 websites, SSD storage, a free domain for the first year, unlimited free SSL certificates, weekly automatic backups, and a malware scanner. Some listings describe the storage differently, so budget conservatively if your site is media-heavy. The missing pieces are daily backups and a CDN — if your site changes every day, that pushes you up a tier.

Hostinger Business (or "Unlimited"): the sensible step up

Business adds NVMe storage, daily backups, a free CDN, and more server resources for roughly a dollar more a month at intro pricing. For a business site that takes orders or gets updated content regularly, daily backups alone are worth the step up — this is the plan most reviewers actually recommend over Premium.

Bluehost Basic: only for one tiny site

Basic covers exactly one website with 10 GB of SSD storage, a free domain for year one, and five email accounts. What it does not include matters more: no daily backups, no domain privacy (that costs extra here), and modest resources. Bluehost's own upsell ladder makes Basic feel like a teaser for Choice Plus. It is fine for one small brochure site — for a blog that stores contact details or takes signups, see our cheapest email marketing tools and plan accordingly.

Bluehost Choice Plus: the only Bluehost plan most businesses should consider

Choice Plus costs the same $5.45 intro price as the tier below it (Plus) but adds daily backups, free domain privacy, and unlimited websites. At intro it is the clear pick in Bluehost's lineup; the pain arrives at renewal, where it becomes one of the most expensive shared plans on the market at $19.99 to $21.99 a month — against Hostinger Business renewing near $16.99 with a CDN included.

Performance: LiteSpeed vs the standard stack

Hostinger runs LiteSpeed web servers with built-in caching across all shared plans; Bluehost runs a more conventional setup. Independent testers consistently find the gap: one benchmark suite measured time-to-first-byte (TTFB) at roughly 142 ms on Hostinger versus about 680 ms on Bluehost, and other reviewers put Hostinger under 300 ms against Bluehost's 400–600 ms. On a plain WordPress site, that can be the difference between a page that feels instant and one that feels merely okay.

Uptime is close on paper — both advertise around 99.9% — but the fine print differs: Hostinger's uptime guarantee is contractual (they credit you if they miss it), while Bluehost markets "industry-leading uptime" without a binding SLA, meaning no compensation if your site goes down.

Geography also favors Hostinger. It offers multiple global data-center regions; Bluehost's infrastructure is primarily US-based, so visitors far from the United States see slower response times. If your audience is mostly American, that hardly matters; if it is global, it matters a lot. And if you plan to run anything beyond a website — scheduled automation tools like n8n or heavy plugins — know that all shared plans throttle CPU and memory, whichever host you pick.

The WordPress experience

This is Bluehost's home turf. It has been one of WordPress.org's officially recommended hosts since 2005, and that recommendation is a genuine trust signal for beginners. Both hosts offer one-click WordPress installation, but Bluehost layers on a guided onboarding wizard, managed WordPress updates, and a staging environment on higher tiers. If your main worry is "I have never run a website before," Bluehost's hand-holding is its strongest argument.

Hostinger's WordPress story is simpler but faster: one-click install, the LiteSpeed Cache plugin pre-configured for WordPress, and built-in AI tools for building pages. Experienced WordPress users tend to prefer it because there is less clutter between them and the site — and the same page loads faster on LiteSpeed. One caveat for anyone planning a real online store: neither host's shared tier is ideal for revenue-generating WooCommerce; Bluehost sells a separate Online Store plan, and Hostinger points bigger stores toward its cloud plans.

If you are still deciding whether WordPress itself is the right call, our WordPress vs Webflow for freelancers comparison walks through the platform decision before you pick a host.

Support quality, honestly

Support is where the two hosts take genuinely different philosophies. Bluehost offers 24/7 phone support plus live chat — it is the only one of the two where you can call a human. Hostinger offers live chat and email only, in multiple languages — no phone line at all.

What do users actually experience? A reviewer who contacted Bluehost support eleven times over a year rated chat well for WordPress-specific problems (plugin conflicts, SSL issues) but noted long phone hold times and reps who sometimes answered slow-site complaints by suggesting an upgrade rather than diagnosing the cause. Hostinger's chat support gets solid ratings in independent reviews, and third-party aggregation has it at 4.3 stars versus Bluehost's 3.8, with Bluehost's marks dragged down mainly by pricing-transparency complaints rather than raw technical competence.

The honest take: if you want to call someone at 2 a.m. when the site is down, Bluehost is your only option here. If you are comfortable troubleshooting in chat, you will find Hostinger's support perfectly adequate — and the money you save each year can buy a lot of premium plugins.

Ease of use: hPanel vs cPanel

Hostinger ships hPanel, its own modern control panel — clean, clearly labeled, and built for beginners. Bluehost ships a customized version of cPanel, the decades-old industry standard. Practically, both get a non-technical person from signup to a live WordPress site in an afternoon.

The real difference is where you get stuck. cPanel has thousands of third-party tutorials written over twenty years. hPanel is easier to understand cold, but its knowledge base is smaller and newer. Call it a tie with an asterisk: Hostinger is more pleasant, Bluehost is more Googleable. And if control panels and hosting dashboards sound exhausting, you might skip shared hosting entirely — our Wix vs Squarespace comparison covers that fork in the road.

The upsell and renewal traps to watch

Neither host is a scam, but both make money from things beginners don't notice until later. Check these six traps before you buy:

  • The 3–4x renewal jump. Both hosts renew at roughly three to four times the intro price. Before buying, divide the renewal price — not the intro price — into your annual budget.
  • The upfront commitment. Intro pricing means paying 36 or 48 months at once: $143.52 for Hostinger Premium, $106.20 for Bluehost Basic. That is a real discount, but you are lending a host money for years; both offer a 30-day money-back guarantee, not a 3-year one.
  • The domain shuffle. The "free domain" is free for the first year only. Bluehost domain renewals run around $15.99 a year — slightly above market — and domain privacy, free on Choice Plus, costs extra on Basic.
  • Migration fees. Bluehost charges $149.99 for its assisted migration service. The free Migrate Guru plugin does the same job for a typical WordPress site in about twenty minutes, and Hostinger offers free migration when you join — so moving away from Bluehost costs you nothing but time.
  • Checkout upsells. Bluehost's checkout is the pushier of the two: site builders, SEO tools, and security add-ons get layered in, each one small and each one recurring. Read every pre-checked box before paying.
  • The calendar reminder. Set one for about 60 days before your term ends, so you can renegotiate, drop to a cheaper plan, or migrate elsewhere — but only if you remember the renewal is coming.

Our verdict

Choose Hostinger if

  • You want the lowest realistic long-term cost — intro and renewal prices are both lower at comparable tiers.
  • Page speed matters to you: LiteSpeed servers plus multiple global data centers win independent benchmarks.
  • You can live without phone support and are comfortable with chat.
  • You want free site migration when you join.

Choose Bluehost if

  • You are a WordPress beginner who wants the official WordPress.org recommendation and a guided setup wizard.
  • You want 24/7 phone support and will actually use it.
  • Your audience is mostly in the US, so the single-region infrastructure is not a penalty.
  • You plan to commit to one term and re-evaluate — and you read every checkbox at checkout.

For most small businesses, Hostinger is the recommendation: cheaper to start, cheaper to keep, and faster. Bluehost is a defensible pick for one specific buyer — the total beginner who wants maximum hand-holding and a phone number to call. Both offer a 30-day money-back guarantee; set a calendar reminder before your term ends.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper, Hostinger or Bluehost?

Hostinger is cheaper at every comparable tier. Premium starts at $2.99 a month and renews at about $10.99, versus Bluehost Basic at $2.95 a month renewing at $11.99. The gap widens on higher tiers: Hostinger Business is $3.99 a month renewing near $16.99, while Bluehost Choice Plus is $5.45 a month renewing near $19.99 to $21.99. Prices were checked in October 2026 and change often — verify at checkout.

Why does my web hosting bill jump so much at renewal?

Almost every budget host sells the first term at a deep promotional discount — typically 3 to 4 times below the regular price — then bills the full regular price when that term ends. The intro price only applies to your first commitment period. Set a calendar reminder about 60 days before renewal to renegotiate, switch plans, or migrate.

Is Bluehost better than Hostinger for WordPress?

Bluehost has been officially recommended by WordPress.org since 2005 and offers guided onboarding, managed updates, and a staging environment, so WordPress beginners get more hand-holding. Hostinger's WordPress is faster on paper thanks to LiteSpeed servers and the LiteSpeed Cache plugin, so experienced WordPress users often get better page speed there.

Does Hostinger have phone support?

No — Hostinger's support is live chat and email, offered in multiple languages. Bluehost offers 24/7 phone support in addition to chat. If you want to call a human when something breaks, that is Bluehost's clearest advantage; if you are comfortable troubleshooting in chat, Hostinger's chat support is rated well.

Can I move my website from Bluehost to Hostinger later?

Yes. Moving away from Bluehost costs nothing if you do it yourself — Bluehost charges $149.99 for its assisted migration service, but the free Migrate Guru plugin moves a typical WordPress site in about 20 minutes. Hostinger offers free site migration when you join. Do the move a few weeks before your Bluehost term renews, and back up everything first.

Conclusion

Hostinger and Bluehost sell similar-looking hosting at similar-looking prices, but the honest comparison favors Hostinger for most small businesses: lower intro prices, lower renewal prices, faster LiteSpeed servers, and free migration. Bluehost's case rests on familiarity and hand-holding — the WordPress.org recommendation, guided onboarding, and 24/7 phone support make it a reasonable choice for beginners who want a voice on the line. Whatever you choose, ignore the banner price, budget for the renewal price, and read every checkbox at checkout. The cheapest hosting is the hosting you understand.
