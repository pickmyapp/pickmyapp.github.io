Best CRM for Freelancers and Solo Consultants (2026)

  • CRM Software
  • Published Oct 12, 2026
  • Updated: October 2026
  • 10 min read
  • By PickMyApp Research Team
Quick answer

For most freelancers, HubSpot's free CRM is the best starting point: $0 for up to 1,000 contacts and 2 users, with pipelines, email tracking, and scheduling included. If you live in Gmail, Streak ($49/user/month billed annually) is the most inbox-native option. For a focused deal pipeline, Pipedrive ($14/user/month annually) is the cheapest credible paid pick. HoneyBook ($29/month annually) also handles proposals, contracts, invoicing, and payments. Prices checked October 2026.

Almost every CRM review is written for teams. The pricing tables assume five seats, the feature lists assume a sales manager, and the "free plan" fine print assumes you will eventually upgrade to a $90-a-seat tier. But freelancers and solo consultants buy software differently: you are the whole team, and every dollar comes out of your own pocket.

What freelancers actually need from a CRM

A solo CRM has four jobs: store every contact and lead in one place, show where each opportunity stands, remind you to follow up, and keep your email connected so nothing lives in two places. If a tool does those four things reliably, it is doing the job; everything beyond that is optional until your revenue says otherwise.

Two capabilities matter most. First, email integration: your inbox is your office, so a CRM that syncs with Gmail or Outlook automatically beats one with fifty extra features and no sync. Second, invoice integration: most CRMs do not invoice, so decide up front whether you want a separate billing tool or one of the rare CRMs that covers the proposal-to-payment path. Ignore multi-seat collaboration, territory management, and marketing automation suites — team tools priced for team budgets.

The solo-operator pricing trap

CRM vendors price per seat, and that quietly punishes solo users. A "$15 per seat" plan looks cheap until you realize the entry tier was designed for a team and you are buying features for a sales manager you will never hire. The real cost often hides one tier up: automation, sequences, and API access frequently sit on a second tier that doubles the price. Always price the tier with the features you need, not the tier the vendor advertises.

For example, Monday Sales CRM lists $12 a seat per month — but with a 3-seat minimum, the real floor is $36 a month. Folk puts deals, sequences, API access, and Zapier on its $48-a-month (annual) Premium plan, double the $24 entry plan.

Quick fact: In 2026, paid CRM entry prices for a solo user span roughly $14 to $49 a month: Pipedrive's Essential plan at $14/user/month billed annually on the low end, Streak's Pro plan at $49/user/month billed annually on the high end. Three tools on this list still offer genuinely usable free plans: HubSpot, Attio, and Capsule.

1. HubSpot CRM — the best free starting point

HubSpot CRM is the rare enterprise-grade tool with a free plan a freelancer can genuinely live on: contact management, deal pipelines, email tracking, meeting scheduling, and live chat at $0, with newer accounts capped at 2 users and 1,000 contacts — plenty for a solo practice. Starter seats begin at $20 a month; Gmail and Outlook both sync, so emails attach to records automatically. HubSpot is friendly to learn, but its paid tiers escalate fast once you want automation. Invoicing is not built in; you pair it with a billing tool through an integration. Migration is easy via CSV import.

More free options are in our best free CRMs for startups in 2026 roundup.

2. Streak — the CRM that lives inside Gmail

Streak is a CRM built as a browser extension, so pipelines, deal tracking, and contact history live inside your Gmail inbox. Open a client's email and a sidebar shows their pipeline stage, past conversations, and related records. The free tier covers email tools only — pipelines need Pro at $49/user/month billed annually ($59 monthly), with automation on Pro+ at $69. There is no Outlook version and no invoicing. Migration is trivial, since your email history is already in Gmail.

3. Pipedrive — the deal-focused pipeline, cheapest paid entry

Pipedrive is a sales-first CRM built around one screen: a kanban board where you drag deals through stages. Contacts, emails, activities, and reminders all hang off the pipeline, and the activity-based design nudges you to book the next action on every open deal. Essential costs $14/user/month billed annually ($24 monthly) — the cheapest paid entry here. There is no free plan, only a 14-day trial, and email sync, automation, and scheduling sit on the Advanced plan at $34 per user per month annually, so budget for the tier you need. Gmail and Outlook both sync; invoicing comes through paid add-ons or integrations. Most solo users are configured in under an hour.

4. HoneyBook — the one CRM that also invoices

HoneyBook is a client-management platform for independent service providers, combining contact tracking, proposals, contracts, invoicing, scheduling, and online payments in one place. A client can go from inquiry to signed contract to paid invoice without you touching a second app, and there is a branded client portal plus a 7-day free trial. Starter costs $29/month billed annually with unlimited clients and projects, invoicing, contracts, and the portal — but limits you to one user profile and disables automated workflows, which unlock on the $49/month Essentials plan. Note the processing fees: roughly 3% on cards and 1.5% on bank transfers. Contacts import cleanly, but you rebuild proposal and contract templates inside the platform.

5. Less Annoying CRM — the simplest paid option

Less Annoying CRM sells simplicity: one plan at $15 per user per month, no tiers, no add-ons, no long-term contracts. You get contacts, pipelines, tasks, a calendar, and Gmail and Outlook sync in an interface that needs no training. The first 30 days are free. The flat pricing is the anti-trap — the bill never surprises you — but reporting is basic, there are no advanced automations, and invoicing is absent. Migration is as simple as the product: upload a spreadsheet and you are running.

6. Attio — the modern free plan for relationship work

Attio is a newer CRM with a strikingly generous free plan: up to 3 seats and 50,000 records at $0. It is built for relationship-driven work — contacts, companies, pipelines, interaction history — with Gmail sync and enrichment that pulls public company data into records automatically. The interface feels modern, and customization (custom objects, views, automations) is unusually flexible for a free tier. Paid plans start with Plus at $29/seat/month billed annually ($36 monthly); Pro at $69 adds advanced reporting. No invoicing, and Outlook support is thinner than Gmail's. CSV import works well, but plan an afternoon to structure your objects and views.

7. Capsule — the quiet, dependable free option

Capsule is a straightforward CRM with a free plan covering 2 users and 250 contacts, one pipeline, and Gmail and Outlook add-ons. It does the fundamentals — contact management, task tracking, opportunities, a shared calendar view — without pushing you toward an upgrade. For a solo consultant with a modest client list, the free tier is a complete tool, not a demo. Starter costs $18 per user per month (roughly $15 with annual billing) for 30,000 contacts; Growth at $36 adds workflow automations and multiple pipelines. No invoicing, no marketing suite — exactly the point.

8. Folk — the relationship CRM for network-heavy consultants

Folk is a CRM built around people and relationships, with contact enrichment, pipelines, campaigns, and AI assistance. It suits consultants who prospect through their network: enrichment finds emails and company details from a name, and the Chrome extension adds people from LinkedIn in a click. Standard costs $24/member/month billed annually ($30 monthly), but the tier gap matters — deals, sequences, API access, and Zapier/Make sit on the $48/month Premium plan, doubling your bill. There is no free plan, only a 14-day trial, and no invoicing. Gmail sync is solid, and enrichment can rebuild sparse records automatically.

Simplicity vs power: the honest tradeoff

Every CRM here sits on one line: at one end, Less Annoying CRM and Capsule, which you set up before lunch and never think about again; at the other, HubSpot and Attio, which can grow with you for years but ask more in return. The tradeoff is not quality — it is attention. A powerful CRM you update monthly is worth less than a simple one you update daily. If you have ever customized a CRM for a week and then reverted to sticky notes, pick the simpler tool. For the bigger picture, browse more of our CRM software guides.

Quick fact: Most CRM abandonment comes from data-entry fatigue, not missing features. The tools freelancers stick with longest are the ones that log emails and meetings automatically — which is why email integration matters more than any other feature on this page.

How much work is it to switch CRMs?

Moving into your first CRM from spreadsheets is the easy migration: export to CSV, map the columns on import, clean up duplicates, and connect your email so history flows in. Budget an afternoon, most of it spent on data cleanup. Moving between CRMs is harder: email threads and activity logs rarely import cleanly, and pipelines, templates, and automations get rebuilt by hand — none of the eight tools offers a one-click competitor importer. That is why the pricing trap matters: choose the tool whose full-featured tier you can afford for a year. To connect your CRM to the rest of your stack, see our guide to cheaper Zapier alternatives. If you later hire and need marketing automation, compare GoHighLevel vs HubSpot first.

Our verdict

Start here if you want free

  • HubSpot CRM — the most complete free plan: 1,000 contacts, 2 users, pipelines, email tracking, scheduling at $0.
  • Attio — 3 seats and 50,000 records free, with unusual customization.
  • Capsule — the calm option: free for 2 users and 250 contacts, with zero upsell pressure.

Pay for one of these when

  • Pipedrive ($14/mo) — you run a real proposal-to-close pipeline and want the most CRM per dollar.
  • HoneyBook ($29/mo) — you want proposals, contracts, invoicing, and payments in one tool.
  • Streak ($49/mo) — you live in Gmail and will pay to keep everything in the inbox.
  • Less Annoying CRM ($15/mo) — for anyone burned by complexity before.

There is no wrong answer here, only a wrong budget: price the tier with the features you need, and decide where invoicing lives before you sign up. When a solo practice grows into a team serving multiple clients and retainers, see our best CRM for consultants and agencies (2026).

Frequently asked questions

Can I use a CRM for free as a freelancer?

Yes. HubSpot's free CRM covers up to 1,000 contacts and 2 users on newer accounts, Attio's free plan supports up to 3 seats and 50,000 records, and Capsule's free plan covers 2 users and 250 contacts. That is enough for most solo operators — but check the vendor pages, since free-plan limits change.

Is Streak still a good CRM for Gmail users in 2026?

Streak is still the most Gmail-native CRM option, with pipelines that live inside your inbox as a browser extension. But the free tier covers email tools only, not CRM pipelines, so you need Pro at $49 per user per month billed annually ($59 billed monthly). If you live in Gmail and want zero tab-switching, it is worth the money; if you just need Gmail sync, cheaper options do the job.

What is the cheapest CRM for a solo consultant?

Free options cost $0: HubSpot's free CRM (1,000 contacts, 2 users), Attio's free plan (3 seats, 50,000 records), and Capsule's free plan (2 users, 250 contacts). The cheapest paid solo options start around $14 to $15 a month: Pipedrive's Essential plan at $14 per user per month billed annually, and Less Annoying CRM's flat $15 per user per month.

Do I need a CRM that also handles invoicing?

Most CRMs do not invoice, so freelancers usually pair their CRM with an invoicing or accounting tool through a native or Zapier-style integration. If you want one tool that covers proposals, contracts, invoicing, and payments, HoneyBook is the specialist: its Starter plan costs $29 a month billed annually and includes unlimited clients and projects, invoicing, contracts, and a client portal.

How hard is it to migrate from a spreadsheet to a CRM?

A contacts-only migration from a spreadsheet typically takes under an hour: export to CSV, map the columns on import, and clean up duplicates. The harder part is history: email threads and past deals come over only if your CRM syncs your inbox, so connect Gmail or Outlook on day one. Moving between CRMs later is harder than leaving a spreadsheet, so pick the tool whose pricing you can live with for a year.

Conclusion

The best CRM for a freelancer matches your workflow and your budget, not the longest feature list. Start free with HubSpot, Attio, or Capsule and upgrade only when a limit constrains you. If you want a paid pipeline from day one, Pipedrive's $14-a-month Essential plan is the value pick. If admin sprawl is eating your week, HoneyBook's $29-a-month Starter plan replaces your proposal, contract, and invoicing tools. And if Gmail is your office, Streak's $49-a-month Pro plan buys a CRM that never asks you to leave the inbox.
