Relay.app shutdown: best no-code AI agent alternatives after September 2026

Relay.app was the tool small teams reached for when Zapier felt too rigid and Make felt too technical: AI agents that watched your inbox, drafted replies, summarized documents, and — most importantly — paused for a human to approve anything risky before it ran. When the shutdown was announced in July 2026, thousands of workflows went with it. This guide confirms what happened, compares the six strongest replacements, and walks through rebuilding your automations. If you are comparing the two biggest names first, our n8n vs Make comparison for small business is a good primer.

What happened to Relay.app?

The shutdown is confirmed and complete. Here is the timeline:

  • July 16, 2026: Relay.app announced it was winding down, with no explanation and no named successor. New signups and upgrades were disabled immediately.
  • August 15, 2026 (11:59 PM PT): free accounts — and all of their data — were permanently deleted.
  • September 14, 2026: paid accounts stopped working and their data was deleted. Until then, paying customers had 60 days of free access with extra AI credits, and annual customers received automatic prorated refunds.
  • After September 14, 2026: the site serves only a shutdown notice. Nothing can be exported any more.

Relay was founded in 2021 by Jacob Bank — previously a co-founder of Timeful (acquired by Google in 2015) and a product lead at Google for Gmail, Calendar, and Chat. It raised about $8.2 million in seed funding from Khosla Ventures and Andreessen Horowitz. After the shutdown, Bank and some Relay staff joined Google's Chrome team, with Bank as VP of Product for Chrome. Critically, neither Bank nor Google has ever described this as an acquisition — it was a shutdown plus a set of hires.

The hard truth: if you exported workflows and run history before the deadline, you have a blueprint to rebuild from. If you did not, those exports are gone forever, and migration means rebuilding each workflow from memory.

What made Relay.app different — and what to look for in a replacement

Before comparing tools, it helps to name exactly what Relay users lost, so you can check each alternative against it:

  • Human-in-the-loop by default. Any AI step could pause and send its output to a human via email or Slack for review before the workflow continued — Relay's signature feature and the hardest to replace.
  • AI-native, not AI-bolted-on. Relay described workflows in plain language, with AI steps (classify, summarize, draft, extract) as first-class citizens.
  • Small-team friendly. Roughly 100+ app integrations on every pricing tier, positioned as "the new Zapier" for teams that wanted AI without engineering.
  • Structured data and MCP support. Later versions supported tables, MCP servers, and run history — features power users relied on.

Shortlist on four things: a free or cheap start, real AI agent capability, some form of human approval, and enough integrations to cover your stack. Our guide to AI agent builders for non-developers covers the broader landscape if you want more options beyond the six below.

The 6 best no-code AI agent alternatives

1. Make — the closest all-round replacement

Make is the single best default choice for most former Relay users. It is a visual automation platform with 3,000+ app integrations, a dedicated AI Agents product, and Maia — a conversational copilot that builds and repairs scenarios from plain-language prompts. Make also supports native MCP servers, so agents you build can be reached from other AI tools. The free plan gives you 1,000 credits a month; the Core paid plan costs about $10.59 a month ($9 billed annually) for 10,000 credits, with AI Agents bundled into every paid plan. The migration pattern is simple: paste your saved Relay build-prompt export into Make's AI builder to regenerate the scenario, then reconnect your app credentials. Our Make vs Zapier guide for beginners covers the platform in depth.

2. Zapier Agents — the biggest app catalog, at the biggest price

Zapier runs Agents as a separate layer on top of the largest integration catalog in no-code automation. You write plain-English instructions about what should trigger the agent and what it may touch, attach actions as tools and documents as knowledge, and the agent runs on a schedule, reacts to app events, answers in chat, or is triggered from a Zap or MCP server. Zapier Copilot helps with setup, and the related Canvas product visually maps how work flows across apps.

The catch is pricing. The Agents free tier offers about 400 activities a month (capped at 10 per run); Pro starts around $33.33 a month on annual billing — on top of the base Zapier plan (from $19.99 a month for 750 tasks). Agent activities are billed separately from Zap tasks, and AI model steps moved to model-tiered pricing in mid-2026. Choose Zapier if your stack depends on apps only it connects to. See our cheaper Zapier alternatives if cost is your main trigger.

3. n8n — the most powerful option, if you can handle the learning curve

n8n is the platform technical teams reach for when they want full control. It ships dozens of dedicated AI nodes (LLM connectors, memory backends, agent tools), a Code node for JavaScript or Python, and support for self-hosted models — the closest thing to Relay's advanced end without writing a whole app. Pricing is fair-code: the Community Edition is free to self-host (you pay for a server, roughly $5 to $10 a month), while n8n Cloud starts at €24 a month (€20 billed annually) for 2,500 executions, counted per whole workflow run. The trade-off is real: n8n expects comfort with JSON and APIs. Non-technical owners will find Make or Gumloop gentler. Our Activepieces vs n8n comparison covers the open-source end of the market.

4. Lindy — the closest to Relay's conversational feel

Lindy frames itself as an "AI teammate" rather than a workflow canvas: you delegate in chat or Slack, save repeatable work as skills and scheduled routines, and Lindy handles inbox management, meeting notes, scheduling, and follow-up. If what you loved about Relay was describing work in words and getting results, Lindy is the closest match in spirit. Approvals are a platform feature — Lindy enforces a read/write split so write actions wait for your sign-off, recreating Relay's human-in-the-loop without DIY workarounds — and model selection is included.

Pricing is per user per month on credits: Plus at $29.99 (3,000 credits), Pro at $99.99 (15,000), Max at $199.99 (35,000), with top-ups at $10 per 1,000 credits. There is no permanent free plan — just a 7-day trial — so budget-conscious solo users should look at Make or Gumloop first.

5. Gumloop — describe it in plain English, get a workflow

Gumloop is a no-code builder for AI-powered workflows with a standout feature: Gummie, its built-in AI assistant, can build an entire workflow just by describing what you want in plain language — the closest thing to Relay's old build-prompt experience. It also bundles access to premium LLMs into its plans, so you do not need separate API keys, and it offers both workflow automation and autonomous agents in one place. The free tier is genuinely useful: $0 a month with 5,000 credits, one seat, and one active trigger. Pro starts at $37 a month with 20,000+ credits and unlimited seats. The honest caveat: it is a young platform, the UI changes often, and large workflows need careful organization. For a solo operator replacing simple Relay flows, it is one of the fastest starts available.

6. Relevance AI — multi-agent teams with a free tier

Relevance AI is built around "AI Workers" — agents with defined roles, skills, knowledge bases, and escalation rules that can work together as multi-agent teams. The marketplace of pre-built agents accelerates deployment, and configurable approval paths replace Relay's human-in-the-loop toggles. Pricing is usage-based around "Actions": a free plan (about 200 actions a month, one user) for testing, and paid plans starting around $19 a month on annual billing (about 2,500 actions plus monthly model credits). Team-tier plans around $349 a month suit production multi-agent use — a significant jump, so confirm your volume justifies it. Relevance AI has reworked its pricing more than once in 2026, so check the current tiers on its pricing page before committing.

Which alternative fits your team

If you want the closest overall replacement

  • Pick Make. AI Agents bundled into every paid plan, a conversational builder that regenerates workflows from plain descriptions, MCP support, and the lowest paid entry price of the group.

If human approvals were your whole workflow

  • Pick Lindy or Relevance AI. Both have approval/escalation as a platform feature, not a workaround — the truest match for Relay's signature toggle.

If you are non-technical and on a budget

  • Pick Gumloop. Free tier with 5,000 credits a month, Gummie builds flows from your description, and Pro is a flat $37 with unlimited seats.

If you need apps only Zapier connects

  • Pick Zapier Agents. Unmatched integration breadth, but budget for the add-on: roughly $53/month stacked on a Pro plan before a single agent run.

Two more worth a sentence each. n8n is the pick if someone on your team is technical and you want self-hosting, private models, or the lowest cost at high volume. Relevance AI fits teams deploying multi-agent workers (research, outreach, ops) from a pre-built marketplace. How to migrate your Relay workflows

There is no one-click importer from Relay to anything — the exports were only available while accounts were live, and they are gone now. Migration is a rebuild, but you can make it fast and safe:

1. Inventory what you lost

List every workflow from memory, screenshots, Slack messages, or the emails Relay sent. Rank them by business impact: start with the flow that touches revenue or customers, not the easiest one. For each, note the trigger, the apps, the AI steps, and which steps had human approval.

2. Use whatever exports you saved

If you downloaded JSON exports or used Relay's "Copy build prompt" before the deadline, paste that plain-language description into Make's Maia, Gumloop's Gummie, or Lindy's chat and let the AI builder regenerate the workflow. If you exported run history or tables as CSV, import those first so the rebuilt workflow has real records to work with.

3. Rebuild approval gates first, not last

Relay's human-in-the-loop was its signature, so reproduce it deliberately: in Lindy and Relevance AI, use their built-in approval/escalation steps; in Make, add a filter branch that sends AI output to Slack or email and only continues on approval; in Zapier, use Paths with a human review step. Never let a rebuilt agent take write actions unsupervised on day one.

3. Reconnect credentials and test in parallel

Reconnect every app with fresh credentials (Relay's stored tokens were deleted with the accounts). Run each rebuilt workflow on a small, safe subset of data first, comparing outputs against known-good examples if you saved any. Give yourself a week of parallel running before switching off the manual workarounds you adopted during the shutdown window.

Timing tip: Make's free plan (1,000 credits/month) and Gumloop's free tier (5,000 credits/month) are both roomy enough to rebuild and test your first two or three workflows before spending anything.

Frequently asked questions

Did Relay.app really shut down?

Yes. The wind-down was announced July 16, 2026; free accounts and their data were deleted August 15, 2026, and paid accounts stopped working September 14, 2026. Founder Jacob Bank joined Google as VP of Product for Chrome — a shutdown plus hires, not an acquisition.

Can I still export my Relay.app workflows?

No. Export tools (workflows as JSON or build-prompt text, run history as CSV) were only available while accounts were active. Everything was permanently deleted after September 14, 2026.

What is the closest Relay.app alternative?

Make is the closest for most teams: AI Agents bundled into every paid plan, a conversational builder, human-in-the-loop approvals, native MCP support, and far lower cost than Zapier's agent add-on. Lindy is closest for conversational agents; Gumloop for plain-English building.

How do I recreate Relay's human-in-the-loop approvals?

Lindy and Relevance AI have built-in approval or escalation steps. In Make, add a filter branch that sends AI output to Slack or email and resumes on approval; in Zapier, use Paths with a human review step. Keep approvals on for the first week before loosening them.

Is there a free Relay.app alternative?

Yes. Make's free plan (1,000 credits/month), Gumloop's free tier (5,000 credits/month), and Relevance AI's free tier (about 200 actions/month) all work without paying. n8n's Community Edition is free to self-host, though you pay for the server.

Conclusion

Relay.app's shutdown is final — free accounts gone August 15, 2026, paid accounts gone September 14 — so the only productive move is picking a replacement. For most small teams, Make is the natural landing spot: bundled AI Agents, a plain-English builder, and the cheapest paid entry point. Lindy fits conversational teammates with built-in approvals; Gumloop is the fastest free start. Zapier Agents wins on app coverage but costs the most; n8n wins on power but demands technical comfort; Relevance AI fits multi-agent teams. Start free, rebuild your approval gates first, and run in parallel for a week.
